## 0. Reading by the coordinating session

Self-questioning (methodology 11, item 2). Before the dossier the coordinating session expected this problem to rank
first, because the Riemann proof of 2026-11-20 contains Tate-Shafarevich bounds, an Euler system and Iwasawa theory.
The dossier overturns that expectation on its primary source: the bound in the Riemann proof is for
Sha^1(G_(E,S), Q_l/Z_l(-e)) of cyclotomic fields with Tate twists e >= 1, and its Euler-system input is used for
newforms of weight 2e + 1 >= 3. Weight 2, the weight of an elliptic curve, is excluded by the hypothesis as stated.
The machinery is native to the field and brings no mechanism for rank two.

Where the prize statement stands [D, from Sections 2 and 6]. The prize statement is the rank statement for every
elliptic curve over Q. If the unrefereed claim of October 2026 holds (the converse for Selmer corank at most 1 at
every prime, no Lean artifact), the statement is open exactly on the set S of curves whose p-power Selmer corank is
at least 2 at every prime p. S contains every curve of rank at least 2, and also any curve of rank at most 1 whose
Tate-Shafarevich group has an infinitely divisible part at every prime. On S two things are missing and neither has a
known mechanism: a construction of a nonzero class from the second derivative of the L-function (to bound the rank
from below), and a comparison between the complex order of vanishing and the p-adic one or the Selmer corank (to
bound it from above) beyond order one.

Consequence for the choice. No row of Section 5 moves the prize statement in days; the concrete first steps are
numerical reproductions in the CM case of rank two and audits of the October claim. The problem is not chosen as the
target (Section 11).

## Summary of the dossier

1. The prize statement is the rank half only for elliptic curves over Q (Wiles's description): L(C,s) = c(s-1)^r + higher terms, c nonzero, r = rank C(Q). The refined formula and finiteness of Sha are a Remark, not the prize statement. Clay lists the problem as "Unsolved" on 2026-11-24 [P].
2. The one 2026 claim that touches the prize statement itself is the OpenAI release of 2026-10-06/07 (repository openai/math, 722 manuscripts). Family 002 claims the full BSD leading-term formula, and the rank statement, for every E/Q whose full q-power Selmer group has corank 0 or 1 for some prime q; family 006 claims Goldfeld's conjecture unconditionally. Both are unrefereed, have no Lean link in the catalogue, and the repository README says "Some of the unformalized results could have issues" [P]. The claim does not cover any curve with Selmer corank at least 2 at every prime, which includes every curve of rank at least 2. So even if it is correct, the prize statement stays open for exactly the curves that were always the hard ones.
3. The Riemann-proof Tate-Shafarevich bound (Alpoge, 2026-11-20) is about Ш^1(G_{E,S}, Q_l/Z_l(-e)) for cyclotomic fields E and Tate twists 0 < e < x^401, not about elliptic curves. Its Selmer-vanishing input (Kato's Euler system plus Kolyvagin's argument) is applied to 2-dimensional representations attached to l-ordinary newforms of weight 2e+1 with e >= 1, so weight at least 3 [P, blueprint]. It contains no new rank-at-least-2 mechanism.
4. The frontier is unchanged in kind: every proven case rests on a nonzero object produced by analytic nonvanishing (Kato zeta element, Heegner point) or on Selmer-to-analytic converses at corank at most 1. There is no construction of a nonzero global class or point from L''(E,1) or from a Selmer corank 2. 2026 human work in rank 2 is CM-only and p-adic (Banwait 2609.08431; Calonge Fabregat 2611.09192).
5. Concrete first steps are in D5 (18 rows, 10 cross-field) and seven precise targets in D6.

## 1. The prize statement

Source: Clay official problem description by Andrew Wiles, https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/birchswin.pdf, read 2026-11-24, archived `src/birch.pdf` (SHA-256 c25dc966dd0051a60ac04593aef344d8aa89a51abedccbedadc5dc42099ddc6a), text `src/birch.txt`. [P]

Exact statement (p. 2), quoted: "Conjecture (Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer). The Taylor expansion of L(C, s) at s = 1 has the form L(C, s) = c(s - 1)^r + higher order terms with c != 0 and r = rank(C(Q)). In particular this conjecture asserts that L(C, 1) = 0 <=> C(Q) is infinite." Here C is an elliptic curve over Q, "L(C, s) := prod over p not dividing 2 Delta of (1 - a_p p^(-s) + p^(1-2s))^(-1)" is the incomplete L-series, whose holomorphic continuation is stated as proved ("This has now been proved ([25], [24], [1])", Wiles, Taylor-Wiles, Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor). The group is C(Q) = Z^r + torsion (Mordell). The Taylor expansion is that of the incomplete series, which differs from L*(C,s) only by nonzero constants at s = 1, so the order and the nonvanishing of c are unaffected [K].

Content of the prize statement, in my words (D): for every elliptic curve C over Q, ord_{s=1} L(C,s) = rank C(Q), and the leading coefficient is nonzero (automatic for the order). The prize statement is not the refined formula and not finiteness of Sha.

Remarks of the same document that are NOT the prize statement (quoted):
- Remark 1: "L*(C, s) ~ c*(s - 1)^r with c* = |Sha_C| R_inf w_inf prod_{p|2 Delta} w_p / |C(Q)_tors|^2 ... a group which is not known in general to be finite although it is conjectured to be so ... It is hoped that a proof of the conjecture would also yield a proof of the finiteness of Sha_C."
- Remark 2: "The conjecture can also be stated over any number field as well as for abelian varieties".
- Remark 3: function fields: "It has been proved in this case by Artin and Tate that the L-series has a zero of order at least r, but the conjecture itself remains unproved. In the function field case it is now known to be equivalent to the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group".
- Remark 4: "one only needs the integrality of the term Sha_C in the expression for L*(C, s) above ... shown by Manin subject to modularity".

Neighbouring statements that are NOT the prize statement: (a) the refined (strong) formula with the Sha term and regulator; (b) finiteness of Sha(E/Q); (c) BSD over number fields, for abelian varieties, for function fields; (d) the p-parts and the p-converse statements (Selmer corank equals analytic order for one prime p); (e) the parity conjecture; (f) Goldfeld's conjecture on average rank of quadratic twists; (g) the Beilinson-Bloch-Kato and Tamagawa number conjectures for general motives; (h) "BSD for a density-one or positive-proportion set of twists or curves" (a family result, not the statement for every curve).

## 4. Solved analogues and model cases

1. Function fields. Wiles's text, Remark 3 [P]: "It has been proved in this case by Artin and Tate that the L-series has a zero of order at least r, but the conjecture itself remains unproved. In the function field case it is now known to be equivalent to the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group". Mechanism [K]: the L-function is a characteristic polynomial of Frobenius on l-adic cohomology of the Neron model over a curve over F_q, so ord at s=1 is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue q, bounded below by the rank through the Tate conjecture for the elliptic surface (Artin-Tate); equality is equivalent to the Tate conjecture for the surface, which is equivalent to finiteness of Sha or of the Brauer group (Milne, Tate). Kato-Trihan (2003) prove BSD for such curves when Sha[p^inf] is finite for one p [K]. Families of large rank over F_q(t): Ulmer (Annals 2002) [K]. Higher Gross-Zagier: Yun-Zhang, "Shtukas and the Taylor expansion of L-functions" (Annals 2017) and (II) arXiv:1712.08026 [P title]: derivative of order r of the L-function expressed through intersection numbers of Heegner-Drinfeld cycles on moduli of shtukas, in function fields only. What transfers: the shape of the formula (higher derivative equals a height of a canonical cycle). What does not: Frobenius and its eigenvalue interpretation, and moduli of shtukas, have no analogue for Spec Z; the rank statement in function fields is a theorem about a finite-dimensional vector space with a Frobenius action, and the number field L-function has no such structure.
2. CM curves. Coates-Wiles; Rubin's Tamagawa result for the p-part [K]; rank 0 converse at all p: Burungale-Tian [P]. Mechanism: Iwasawa theory over the imaginary quadratic field and elliptic units give a main conjecture proved (Rubin); L-function equals a Hecke L-function. Transfers to GL2-type via base change only after Skinner-Urban. Does not transfer to rank 2: 2026 work stays p-adic and conditional [P rows 11 and 12 of D2].
3. Analytic rank 0 and 1 over Q: Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin, Kato, Skinner-Urban (D3 rows 2 to 4); over totally real fields: Zhang (Gross-Zagier for Shimura curves), Nekovar [K].
4. Iwasawa-theoretic analogue: the main conjecture for E over the cyclotomic Z_p-extension (Kato one divisibility, Skinner-Urban the other); the Iwasawa algebra's characteristic ideal controls Selmer corank in the tower, but the specialization at the trivial character is where a pole of the control theorem appears (exceptional zeros, Castella arXiv:2409.01360 [P title]).
5. Sha in families: for twist families, Smith [P titles] gives the distribution of the Selmer 2-infinity groups; the Poonen-Rains and BKLPR models (arXiv:1009.0287, 1304.3971 [P titles]) predict the distribution; this settles the analogue "average" statements, not individual curves.
6. What provably does not transfer (D): any argument whose nonzero object comes from a value L(E,1) or L'(E,1); any argument that needs a Frobenius; averaging arguments (they give zero proportion information about rank at least 2 curves).

## 6. Candidate targets

Write E/Q elliptic, L(E,s) its L-series, a(E) = ord_{s=1} L(E,s), r(E) = rank E(Q), s_p(E) as in D3, S = {E : s_p(E) >= 2 for all primes p}.

- T1 (the prize statement). For every E/Q, a(E) = r(E). Known: a(E) <= 1 (Gross-Zagier-Kolyvagin) [K]; claimed (unverified) for E not in S [D2 rows 1, 2]. Open because nothing produces a nonzero class from L''. Attacked by D5 rows 2, 3, 4, 7. Proof would be the prize.
- T1' (reduction after the OpenAI claim, if verified). For every E in S, a(E) = r(E). Known: S contains all curves with r >= 2; for E in S with a(E) <= 1 the statement already holds [K]. Open: as T1. Rows 2, 3, 4, 7, 16. Proof would give the prize statement with no assumption beyond the verification of row 1, D2.
- T2 (existence direction in rank 2). If a(E) = 2 then r(E) >= 2, for all E/Q. Known: only curve by curve, by exhibiting points. Open: no construction of two independent points from L''(E,1) = 0. Rows 2, 3, 7, 16. Proof gives the half r >= a of the prize statement for a(E) = 2.
- T3 (p-converse in corank 2). For a good ordinary prime p of E/Q: s_p(E) = 2 implies a(E) = 2 (Keller-Yin Conjecture A, quoted in `oa_converse` [P], states a(E) = s_p(E) in general). Known: corank <= 1 (claimed unconditional) [D2]. Open: a corank 2 Selmer group exhibits no class. Rows 2, 3, 4. Proof gives r = a for E with Sha[p^inf] finite and s_p = 2 once combined with T5.
- T4 (finiteness of Sha for one curve of rank at least 2). There exists E/Q with r(E) >= 2 and #Sha(E/Q) < infinity. Known: no such curve [K]; Banwait 2609.08431 gives a p-adic criterion for Sha[p^inf] = 0 for five CM curves for p < 30,000 [P]. Open: no method gives Sha[p] = 0 for all p simultaneously. Rows 4, 16. Proof is on the route to Remark 1 and to the strong form, not part of the prize rank statement.
- T5 (Selmer rank at most analytic rank). For every E/Q and prime p, s_p(E) <= a(E) + (parity defect 0), equivalently the divisible part of Sha[p^inf] is zero when s_p(E) = a(E). Known: a(E) <= 1 case [K]. Open: needs Sha[p^inf] finite. Rows 2, 4, 8. Proof plus Kato-type nonvanishing would turn T3 into T1 for corank >= 2.
- T6 (CM rank 2 p-adic equality). For E: y^2 = x^3 - D x of rank 2 and p = 1 mod 4 good: q_p = q_infinity, where q_p is the leading coefficient of the cyclotomic p-adic L-function divided by the Euler factor and the p-adic regulator, and q_infinity = L''(E,1) / (2 Omega_E Reg_infinity) is rational (Calonge Fabregat 2611.09192 [P]). Known: exact identity for the derived Heegner class (same paper). Open: the conjecture. Rows 2, 4. Proof would be the first rank-2 case of the p-adic formula and gives a(E) = 2 for those E.
- T7 (Goldfeld, unconditional). For every E/Q, among squarefree n, ord_{s=1} L(E^{(n)}, s) is 0 for density 1/2, 1 for density 1/2 [P claim, D2 row 3]. Open as a verified theorem. Rows 1, 18. Proof reduces the "average rank" part of the picture, leaves rank >= 2 twists (density 0) which is the prize problem.
