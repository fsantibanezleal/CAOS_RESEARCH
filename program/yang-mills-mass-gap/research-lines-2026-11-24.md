## 0. Reading by the coordinating session

Self-questioning (methodology 11, item 2). Before the dossier the coordinating session ranked this problem high on
one ground: with existence claimed, a single statement would be missing. The dossier confirms the single statement
and shows what it is. The committee that checked the Lean proof of 2026-10-28 writes that the submission constructs a
theory satisfying the Wightman axioms "as a scaling limit of four-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory for any
simple compact gauge group" and that it "does not prove that the infinite-volume limit is unique or that there is a
mass gap". The infinite-volume limit is taken along a subsequence by compactness and decomposed into pure phases; each
phase clusters, with no rate.

What the missing statement is [D, from Sections 2.1 and 6]. A mass gap for one pure phase is exponential clustering of
its Euclidean correlations with some rate m > 0 (target T5). A proof needs decay of correlations of the lattice
theory that is uniform in the lattice spacing along the asymptotically free trajectory, where the inverse coupling
grows like log(1/a) (target T3). The lattice methods that give decay (Dobrushin uniqueness, cluster expansions, the
Bakry-Emery criterion) work below a fixed inverse coupling; the renormalization group that built the continuum limit
works while the effective coupling is small. Between the two lies the regime of intermediate coupling at the physical
scale, for which no analytic tool exists and where a finite-size criterion would need certified integrals over
thousands of group variables. The construction contains no statement that decays with distance (text search of the
Lean source by the agent, weak evidence).

Consequence for the choice. The gap is one statement and it is the whole infrared problem. The first steps that can
be checked in days are readings (does any estimate of the construction decay with distance; is clustering in a phase
stated with a rate) and two-dimensional or strong-coupling exercises that do not reach the statement. The problem is
not chosen as the target (Section 11).

## Summary of the dossier

1. The prize statement is the Jaffe-Witten statement on the Clay page: for every compact simple gauge group G, a non-trivial quantum Yang-Mills theory exists on R^4 with a mass gap Delta > 0. Clay's label on 2026-11-24 is "Unsolved" [P].
2. The 2026 existence claim is real and its primary sources are open. OpenAI announced it on 2026-10-26 [P]. A five-member committee chaired by D. Gross published, on 2026-11-10, a 23-page validation (SHA-256 in `SHA256SUMS`) and the full Lean code [P]. Their judgment: the submission "proves that a relativistic quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms can be constructed as a scaling limit of four-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory for any simple compact gauge group. It does not prove that the infinite-volume limit is unique or that there is a mass gap." The committee also writes: "We do not claim to fully understand the proof yet."
3. The construction is a Balaban-type renormalization group on the Wilson lattice (fixed physical volume L_p, spacing a_N to 0 along a chosen sequence), then a subsequential infinite-volume limit by compactness (reflection positivity, chessboard estimates, transfer-operator representation), a decomposition into pure phases that cluster, and Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction per phase. No lattice-uniform decay estimate of connected correlations, no mass gap, no area law, no uniqueness, no probability measure on a continuum space, no Wilson loops are constructed (committee Section 8, five open questions) [P].
4. What a gap proof can reuse from the construction (all [P], committee text): a reflection-positive family of lattice measures, a fixed-volume continuum limit with local bounds that are uniform in the volume (Theorem 5.1, bounds (11)), a stress tensor with Ward identity, a running coupling with the correct b0, and a fully formalized OS reconstruction. What it lacks is exactly Clay's own bottleneck: Clay writes "no present ideas point the direction to establish the existence of a mass gap that is uniform in the volume" [P]; the committee lists "Establish a uniform mass gap" as open question 3 [P].
5. Everything else that claims the gap in 2026 is unrefereed or disputed (Section 2). The only machine-checked gap theorem found is a lattice, strong-coupling one (Lean repository `mrdouglasny/lgt`), which lives in the regime Osterwalder-Seiler already covered and does not reach the continuum.

## 1. The prize statement

### 1.1 The statement [P]

Source: Clay Mathematics Institute, A. Jaffe and E. Witten, "Quantum Yang-Mills Theory", PDF https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/yangmills.pdf, read 2026-11-24, archived at `src/clay_yangmills.pdf` (14 pages), SHA-256 `3558403ca14c11e382f73a09e548222708540bfdf478cf96aa11c52d43e23e09`.

Exact statement (page 6):

> "Yang-Mills Existence and Mass Gap. Prove that for any compact simple gauge group G, a non-trivial quantum Yang-Mills theory exists on R^4 and has a mass gap Delta > 0. Existence includes establishing axiomatic properties at least as strong as those cited in [45, 35]."

References [45] and [35] are Streater-Wightman and Osterwalder-Schrader. Definition of the gap in the same text: "A quantum field theory has a mass gap Delta if H has no spectrum in the interval (0, Delta) for some Delta > 0. The supremum of such Delta is the mass m, and we require m < infinity."

Other items fixed by the Clay text [P]: the theory must have local operators corresponding to gauge-invariant polynomials in the curvature F and its covariant derivatives, and the expected short-distance behaviour (asymptotic freedom, perturbative renormalization theory, operator product expansion), as the committee also reads it (Assessment, Section 1: four required features: a non-trivial axiomatic theory, Yang-Mills local fields, predicted short-distance behaviour, a positive mass gap).

What the statement says about method, same document, Section 6 [P]: "even having a detailed mathematical construction of Yang-Mills on a compact space would represent a major breakthrough. Yet, even if this were accomplished, no present ideas point the direction to establish the existence of a mass gap that is uniform in the volume. Nor do present methods suggest how to obtain the existence of the infinite volume limit T^4 to R^4." Section 6.6: the gap "may be tied to curvature in the space of connections, see [44]" and may need "a non-classical change of variables or duality transformation"; a 1/N string description "might give a clear-cut explanation of the mass gap and confinement and perhaps a good starting point for a rigorous proof (for sufficiently large N)".

### 2.1 What the construction is, as the committee records it [P, Assessment, Parts I and II]

- Gauge groups: "a closed matrix subgroup G in U(m) with nonabelian simple real Lie algebra g" (Theorem 4.1), claimed for every simple compact group; the committee glossary stresses the theory is "not free" (non-trivial: Theorem 7.1, a trace two-point function with leading law r^-8 log^-2(1/r)).
- Lattice and limit: Wilson action on a torus of side L_p and spacing a_N, "The construction chooses both sequences, and thus the UV convergence theorem does not assert convergence over all possible sequences of spacings." Fixed-volume continuum limit by an explicit RG map "following the general approach of Balaban but with many improvements such as a gauge fixing invariant under discrete lattice symmetries"; the inverse coupling satisfies beta_{N,p}/log(1/a_N) tending to 11 C_g /(24 pi^2) uniformly in the volume (equation (7)); correlations are taken with test functions supported away from coincident points. No universality over spacing sequences.
- Infinite volume: "only taken subsequentially"; one subsequence works for all correlations; bounds (11), sup |S_p| <= M_K and an equicontinuity modulus, "hold in fixed boxes uniformly in the volume", proved "through general arguments based on reflection positivity, chessboard estimates, transfer-operator representation, and Cauchy estimates".
- Axioms: the subsequential limit S_mix is decomposed (Gelfand and Riesz-Markov) as S_mix = integral of S_chi d mu(chi) over a compact metric space of characters; each component S_chi obeys OS axioms including clustering; OS reconstruction (proved in the submission) gives Wightman theories with positive energy and a unique translation-invariant vacuum per component. Clustering of S_mix itself is not proved, so the vacuum of the mixed theory is not proved unique. Euclidean invariance is obtained from a stress tensor with a divergence-free Ward identity and "reflection positivity in multiple orthogonal planes".
- Short distance: running coupling g_W(r) with log(1/r) g_W(r)^2 tending to a limit fixed by 1/(2 b0), b0 = 11 C_g /(48 pi^2) (equation (19); the text extraction lost the sign and the arrow, read the PDF for the exact form); OPE with remainder bounds; convergence along one sequence of stress-tensor correlations to those of dim g free Maxwell fields. Perturbative agreement to all orders "remains unproved".
- Not delivered (Section 8): (1) convergence and uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit; (2) a probability measure on a continuum space; (3) "a uniform mass gap, giving exponential decay of corresponding correlations and a spectral gap in the Osterwalder-Schrader Hamiltonian"; (4) Wilson loops and the area law; (5) single-particle states and their energy-momentum spectrum.
- Lean search of the clone (`repos/openai`, text search, no build): the declarations are opaquely named; I found no theorem whose name or statement expresses exponential clustering or a spectral gap of the continuum or lattice transfer operator uniform in (a, L). The word "chessboard" occurs in 9 files, "reflection" in many. This is a text search only, not a reading of the 100,168 relevant declarations.

## 3. Anatomy of the strongest approaches

Each entry: what it proves today; where it stops; named barrier; references. Statements of classical papers are [K] unless a [P] source is cited.

1. **The Jaffe-Witten statement and its torus programme.** Proves nothing; it fixes the target and states the route (construct on T^4, find a gap uniform in the volume, take T^4 to R^4). Stops at: "no present ideas point the direction to establish the existence of a mass gap that is uniform in the volume" [P]. Barrier: uniformity in volume of the gap, plus the infinite-volume limit. Reference: Clay PDF [P].
2. **Balaban's renormalization group and its descendants (Balaban 1980s; Magnen-Rivasseau-Seneor; Dimock's exposition; the 2026 OpenAI construction).** Proves, on the 3-torus and 4-torus in Balaban's papers [K], an ultraviolet stability bound for the effective action in a small-field region, and now (committee-checked) a fixed-volume continuum limit of correlations of local gauge-invariant observables on the 4-torus [P]. Dimock's account: the method is "illustrated with a treatment of the ultraviolet problem for the scalar phi^4 model on toroidal lattice in dimension d=3. In this second paper we control the large field contribution" [P, arXiv 1212.5562]. Stops at: the effective action stays on the weak-coupling RG trajectory at short distance; at the scale where the coupling becomes order one the expansion loses control, so the infrared (the gap, the infinite-volume limit) is not reached. Barrier: strong coupling at large distance in the continuum (the infrared problem, the transmutation of the scale). Magnen-Rivasseau-Seneor 1993 [K] gave a construction of the infrared-cutoff theory in a finite volume.
3. **Osterwalder-Seiler and the strong-coupling cluster expansion.** Proves, for the lattice theory at small inverse coupling beta, exponential decay of correlations (a gap) and an area law (Osterwalder-Seiler 1978, cited in Clay's reference list [36] [P]). Quantitative extensions: Shen-Zhu-Zhu prove uniqueness of the infinite-volume measure, exponential ergodicity of the Langevin dynamic, log-Sobolev and Poincare inequalities, large-N factorization and "a strictly positive mass gap" when |beta| < (N-2)/(32(d-1)N) for SO(N) or |beta| < 1/(16(d-1)) for SU(N) [P, arXiv 2204.12737]; Cao-Nissim-Sheffield enlarge the area-law regime when N is large [P, arXiv 2505.16585]. Stops at: the convergence radius is fixed in beta while asymptotic freedom sends beta to infinity as the spacing goes to 0 (the committee's equation (7): beta grows like (11 C_g / 24 pi^2) log(1/a)). Barrier: the strong coupling expansion lives on the wrong side of the roughening/crossover region; no phase-transition-free path to weak coupling is proved.
4. **Uniform lattice decay and the Tomboulis argument.** The programme: prove exponential decay of correlations, uniformly in a, along beta(a) from asymptotic freedom (Chatterjee's formulation, arXiv 1803.01950 [P]). Tomboulis's line: confinement for all beta by upper and lower bounds on the partition function and the twisted partition function through approximate decimations: "derivation of the statement that the SU(2) gauge theory is in a confining phase for all values of the coupling, 0 < beta < infinity" [P, arXiv hep-lat/0409019]; the Tomboulis-Yaffe inequality (from reflection positivity) says a box insensitive to boundary conditions has a gap, generalized to SU(N) by Kanazawa [P, arXiv 0808.3442]. Stops at: the published decimation bounds are an outline ("We outline the steps"), and I found no critique or acceptance of the full argument in the sources opened (searched: Tomboulis critique, Balaban critique; nothing). UNVERIFIED as to its status; treat as not accepted. Barrier: control of the vortex free energy and of decimation inequalities at weak coupling, uniformly.
5. **Two dimensions: exact solvability.** Yang-Mills measure on surfaces (Driver, Sengupta, Gross-King-Sengupta, Levy) [K]; large-N master field on the plane: Levy proves that "each Wilson loop converges in probability towards a deterministic limit" for orthogonal, unitary and symplectic groups [P, arXiv 1112.2452]. Langevin dynamic and state space in 2D: Chandra-Chevyrev-Hairer-Shen [P, arXiv 2006.04987]; invariance of the 2D Yang-Mills measure on the torus for the renormalized Langevin dynamic, with universality for Wilson, Villain and Manton lattice actions [P, arXiv 2302.12160]. Stops at: there is no local dynamics (the 2D theory has no propagating degrees of freedom: no particle, no gap question in the Wightman sense); barrier: dimensional (non-local area-law structure only).
6. **Three dimensions: stochastic quantisation.** Chandra-Chevyrev-Hairer-Shen construct a state space and Markov process for the stochastic quantisation of Yang-Mills-Higgs in 3D, with local-in-time solutions by regularity structures and gauge covariance "in law" fixed by a unique choice of counterterms [P, arXiv 2201.03487]. Karabali-Kim-Nair give a non-rigorous Hamiltonian analysis of the 2+1 dimensional string tension and gap [K, not opened]. Stops at: solutions are local in time, "up to a potential finite time blow-up"; global-in-time existence, invariant measure and a gap for pure 3D Yang-Mills are not proved (the 2D case has invariance, row 5). Barrier: the Yang-Mills flow's behaviour at large field and large time (global well-posedness, the spectral gap of the generator).
7. **Chatterjee's probabilistic programme.** (a) Formulation as probability problems [P, 1803.01950]. (b) Confinement from unbroken center symmetry: "a theory is confining when center symmetry is unbroken", with the sufficient condition "if the center of the gauge group is nontrivial, and correlations decay exponentially under arbitrary boundary conditions, then center symmetry does not break" [P, arXiv 2006.16229]; this is the same structure as Tomboulis-Yaffe, boundary-condition insensitivity. (c) The first scaling limit of a non-abelian theory above two dimensions: SU(2) lattice Yang-Mills-Higgs, where a stereographic projection of the gauge field converges to a massive Gaussian field; "the question of constructing a non-Gaussian scaling limit remains open" [P, arXiv 2401.10507]. (d) Strongly coupled SO(N) at large N: Wilson loops as an absolutely convergent string sum [P, arXiv 1502.07719]. (e) Leading term of the free energy (cited as Cha1 in [P, arXiv 2511.07297], Brennecke). Adhikari-Cao: exponential decay of correlations for finite lattice gauge groups at weak coupling, including arbitrary functions of Wilson loops [P, arXiv 2202.10375]; Cao-Park-Sheffield: Wilson loop expectations as sums over embedded planar maps for any N, beta, d [P, arXiv 2307.06790]. Stops at: finite groups and Higgs-massive regimes, or strong coupling; the continuous non-abelian group at weak coupling without Higgs is the gap. Barrier: weak-coupling correlation decay needs a mechanism that, for continuous groups, has no massive Gaussian fixed point.
8. **Stochastic quantization and Bakry-Emery arguments.** Shen-Zhu-Zhu: the Langevin dynamic of the lattice Yang-Mills model at strong coupling satisfies functional inequalities, from which the gap follows (row 3). Mechanism: if the generator L satisfies a Bakry-Emery condition Ric_infinity of the potential (curvature of the action plus curvature of the group) at least rho > 0 then log-Sobolev holds with constant rho and the measure has a spectral gap at least rho [K]. Stops at: the Hessian of the Wilson action has negative directions (plaquette products can lower the curvature) once beta is of order one; the condition fails, the barrier is the loss of uniform convexity (the same convergence radius as row 3). The positive curvature of the gauge-orbit space cited by Clay ("tied to curvature in the space of connections, see [44]", Singer [K]) is the geometric ingredient that could repair this, not yet used rigorously.
9. **Abelian models and the Higgs mechanism.** Frohlich-Spencer and Guth for U(1) (confinement and its absence by dimension and coupling) [K]; Balaban-Imbrie-Jaffe: "The mass gap for Higgs models on a unit lattice" is Clay's reference [4] [P, reference list]; Balaban-Brydges-Imbrie-Jaffe [P, same list]; Chatterjee's massive Gaussian limit [P, 2401.10507]; A 2025 preprint on small-mass U(1) Higgs correlation decay (authors not recorded) "extends the classical result of Osterwalder and Seiler (1978), who required in addition that m be sufficiently large" [P, arXiv 2509.19176]. Stops at: in these models the gap is a mass of the Higgs or photon obtained from a perturbative expansion around a Gaussian; pure non-abelian Yang-Mills has no such classical mass. Barrier: no classically visible gap, as Clay writes ("the mass gap is not classically visible").
10. **Supersymmetric and holographic evidence.** Seiberg-Witten: monopole condensation and confinement in N=2 supersymmetric Yang-Mills [P, arXiv hep-th/9407087, title and date only]; Witten: holographic description of thermal phase transition and confinement in gauge theories [P, arXiv hep-th/9803131, title only]. Both are exact or large-N results for different theories (supersymmetric, or strongly coupled at infinite N); stops at the lack of a rigorous bridge to pure SU(N). Barrier: no rigorous gauge-string duality; Clay: "no effective approach to the gauge theory without fermions" [P].
11. **Functional methods (Dyson-Schwinger, Gribov-Zwanziger) and numerical lattice gauge theory.** Dyson-Schwinger and Gribov-Zwanziger give approximate infrared propagators, not controlled errors [K, not opened]. Numerical: the SU(3) glueball spectrum for beta up to 6.50 extrapolated to the continuum "using the confining string tension as our energy scale" [P, arXiv 2007.06422]; early anisotropic study [P, arXiv hep-lat/9901004, title only]. Matrix bootstrap: Kazakov-Zheng obtain "rigorous upper and lower bounds on the plaquette average" for SU(infinity) lattice Yang-Mills in D = 2, 3, 4 combining Makeenko-Migdal loop equations with positivity of Wilson loop matrices [P, arXiv 2203.11360]. Stops at: numerics are evidence, not proof; the bootstrap gives two-sided bounds on plaquette averages, not on the exponential rate. Barrier: certification and the continuum extrapolation.

## 6. Candidate targets

Notation: G compact simple, mu_{a,L} the Wilson measure on the torus (Z/(L/a)Z)^4 with beta = beta(a), T_{a,L} its reflection-positive transfer operator, H_{a,L} = -(1/a) log T_{a,L}.

**T1 (the prize).** For every compact simple G there is a non-trivial theory on R^4 satisfying the Wightman or OS axioms with spec(H) intersect (0, Delta) empty for some Delta > 0. Known: existence of a Wightman theory per pure phase for every simple compact G (subsequential limit, committee) [P]. Open because: uniqueness, a gap, a measure, Wilson loops all unproved [P]. Attacked by rows 1 to 4, 6, 11. Meaning: the prize itself, after Clay's two-year acceptance rule.

**T2 (uniform finite-volume gap).** There exist Delta_0 > 0 and L_0 such that, in the fixed-volume continuum limit of the 2026 construction, the Hamiltonian of the theory on the torus of side L satisfies spec(H_L) intersect (0, Delta_0) = {} (except the vacuum) for all L >= L_0, uniformly in the volume. Known: a gap exists at each finite lattice by Perron-Frobenius [K]; Clay says no idea exists for the uniform one [P]. Open because: no uniform control of the second eigenvalue. Attacked by rows 1, 2, 3, 14. Meaning: with the infinite-volume limit (T4) it gives T1 via Clay's route.

**T3 (a-uniform lattice decay).** Along beta(a) from asymptotic freedom (beta(a) ~ (11 C_g/(24 pi^2)) log(1/a) [P, equation (7)]), there are c, C > 0 independent of a and of L such that for gauge-invariant local observables O_x, O_y with ||O|| <= 1: |<O_x O_y> - <O_x><O_y>| <= C exp(-c m |x - y|), with m the physical mass in units where the physical length is fixed, i.e. a xi_lattice(beta(a)) bounded as a tends to 0. Known: true for beta < 1/(16(d-1)) for SU(N) [P, 2204.12737]; Lean proof for U(n), beta < 1/(32 n (d-1)) [P]. Open because: the radius does not move with asymptotic freedom. Attacked by rows 2, 3, 5, 6, 14. Meaning: with Euclidean covariance, T3 gives the gap by OS reconstruction in each pure phase (row 11).

**T4 (uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit).** The limit L to infinity of the fixed-volume continuum correlations S_p exists and S_mix equals its own phase components: S_chi = S_mix for mu-almost every chi. Known: only subsequential limits; clustering of S_mix would force all components to coincide [P, Assessment Section 6.2]. Open because: infrared control. Attacked by rows 1, 3, 11, 12. Meaning: the committee's first open question; a precondition for the "unique vacuum" form of T1.

**T5 (Euclidean clustering with a rate in a pure phase).** For some chi in the character space of the 2026 theory, there are m > 0, C such that for local operators O_1, O_2 with supports at distance r: |S_chi(O_1 O_2) - S_chi(O_1) S_chi(O_2)| <= C(O_1, O_2) exp(-m r). Known: each S_chi clusters without a rate [P]. Open because: no spectral information. Attacked by rows 1, 11, 12. Meaning: by the OS construction this is the existence of a mass gap Delta >= m for the Wightman theory of that phase, a gap for one phase.

**T6 (large-N gap).** For SU(N) with 't Hooft coupling lambda = g^2 N fixed along the asymptotically free trajectory, the master field's Wilson-loop correlations at N = infinity decay exponentially: |W(l_1 union l_2) - W(l_1) W(l_2)| <= C exp(-m dist(l_1, l_2)) uniformly in a, with corrections O(1/N^2) uniform in a. Known: strong coupling [P, 2204.12737, 1502.07719]; loop-equation bounds on plaquette averages [P, 2203.11360]. Open because: no uniformity in a. Attacked by rows 6, 7. Meaning: a gap at large N uniform in a, which with controlled 1/N corrections gives T1 for large N, the case Clay mentions ("for sufficiently large N") [P].

**T7 (Tomboulis-Yaffe form).** For SU(2), define the twisted partition function ratio R(beta, L) = Z_twisted / Z. If R(beta(a), L) >= c > 0 uniformly for L >= L_0, then the transfer-operator gap satisfies m >= f(c) > 0. Known: the inequality, for SU(N) and general classical spin systems, by reflection positivity [P, arXiv 0808.3442]; Tomboulis's claim of the lower bound on R for all beta is only an outline [P, hep-lat/0409019]. Open because: the lower bound on R at weak coupling. Attacked by rows 2, 14. Meaning: a gap from a free-energy statement; the proof of the lower bound would be a boundary-condition insensitivity theorem.

**T8 (existence of a single-particle state, upper gap).** Under T5, the spectrum of H in the pure phase contains an isolated eigenvalue m_1 of the mass operator with spec(H^2 - P^2) in {0} union {m_1^2} union [m_2^2, infinity), m_2 > m_1 (the committee's open question 5; Clay's "upper gap" [P]). Known: nothing in 4D; established in many 2D and 3D constructive models [P, Assessment Section 8]. Meaning: beyond the prize.
