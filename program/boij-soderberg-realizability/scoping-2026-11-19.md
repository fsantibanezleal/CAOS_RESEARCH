## Problem statement and status

S = k[x,y,z] standard graded. A degree sequence d = (0, d1, d2, d3) indexes an extremal ray of the Boij-Soderberg cone of
finite length S-modules (Eisenbud-Schreyer 2009); its integral points are the multiples m beta_d of the primitive
Herzog-Kuhl point, beta_d proportional to (prod over j != i of |d_i - d_j|^-1). An integral point is realizable over a
graded algebra A if some finite length graded A-module has exactly that pure Betti table.

- **Erman-Sam Question 6.1** (arXiv:1606.01867, Section 6): is every integral point of every pure ray in three variables
  realizable over S or over U(H), the enveloping algebra of the Heisenberg Lie algebra ([x,y] = z, deg z = 2)?
  **Answered negatively** by Jiang, Li, Sun, Wang, Xiao, Yu with the Rethlas system (arXiv:2605.25259v2): the primitive
  point (1,2,9,8) of (0,6,20,21) fails over S by Krull's height theorem and over U(H) by the Hilbert-series parity test
  p(-1) != 0 [V]. Question 6.2 (any n, any graded Lie algebra) is answered negatively with (0,1,4,5,6) [V].
- Nothing beyond one point per question is characterized: no smallest counterexample, no classification, no statement on
  multiples [V by reading].
- **Erman 2009, Section 7 question (2)** (arXiv:0806.4401): the least realizable multiple c_d of each ray; open in
  general, no table in three variables [V for the question; the absence of a table rests on the searches of the dossier
  and of the literature pass of 2026-11-19].
- **EFW Conjecture 6.1** (arXiv:0709.1529): every sufficiently large integral point of a pure ray is realized; open
  (Erman-Sam 2016, Section 8) [V].

## Why the reference matters

A question posed by two leaders of the field, answered in May 2026 by an AI system with a single example, and left
without any structural follow-up for six months. The dossier already holds two smaller counterexamples (d3 = 10 against
the published 21), obtained from published obstructions [D]. Three-variable realizability is a finite, exactly computable
surface ray by ray, with mature tooling (Macaulay2 BoijSoederberg, randomSocleModule, randomModule; Singular).

## Feasibility class and surface

Class A (exact, finite per point). Exact surface: Betti tables of explicit modules over prime fields and over QQ
(Macaulay2 and Singular resolutions are exact); formula constructions; published obstructions as proofs. Numeric surface:
none. The 220 rays with d3 <= 12 form the first window (84 with d3 <= 9).

## Known constructions, obstructions and closest prior work

Constructions realizing a multiple of the primitive point: Eisenbud-Schreyer (any field; multiple 1 to 5775 on the
window), Eisenbud-Floystad-Weyman (characteristic 0, two constructions), tensor complexes (Berkesch-Erman-Kummini-Sam),
the classical complexes (Koszul, S/m^a, its dual, generic Pfaffians, Buchsbaum-Rim), the flat scaling x to x^e and
Ext^3 duality. Obstructions: Erman 2009 Proposition 3.1 (second syzygy, codimension with its Buchsbaum-Rim equality case,
regularity), Buchsbaum-Eisenbud (codimension 3 Gorenstein ideals have an odd number of generators), the regular-sequence
argument for cyclic points with three relations, Krull. Over U(H): the parity test p(-1) = 0, scale invariant, kills 191
of the 220 rays [D]; Erman-Sam realize (0,1,3,4) over U(H) by the Chevalley-Eilenberg resolution of k [V].

## Invariants and lenses

Invariants: the primitive point, the U(H) parity p(-1), the Hilbert function over S and over U(H), gcd(d), the
duality partner (0, d3 - d2, d3 - d1, d3), the obstruction lower bound and the construction upper bound on c_d.
Lenses: (1) exact census with formula bounds and closures (scaling up, obstructions down, duality); (2) random and
structured module search (generic level modules, generic presentations, inverse systems); (3) obstructions (linear
strand and maximal minors, Buchsbaum-Eisenbud-type structure in codimension 3, almost complete intersections); (4) U(H)
as a cubic Artin-Schelter regular algebra (noncommutative Groebner bases and resolutions); (5) characteristic
dependence.

## Compute, costs and stop conditions

Stage 0 is seconds. A random trial resolves a module of length at most a few thousand over GF(32003): seconds to minutes;
about 150 undecided rays times a few multiples times a few trials fits in hours on the workstation. QQ re-verification of
each hit is the expensive step and is budgeted per hit. U(H) computations are small (lengths of a few dozen) but need a
noncommutative system (Singular Plural, Macaulay2 AssociativeAlgebras, both installed in WSL). Stop when the undecided
set stops shrinking under the generators available; residual rays are recorded as open.

## Novelty risks, publication risks, non-claims

Novelty candidates (dossier section 5): (A) the realizability table for d3 <= 12 with explicit modules and obstructions;
(B) the smallest counterexample to Question 6.1 (d3 = 10 now; d3 <= 9 needs U(H) realizations of five rays and S
realizations or new obstructions for the rest); (C) U(H) realization or obstruction theorems; (D) a scale-sensitive
obstruction on pure rays over S; (E) c_d on the Rethlas ray (3 <= c_d <= 27132); (F) scaling and duality structure;
(G) characteristic dependence in three variables. Risks: the level-algebra literature may already settle many cyclic
rays (checked by the literature pass before EXP-001 runs); realizability is field dependent, so every realization
states its field. Non-claims: a random search that misses a point proves nothing about it; a counterexample is claimed
only with a proof over every field (or the field stated) and a machine check.
