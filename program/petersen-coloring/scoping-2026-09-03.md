## 1. Problem statement and verified status

Let `P` be the Petersen graph. A Petersen coloring (P-coloring) of a cubic graph `G` is a map
`sigma: E(G) -> E(P)` such that at every vertex `v` of `G` the three incident edges map onto the
three edges incident with some vertex of `P` (equivalently, adjacent edges of `G` go to distinct
adjacent edges of `P`). A normal `k`-edge-coloring is a proper `k`-edge-coloring in which every
edge is poor (its two end stars carry three colors in total) or rich (five colors).

- Jaeger 1988 conjectured every bridgeless cubic graph has a P-coloring `[V via GJMMM ref 4]`.
- Jaeger 1985: `G` has a P-coloring iff `G` has a normal 5-edge-coloring `[V via GJMMM ref 3]`.
- The conjecture implies the Berge-Fulkerson conjecture and the 5-cycle double cover conjecture
  `[V, Open Problem Garden entry; primary derivation to be pinned in the context dossier]`.
- FALSE since 2026-08-06: Putman's two nonisomorphic 112-vertex graphs `[V]`, human-checkable
  proof by Jooken (arXiv:2608.10028) `[V]`, 68-vertex example posted on X `[U]`, 52-vertex
  cyclically 4-edge-connected girth-5 example by Goedgebeur-Jooken-Macajova-Mattiolo-Mazzuoccolo
  `[V, edge list read]`, infinite cyclically 4-edge-connected families `[V]`.
- Every counterexample has at least 38 vertices (weak snarks through 36 all P-colorable) `[V via
  GJMMM citing BGHM 2013 and GMS 2019; the two census papers to be read at open time]`.

## 2. Why the supplied references matter

They are the complete public record of the disproof as of 2026-09-03: the objects (four explicit
graphs, three retrievable), the certificates (CNF plus DRAT for both 112-vertex graphs), the
mechanism (P-coloring sets of the 4-poles `F`, `L`, `C`; series composition preserving
`P-Col`), and the explicitly open questions (smallest order in `[38,52]`; cyclically
5-edge-connected counterexamples; normal 6-edge-colorings).

## 3. Feasibility class and surface

Class A, exact. Every target is a finite decision on a fixed graph of at most 112 vertices:
existence of a P-coloring, a normal `k`-edge-coloring, a Berge-Fulkerson cover (six perfect
matchings covering every edge exactly twice), a Berge cover (five perfect matchings covering
every edge), three perfect matchings with empty intersection (Fan-Raspaud), a 5-cycle double
cover (five even subgraphs covering every edge exactly twice), a nowhere-zero 5-flow, and the
minimum number of non-normal edges over all proper 5-edge-colorings (the normal-5 defect) and
the minimum number of vertices violating the P-coloring condition over all edge maps (the
P-defect). Negative answers carry DRAT proofs; positive answers carry explicit witnesses checked
by a standard-library script. Grammar-restricted minimality is a bounded exact search over
compositions of the 8-vertex 4-pole `F` with small connectors, decided through exact
`P-Col` sets. Cyclic 5-edge-connectivity of any candidate is exact.

## 4. Known examples, counterexamples, and closest prior work

- Petersen graph, Blanusa snarks, flower snarks, Goldberg snarks: P-colorable (Hagglund-Steffen
  2014 and the census) `[U until read]`: positive controls.
- The 112, 68, 52 graphs: counterexamples. All contain `F` as a submultipole `[V, GJMMM]`.
- Closest prior work on consequences: BGHM 2013 tested cycle double covers and Petersen
  colorings on all snarks through 36 and reported no counterexample to Fulkerson among them
  `[V, abstract]`; no source reports Berge-Fulkerson or cycle double covers for any of the 2026
  counterexamples.

## 5. Candidate invariants and lenses

- Invariant-first probe: a Berge-Fulkerson cover of the 112-vertex graph (one SAT call). A
  negative result would be a new counterexample of the first magnitude; a positive result is the
  first consequence-audit fact.
- Lenses: exclusion (DRAT-certified non-existence), anatomy (the `P-Col` calculus of `F`, `L`,

  grammar composition is a colorable graph whose `P-Col` data feeds the next composition),
