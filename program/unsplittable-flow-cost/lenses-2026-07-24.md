# Research lenses applied: unsplittable-flow cost conjecture (2026-07-24)

Contract: `methodology/10-research-lenses.md` (the exclusion spine plus at least two more
lenses, invariants first) and `methodology/11-exploration-cadence.md` (every round mints a
new viewpoint or records why none). This is the plan-stage lens pass; the RESUME carries
the per-round ledger of what each lens produced.

## 1. Exclusion / obstruction (the spine, and its unusual shape here)

The spine of this problem is inverted relative to our other programmes. In the Jacobian
and central-configurations programmes we exclude a hypothetical object degree by degree.
Here the hypothetical object (a counterexample) is CLAIMED TO EXIST and is finite, so the
exclusion engine turns around and becomes an ADJUDICATION engine: constrain the claimed
object against every proved theorem until either it contradicts one (and dies) or it
survives all of them (and stands). The C1-C9 battery in the literature dossier is exactly
an exclusion sweep run against a candidate rather than against a class.

What the spine then attacks, after adjudication, is the SPACE of obstructions: exclude the
existence of a counterexample smaller than a given size, by exhausting canonical forms.
That is a genuine exclusion ladder with verifiable per-size floors, and it is the same
shape of result our other programmes produce.

## 2. Anatomy / construction (the known object, dissected)

There is exactly one known candidate object, which makes this lens unusually cheap and
unusually valuable. Its anatomy, read off the certificate and to be verified in EXP-002:

- three terminals, each with exactly two routing choices, one expensive and direct
  ($E_i$), one free and routed through a shared spine ($Z_i$);
- the free choices are PAIRWISE congestion-incompatible, forming a triangle of conflicts;
- the fractional flow buys free-choice mass $1/3 + 2/5 + 1/3 = 16/15 > 1$;
- any congestion-good integral routing buys at most 1, and the costs are calibrated so
  that the missing $1/15$ of free mass costs strictly more than the fractional saving.

The mechanism is therefore not flow-theoretic at all: it is the LP-integrality gap of the
stable-set relaxation on an odd cycle, transported into flow language. That reading is the
single most useful output of this lens, because it says immediately where to look for
STRONGER counterexamples: longer odd cycles of conflicts, and conflict cliques, whose
fractional stable-set values reach $k/2$ and $k$ respectively while integral selection
stays at 1. Whether the flow structure can realise those gaps under the $+d_{\max}$ budget
is exactly the UF-P3 question. NEW PATH: UFB-020 (odd-cycle conflict families), UFB-021
(clique conflict families).

## 3. Recognition / classification (where the boundary actually is)

Reframe "for which instances does the conjecture hold" as a graph-class recognition
question. The literature has already done most of it: MSW25 proves it for series-parallel
digraphs, and series-parallel is precisely the $K_4$-subdivision-free class. The claimed
counterexample is reported to be a $K_4$ subdivision, i.e. the minimal object OUTSIDE the
proved class. If both facts verify, the class boundary is tight, and the correct statement
is not "the conjecture is false" but "the conjecture is true exactly on the
series-parallel side of the $K_4$ boundary, and fails on the first structure past it".
That is a recognition-lens statement, machine-checkable on our side (series-parallel
recognition and $K_4$-subdivision detection are exact combinatorial decisions), and it is
the framing our wiki and manuscript should carry. NEW PATH: UFB-022 (exact series-parallel
/ $K_4$-subdivision recogniser, validated on known SP and non-SP graphs).

## 4. Invariant (cheapest leverage, run first)

The distinguishing invariant of this problem is the **fractional free-choice mass on a set
of pairwise congestion-incompatible cheap choices**. Formally, given the instance, build
the CONFLICT GRAPH $H$ on the cheap path choices, with an edge between two choices when
selecting both violates some arc bound. Then

- any congestion-good routing selects an independent set of $H$;
- the fractional flow selects the choices with weights $\rho_i = x(\text{cheap choice } i)/d_i$;
- if $\rho$ lies outside the stable-set polytope of $H$, the fractional flow is buying
  more cheap routing than any integral routing can, and the only remaining question is
  whether nonnegative arc costs can separate that violated inequality.

This one invariant explains the entire counterexample in a line, predicts the family, and
gives a cheap PRE-FILTER for the exhaustive search: compute $\rho$ and $H$, test $\rho$
against the odd-cycle inequalities of the stable-set polytope, and only run the separation
LP on instances that fail one. Run this BEFORE any heavy sweep, per methodology/11 item 4.
NEW PATH: UFB-023 (conflict-graph invariant instrument and the stable-set pre-filter).

Second invariant, for the dual direction: at violation budget $\alpha d_{\max}$, the
conflict graph THINS (more pairs become compatible), so $\alpha^\*$ is the smallest budget
at which the conflict graph of every instance becomes "LP-integral enough" to admit an
independent set of fractional weight at least the fractional mass. That reformulation of
the frontier is, as far as the read literature shows, not stated anywhere, and it is the
most promising thing this lens pass produced. It goes to the research-lines file.

## 5. Symmetry / representation theory

Weak here, but not empty. The instance has an evident $t_1 \leftrightarrow t_3$ symmetry
(equal demands 15, mirrored roles), and the parametric family is symmetric in $r$. The
practical use is in the exhaustive search: canonical forms must quotient by digraph
isomorphism AND by terminal relabelling that preserves demands, or the enumeration counts
the same instance many times and the completeness claim becomes unverifiable. Symmetry
here is a correctness requirement of the search, not a source of theorems. NEW PATH:
folded into UFB-011 (canonical form with demand-preserving terminal relabelling).

## 6. The two-sided reading

Every exclusion attempt read backwards is a construction attempt. Applied here: the
proposer's transcript is a long record of FAILED constructions (two-backbone prefix
networks, three-track permutation enforcers, cube gadgets), each failure a partial no-go
theorem about where counterexamples cannot live. Those no-go statements, if we verify
them, are exactly the pruning rules that make our exhaustive search feasible, and they
also tell us where the search must NOT be pruned. Treat them as hypotheses to test, never
as facts: they are assertions in a model transcript. NEW PATH: UFB-024 (verify or refute
the transcript's no-go claims, one experiment per claim, as pruning rules for UF-P2).

Read the other way: our exhaustion in UF-P2, when it finds nothing below a size, is
evidence that the conflict-triangle mechanism is the minimal mechanism, which feeds the
anatomy lens back.

## 7. Reformulation / dictionary

Three dictionaries, in decreasing order of usefulness:

- **Stable-set / LP integrality gap** (lens 4 above). The strongest, and it imports the
  whole theory of the stable-set polytope: odd holes, cliques, the Lovasz theta bound.
- **Discrepancy.** TVZ24's planar proof runs through "a highly structured discrepancy
  problem", and STVZ25 route through a rounding framework with an error body. Any upper
  bound on $\alpha^\*$ we might ever produce comes from this dictionary, not from flows.
- **Convex combinations.** Goemans' conjecture is equivalent (MSS07, via MSW25 footnote 1)
  to expressing $x$ as a convex combination of congestion-good unsplittable flows. In that
  language a counterexample says: $x$ lies OUTSIDE the convex hull of congestion-good
  unsplittable load vectors, and the separating hyperplane is exactly the cost vector.
  That geometric picture is the cleanest way to explain the result and is the natural
  figure for the wiki. It also immediately suggests the quantitative question in its
  sharpest form: how far outside the hull can $x$ get, and does the hull swallow $x$ once
  the budget grows to $\alpha d_{\max}$?

## 8. Dimension / parameter ladder

The ladders that locate the hardness: the number of terminals $k$ (with $k \le 2$ the
conflict graph has no odd cycle, so by the lens-4 reading no counterexample can exist;
$k = 3$ is where the triangle first appears, and the claimed counterexample has $k = 3$),
the graph class ladder (series-parallel proved, $K_4$ subdivision breaks it), and the
demand structure ladder (all-multiples proved by Sku02, so a counterexample needs demands
that are not multiples of one another, and 15, 10 is the smallest interesting pair). Each
rung predicts a MINIMALITY statement that UF-P2 can settle by exhaustion. The $k \le 2$
claim above is a one-line argument that we should prove properly and machine-check, since
it would be the first rung of the minimality ladder. NEW PATH: UFB-025.

## 9. At-infinity / boundary

Not obviously applicable: the problem is finite and combinatorial with no compactification
boundary. The nearest analogue is the asymptotic regime of the parametric family (large
$b$, $m$, $g$), where the question is whether $\alpha_{\mathrm{inst}}$ tends to a limit
above 1 or degrades back to 1. That is a concrete question for UF-P3 and the only sense in
which "at infinity" has content here. Recorded honestly as a lens with little to give on
this problem.

## 10. Adversarial / audit

In force from round 1 and unusually load-bearing, because this programme's first act is
adjudicating someone else's claim in a climate of publicity. Rules for this problem:
hypothesis declared before every run; the proposer's code archived and never executed; our
enumerator regression-gated on hand-built instances with known answers; the C1-C9 battery
run as machine assertions; and the verdict written to honour the machine result whichever
way it falls, including the outcome "the celebrated counterexample is wrong", which would
be a far bigger claim than confirming it and would therefore need the strongest validation
we can mount before it left this repo.

## 11. External dialogue

The external analysis here is a model transcript plus five secondary write-ups, none of
them a primary mathematical venue. The lens applies in its strict form: engage the
reasoning, extract the leads (the separation LP, the no-go claims, the parametric family),
and RE-DERIVE every one of them in-repo before any of it carries weight. Nothing from the
transcript is quoted as fact in our wiki or manuscript without an experiment behind it.
The other external dialogue that matters is with the actual literature: this area is
active and the authors of the positive results are likely to respond publicly within
weeks; a standing backlog row keeps a literature re-search in every round, and any
response found is read from the primary source, not from coverage of it.
