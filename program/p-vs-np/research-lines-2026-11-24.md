## 0. Reading by the coordinating session

Self-questioning (methodology 11, item 2). Before the dossier the coordinating session ranked this problem last of the
four: "no approach, three barrier theorems". That prior was built on the state of the field before October 2026 and
it was wrong about where the movement is. Three things changed in five weeks, all from fields this repository had not
connected to the problem:

1. Two independent deterministic algorithms count the solutions of k-CNF formulas in time 2^(o(n)) (OpenAI
   2026-10-25, Anthropic 2026-10-26, both with Lean artifacts): tensor-network contraction with signed sums, from the
   asymptotic theory of tensors and from quantum information.
2. Two independent superpolynomial lower bounds for general arithmetic circuits (Anthropic and OpenAI, both dated
   2026-11-23, both with Lean artifacts, neither read by a human mathematician): the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial needs
   more than m^(c (log m)^(1/3)) gates over every field. The Anthropic proof runs through Strassen's asymptotic
   spectrum, metric spaces of nonpositive curvature, a Poincare inequality on unitriangular groups and a diagonal
   argument over weighted nondeterministic programs.
3. The Anthropic paper says in its Section 2.5 where its proof stops for Boolean circuits: exactly two steps look
   inside the circuit (`lem_balanced`, `lem_lowdeg`), and both use a part of the computed polynomial (the part linear
   in each block of variables, the part of low degree) that a circuit correct only on inputs 0 and 1 need not have.

What the prize statement needs and what these results give. The prize statement is P against NP for Turing machines
(Section 1). A lower bound for Boolean circuits that compute the number of Hamiltonian cycles modulo 2, or over the
integers, separates P from the counting classes (parity P, PP), which every proof of P != NP must also do, since NP is
inside PP. It does not by itself separate P from NP. So the transfer of the algebraic bound to Boolean circuits is a
necessary station on the route, not the prize statement. This limit is stated wherever a target of this file is named.

The ladder between the algebraic theorem and the Boolean statement [D]. For a field k and an integer c >= 1 let S(c)
be the statement: every arithmetic circuit over k that computes a polynomial g with g = HC_m on {0,1}^(m x m) and with
degree at most c in each variable has more than quasi-polynomially many gates. Then:

- S(1) is the theorem claimed on 2026-11-23, because a multilinear polynomial is determined by its values on the cube.
- S(infinity) (no bound on the degree) over the field with two elements is a lower bound for Boolean circuits over the
  basis AND, XOR that compute the parity of the number of Hamiltonian cycles; over the rationals with constants of
  polynomial bit length it is a lower bound for the Boolean computation of the number itself.
- The statements in between are new questions that the theorem of 2026-11-23 makes natural. Earlier functional lower
  bounds (Forbes, Kumar and Saptharishi, 2016) are for circuits of depth three and four with low individual degree.

First results of the coordinating session on the ladder [D, unreviewed; the note with the proofs is the first unit of
the programme, Section 12]:

- In the contracting case of the Anthropic proof, S(c) holds for every fixed c, and for c up to a small power of m.
  Reason: the evaluation lemma (Lemma K.2 of the paper) evaluates the sum of the homogeneous parts of degree at most d
  of the polynomial that ANY circuit computes, at the indicator assignment of a word, in about d polylog(L) steps; the
  reduction to the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial (Proposition M.2) substitutes matrices whose entries are 0, 1 or
  affine in one variable each, so a polynomial g of individual degree at most c that agrees with HC_m on 0/1 matrices
  becomes a polynomial of degree at most c times the number of non-constant entries, which is of the order of the
  length n of the word, and its values at indicator assignments are the sums the argument needs. The rest of the
  contracting case uses the circuit only through this accelerator.
- In the flat case the decomposition lemma (`lem_balanced`) does not extend to c = 2 as it stands. After polarizing a
  component of block degree 2, a balanced cut of the parse tree separates copies of blocks, not blocks; a block with a
  copy on each side is wired to both hubs, and the comparison of lengths that gives the bound is then satisfied
  trivially (the extreme case, every block shared, writes any tensor as a pointwise product). A circuit of block
  degree k - 1 computes the tensor of the complete network at one-hot inputs with polynomially many gates, so the
  one-hot evaluations alone cannot carry a functional bound; the whole cube has to be used.
- So the first open rung is S(2) in the flat case, and the whole ladder up to a power of m follows if the contracting
  case is the one that occurs.

Which case occurs [D for the reformulation, P for the paper's own statements]. The paper proves that one of two
hypotheses holds and says "We do not know which of the two hypotheses holds, even over R". Its Appendix D shows that
if the contracting hypothesis fails then, for every mu >= 3, the metric sqrt(rank(a - a')) on the mu x mu matrices
over the field with two elements is a price (a limit of metrics coming from spectral points). Three remarks:

- The converse is immediate, so the contracting hypothesis holds if and only if, for some mu, the metric
  sqrt(rank) on Mat_mu(F_2) is not a price.
- Nonpositive curvature alone cannot decide it: the metric sqrt(rank) is realized in a CAT(0) space. The mu x mu
  matrices are the mu-dimensional subspaces of F_2^(2 mu) complementary to a fixed one (a matrix a goes to its graph),
  and rank(a - a') is mu minus the dimension of the intersection; these are vertices of one type of the spherical
  building of type A_(2 mu - 1) over F_2, where two of them at this relative position make the angle
  arccos(1 - 2 rank/mu); the Euclidean cone over the building is CAT(0) and the points at distance rho on these rays
  are at distance 2 rho sqrt(rank/mu) from each other. The paper does not mention buildings.
- By Strassen's duality a metric is a price if and only if no asymptotic restriction between products of wires
  shortens it. So the contracting hypothesis is equivalent to the existence of one asymptotic conversion between
  networks of maximally entangled pairs on the parties Mat_mu(F_2) that is shorter in the metric sqrt(rank) on the
  source side than on the target side. This is a question of quantum information theory (asymptotic conversion of
  entanglement structures under stochastic local operations), with a finite first instance at mu = 2 (16 parties),
  where the paper's argument does not force the metric (it needs mu >= 3) but the same question can be asked.
- For Euclidean metrics the contraction is true with room: for maps of Mat_mu(F_2) into a Hilbert space the ratio
  D(mu)^2 / D(1)^2 is at most the largest ratio of the eigenvalues of the two Cayley graphs, which is 3/2 for mu = 2
  and tends to 2, against mu for the metric sqrt(rank).

How the barriers apply. The Anthropic paper states that it has not analysed relativization and algebrization. The
contracting case uses "no property of polynomials other than low degree" and ends in a diagonal argument, which is the
profile of an algebrizing proof; Aaronson and Wigderson show that such proofs cannot separate NEXP from P/poly. The
ladder above is consistent with that: what transfers to Boolean inputs is bounded by the degree, and an unrestricted
Boolean circuit has no small degree. Path R3 of Section 5 (a written test of each step against an oracle gate) stays
a first step of the programme.

## Summary of the dossier

1. The prize statement is Cook's: "Does P = NP?" over Turing machines with at least two symbols (Section D1). Clay's page still reads "Unsolved" on 2026-11-24 [P]. No 2026 claim about P versus NP itself has passed Clay's three conditions (outlet, two years, general acceptance), and none can have, since the earliest is from June 2026.
2. Two 2026 claims about the prize statement exist and both fail on their own repositories' terms: Arthanari's Lean P = NP (arXiv:2606.03194) declares the Maurras step, the Grotschel-Lovasz-Schrijver step and the Cook-Karp step as `Axiom` rows in its own status table [P]; the "Mintpath" `p_ne_np` package (P != NP through 2^{Omega(n)} circuit size for Hamiltonian cycle, "0 sorries, 2 axioms") has a repository that returned 404, so its content is NOT OPENED [P for the registry page only].
3. The week of 2026-10-25 to 2026-11-23 moved the neighbouring landscape. Two AI systems published, with Lean artifacts, (a) a deterministic subexponential algorithm for counting satisfying assignments of k-CNF formulas, which refutes the exponential time hypothesis (OpenAI 2026-10-25, Anthropic 2026-10-26), and (b) a quasipolynomial lower bound for the permanent and the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial, so VP != VNP over every field (Anthropic 2026-11-23, 70 pages, 324 Lean files; OpenAI 2026-11-23, obtained 2026-11-06, border circuits, exp(Omega((log n)^{5/4}))). Both authors say these do not settle P versus NP. For (a) a working group (Impagliazzo, Paturi, Williams) examines the proof and a blog by Fortnow says it is "safe to assume now that the exponential-time hypothesis is toast" [S]; for (b) no human review was found.
4. The only direct consequence for the Boolean side that is stated on a primary source: ETH false implies, by Williams' theorem, that E^NP has no Boolean circuits of linear size [P, Anthropic ETH paper, Section 1]. A second consequence is NTIME[t] in DTIME[2^{O(t/sqrt(log t))}] (arXiv:2611.11832 [P]). Neither is P != NP; the open window for the sub-statement "NP not in SUBEXP" is now exactly between 2^{n/sqrt(log n)} and 2^{n^eps}.
5. The proofs of (a) and (b) share machinery that this programme has not used: permanents of sparse matrices with rank-structured interpolation (a), tensor networks priced in a CAT(0)-type metric (spectral points of Strassen's asymptotic spectrum) and diagonalization by replay of computations inside a wire budget (b). The Anthropic paper states which two lemmas (`lem_balanced`, `lem_lowdeg`) fail for Boolean circuits. That is the most concrete first step toward the Boolean world in this report (path rows R2 and R3).
6. All 2026 Lean artifacts here were checked only by their own CI (Anthropic's `formal-math` CI run on PR 51 ended `success` [P]); nothing was built or independently re-run by this report, and both labs state that no machine checks that the statement file means what the English says.

## 1. The prize statement

Source: Clay's official problem description, S. Cook, "The P versus NP problem", archived `src/clay_pvsnp.pdf`, SHA-256 `018f3d473d16c35e807e8cdfbd0bed3ccf7a56452e3e10c6b1d84b56eaf2cf59` (URL https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/pvsnp.pdf, read 2026-11-24, 6 pages). Extracted text: `src/clay_pvsnp.txt`.

Exact statement [P], quotes from that PDF:
- "Problem Statement. Does P = NP?"
- Class P: "We say that M runs in polynomial time if there exists k such that for all n, TM (n) <= n^k + k." and "P = {L | L = L(M) for some Turing machine M that runs in polynomial time }." (the PDF prints the symbols as in Cook's text; the Turing machine model is fixed in the appendix.)
- Class NP: "a language L over Sigma is in NP iff there is k in N and a polynomial-time checking relation R such that for all w in Sigma*, w in L <=> exists y (|y| <= |w|^k and R(w, y))", where R is polynomial-time iff the language L_R = {w#y | R(w,y)} is in P.
- Alphabet: "Let Sigma be a finite alphabet ... with at least two elements". And: "It is easy to see that the answer is independent of the size of the alphabet Sigma (we assume |Sigma| >= 2) ... (For |Sigma| = 1 the problem is still open, although it is possible that P = NP in this case but not in the general case.)"

In symbols: for all finite Sigma with |Sigma| >= 2, decide whether P_Sigma = NP_Sigma, where P_Sigma = { L subset Sigma* : exists TM M, exists k, L = L(M), T_M(n) <= n^k + k for all n }. The inclusion P subset NP is trivial in the PDF, so the question is NP subset P, equivalently (by NP-completeness, discussed in the PDF) whether SAT is in P.

Clay's label, read today [P]: page https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/ (archived `src/clay_page.html`): "P vs NP Unsolved". No claim is named on the page.

- 5.a: "Only a complete mathematical solution to a Problem as it is defined in its official Problem description (a "Proposed Solution") will be eligible for consideration for a Prize."
- 5.b: "In the case of the P versus NP Problem and the Navier-Stokes Problem, a resolution in either direction will be evaluated by the standard evaluation procedure set forth in Section 7."
- 5.d: "A paper that does not address or refer to the specific mathematical questions set out in detail in the official Problem description will not be considered to be a Potential Proposed Solution" (the PDF wraps the line; the text is in `src/clay_rules.txt`, lines 61 to 62).
- Rules page: "(i) the proposed solution must be published in a Qualifying Outlet (see 6), and (ii) at least two years must have passed since publication, and (iii) the proposed solution must have received general acceptance in the global mathematics community"; "CMI does not accept direct submission of proposed solutions."
- Counterexample: 5.c ("In the case of the other Problems, if a counterexample is proposed ...") is stated for the problems other than P versus NP and Navier-Stokes. For P versus NP both P = NP (a polynomial-time algorithm for an NP-complete language, with proof) and P != NP are "a resolution in either direction" under 5.b. There is no smaller "counterexample" prize category for this problem. (Inference from 5.b and 5.c, [P] text.)

Neighbouring statements that are NOT the prize statement (each is a different question, even when a 2026 claim is about it):
1. The exponential time hypothesis and SETH (refuted by the 2026 claims below; P != NP is not touched, as both papers say [P]).
2. VP versus VNP and the permanent versus determinant question (an arithmetic-circuit question over a field; "This is the algebraic analogue of the conjecture P != NP, but it does not settle that conjecture", Anthropic abstract [P]).
3. NP versus P/poly (non-uniform): NP not in P/poly implies P != NP, not the converse [K]. Circuit lower bounds for E^NP, NEXP, NQP.
4. NP versus coNP, P versus PSPACE, P versus BPP, L versus NL, NL versus UL (each is a separate open or newly claimed statement; the PDF discusses NP vs coNP as a consequence, not as the prize).
5. Search versus decision versions, average-case hardness, existence of one-way functions, factoring and graph isomorphism (the PDF notes factoring is thought neither in P nor NP-complete).
6. The unary-alphabet case |Sigma| = 1 (left open by the PDF itself).
7. P versus NP in other models (Blum-Shub-Smale over R or C, oracle worlds, quantum, nondeterministic restricted classes).
8. Any formal statement in Lean: the prize statement is the English one; a Lean statement is a formalization whose fidelity is a separate matter (Section D7).

## 4. Solved analogues and model cases

Mechanism, then what transfers and what provably does not. Sources: Clay PDF (restricted models), the Anthropic paper (algebraic), classical background marked [K] where I opened nothing.

1. Monotone circuits. Exponential lower bound for explicit monotone functions: "monotone circuits [26], [2]" (Razborov; Alon and Boppana) [P, Clay PDF lines 345 to 346]. Mechanism: approximation by sunflower-style method [K]. Does not transfer: the method needs monotonicity of the function class (a NOT-free model); the same function with negations has no known bound; the matching problem is monotone and in P yet has exponential monotone complexity [K, Razborov 1985 / Tardos], so monotone bounds cannot detect P versus NP.
2. Constant depth, AC0 and AC0[p] (p prime). "bounded depth circuits with unbounded fan-in gates [17], [35]" (Hastad; Smolensky) [P, Clay PDF]. Mechanism: switching lemma (AC0), polynomial approximation over F_p (Razborov-Smolensky, [K]). Does not transfer past depth-bounded circuits; MOD_m for composite m is not covered, which is the ACC frontier (Williams, Murray-Williams [P] row A3). OpenAI 2026: explicit language in P with 2^{omega(sqrt n)} OR-AND-OR size (row 13), crossing the square-root exponent (depth three).
3. Multilinear formulas, noncommutative formulas and ABPs. Superpolynomial multilinear formula bounds (Raz) and exponential noncommutative formula bounds (Nisan 1991) [P, as cited in Anthropic Section 1.2]. Mechanism: rank of partial-derivative or partition matrix. Barrier: Kush (arXiv:2604.00746 [P]) shows min-partition rank cannot give superpolynomial multilinear ABP bounds; these methods fail on unrestricted circuits since low rank across varying cuts is not a single matrix [P, OpenAI Section 1].
4. Constant-depth algebraic circuits. Limaye, Srinivasan, Tavenas, Forbes [P row A5]: first two-step method, set-multilinear lower bound then simulation of low depth by set-multilinear circuits. Transfer to Boolean: none known; "homogeneous depth four reaches the square-root exponent" (GKKS, KLSS, KS as cited [P]).
5. Tropical (min-plus) circuits. Status: the min-plus VP and VNP are studied (Komarath, Mittal, Sarma, arXiv:2605.09551, ICALP 2026 [S, abstract surfaced by search, page not opened]); exponential lower bounds for monotone-type tropical circuits are classical (Jukna) [K, not opened]. Transfer: tropical bounds are monotone-type and carry the same limitation as item 1.
6. Blum-Shub-Smale models. [K, nothing opened today]: P versus NP over C and over R are open (Smale's problem); known separations hold for restricted real machines. UNVERIFIED. The Anthropic result is a related but different object (formal polynomials, nonuniform) and implies nothing for BSS uniform P versus NP [inference, no source].
7. Relativized and random-oracle worlds. [P, Clay PDF]: oracle A with P = NP; generic oracle with P != NP; Proposition 3: relative to a PSPACE-complete oracle "Assertion A holds and BPP = P = NP". The Anthropic paper says it has not analysed algebrization (Section 1.2) and the OpenAI paper does not claim to avoid it.
8. Bounded-depth Frege. [K]: exponential lower bounds for constant-depth Frege (Ajtai; Krajicek, Pudlak, Woods; Pitassi, Beame, Impagliazzo). Mechanism: switching lemmas and the pigeonhole principle. Transfer: AC0[p]-Frege lower bounds exist only for restricted formulas (arXiv:2509.16824 [P], as hard tautology or unprovable). Not P versus NP.
9. Other base fields and other problems with proved separations. The algebraic class separation over every field (rows 8 and 9, claims). Space: L versus NL in the multi-tape model "NL = UL" (Hoza, row 12). Time-space: n^{2-eps} for multitape (Williams 2025 [P]). Unary alphabet |Sigma| = 1: "still open" [P, Clay PDF].
10. Special families of instances. Random k-SAT thresholds (Ding, Sly, Sun arXiv:1411.0650 [P] for large k; Carenini 2026 [P]) are average-case solved; algorithms and low-degree barriers: Bresler and Huang, arXiv:2106.02129 [P]. Transfer: none to worst case.

What provably does not transfer, in short: (a) monotone, depth-bounded and rank-based proofs are blocked by natural-proof and rank-method barriers [P: EGOW, Kush, Clay PDF]; (b) algebraic bounds over formal polynomials do not imply Boolean bounds, and the converse direction is conditional [P, Anthropic abstract and Section 1.3]; (c) LP or SDP lift bounds do not separate P from NP because matching is in P [P rows A12].

## 6. Candidate targets

Notation: SAT is satisfiability; SIZE[s(n)] is nonuniform Boolean circuit size.

T1 (the prize statement). For all finite Sigma with |Sigma| >= 2, NOT exists k such that SAT in DTIME(n^k + k) on a Turing machine as in the Clay PDF; equivalently P_Sigma != NP_Sigma. Known: nothing beyond the statement of D3. Why open: all barriers of A1, A2, A4 to A6. Rows attacking it: all. If proved: a resolution under Clay rule 5.b.

T2 (non-uniform). exists L in NP, for all c, for infinitely many n: size_L(n) > n^c, i.e. NP not in P/poly. Strictly sufficient for T1 [K]. Known: unrestricted Boolean bounds are linear-scale (A2). Why open: natural proofs and the lack of a technique beyond rank and switching. Attacked by R1, R2, R5, R9, R10, R14, R15. Meaning: P != NP together with a non-uniform separation (stronger).

T3 (linear size for NP). For every c: SAT not in SIZE[c n]. Known: E^NP does not have linear-size circuits [P, ETH paper from Williams' theorem] and the best earlier explicit bound 3.1n - o(n) for E^NP [P as quoted in ETH paper]; for NP the statement is open as far as the sources read here state. Why open: the Williams implication goes through E^NP, not NP. Attacked by R1, R2, R15. Meaning: the first unconditional step from E^NP to NP; a strict sub-statement of T2.

T4 (ACC0 for NP). For all c, d, m: SAT not in ACC0 circuits of depth d, modulus m and size n^c, i.e. NP not in ACC0. Known: NEXP not in ACC0 [K] and "NQP is not contained in ACC0 composed with threshold gates" (Murray-Williams, TR17-188 [P]). Why open: the method needs a savings algorithm for the NP-side class, so it lifts NEXP to NQP but not to NP. Attacked by R1, R10, R14. Meaning: a constant-depth separation for NP; strictly a route (restricted-model analogue of T2).

T5 (extended Frege). For all c there exist tautologies phi_n with shortest extended Frege proof of size > |phi_n|^c. Known: no such bound is known for Frege or extended Frege (classical statement [K], source not opened). Implies NP != coNP [P, Clay PDF] which implies P != NP [K]. Attacked by R13, R16. Meaning: a stronger conclusion (NP != coNP), strictly sufficient for T1.

T6 (sub-exponential window). NP not in SUBEXP, where SUBEXP = intersection over eps > 0 of DTIME(2^{n^eps}); equivalently, exists eps > 0 with 3-SAT not in DTIME(2^{n^eps}). Known: NOT ETH is now established on 2026 claims [P rows 3 and 4]: 3-SAT in DTIME(2^{O(n log log n / sqrt(log n))}); so any lower bound must stay below that, and the gap is between 2^{n/sqrt(log n)} and every 2^{n^eps}. Also NTIME[t] in DTIME[2^{O(t / sqrt(log t))}] [P]. Why open: no technique. Attacked by R1, R12, R17. Meaning: NP not in SUBEXP implies P != NP (since P is inside SUBEXP) [K]; a bounded-window statement that the new algorithms localize.

T7 (time-space). For all c: SAT not in DTIME[n^c] intersect DSPACE[n^{o(1)}]. Known: for multitape machines, explicit linear-space problems need n^{2-eps} time (Williams 2025, arXiv:2502.17779 [P]); the earlier time-space tradeoff exponent for SAT is [K] 2 cos(pi/7) approx 1.8019 (Williams 2008, UNVERIFIED, not opened). Why open: simulation techniques stop at tree evaluation. Attacked by R17. Meaning: a restricted consequence of T1, not sufficient for it.

T8 (algebraic bridge, necessary direction not available). VP_F != VNP_F for every field F implies VP_0 != VNP_0? The sources read here establish only: "if NP not in P/poly then VP != VNP over C, provided that the Riemann hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions holds" [P, Anthropic Section 1.3, citing Burgisser] and "it is not known whether Valiant's conjecture implies Boolean separations such as P != NP" [P same]. Target statement: exhibit a Boolean family f_n in NP with size_{f_n} >= size of arithmetic circuits for a related polynomial restricted to {0,1}^n. Known: the Anthropic proof breaks in two named lemmas for Boolean circuits (R2). Meaning: this is the precise form of the transfer question and is strictly on a route to T2.
