\begin{abstract}
Let $C_3=0.6567752140190419405\ldots$ be the certified rank-three
diagonal constant of Pearce-Crump's sign-preserving Selberg detector. We
localize that detector and prove that, for every fixed $1/2<\theta<1$, the
lower asymptotic proportion of distinct odd-multiplicity critical zeros in
$(T,T+T^\theta]$ is at least $(\theta-1/2)/(4eC_3)$. Combining this result
with a parity refinement of the stable rank--trace inequality gives
\[
 \liminf\frac{N_0^s(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge\max\left\{0,c(\theta),
 \frac{c(\theta)+(\theta-1/2)/(2eC_3)}3\right\},
\]
where $c(\theta)=2-\theta/2-\cot(\theta/\sqrt2)/\sqrt2$. Exact rational
enclosures place the new positivity threshold in $(0.5459,0.546)$, below
the cosine threshold $0.5501939647\ldots$. At $\theta=0.546$, a fixed
legal mollifier proves a simple-critical proportion above
$0.0000976239413345$. We also strengthen the positive cosine curve by an
odd-frame pressure transfer; at $\theta=3/4$, a two-variable interval
certificate gives the simple-critical bound $0.4190878881701117\ldots$.
The finite stability inequality, Selberg detector, and global pressure
methods are prior work. The localization uses an arbitrary-subinterval
rational-frequency estimate from Pearce-Crump and Wang's fixed-test pair
theorem. No global record, effective starting height, universal simplicity
theorem, or solution of the Riemann hypothesis is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Summary of results}

The central result is an explicit localization of Pearce-Crump's optimized
Selberg detector. Retaining the actual interval length in its
rational-frequency estimate gives positive density of distinct odd critical
zeros for every fixed exponent above one half. A parity transfer then turns
that input into a numerical simple-critical positivity threshold below the
zero of Wang's cosine bound.

Further results are the direct finite Hilbert transfer with its explicit
compact energy bound, the odd-frame pressure theorem, its stronger
consequence throughout the positive short-interval curve, an odd-frame
bound from the compact finite certificate, and a certified two-variable
pressure bound. The earlier classical parity theorem remains useful as a
weaker-input qualitative result and gives a separate distinct-zero consequence.

\section{Statement and relation to prior work}

Let $N(T,H)$ count the nontrivial zeros $\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ of $\zeta(s)$
with $T<\gamma\le T+H$, including multiplicity. Let $N_0^s(T,H)$ count the
simple zeros among them with $\beta=1/2$, and let $N^d(T,H)$ count distinct
complex zeros, each once. Let $N_{\rm odd}(T,H)$ count distinct critical
zeros of odd multiplicity, also each once. It does not count their
multiplicity. Every proportion below has denominator $N(T,H)$.

The recent argument discovered by Claude and communicated by Alp\"oge and % voice-ok: external attribution, as the cited paper states it
Furman~\cite{AF} made the optimized Montgomery--Taylor simple-critical
proportion $0.672500703679\ldots$ unconditional. Lamzouri~\cite{Lamzouri}
replaced its finite-compression machinery by a Hilbert-space inequality for
conjugation-invariant multisets. Wang~\cite{Wang} established the needed
short-interval pair-correlation theorem and deduced, for fixed $0<\theta<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wang}
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}\ge c(\theta),
 \qquad
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N^d(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge\frac{1+c(\theta)}2,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c}
 c(\theta)=2-\frac\theta2-\frac1{\sqrt2}\cot(\theta/\sqrt2).
\end{equation}
The derivative is $c'(\theta)=\tfrac12\cot^2(\theta/\sqrt2)>0$. Since
$c(\theta)\to-\infty$ as $\theta\downarrow0$ and $c(1)>0$, there is a
unique zero $\theta_0\in(0,1)$, numerically
$\theta_0\approx0.550193964744154\ldots$, as printed by Wang. The
proof below uses its defining equation and rational comparisons, not a
numerical root certificate.

The stable rank--trace inequality, convex Gram defect, pinching method, and
cosine-root non-additivity used here are prior work in the
\texttt{ainta} repository~\cite{Ainta}. Global pressure windows, larger
frames, nonuniform pair capacities, spectral envelopes, and combinations
of certificates are developed in~\cite{Trmdy,Tawan,Shi}. The alternating
schedule used below is a simple choice within this established framework.
We do not claim these ingredients or an improved global record. Our
contributions are their direct finite-Hilbert transfer, an explicit
quantitative three-point bound, a strict refinement of Wang's positive
short-interval curve, and three consequences. Classical odd-multiplicity
density and the retained multiplicity excess extend the positivity range
qualitatively. Odd-frame pressure strengthens the positive curve
quantitatively. Finally, Pearce-Crump's coefficient-uniform Selberg detector
localizes to every fixed power interval above one half, giving an explicit
odd-density curve and a numerical positivity threshold. The finite proof
keeps off-line signs, multiplicities, and singular Gram matrices explicit.

Selberg's positive density theorem at lengths $T^{1/2+\epsilon}$ is
reproduced as Theorem B in Karatsuba's primary article~\cite{Karatsuba}.
Karatsuba's own theorem works at $T^{27/82+\epsilon}$. These count distinct
odd-order critical zeros. They do not already assert a positive simple-zero
proportion. Steuding's later simple-critical theorem gives the explicit
exponent $0.552$~\cite{Steuding,SteudingThesis}; Wang gives the smaller
explicit threshold $\theta_0$. Pearce-Crump~\cite{PearceCrump} later gave a
coefficient-uniform sign detector with a certified diagonal constant. Its
global theorem exceeds seven percent on the critical line. The localization
below uses the paper's arbitrary-subinterval off-diagonal estimate; it does
not alter the detector or claim the global percentage as a new result.

The labels [D] and [MV] distinguish mathematical deductions from finite
machine-verifiable statements. The following main range result is a
deduction from the stated primary analytic inputs. Exact finite checks
verify its algebra; they do not replace those inputs or prove all-height
statements by enumeration.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit local Selberg transfer, D+MV]
\label{thm:localselberg}
Let
\[
C_3=C[q_3]\in
0.6567752140190419405677628751089899133\pm10^{-17}
\]
be the certified rank-three diagonal constant of Pearce-Crump's detector.
For every fixed $1/2<\theta<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localodd}
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}
 \frac{N_{\rm odd}(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge\frac{\theta-1/2}{4eC_3}.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localsimple}
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}
 \frac{N_0^s(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge\max\left\{0,c(\theta),
 \frac{c(\theta)+(\theta-1/2)/(2eC_3)}3\right\}.
\end{equation}
The unique zero of the third numerator in \eqref{eq:localsimple} lies in
$(0.5459,0.546)$. At $\theta=0.546$, the fixed mollifier exponent
$u=0.02299$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localpoint}
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}
 \frac{N_0^s(T,T^{0.546})}{N(T,T^{0.546})}
 >0.0000976239413345396825264438351212564.
\end{equation}
The optimized limiting curve gives the stronger lower bound
$0.0000994910410327$ at this exponent.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:localselberg} refines the simple-critical range numerically
for $\theta>1/2$. The next theorem uses only a fixed classical sign-change
seed and remains a separate qualitative statement under weaker input.

\begin{theorem}[Parity transfer and qualitative range extension, D]
\label{thm:parity}
Put $\alpha=51/100$. There exists a constant $\kappa>0$ such that, for
every fixed $\alpha<\theta<1$,
\begin{align}
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
&\ge\max\left\{0,c(\theta),\frac{c(\theta)+2\kappa}{3}\right\},
\label{eq:paritysimple}\\
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N^d(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
&\ge\max\left\{\kappa,\frac{1+c(\theta)}2,
                    \frac{3+2c(\theta)+\kappa}{6}\right\}.
\label{eq:paritydistinct}
\end{align}
One fixed $\kappa$ works for all these fixed exponents; the eventual height
may depend on $\theta$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paritydelta}
\delta=\min\left\{\frac{\theta_0-\alpha}{2},\frac\kappa4\right\}>0,
\qquad \theta_1=\theta_0-\delta.
\end{equation}
Then $\alpha<\theta_1<\theta_0$ and the lower simple-critical proportion
at every fixed $\theta_1\le\theta<1$ is at least $\kappa/3>0$.
Separately, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:halfdelta}
\delta_{\rm half}=\min\left\{\frac{\theta_0-\alpha}{2},
                                      \frac\kappa{16}\right\},
\qquad \theta_{\rm half}=\theta_0-\delta_{\rm half}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $\theta_{\rm half}\le\theta<1$, the lower distinct-zero
proportion is at least $1/2+\kappa/12$.
\end{theorem}

The distinct claim is greater than one half on some shorter intervals; it
is not a new exponent for merely positive distinct density, which already
follows from the classical odd-zero theorem. Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}
does not give an effective value of $\kappa$, a numerical value of either
new exponent, or a uniform theorem when $\theta$ varies with $T$.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit three-point refinement, D]\label{thm:main}
Fix $\theta_0<\theta<1$ and $R>2/c(\theta)$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit}
\begin{aligned}
 a&=\theta/\sqrt2,\qquad q=a\tan a,\qquad M=\pi\theta R,\\
 B&=q(1+M^2/a^2),\\
 b&=\min\left\{\frac q2,
       \frac{q^4}{4(q+M)^2(M+3q/2)}\right\},\qquad
 \delta=\frac12\min\{1,2(b/B)^2\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then $0<\delta\le1/2$, and, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cstar}
 c_*(\theta,R)=c(\theta)
       +\frac{\delta\{c(\theta)-2/R\}}{3-\delta}>c(\theta),
\end{equation}
we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainbounds}
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge c_*(\theta,R),\qquad
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N^d(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge\frac{1+c_*(\theta,R)}2.
\end{equation}
In particular, $R=4/c(\theta)$ gives a fully specified positive improvement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Odd-frame pressure refinement, D]\label{thm:pressure}
Fix $\theta_0<\theta<1$. Suppose $p,\varepsilon>0$ and the cosine kernel
$K_\theta$ of \eqref{eq:density} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressure}
 2\{K_\theta(u)^2+K_\theta(v)^2+K_\theta(u+v)^2\}
 +p(u+v)\ge\varepsilon\qquad(u,v\ge0).
\end{equation}
For every fixed integer $k\ge1$ with $k\varepsilon\le1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pressureconstant}
 c_{\rm odd}=c(\theta)+
 \frac{k\{\varepsilon c(\theta)-2p\}}{2k+1-k\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}
Then the lower asymptotic simple-critical and distinct proportions are at
least $c_{\rm odd}$ and $(1+c_{\rm odd})/2$, respectively. The gain is
strictly positive when $\varepsilon c(\theta)>2p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Stronger entire positive curve, D]\label{cor:wholecurve}
For the analytic $\delta$ in \eqref{eq:explicit}, one has $0<\delta<1/4$.
Every integer $k>1$ with $k\delta\le1$ strengthens
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} by replacing its gain coefficient
$\delta/(3-\delta)$ with $k\delta/(2k+1-k\delta)$. In particular $k=2$
is available for every fixed $\theta_0<\theta<1$.
\end{corollary}

The conservative gap in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} requires no numerical
search. A stronger finite certificate gives the following example.

\begin{corollary}[Compact-certificate example, D+MV]\label{cor:example}
Set $\theta=3/4$, $R=21/4$, $d=1/7000$, and
\[
 C_*:=\frac{3c(3/4)-2d/R}{3-d}
 =0.419076828425303996736665787527\ldots.
\]
Then the simple-critical proportion in \eqref{eq:mainbounds} is at least
$C_*$, and the distinct-zero proportion is at least
\[
 \frac{1+C_*}2=0.709538414212651998368332893763\ldots.
\]
The improvement in the simple-critical proportion is
$0.000001815449879663002112176828\ldots$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Odd-frame bound and two-variable pressure certificate, D+MV]\label{cor:pressureexample}
At $\theta=3/4$, the compact energy certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:certificate}, with $d=1/7000$ and $R=21/4$, gives, with
$k=7000$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reuseexample}
 C_A=c(3/4)+\frac{c(3/4)-8/21}{14000}
 =0.419077736020568836833221725071\ldots.
\end{equation}
The all-gap pressure certificate of Appendix~\ref{sec:pressurecert} has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newparams}
 p=\frac1{12500},\qquad\varepsilon=\frac{443239}{10^9},
 \qquad k=2256,\qquad 2k+1=4513.
\end{equation}
It yields
\begin{align}\label{eq:newexample}
 C_B&=c(3/4)+\frac{2256\{(443239/10^9)c(3/4)-2/12500\}}
                         {4513-2256(443239/10^9)}\notag\\
 &=0.419087888170111727959091183775\ldots.
\end{align}
The respective distinct companions are
\[
 (1+C_A)/2=0.709538868010284418416610862535\ldots,
\]
\[
 (1+C_B)/2=0.709543944085055863979545591887\ldots.
\]
\end{corollary}

The conclusion is for each fixed exponent and all sufficiently large
heights in the asymptotic sense. We do not provide an effective starting
height or uniformity as an exponent approaches its endpoint. The pressure
corollaries themselves concern $\theta>\theta_0$; the distinct parity
argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:parity} supplies the qualitative extension
below $\theta_0$. The common analytic dependency
is Wang's inspected preprint theorem, whose underlying pair-correlation
method is due to Baluyot, Goldston, Suriajaya, and
Turnage-Butterbaugh~\cite{BGSTB,BGSTBcorrection}.

\section{A stable inequality for finite multisets}

Let $Z$ be a finite conjugation-invariant multiset in $\C$. We sum over its
distinct support with positive integer multiplicities $m_z=m_{\bar z}$,
and put $N=\sum_zm_z$. Choose a real even compactly supported
$\eta\in L^2(\R)$ with $\int\eta^2=1$, and define
\[
 v_z(u)=\eta(u)e^{-2\pi izu},\qquad
 K(t)=\widehat{\eta^2}(t),\qquad
 \widehat f(t)=\int_{\R}f(u)e^{-2\pi itu}\,du.
\]
Every $v_z$ belongs to $L^2$ because $Z$ is finite and the support is compact.
Work in the finite-dimensional span $\mathcal H$ of these vectors. Our inner product
is $\langle v,w\rangle=\int\bar v w$, linear in the second variable; write
$|v\rangle\langle w|$ for $h\mapsto v\langle w,h\rangle$.

Define the reflected rank-one operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
 A=\sum_zm_z|v_z\rangle\langle v_{\bar z}|.
\end{equation}
It is selfadjoint by conjugation invariance. Direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace}
 \tr A=N,\qquad
 Q:=\|A\|_{\HS}^2=\tr A^2
   =\sum_{z,w}m_zm_wK(z-w)^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\langle v_{\bar z},v_z\rangle=\int\eta^2=1$, while the trace
of the product of the $z$ and $w$ terms, before multiplicity factors, is
$K(w-z)K(z-w)=K(z-w)^2$, since $K$ is even. The ordinary square in
\eqref{eq:trace} is essential. Individual terms for complex arguments need
not be positive or even real; their complete sum is a nonnegative operator
norm.

Let $x_1<\cdots<x_s$ be the simple real points of $Z$. Let $r$ be the
number of distinct multiple real points and $k$ the number of distinct
nonreal conjugate pairs. Put
\[
 P=\sum_{j=1}^s|v_{x_j}\rangle\langle v_{x_j}|=VV^*,\qquad
 G=V^*V=(K(x_i-x_j))_{i,j=1}^s,\qquad C=A-P.
\]
Then $G$ is positive semidefinite with diagonal one and $\tr P=s$.
For each nonreal pair put
$g=(v_z+v_{\bar z})/2$ and $h=(v_z-v_{\bar z})/(2i)$. Its contribution
to $C$ is $2m_z(|g\rangle\langle g|-|h\rangle\langle h|)$.
The form of $C$ is nonpositive on the orthogonal complement of the $r$
multiple-real vectors and $k$ vectors $g$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inertia}
 n_+(C)\le r+k,\qquad N\ge s+2r+2k.
\end{equation}
No distance of a nonreal point from the real axis is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Known stable rank--trace inequality]\label{lem:stable}
Suppose $P=VV^*$ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, the $s$ columns of
$V$ have norm one, $C$ is selfadjoint, and $n_+(C)\le d$. Define
\[
 \Psi(t)=\begin{cases}(t-1)^2,&0\le t\le2,\\2t-3,&t\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable}
 \|P+C\|_{\HS}^2\ge4\tr(P+C)-3s-4d+\tr\Psi(V^*V).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the unit-column case of the stability inequality in
\cite[Section 2]{Ainta}. We give a spectral proof that keeps all zero
eigenvalues. Let $m=\dim\mathcal H$ and let $p_1\le\cdots\le p_m$ be the
eigenvalues of $P$. If $d<m$, the min-max principle gives
$\lambda_i(P+C)\le p_{i+d}$ for $1\le i\le m-d$: intersect the span
of the first $i+d$ eigenvectors of $P$ with a codimension-at-most-$d$
subspace on which $C\le0$.

It follows that
\[
 \tr(P+C-2I)^2\ge\sum_{j=d+1}^m(2-p_j)_+^2
 \ge\sum_{j=1}^m(2-p_j)_+^2-4d.
\]
Here $0\le(2-p_j)_+^2\le4$. If $d\ge m$, the final right-hand side is
nonpositive and the same inequality is immediate. For every $p\ge0$,
$(2-p)_+^2=\Psi(p)-2p+3$. The nonzero spectra of $VV^*$ and $V^*V$
agree, and $\Psi(0)=1$, so
\[
 \tr\Psi(P)=\tr\Psi(V^*V)+m-s.
\]
Substitute these identities and $\tr P=s$, and expand the left-hand
square. All dimensional terms cancel and give \eqref{eq:stable}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite Hilbert stability transfer]\label{prop:finite}
For the multiset and operator above, write $D(G)=\tr\Psi(G)$ and
$D_Z=s+r+2k$ for the number of distinct support points. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite}
 s\ge2N-Q+D(G),\qquad
 D_Z\ge\frac{3N-Q+D(G)}2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:stable} and \eqref{eq:inertia},
$Q\ge4N-3s-4(r+k)+D(G)$. The multiplicity bound in
\eqref{eq:inertia} makes this at least $2N-s+D(G)$. For the second
claim, the difference between $4N-3s-4(r+k)$ and $3N-2D_Z$ is
$N-s-2r\ge0$.
\end{proof}

This proof retains the signed off-line remainder throughout. The positive
Gram defect belongs only to the actual simple real points. The second
inequality in \eqref{eq:finite}, rather than elementary counting from a
simple-zero proportion, will yield the distinct-zero companion.

\subsection{Retaining multiplicity excess and parity}

Keep the notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:finite}; in this subsection $k$
counts nonreal pairs, not frame length. Let $O$ count the distinct real
support points of odd multiplicity, and define the nonnegative excess
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excess}
 E=N-s-2r-2k.
\end{equation}
An atom of real multiplicity $m\ge2$ contributes $m-2$ to $E$; a nonreal
pair of multiplicity $m$ contributes $2(m-1)$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oddexcess}
 O\le s+E.
\end{equation}
Indeed, a nonsimple odd real point has multiplicity at least three and
contributes at least one to $E$. This is a statement about distinct odd
points, not a bound for odd-multiplicity copies.

\begin{proposition}[Exact parity and distinct residuals]\label{prop:parity}
For the finite multiset above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finiteparity}
 3s\ge2N-Q+2O+D(G),\qquad
 6D_Z\ge7N-2Q+O+2D(G).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The strong form already proved in Proposition~\ref{prop:finite} is
\[
 Q\ge4N-3s-4r-4k+D(G)=2N-s+2E+D(G).
\]
Write its nonnegative slack as
$\sigma=Q-(4N-3s-4r-4k+D(G))$. Direct substitution gives the exact
identities
\begin{align}
3s-\{2N-Q+2O+D(G)\}&=2(s+E-O)+\sigma,
\label{eq:parityresidual}\\
6D_Z-\{7N-2Q+O+2D(G)\}&=(s+E-O)+6k+2\sigma.
\label{eq:distinctresidual}
\end{align}
Both right-hand sides are nonnegative by \eqref{eq:oddexcess}.
\end{proof}

For clarity, the atomic contributions to $s+E-O$ are zero at a simple
real point, $m-2$ at a real even point, $m-3$ at a real odd point of
multiplicity at least three, and $2(m-1)$ at a nonreal pair. This proves
the bookkeeping for all integer multiplicities without a finite cutoff.
The parity inequalities are consequences of the known stable multiplicity
bound, not a claim of a new rank--trace theorem.

\begin{remark}[Sharp accounting and a false shortcut]
The inequality $2D_Z\ge N+s$ is false for general multisets: one real
point of multiplicity three has $(N,s,D_Z)=(3,0,1)$. The exact difference
is $2D_Z-N-s=2k-E$. Thus one cannot take one half of one plus the parity
simple-zero bound to obtain \eqref{eq:paritydistinct}.

Both residuals can vanish in an actual finite Hilbert model. Take the
probability density $\mathbf1_{[-1/2,1/2]}$ and distinct integer support
points with multiplicities one or two. Their features are orthonormal,
so $Q=s+4r$, $N=s+2r$, $O=s$, and $E=k=D(G)=\sigma=0$.
This is sharpness of the finite bookkeeping. It does not assert that zeta
zeros realize these configurations.
\end{remark}

One can also express the algebra as a scalar optimization. From normalized
bounds $s-2e\ge c$ and $s+e\ge o$, with $s,e,o\ge0$, the exact minimum
of $s$ in this relaxation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relaxedLP}
 \max\{0,c,(c+2o)/3\}.
\end{equation}
The three terms are immediate lower bounds. Conversely, take $s$ equal
to the displayed maximum and $e=\max\{0,o-s\}$. If $s\ge o$, then
$e=0$ and $s\ge c$; otherwise $s-2e=3s-2o\ge c$. This proves
feasibility and the stated relaxation optimum. Extra total-mass or Gram
constraints are not included, so no optimum over actual zero configurations
is claimed.

\section{From three-point energy to a density gain}

For a positive semidefinite matrix $M$ with diagonal one, define
$\cE(M)=2\sum_{i<j}|M_{ij}|^2$.

\begin{lemma}[Block defect and pinching]\label{lem:pinch}
We have $D(M)\ge\min\{1,\cE(M)\}$. If $M_j$ are the principal blocks
of a partition of $M$, then $D(M)\ge\sum_jD(M_j)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If all eigenvalues of $M$ are at most 2, then
$D(M)=\tr(M-I)^2=\cE(M)$. If some eigenvalue exceeds 2, its own
$\Psi$ value exceeds 1 and all other terms are nonnegative.
For pinching, diagonalize each principal block and let $(w_i)$ be the
resulting orthonormal basis of the full space. Scalar Jensen applied to
the spectral resolution of $M$ gives
\[
 \sum_jD(M_j)=\sum_i\Psi(\langle w_i,Mw_i\rangle)
 \le\sum_i\langle w_i,\Psi(M)w_i\rangle=D(M).
\]
Only scalar convexity of $\Psi$ is used; no operator-convex assertion is
needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three shifted partitions]\label{lem:triples}
Suppose $0<d\le1$, $R>0$, and the real even kernel satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
 2\{K(u)^2+K(v)^2+K(u+v)^2\}\ge d,
 \qquad u,v\ge0,\quad u+v\le R.
\end{equation}
If $\ell=x_s-x_1$ is the span of the simple real points, with $\ell=0$
when $s\le1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packing}
 D(G)\ge\frac d3\left(s-2-\frac{2\ell}{R}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $s\ge3$, the sum of the spans of its $s-2$ consecutive triples is
at most $2\ell$, since each successive gap occurs at most twice. Hence
at most $2\ell/R$ triples have span greater than $R$. Every remaining
triple has defect at least $d$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:pinch}.

Separate the starting indices of consecutive triples into their three
residue classes modulo 3. Within each class the triples are disjoint
principal blocks; add the unused points as singleton blocks. Apply
pinching to each partition and average the three inequalities. Every
short triple has been counted once, proving \eqref{eq:packing}. If its
right-hand side is negative, nonnegativity proves the inequality; this
also covers $s<3$.
\end{proof}

The factor $1/3$ records the three shifted partitions. Overlapping triples
cannot simply be summed without this step.

\subsection{Odd frames and exact pressure accounting}

The next construction adds energies of pair-disjoint triples inside one
frame. It applies defect pinching only between disjoint full frames. This
distinction is essential: arbitrary overlapping triple defects cannot be
summed. For instance, two consecutive three-by-three all-ones blocks have
total defect ten, while their four-by-four all-ones parent has defect eight.

\begin{lemma}[Odd-frame pressure bound]\label{lem:oddframe}
Let $K$ be a real even positive-type kernel with $K(0)=1$, satisfying
\eqref{eq:pressure} with parameters $p,\varepsilon>0$. Let $k\ge1$,
$M=2k+1$, and $k\varepsilon\le1$. For the Gram matrix of $s$ ordered
points of total span $\ell$, set
\[
 \alpha=\frac{k\varepsilon}{M},\qquad
 \beta=\frac{(M-1)p}{M}=\frac{2kp}{M}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oddpacking}
 D(G)\ge\alpha(s-M+1)-\beta\ell.
\end{equation}
As before, set $\ell=0$ for $s\le1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an $M$-point frame $y_1\le\cdots\le y_M$, select triples
$(y_{2j-1},y_{2j},y_{2j+1})$, $1\le j\le k$. They share at most one
vertex and no unordered pair. Thus their doubled pair energies sum to at
most $\cE(G_F)$, while their spans telescope:
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^k(y_{2j+1}-y_{2j-1})=y_M-y_1=:\ell_F.
\]
Adding their pressure inequalities gives
$\cE(G_F)+p\ell_F\ge k\varepsilon$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pinch},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:framepressure}
 D(G_F)+p\ell_F\ge k\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $\cE(G_F)\le1$, then $D(G_F)\ge\cE(G_F)$; otherwise
$D(G_F)\ge1\ge k\varepsilon$. This is where the unit cap is used.

Partition the full sequence into complete frames with starts in one
residue class modulo $M$, adding unused indices as singletons. Within
each class the frames are disjoint. Across all $M$ classes, there are
exactly $(s-M+1)_+$ complete frames, one for each possible start. Expanding
their spans into adjacent gaps gives
\[
 \sum_{a=1}^{s-M+1}(x_{a+M-1}-x_a)\le(M-1)\ell,
\]
because each gap occurs at most $M-1$ times. Apply pinching and
\eqref{eq:framepressure} in each class and average. This gives
\eqref{eq:oddpacking} for $s\ge M$. For $s<M$ its right side is
nonpositive, so nonnegativity of $D$ completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The construction is a simple nonuniform pair-capacity schedule within
the prior global pressure/frame methods of~\cite{Ainta,Trmdy,Tawan,Shi}.
Its short-interval consequence is obtained below from the exact finite
operator, without an additional large-frame Gram approximation.

\section{A quantitative obstruction for the cosine kernel}

For $0<\lambda\le1$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 f_\lambda(u)=\frac{\cos(\sqrt2u)}{\sqrt2\sin(\lambda/\sqrt2)}
       \mathbf1_{[-\lambda/2,\lambda/2]}(u),\qquad
 K_\lambda=\widehat f_\lambda.
\end{equation}
This is a nonnegative probability density. Set $a=\lambda/\sqrt2$,
$q=a\tan a$, and $X=\pi\lambda t$. Integration yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 K_\lambda(t)=
 \frac{\cos X-(X/a)\cot(a)\sin X}{1-X^2/a^2}.
\end{equation}
The apparent singularities at $X=\pm a$ are removable. Equivalently,
with $\operatorname{sinc}(x)=\sin(x)/x$ continuously extended at zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinc}
 K_\lambda(t)=
 \frac{\operatorname{sinc}(X-a)+\operatorname{sinc}(X+a)}
      {2\operatorname{sinc}(a)}.
\end{equation}

An actual positive root satisfies $X\tan X=q$. If $X,Y,X+Y$ all
corresponded to positive roots, tangent addition would force
$X^2+XY+Y^2+q^2=0$. The following estimate gives a quantitative version
without dividing by a possibly vanishing cosine or removable denominator.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit positive triangle energy]\label{lem:gap}
Fix $R>0$, put $M=\pi\lambda R$, and define $B,b$ as in
\eqref{eq:explicit}, using $a=\lambda/\sqrt2$ and $q=a\tan a$.
Then, throughout $u,v\ge0$, $u+v\le R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 2\{K_\lambda(u)^2+K_\lambda(v)^2+K_\lambda(u+v)^2\}
 \ge2(b/B)^2>0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $A_X=q\cos X-X\sin X$. For $Z=X+Y$, angle addition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:addition}
 (X^2+XY+Y^2+q^2)\sin X\sin Y
 =A_XA_Y-XA_X\sin Y-YA_Y\sin X-qA_Z.
\end{equation}
Take $X,Y\ge0$, $Z\le M$, and put
$h=\max\{|A_X|,|A_Y|,|A_Z|\}$. If $h\le q/2$, then
\[
 q\le q|\cos X|+q|\sin X|
 \le h+(q+X)|\sin X|,
\]
so $|\sin X|\ge q/[2(q+M)]$, and likewise for $Y$. Taking absolute
values in \eqref{eq:addition} therefore yields
\[
 \frac{q^4}{4(q+M)^2}\le h^2+(M+q)h
 \le(M+3q/2)h.
\]
If $h>q/2$, the other alternative in the definition of $b$ applies.
In either case $h\ge b>0$. The identity
\[
 A_X=q(1-X^2/a^2)K_\lambda(X/(\pi\lambda))
\]
holds also at the removable points by continuity. Since
$|q(1-X^2/a^2)|\le B$ for $0\le X\le M$, at least one of the three
kernel values has absolute value at least $b/B$. This proves
\eqref{eq:gap}.
\end{proof}

The zero-set obstruction itself is already used in~\cite{Ainta}. The
explicit formula in Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} makes its positive compact gap
available for every fixed exponent without a numerical minimum. Its
small size is acceptable for the qualitative strict-improvement theorem;
Section~\ref{sec:certificate} certifies a more useful gap in one example.

\section{Short-interval arithmetic and passage to the sharp density}

We record the exact analytic interface to avoid a support or smoothing
uniformity assumption. Fix $0<\lambda<\theta<1$, put $H=T^\theta$,
$L=\log T$, and $I=(T,T+H]$. Wang's Theorem 2.2 states that for every
fixed real even $g\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ supported in $[-\lambda,\lambda]$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:wangtest}
 W_I(g)&:=\sum_{\gamma,\gamma'\in I}
 \widehat g\!\left(\frac{i(\rho-\rho')L}{2\pi}\right)
 \frac4{4-(\rho-\rho')^2}\notag\\
 &=\frac{HL}{2\pi}\left(g(0)+\int_\R|\alpha|g(\alpha)d\alpha\right)
      +O_g(H+T^\lambda L^2).
\end{align}
The constants may depend on fixed $\lambda,\theta$. This is an
unconditional complex-zero formula; RH is not an assumption.

Choose a fixed real even $\eta\in C_c^\infty((-\lambda/2,\lambda/2))$
with $\int\eta^2=1$, and write $f=\eta^2$, $K=\widehat f$, $g=f*f$.
Both $g$ and $g''$ meet the fixed-test hypotheses. At
$z=i(\rho-\rho')L/(2\pi)$, differentiation of the Fourier transform gives
\[
 \widehat {g''}(z)=-4\pi^2z^2K(z)^2
                  =L^2(\rho-\rho')^2K(z)^2.
\]
The exact identity
\[
 \left(\widehat g(z)-\frac{\widehat {g''}(z)}{4L^2}\right)
 \frac4{4-(\rho-\rho')^2}=K(z)^2
\]
removes the rational weight. Apply \eqref{eq:wangtest} separately to
the two fixed tests and combine the resulting estimates. As in
\cite[Lemma 3.1]{Wang}, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unweighted}
 \sum_{\gamma,\gamma'\in I}K(z_\rho-z_{\rho'})^2
 =\cC(f)\frac{HL}{2\pi}+O_f(H+T^\lambda L^2),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:functional}
 z_\rho=\frac{i(\rho-1/2)L}{2\pi},\qquad
 \cC(f)=\int f^2+\iint|u-v|f(u)f(v)\,du\,dv.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the main term for $g''$ is
$-\int(f')^2+2\int f^2$, finite for the fixed smooth density, and its
coefficient is $1/(4L^2)$. A varying scalar coefficient after applying
the theorem to two fixed tests does not require a varying-test theorem.

Reflection $\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho$ preserves the height interval and
multiplicity and maps $z_\rho$ to $\bar z_\rho$. Thus the multiset
$Z_T=\{z_\rho:\gamma\in I\}$ satisfies Proposition~\ref{prop:finite}.
Its simple real points are precisely the simple critical zeros. Their
real-coordinate span is at most $X_T=HL/(2\pi)$. The short-interval
Riemann--von Mangoldt formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalization}
 N(T,H)=X_T+O(H+\log T),\qquad X_T/N(T,H)\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
After division by $N$, the error in \eqref{eq:unweighted} is
$O_f(1/L+T^{\lambda-\theta}L)=o(1)$. The strict support gap
$\lambda<\theta$ is therefore necessary at this stage.

The cosine density \eqref{eq:density} is Wang's minimizing density and
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
 \cC(f_\lambda)=\frac\lambda2+
         \frac1{\sqrt2}\cot(\lambda/\sqrt2)=2-c(\lambda).
\end{equation}
It is not a smooth compactly supported test because its endpoint values
do not vanish. Choose even cutoffs $0\le\chi_\varepsilon\le1$, supported
strictly inside $(-\lambda/2,\lambda/2)$ and approaching one in the
interior. Normalizing $\eta_\varepsilon=\chi_\varepsilon\sqrt{f_\lambda}/
\|\chi_\varepsilon\sqrt{f_\lambda}\|_2$ gives densities
$f_\varepsilon=\eta_\varepsilon^2$ converging in both $L^1$ and $L^2$
to $f_\lambda$. Moreover $f_\lambda\to f_\theta$ in those spaces as
$\lambda\uparrow\theta$, and $\cC$ is continuous for these convergences
on bounded support~\cite[Section 4]{Wang}.

For normalized nonnegative densities,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform}
 \sup_{x\in\R}|\widehat f(x)-K_\theta(x)|\le\|f-f_\theta\|_1,
 \qquad |\widehat f(x)|,|K_\theta(x)|\le1.
\end{equation}
Thus the doubled triple energy changes by at most
$12\|f-f_\theta\|_1$. This retains a fixed positive energy margin
through the smooth approximation.

\begin{proposition}[Transfer with a certified limiting gap]\label{prop:transfer}
Fix $0<\theta<1$, $R>0$, and $0<d\le1$. Suppose \eqref{eq:triangle}
holds for $K_\theta$. Define
\[
 c_d=\frac{c(\theta)-2d/(3R)}{1-d/3}.
\]
Then the simple-critical and distinct proportions in \eqref{eq:mainbounds}
are at least $c_d$ and $(1+c_d)/2$, respectively.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First fix $0<d'<d$. Choose a sequence of fixed smooth densities $f_j$
supported in $(-\lambda_j/2,\lambda_j/2)$, with
$\lambda_j<\theta$, converging to $f_\theta$ in $L^1$ and $L^2$.
By \eqref{eq:uniform}, every sufficiently large $j$ has triple energy
at least $d'$. Fix such a $j$ before letting $T$ grow.

Write $s=N_0^s(T,H)$, $N=N(T,H)$, and $Q$ for the pair sum with this
fixed kernel. Proposition~\ref{prop:finite} and Lemma~\ref{lem:triples},
with actual span at most $X_T$, imply
\[
 \left(1-\frac{d'}3\right)\frac sN
 \ge2-\frac QN-\frac{2d'}{3N}-\frac{2d'X_T}{3RN}.
\]
Let $T\to\infty$, using \eqref{eq:unweighted} and
\eqref{eq:normalization}, and then let $j\to\infty$. The result is
$\liminf s/N\ge c_{d'}$.

For the distinct count, the other finite inequality gives
\[
 \liminf\frac{N^d(T,H)}N
 \ge\frac{1+c(\theta)}2+\frac{d'}6(c_{d'}-2/R)
 =\frac{1+c_{d'}}2.
\]
The equality uses $c_{d'}-c(\theta)=d'(c_{d'}-2/R)/3$.
Finally let $d'\uparrow d$; all scalar expressions are continuous since
$d\le1<3$. No test function changes inside a large-$T$ limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} with $\lambda=\theta$ proves a limiting-kernel gap
at least $2(b/B)^2$, and hence at least the positive $\delta$ in
\eqref{eq:explicit}. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} with
$d=\delta$. Its constant equals \eqref{eq:cstar}. The gain is strictly
positive because $R>2/c(\theta)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pressure}]
First fix $0<\varepsilon'<\varepsilon$, keeping $p$ and $k$ fixed.
Choose fixed smooth densities $f_j$ supported inside
$(-\lambda_j/2,\lambda_j/2)$, with $\lambda_j<\theta$, converging to
$f_\theta$ in $L^1$ and $L^2$. The uniform estimate
\eqref{eq:uniform} changes the triple energy by at most
$12\|f_j-f_\theta\|_1$. Thus every sufficiently large $j$ satisfies
the all-gap pressure inequality with the same $p$ and target
$\varepsilon'$. Also $k\varepsilon'<1$.

Put $M=2k+1$, $\alpha'=k\varepsilon'/M$ and $\beta=2kp/M$.
For each individual fixed $j$, Proposition~\ref{prop:finite} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:oddframe} give
\[
 (1-\alpha')\frac{s}{N}\ge2-\frac QN
 -\beta\frac{X_T}{N}-\frac{\alpha'(M-1)}{N}.
\]
Take $T\to\infty$ with this test fixed. If $c_j=2-\cC(f_j)$ and
$b_j=(c_j-\beta)/(1-\alpha')$, then $\liminf s/N\ge b_j$.
The stronger finite inequality for distinct points gives
\[
 2\liminf\frac{N^d(T,T^\theta)}{N}
 \ge1+c_j+\alpha'b_j-\beta=1+b_j.
\]
The identity follows from $(1-\alpha')b_j=c_j-\beta$. It does not
use the false general counting shortcut $N^d\ge(N+s)/2$.

Now let $j\to\infty$, so $c_j\to c(\theta)$, and finally let
$\varepsilon'\uparrow\varepsilon$. The limiting denominator is at
least $M-1>0$, including the endpoint $k\varepsilon=1$.
This proves both proportions in Theorem~\ref{thm:pressure}.
No support parameter or smoothing scale varies within a height limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:wholecurve}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:gap} gives the compact energy bound with $d=\delta$.
Nonnegativity outside its triangle gives the all-gap inequality
\[
 2\{K_\theta(u)^2+K_\theta(v)^2+K_\theta(u+v)^2\}
 +(d/R)(u+v)\ge d.
\]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:pressure} with $\varepsilon=d$ and $p=d/R$.
The gain coefficient satisfies
\[
 \frac{kd}{2k+1-kd}-\frac d{3-d}
 =\frac{d(k-1)}{(2k+1-kd)(3-d)}>0
\]
for $k>1$. Finally $b\le q/2$ and $B>q$ in \eqref{eq:explicit}, so
$2(b/B)^2<1/2$ and $0<\delta=(b/B)^2<1/4$. Thus $k=2$ is always
admissible, giving the stronger entire positive curve.
\end{proof}

The same compact-to-pressure conversion works with any stronger certified
$d$, using strict lower targets during smoothing before the final scalar
limit. In particular $d=1/7000$, $R=21/4$, $k=7000$ gives
\eqref{eq:reuseexample}. Its gain relative to the original triple bound
is exactly $(3-d)/2=20999/14000$. This is a ratio of gains above Wang's
baseline, not a ratio of the full proportions.

\section{Classical odd zeros and the range extension}\label{sec:parityproof}

The positive Gram defect is useful for quantitative pressure improvements.
For the qualitative parity theorem we may discard it: the multiplicity
excess in the same finite inequality already supplies the missing
information when combined with a classical sign-change theorem.

\subsection{The exact classical count and interval packing}

We use the following fixed consequence of Selberg's theorem, stated as
Theorem B on p.~523 of Karatsuba~\cite{Karatsuba}: for the fixed
$\alpha=51/100$ there exist constants $a>0$ and $t_a$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seed}
 N_{\rm odd}(t,t^\alpha)\ge a t^\alpha\log t\qquad(t\ge t_a).
\end{equation}
The zeros in this count are distinct and of odd order on the critical
line. Karatsuba's stronger main theorem, p.~524, gives the same kind of
bound for $\alpha=27/82+\epsilon$, $0<\epsilon\le0.001$. His final
argument in Section~12, p.~536, selects disjoint sign-changing intervals;
this confirms the distinct-count convention. We import the classical
theorem and its analytic estimates; we do not reprove them. Selberg's
original paper was not consulted, so our source for its exact statement
is Karatsuba's primary article.

Fix $\alpha<\theta<1$ and put $H=T^\theta$. Starting at $t_0=T$, define
$t_{j+1}=t_j+t_j^\alpha$ and keep all complete adjacent intervals lying
inside $(T,T+H]$. Their total length is $H-O(T^\alpha)=H(1-o(1))$.
Their endpoints are all $T(1+o(1))$, and hence their logarithms are
$\log T+o(1)$. Summing \eqref{eq:seed} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packedodd}
 N_{\rm odd}(T,H)\ge (a+o(1))H\log T.
\end{equation}
If a seed is stated with different open or closed endpoint conventions,
at most two distinct points per block must be removed. There are
$O(H/T^\alpha+1)$ blocks, so this loss is $o(H\log T)$ and does not
change the conclusion. Counting odd copies instead would not justify
this bounded endpoint loss, which is another reason to keep the precise
classical convention.

The Riemann--von Mangoldt formula gives
$N(T,H)\sim H\log T/(2\pi)$. Choose once and for all a sufficiently
small $\kappa>0$ with $\kappa<2\pi a$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oddseedlimit}
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_{\rm odd}(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge\kappa
 \qquad\hbox{for every fixed }\alpha<\theta<1.
\end{equation}
The same $\kappa$ works because it was chosen from the one fixed seed.
Only the eventual height is allowed to depend on $\theta$. Nothing here
permits $\theta-\alpha$ to shrink with $T$.

\subsection{Fixed-test transfer, including nonpositive cosine bounds}

Fix $\alpha<\theta<1$ and $0<\lambda<\theta$. Use the transformed
zeta multiset and one fixed smooth probability density $f=\eta^2$ with
support in $(-\lambda/2,\lambda/2)$ from the arithmetic transfer above.
Its real simple and odd points are precisely those counted by $N_0^s$
and $N_{\rm odd}$. Equations \eqref{eq:unweighted} and
\eqref{eq:functional} give
\[
 Q/N\longrightarrow\cC(f).
\]
This is an exact unweighted pair sum followed by an asymptotic limit.
The weight removal uses Wang's theorem separately for two fixed test
functions, including the differentiated test; it does not assume that
individual complex pair terms are nonnegative. Its normalized error is
$O_f(1/\log T+T^{\lambda-\theta}\log T)=o(1)$.

Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:parity}, Proposition~\ref{prop:finite},
$D(G)\ge0$, $N^d\ge N_{\rm odd}$, and \eqref{eq:oddseedlimit}.
For this fixed test we obtain
\begin{align*}
\liminf N_0^s/N&\ge\max\{0,2-\cC(f),[2-\cC(f)+2\kappa]/3\},\\
\liminf N^d/N&\ge\max\{\kappa,[3-\cC(f)]/2,
                            [7-2\cC(f)+\kappa]/6\}.
\end{align*}
Choose the normalized squares of smooth cutoffs of the cosine square
root described earlier, with fixed support parameter $\lambda$.
After the height limit, let the cutoff converge in $L^1$ and $L^2$ to
$f_\lambda$. Then $\cC(f)\to2-c(\lambda)$ by \eqref{eq:cost}.
Finally let $\lambda\uparrow\theta$. Continuity gives
\eqref{eq:paritysimple}--\eqref{eq:paritydistinct}. This argument is
valid when $c(\lambda)\le0$; positivity was needed for the pressure gain,
not for the pair-sum limit or the finite inequalities.

For completeness, the cosine cost also has a short direct evaluation.
Let $J(x)=\int|x-y|f_\lambda(y)\,dy$. On the support interior,
$J''=2f_\lambda$ and $f_\lambda''=-2f_\lambda$. The even function
$f_\lambda+J$ is therefore constant. At $b=\lambda/2$ it equals
$b+\cot(\lambda/\sqrt2)/\sqrt2$, since $J(b)=b$. Multiplication by
$f_\lambda$ and integration gives
\[
 \cC(f_\lambda)=\frac\lambda2+
               \frac1{\sqrt2}\cot(\lambda/\sqrt2)=2-c(\lambda).
\]
The legal order is thus fixed test, height limit, smooth approximation,
and support limit. No test or derivative degree grows with the height.

\subsection{Proof of the strict range statements}

The definition of $\theta_0$ and the derivative formula require no
computed root. To verify $\alpha<\theta_0$, put $a_0=\alpha/\sqrt2$.
The elementary bounds $\cos a_0\ge1-a_0^2/2>0$ and
$0<\sin a_0/a_0\le1$ imply
\[
 a_0\cot a_0\ge1-\alpha^2/4,
 \qquad c(\alpha)\le2-\frac1\alpha-\frac\alpha4
                  =-\frac{1801}{20400}<0.
\]
Also $\tan x>x$ on $(0,\pi/2)$ gives $c(1)>1/2$, and
$c'(\theta)>0$ gives the unique zero. On $[\alpha,\theta_0]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivativecap}
 c'(\theta)=\tfrac12\cot^2(\theta/\sqrt2)
     <\frac1{\theta^2}\le\frac{10000}{2601}<4.
\end{equation}

For $\delta$ in \eqref{eq:paritydelta}, we have
$\alpha<\theta_1<\theta_0$ and
\[
 c(\theta_1)=-\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_0}c'(t)\,dt
             >-4\delta\ge-\kappa.
\]
The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:paritysimple} is therefore greater
than $\kappa/3$ at $\theta_1$, and is no smaller at any fixed larger
exponent. With $\delta_{\rm half}$ in \eqref{eq:halfdelta}, the same
argument gives $c(\theta_{\rm half})>-\kappa/4$; the separately proved
distinct formula yields
\[
 \frac{3+2c(\theta_{\rm half})+\kappa}{6}
      >\frac12+\frac\kappa{12}.
\]
This proves the remaining claims of Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}.

At $\theta_0$ itself, \eqref{eq:paritysimple} gives a lower proportion
at least $2\kappa/3>0$. The strict extension follows from this positive
margin and continuity, without a new pair-correlation support estimate.
Its numerical size remains unknown because the imported fixed classical
constant has not been evaluated.

\section{Localizing the optimized Selberg detector}
\label{sec:localselberg}

Pearce-Crump~\cite{PearceCrump} constructs a positive-semidefinite sum of
three squared mollifiers whose critical-line value is Hardy's real function
times a nonnegative weight. Sign changes therefore detect distinct
odd-multiplicity critical zeros. Write $\beta_U$ for the analytic half and
$U\asymp T^u$ for the mollifier length. The source normalization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selbergnormalization}
 \aleph(s)Y_U(s)=2\operatorname{Re}\beta_U(s)
 \qquad (\operatorname{Re}s=1/2).
\end{equation}
For the rank-three profile, the diagonal quadratic form satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c3diagonal}
 S_C(1,U)\le\frac{C_3+o(1)}{\log U}.
\end{equation}
The profile is first regularized by a fixed $\varepsilon>0$ so that the
terminal coefficient needed by the horizontal Jensen estimate is nonzero.
The limit $T\to\infty$ is taken first, followed by
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$; no estimate uniform in $\varepsilon$ is used.

Fix $1/2<\theta<1$, put $H=T^\theta$, and choose a fixed
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localurange}
 0<u<\frac{\theta-1/2}{2}.
\end{equation}
Bertrand's postulate supplies a prime $U$ with
$T^u\le U\le2T^u$, hence $\log U=u\log T+O(1)$. Use the source's fixed
analytic half on the dyadic block $[T,2T]$ and apply Littlewood's rectangle
argument only on $[T,T+H]$. Since $H<T$ for all sufficiently large $T$,
the shorter rectangle stays inside the same block.

Put $x=1-2\delta\asymp1/\log T$. The horizontal increment bound in the
source is
\[
 O\!\left(U\log(2T)\{\log^2(2T)+\eta_U+1\}\right),
\]
where $\eta_U=O_\varepsilon(1)$ for fixed regularization. This is $o(H)$
under \eqref{eq:localurange}. The auxiliary zeta and mollifier increments
are $O(\log T)$ and $O(U)$. On the right auxiliary edge, the principal
logarithms have absolutely convergent Dirichlet expansions with no constant
term. Termwise integration therefore gives a uniform $O(1)$ contribution.
The short rectangle inequality is consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shortrectangle}
 \frac{\pi x}{2}N_{\rm odd}(T,H)
 \ge-\int_T^{T+H}\log|\beta_U(\delta+it)|\,dt
       -H\log2-o(H).
\end{equation}

It remains to retain the mean-square constant on the shorter interval. The
diagonal estimate is pointwise before averaging. Its remainder is
\[
 O(T^{-1/2}U^2\log^2(2U))=o(1)
\]
because $u<1/4$. The approximate-functional-equation remainder is also
pointwise:
\[
 O(T^{-1/4}U\log^3T)=o(1).
\]
The source's rational-frequency lemma applies to arbitrary intervals
$I_{\xi,\eta}\subseteq[T,2T]$. For the moving square-root truncation
$X\asymp T^{1/2}$, divide its off-diagonal estimate by $H$ instead of $T$.
Since $T^{\delta-1/2}=T^{-x/2}=O(1)$, the result is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localoffdiagonal}
 O\!\left(T^{1/2+2u-\theta}\log T\right)=o(1),
\end{equation}
precisely by \eqref{eq:localurange}. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localmeansquare}
 \frac1H\int_T^{T+H}|\beta_U(\delta+it)|^2dt
 \le\frac{C_3+o(1)}{x\log U}.
\end{equation}

Jensen's inequality, \eqref{eq:shortrectangle}, and
\eqref{eq:localmeansquare} give
\[
 N_{\rm odd}(T,H)\ge\frac{H}{\pi x}
 \left\{\log\frac{x\log U}{C_3}-2\log2+o(1)\right\}.
\]
The factor $2\log2$ is forced by \eqref{eq:selbergnormalization}. The
right side is optimized at $x=4eC_3/\log U$, yielding
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localsignchanges}
 N_{\rm odd}(T,H)\ge
 \frac{H\log U}{4\pi eC_3}(1+o(1)).
\end{equation}
Since
$N(T,T^\theta)\sim T^\theta\log T/(2\pi)$,
\eqref{eq:localsignchanges} gives
\[
 \liminf\frac{N_{\rm odd}(T,T^\theta)}{N(T,T^\theta)}
 \ge\frac{u}{2eC_3}.
\]
This holds for every fixed $u$ in \eqref{eq:localurange}. Taking the
supremum over those fixed choices proves \eqref{eq:localodd}; the mollifier
exponent never varies with $T$.

Apply the pointwise parity inequality
$3N_0^s\ge2N-Q+2N_{\rm odd}+D$ with $D\ge0$. Wang's fixed-test limit gives
the $c(\theta)$ contribution from $(2N-Q)/N$, and \eqref{eq:localodd}
supplies the odd support. This proves \eqref{eq:localsimple}.

For the threshold, put
\[
 F(\theta)=c(\theta)+\frac{\theta-1/2}{2eC_3}.
\]
It is strictly increasing because
\[
 F'(\theta)=\frac12\{\csc^2(\theta/\sqrt2)-1\}
             +\frac1{2eC_3}>0.
\]
Exact rational Taylor enclosures prove
$F(0.5459)<0<F(0.546)$, giving the asserted root bracket. At
$\theta=0.546$, the fixed choice $u=0.02299$ has strict exponent margin
$\theta-1/2-2u=0.00002$ and gives \eqref{eq:localpoint}. The boundary
choice $u=0.023$ is excluded because it leaves no decay in
\eqref{eq:localoffdiagonal}.

\section{The compact certificate}\label{sec:certificate}

For $\theta=3/4$ and $R=21/4$, a finite interval computation proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numericalgap}
 2\{K_{3/4}(u)^2+K_{3/4}(v)^2+K_{3/4}(u+v)^2\}\ge\frac1{7000}
 \quad(u,v\ge0,\ u+v\le21/4).
\end{equation}
The certificate is a complete binary partition of the square $[0,R]^2$.
Every endpoint and threshold is rational. A box is either subdivided,
proved wholly outside the triangle, or accepted using an energy lower
bound valid on the entire box. The final tree contains 48,761 nodes,
24,252 accepted energy leaves, 129 outside leaves, and no unresolved
boxes (computation EXP-002~\cite{Record}).

For completeness, the analytic enclosure used at each leaf is simple.
Since $f_\theta$ is a probability density on $[-\theta/2,\theta/2]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lipschitz}
 |K_\theta'(x)|\le2\pi\int|u|f_\theta(u)du\le\pi\theta.
\end{equation}
On a real interval with center $x_0$ and radius $r$, an outward interval
evaluation of $K_\theta(x_0)$ therefore supplies the valid lower bound
\[
 |K_\theta(x)|\ge
 \max\{0,\operatorname{lower}(|K_\theta(x_0)|)-\pi\theta r\}.
\]
Apply this separately to the box ranges for $u$, $v$, and $u+v$, square
the nonnegative lower bounds, and double their sum. Every acceptance
comparison requires the entire lower enclosure to exceed the rational
threshold. An outside box is discarded only if the sum of its two lower
coordinates is strictly greater than $R$, so the triangle boundary is
retained.

Discovery used Arb through \texttt{python-flint==0.9.0} at 160-bit
precision and the removable-singularity-free formula \eqref{eq:sinc}.
Each accepted leaf was replayed at 256 bits with a separately derived
Taylor evaluator. For real $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:taylor}
 \operatorname{sinc}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{95}\frac{(-1)^jx^{2j}}{(2j+1)!}
 +\mathcal R(x),\qquad
 |\mathcal R(x)|\le\frac{|x|^{192}}{193!}.
\end{equation}
The remainder follows by applying the real sine Taylor theorem through
degree 192 and dividing by $x$, with continuous extension at zero.
Arb encloses both the polynomial and its remainder. This replay is an
independent kernel evaluator; it shares the partition reconstruction,
Lipschitz argument, and Arb library with the first computation. It is
not a second independent full verifier or a Lean proof.

Use the finite certificate \eqref{eq:numericalgap} in
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}, with $d=1/7000$ and $R=21/4$.
The full threshold $d$ is available because the proposition first uses
every $d'<d$ through smoothing and then passes to $d$. Substitution
gives the stated constants. The outward rational enclosures for their
values and positive difference are recorded with the certificate.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusions and further questions}

Finite stability retains two different kinds of information. The Gram
defect yields quantitative gains when simple critical points occur close
enough for certified pressure windows to interact. Multiplicity excess,
combined with distinct odd-multiplicity density, yields a qualitative
range extension even at the zero of the baseline cosine bound. The
two mechanisms have separate hypotheses, certificates, and distinct-zero
consequences. The localized Selberg detector makes the odd density explicit
for every fixed exponent above one half and gives a simple-critical positivity
threshold below $0.546$. None of these results proves the Riemann hypothesis.

The next analytic task is to reduce the $T^{1/2}U^2/H$ off-diagonal barrier.
A stronger rational-frequency spacing estimate or a different local mean
square could move the explicit threshold toward one half. Pearce-Crump's
rank-six benchmark gives a slightly smaller diagonal constant, but the full
rank-six profile data are not printed; the fully reproducible rank-three
profile is used here. For the pressure route, richer windows may improve
constants within the established framework; their existence does not
establish global optimality.

scoped contribution claimed here, without establishing exhaustive
priority or detecting unpublished concurrent work. The known finite

complete primary paper, Steuding's archived dissertation and published
abstract, and
\emph{Short mollifiers of the Riemann zeta-function}~\cite{CFKL}.
The short-mollifier result concerns critical-zero density
using higher derivatives; it supplies no automatic simple-zero theorem.
Steuding's stated localized theorem uses the first derivative. His proof
suggests a route to fixed higher derivatives, but a uniform two-shift
extension, the detector's exact counting convention, and a certified

review.
