\begin{abstract}
The July 2026 counterexample settled the Jacobian conjecture in every dimension $N \ge 3$ and
left the planar case $JC(2)$ open. This paper studies the planar case in exact arithmetic.
Four exclusion theorems follow from a weight-class calculus: a two-term
$P = x + a x^u y^v$ is never a Keller component, nor is any lower-weight perturbation of it;
no component has a Newton top edge anchored at the linear vertex $x$ with interior direction;
and if $x$ is a vertex of the Newton polygon then $P = x + f(y)$. A fifth exclusion, the
transport of the Keller constant along the staircase that swallows $x$, is proved at every
partner degree for six certified grid shapes. For the single degree pair $(72,108)$ left open
below $125$, the reduction of Guccione, Guccione, Horruitiner and Valqui has two coefficient
systems, Cases 1 and 2. We give a chart atlas of determinantal strata that closes
four-coefficient sub-families of Case 1 exactly, an independent derivation of Helali's
equations in which Case 2 is proved empty and Case 1 reduces to the unit ideal at five primes,
and the measured cost of a Case 1 certificate: exact standard bases did not finish in six
hours, and multi-modular certificates exceed $20{,}000$ primes. Case 1 is not closed, so
$(72,108)$ and $JC(2)$ remain open. Every result is marked machine-verified, derived or
conjectural.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and problem formulation}\label{sec:intro}

Let $P, Q \in \C[x, y]$ with $J(P, Q) := P_x Q_y - P_y Q_x \in \C^\times$ (a \emph{Keller
pair}; each member is a \emph{Keller component}). The two-variable Jacobian conjecture $JC(2)$
asserts every Keller pair is an automorphism pair. Following the July 2026 counterexample in
dimension 3 \cite{Alpoge2026}, $JC(2)$ is the surviving case, and the classical planar theory
(Newton polygons, approximate roots, gcd coverage) is its context. The three-dimensional
counterexample itself (validation, the seed family, the geometry of non-properness,
two-dimensional equivariant rigidity) is studied in the companion paper
\cite{SLfoundational}; this paper treats the planar case. Every computer-assisted statement
cites the archived computation that establishes it (EXP-$nnn$; see the section on data and
code availability).

The planar problem has one structural feature that makes exact computation possible and one
that makes it hard. The Keller condition is bilinear in the coefficients of $P$ and $Q$, so for
fixed $P$ it is a linear system in the coefficients of $Q$; deciding whether a given $P$ has a
partner of bounded degree is linear algebra, and a left-null covector of that system with
nonzero pairing is a finite certificate that no partner exists. The hard feature is that a
certificate is attached to a window of bounded degree, whereas the conjecture concerns partners
of every degree, and that the shapes that matter, the ones left by the classical coverage, carry
free coefficients. The paper addresses both.

The contributions are the following, each stated with the section that proves or measures it.
\begin{itemize}
\item A planar machine (eliminate, parametrize, complete, invert) that closes $\min\deg \le 2$
uniformly and forces perfect-power tops at bidegrees $(2,n)$ and $(3,n)$
(Sections~\ref{sec:methods} and~\ref{sec:theory}).
\item Four exclusion theorems from a weight-class calculus, with complete proofs, and a fifth
exclusion, for the staircase that swallows the linear vertex, proved at every partner degree on
six certified grid shapes through a certificate tower (Section~\ref{sec:theory}, Table~\ref{tab:exclusions}).
\item A recalibration of the frontier against primary sources, in a table that gives every
gcd and degree floor with its source and the evidence behind the reading
(Section~\ref{sec:frontier}, Table~\ref{tab:ladder}).
\item For the one degree pair $(72,108)$ left open below $125$: the map from the three
branches of the reduction onto the two coefficient systems of its Proposition~4.3 (Cases 1 and
2), a chart atlas of constructible determinantal strata that closes four-coefficient restrictions
of Case 1 exactly (Table~\ref{tab:atlas}, Figure~\ref{fig:atlas}), the independent derivation
and checks of Helali's claimed exclusion, and the measured cost of an independent certificate that
Case 1 is empty (Section~\ref{sec:helali}, Table~\ref{tab:cost}).
\item The truncation ladder for a simultaneous covector: no covector of degree $1$, $2$ or $3$,
hence a minimal degree $d_0 \ge 4$, with the finite ceiling and the natural $\mathfrak{sl}_2$
premise both refuted (Section~\ref{sec:ladder}).
\end{itemize}
Case 1 is not closed. The paper does not exclude $(72,108)$, does not raise the planar degree
floor, and does not decide $JC(2)$.

Machine verification is exact arithmetic: sympy over $\Q$ or $\Q(i)$, Singular standard

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The published methods on the planar conjecture fall into four groups. Each is characterized
here by what it proves and under which hypotheses, as the programme's literature records read
it; a statement taken from a source that was read only through another source is marked as such.

\emph{gcd and primality results.} For a Keller pair $(P,Q)$ the number $B=\gcd(\deg P,\deg Q)$
is constrained by results that treat a counterexample as a minimal object. Magnus
\cite{Magnus1955} proves invertibility when $B=1$ (the theorem is credited to the 1955 paper;
the formula that bears his name is in the 1954 paper). Nakai and Baba \cite{NakaiBaba1977}
cover $B\le 2$. Appelgate and Onishi \cite{AppelgateOnishi1985} and Nagata \cite{Nagata1989}
cover $B\le 8$ or $B$ prime; the record of the former states the result under the assumption
that the degree of one component has at most two prime factors. Abhyankar \cite{Abhyankar2008}
shows $B\ne p$ (Lemma 6.1 of the second part) and $B\ge 9$ (Corollary 8.9 of the third part), as \cite{GGV2017} reports. Heitmann
\cite{Heitmann1990} proves $B\ge 16$, a result Guccione, Guccione and Valqui reprove
elementarily \cite{GGV2017}, together with $B\ne 2p$ and the dichotomy $B=16$ or $B>20$. These are
theorems about every counterexample; none restricts a single polynomial the way the exclusions
of Section~\ref{sec:theory} do. The reading of the gcd ladder is tabulated in
Table~\ref{tab:ladder}.

\emph{Degree floors by enumeration.} Moh \cite{Moh1983} discards every counterexample with
$\max\deg\le 100$ by an analysis of configurations of roots, with complete published detail
only for the smallest case, of maximal degree $64$; the modern corner terminology turns his four exceptional cases into
six configurations that Guccione, Guccione, Horruitiner and Valqui enumerate and discard
\cite{GGHV2017, GGHV2022}. The first of these papers computes every possible corner and every
pair of degrees with $\max\deg\le150$; the second raises the floor to $\max\deg\ge125$ with the
single exception of $(72,108)$ and $(108,72)$, whose coefficient system the authors
state they could not solve. A companion result on the lower side of the Newton polygon
\cite{GGV2} discards corners and infinite families. Xu \cite{Xu2016} treats the
split of minor roots for the degrees $(99,66)$ and leaves one split unknown; one of his formulas
is obtained in \cite{GGHVApproxRoots} only as an inequality. Orevkov \cite{Orevkov2001}
analysed the possible counterexample of the family $F_{13}$ with $j=1$ of \cite{GGHV2017}. Zoladek
\cite{Zoladek2008} claims a larger lower bound for $B$, but the proof relies on a lemma with a
gap according to \cite{GGV2017}, so the claim is not used here.

\emph{Shape and vertex constraints.} Abhyankar's similarity of Newton polygons
\cite{Abhyankar1977}, in the form recorded by van den Essen \cite{vdEssen2000}, makes the polygons
of a Keller pair similar; the geometric fact that cuts edges above the diagonal was known to Joseph \cite{Joseph1975}, who used it for the polarization theorem. Makar-Limanov and Trakhtenberg \cite{MakarLimanovTrakhtenberg2024}
give a Newton-resolution list of admissible leading configurations, and the series of Hurst, Lee, Li and Nasr
\cite{HurstLeeLiNasr2022} gives vertex-region constraints on the polygons of counterexamples; its fourth part, by Lee and Li \cite{LeeLi2024}, locates the northeastern vertex of each inner
polynomial. These results are applied here to the degree-$72$ component of the open pair in
Section~\ref{sec:necessary}; the first retains the branch and the second leaves seven vertices.

\emph{Claimed exclusions of the last pair.} Helali \cite{Helali2026} publishes exact certificates
that both coefficient systems of Proposition~4.3 of \cite{GGHV2022} are empty, conditional on that
proposition being exhaustive. A second conditional claim that $(72,108)$ is excluded, by Guo, circulated
as a preprint in August 2026; the programme's records name it but hold no title, address or archive, so it is
neither cited as a work nor used here [U]. Section~\ref{sec:helali} reports the independent
verification of Helali's equations and certificates.

\emph{Equivariant and structural results.} Miyanishi \cite{Miyanishi2023} shows that an equivariant
\'etale endomorphism of $\mathbb A^2$ under a small finite group of even order is an automorphism.
Dubouloz and Palka \cite{DuboulozPalka2018} show that the equivariant statement fails for $\Gm$ on
$\mathbb Q$-acyclic pseudo-planes, so torus-equivariance does not rigidify \'etale maps on general
surfaces; the plane-specific statement is the one used here, with Shaska's classification by the signature of the weights
\cite{Shaska2026} as a parallel treatment. Three 2026 papers bear on the programme without
changing the planar status: Long \cite{Long2026} gives an explicit counterexample to the rank-two
Poisson conjecture, built from the three-dimensional counterexample; van den Essen \cite{vdEssen2026}
recovers that counterexample by an elementary search and recalls the Magnus criterion for planar maps;
Kanel-Belov and Fedorov \cite{KanelBelovFedorov2026} show that $JC(2)$ implies a generalized Dixmier
statement for the first Weyl algebra, a reformulation that does not bear on the degree frontier. Lu, Ruan,
Wang and Xiao \cite{LuRuanWangXiao2026} give Smith-form equivalence criteria for multivariate polynomial
matrices; Section~\ref{sec:atlas} explains why that theory is a comparison lens here and not a shortcut.

What the programme adds to this literature is of two kinds. The weight-class exclusions are
statements about one polynomial shape at every partner degree, proved with certificates that are
exact and archived; they sit beside, and do not replace, the gcd and degree results. The
$(72,108)$ computations are independent: they derive the system from the polygons, they attack it
with a different method (constructible determinantal strata), and they verify an external claim
with code written from the definitions.

\section{Methods: algorithms, software and reproducibility}\label{sec:methods}

This section states the six procedures behind the results, each with its inputs, parameters,
cost and the record that archives it, and then the software, the entry points and the checksums.
Costs are the ones the archived records print; a cost the record does not print is marked n/r (not
recorded).

\subsection{Software and arithmetic}

The planar machine and the window certificates run in sympy over $\Q$ and over fraction fields
$\Q(a,b)$; the first validation record names sympy 1.14 (EXP-001). The long certificate runs
(EXP-064, EXP-103 to EXP-135) use exact rational or modular arithmetic on plain lists, with
incremental elimination in place of repeated rank calls (EXP-064), number-theoretic transforms at
the moduli $998244353$ and $1004535809$ for interpolation of determinants (EXP-103, EXP-104),
and exact integer determinants where an endpoint of a support must be proved over characteristic
zero (EXP-104, EXP-105, EXP-108). Singular supplies the standard bases and unit-ideal tests of
EXP-137, EXP-139 and EXP-140 and the exact check of EXP-141; the Gr\"obner calculations of the atlas (for example
EXP-120) run in sympy. The archived outputs of EXP-137 and EXP-139 to EXP-141 do not record the Singular version; the version installed on the device that ran them was
Singular 4.3.2 when checked on 2026-11-14. The Case 1 certificate attempts use PARI/GP
(\texttt{polredabs}, \texttt{nffactor}), python-flint over FLINT 3.0.1, Singular, and a C
program for Chinese-remainder and rational reconstruction, run in WSL Ubuntu-24.04 (EXP-141,
EXP-142). Singular is invoked as an external program on exported ideals, with the exact input and the printed output archived next to the record. The covector exclusions of Section~\ref{sec:ladder} use the primes $2147483629$ and
$2147483587$ (EXP-067, EXP-072).

\subsection{A1: the planar machine}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{A1, the planar machine for a degree pair $(m,n)$ (EXP-017 to EXP-022)}\label{alg:machine}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require degrees $(m,n)$; $P$ in the affine gauge with symbolic coefficients $A$; partner $Q$ of degree $n$ with unknown coefficients $q$
\State form $J(P,Q)$; it is bilinear in $(A,q)$, so $J(P,Q)=1$ reads $M(A)\,q=e$ with $e$ the constant vector
\State eliminate $q$ by a lexicographic Gr\"obner basis of the minors of $[M(A)\mid e]$: the \emph{consistency ideal} $I(A)$
\State parametrize the variety $V(I(A))$; for each branch solve $M(A)\,q=e$ (one branch with a free tail at $(2,n)$)
\State invert each completed pair by a closed formula or by lexicographic Gr\"obner extraction
\State verify $G\circ F=\mathrm{id}$ and $F\circ G=\mathrm{id}$ by exact composition over $\Q(A)$ or at exact rational points
\Ensure $I(A)$, the completions, the inverses
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:machine} is the machine of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The only nonlinear step is the elimination of step 2;
steps 3 to 5 are linear algebra over the parametrized variety, and the output is checked by exact composition, never by
sampling alone. The machine proves a statement for every $P$ of a degree pair only when the consistency ideal is
computed symbolically: at $(3,4)$ the $6\times5$ linear map $Q_4\mapsto J(P_3,Q_4)$ is rank-deficient exactly when $P_3$ is a
perfect cube, and the remaining orbits are handled by $GL_2$-equivariance. Its measured scope: the
$(2,3)$ consistency ideal is generated by $(4A_0A_2-A_1^2)^2$, and the $(2,4)$ elimination returns
the same generator (EXP-019, EXP-020); the exhaustive grid of EXP-017 has $216$ samples at $(2,3)$ ($16$
admit Keller completions, $200$ give an inconsistent linear system) and $45$ at $(3,3)$ ($5$ admit
completions). The cost of one solve is seconds; the cost of the consistency ideal grows quickly with the
degree, which is why $(3,4)$ is treated through $GL_2$-equivariance and orbit representatives
(Theorem~\ref{thm:forced}).

\subsection{A2 and A3: the cleared window certificate and the classwise transport}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{A2, the cleared window certificate (EXP-042)}\label{alg:cleared}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require a shape $P=x+a\,x^uy^v+b\,x^d$ (or a multi-term staircase); window $N\ge\deg P-1$
\State list the monomials $m$ of the ansatz $Q$ of degree at most $N$; build the completion matrix $M_N$ whose column of $m$ is $L(m)=J(P,m)$ and the right-hand side $e$ ($J=1$)
\State compute a left-null covector $c$ of $M_N$ over $\Q(a,b)$ and clear denominators to polynomial entries, so $c^TM_N=0$ identically
\State pair: $\pi=c^Te$
\State \textbf{if} $\pi$ is a nonzero monomial in the parameters \textbf{then} the window is empty for every parameter value off the monomial's zero set
\Ensure covector $c$, pairing $\pi$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{A3, the classwise transport elimination (EXP-037)}\label{alg:transport}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $P$ with linear part $x$; grading $w=(v,1-u)$ of the minimal edge; window $N$
\State group the $Q$-monomials and the output rows by $w$-class; order classes ascending
\State \textbf{for} each class $s$ from the minimal $P$-class upward \textbf{do}
\State \quad solve the diagonal block (banded for the pure staircase), inject its kernel parameters into later classes
\State \quad emit its cokernel equations as obstructions
\State report the first class whose obstruction is inconsistent
\Ensure first inconsistent class; kernel parameters
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The input of Algorithm~\ref{alg:cleared} on the certified grid is $N=7$ for the eight grid points and
$N=5,\dots,9$ at $(u,v,d)=(2,1,2)$; the covectors have $4$ to $25$ rows in their support (EXP-042,
output of parts A and B). The elimination of Algorithm~\ref{alg:transport} reproduces the monolithic
test on five staircase shapes and on the positive control $x+(x+y)^2$, which stays consistent with $5$
kernel parameters, and on all $8$ grid samples the first inconsistent equation is at the constant's class
(EXP-037). The run times of these two procedures are n/r in their records; the window sizes bound them. The two procedures share the completion
matrix and differ in what they return: Algorithm~\ref{alg:cleared} returns one covector for the whole window and its pairing, which is the
quantity compared across windows (the law $-c_Na^N$ of Table~\ref{tab:exclusions}), while Algorithm~\ref{alg:transport} returns the
class at which the system first fails, which localizes the obstruction. A certificate is sound at every parameter value because its
entries are polynomial and the identity $c^TM_N=0$ is checked identically; the symbolic equations of the transport run over the fraction field and are
generic in the parameters, so soundness at a given value rests on the cleared certificates.

\subsection{A4: the constructible chart atlas}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{A4, one step of the constructible chart atlas (EXP-098, EXP-112 to EXP-131)}\label{alg:atlas}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the $302\times125$ augmented matrix $[M(\varepsilon)\mid b]$ with the constant column removed; a residual closed locus $Z$ in the parameters
\State select a $125$-row basis by modular pivoting at good primes (a deterministic extraction; the basis may differ from the previous one by tens of rows)
\State build the support graph of the selected $125\times125$ minor; compute its strongly connected components (SCC)
\State reconstruct the determinant of each cyclic block in characteristic zero: interpolate under structural degree bounds after removing the weights of a connected row/column grading, or use exact integer determinants at enough points; check with direct $125\times125$ determinants at rational controls
\State factor the determinant; for each factor classify $V(\text{factor})\cap Z$: unit, contained in $Z$, or residual
\State on a residual curve, take the class of the next section in the function field and compute its norm (Sylvester resultant and multiplication matrix); classify the roots
\State certify closure: an explicit Bezout identity, a gcd of norms, or a unit ideal in $\Q[\cdot]$
\State \textbf{if} a residual remains \textbf{then} set $Z$ to it and repeat
\Ensure closed strata with their certificates, and the residual left
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The parameters of Algorithm~\ref{alg:atlas} are the degree bounds of each interpolation, the primes, the
time limit of each exact step (from $180$ s for an FGLM conversion to $300$ s for a
generic rational Gr\"obner route) and the number of direct controls. Their values, the largest SCC, the
number of primes and the run time of every step are in Table~\ref{tab:atlas}. The method rests on a
commutative-algebra qualification proved in EXP-098: a cover of the parameter space by principal opens, each with a localized
syzygy, collapses to one global covector, so the strict generalization is a constructible rank
stratification in which new syzygies appear after a non-flat specialization; the atlas is built on that
distinction (Section~\ref{sec:atlas}). Every step persists its inputs and outputs as checkpoint and result files in the record's
folder; the larger steps record a SHA-256 of their result file, and a step that is stopped by its time limit is kept as a record
of the stop, not as a result. A selected basis is validated before it is used: the exact determinant must agree with direct
$125\times125$ determinants at independent rational controls, and the specialization of a multivariate determinant at a boundary value must
reproduce the previously certified determinant there.

\subsection{A5: the low-rank CRT certificate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{A5, the low-rank transfer certificate (EXP-135)}\label{alg:crt}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the normalized core $H(A,B,C)$ and a perturbation $K_T$ with $K_T^3=0$
\State compute ranks and a rank factorization $K_T=UV^{\mathsf T}$; derive separate degree bounds $(d_A,d_B,d_C,d_T)$ of the determinant difference from the ranks of the four coefficient matrices
\State \textbf{for} each of $30$ primes and each grid node $(A,B,C)$ with $A\in\{1..d_A{+}1\}$, $B\in\{0..d_B\}$, $C\in\{0..d_C\}$ \textbf{do}
\State \quad check that $H$ is invertible and $(V^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}U)^2=0$; then $\det(I+TV^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}U)=1$ in $T$
\State interpolate on the tensor grid: every coefficient of the cleared difference vanishes modulo every prime
\State bound the coefficients: $D=5{,}241{,}600$ clears denominators; the product of row $\ell^1$-norms bounds the determinant; compare with the prime product
\State \textbf{if} the prime product exceeds twice the bound \textbf{then} every coefficient is zero over $\Q$
\Ensure $\det(H+TK_T)=\det H$ in $\Q[A,B,C,T]$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Parameters in EXP-135: bounds $(25,24,6,7)$, a grid of $26\cdot25\cdot7=4{,}550$ nodes per prime, hence
$136{,}500$ controls over $30$ primes, $885$-bit coefficient bound against an $897$-bit prime product, and the
ranks $7,3,0$ of $K_T,K_T^2,K_T^3$ with a rank-six update in $C$. The run time is n/r in the record, which
reports the certificate artifact and its checksum instead. The structure of the argument explains its cost profile. The
determinant difference of a $33\times33$ core in four variables is never expanded: the ranks of the four coefficient matrices fix the
degrees, so the identity is a finite number of evaluations; each evaluation is a rank-seven determinant lemma at a prime; and the
height bound converts vanishing modulo thirty primes into vanishing over $\Q$. Because $K_T^3=0$ the transfer matrix is nilpotent, which is
what makes the pencil constant in $T$. The method applies to any section for which explicit degree and height bounds are available, and not to a
section that is affine or quadratic in $T$, whose actual $T$-coefficients must then be computed.

\subsection{A6: the multi-modular unit-ideal certificate}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{A6, the multi-modular cofactor certificate for Case 1 (EXP-141 to EXP-143)}\label{alg:multimod}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the $13$ Case 1 equations $E_e$ over $L=\Q[y]/(y^5-y^4+3y^3+3y^2+26)$, split into the branches $q_1=\pm c$
\State \textbf{for} each prime $p\in[2^{30},2^{31})$ and each root of the quintic modulo $p$ \textbf{do}
\State \quad lift cofactors $g_e$ with $\sum_e g_e E_e=1$ over $\mathbb F_p$ (a unit-ideal computation in Singular); discard unlucky primes
\State combine the roots by interpolation into residues in $L[q]$; accumulate residues by CRT
\State \textbf{if} a (sampled, then full) coordinate reconstructs rationally \textbf{then} \textbf{verify} $\sum_e g_eE_e=1$ exactly in $L[q]$ (python-flint) and over $\Q(y)$ (Singular)
\State \textbf{else} continue until the prime budget is exhausted
\Ensure a certified identity, or the measured height at the budget
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The pipeline is gated before production: the five gate primes $1021$, $1031$, $1033$, $1049$, $1061$ must give the unit ideal for the full system and a

\section{Theory: the planar machine and the exclusion theorems}\label{sec:theory}

This section states the theorems with their proofs. The machine of Algorithm~\ref{alg:machine} gives the uniform closure at
$\min\deg\le2$ and the forced tops; the weight-class calculus gives the four exclusions; the staircase transport and the certificate tower
give the fifth. The marks [MV], [D] and [C] qualify each statement as in the introduction, and a proof that rests on instances says so.

\subsection{The planar machine}

The Keller condition is bilinear in the coefficient vectors of $P$ and $Q$, hence
\emph{linear in $Q$ given $P$}. In the affine gauge (identity linear parts) this yields a
machine per degree pair: eliminate $Q$ (the consistency ideal in $P$'s coefficients),
parametrize, complete linearly, and invert or fiber-test (EXP-017, EXP-019, EXP-020).

\begin{theorem}[{[MV]}; uniform closure at $\min\deg \le 2$ (EXP-021/022)]\label{thm:uniform}
Write $\ell = \alpha x + \beta y$, $\beta \ne 0$. In shear coordinates the Keller condition
for $P = x + f(\ell)$ (any polynomial $f$) is a linear first-order PDE with characteristic
invariant $P$; its complete polynomial solution space is $Q = \ell/\beta + H(P)$, $H$
arbitrary, and the pair inverts by one closed formula. Consequently every planar Keller map
with $\min(\deg P, \deg Q) \le 2$ is invertible, and the whole quasi-triangular class
$P \sim x + f(\ell)$ is invertible at every degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV]}; forced tops at $(2, n)$ and $(3, n)$ (EXP-019/020/022/039)]
\label{thm:forced}
The $(2,3)$ consistency ideal is the single equation $(4A_0A_2 - A_1^2)^2 = 0$ ($P$'s top
form is a perfect square); the $(2,4)$ elimination returns the same ideal. At $(3, 4)$, the
$6 \times 5$ linear map $Q_4 \mapsto J(P_3, Q_4)$ is rank-deficient exactly when $P_3$ is a
perfect cube: all $5 \times 5$ minors vanish on the cube parametrization, every minor lies in
the Hessian-covariant ideal, and completeness follows from $GL_2$-equivariance
($J(f \circ A, g \circ A) = \det(A)\, J(f, g) \circ A$, verified identically) plus full rank
at the two non-cube orbit representatives $x^2 y$ and $x y (x + y)$. On the cube stratum,
sampled completions are Keller and invert with explicit polynomial inverses ($4/4$ by lex
Gr\"obner extraction). At $(3,3)$ the consistency ideal forces the quasi-triangular alignment
at the radical level.
\end{theorem}

The degree columns close by classical coverage: $\gcd(2, n) \le 2$ and $\gcd(3, n) \in
\{1, 3\}$ are inside the verified $\gcd$ landscape of Section~\ref{sec:frontier}, so the
machine's contribution there is the \emph{constructive} loop (explicit consistency varieties
and inverses), stated as replication. The classical degree descent is an algorithm on this
machine: subtract $c P^k$ while the tops are power-proportional, finish with
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}; it explicitly inverted a deterministic library of composed Keller
maps, and rotate-descent (rotation of linear-base tops plus de Jonquieres subtraction,
EXP-034) linearizes that entire library in $0$ to $6$ steps. Its exact limit: mixed-corner
tops stay mixed under rotation; those are the classical hard shapes, and they are where the
exclusion theorems below take over.

\subsection{Four unconditional exclusions}

Throughout, $P$ has linear part $x$ after affine gauge. The weight-class calculus: under
$w = (v, 1 - u)$, the operator $L=J(P, \cdot)$ shifts weight by a constant, so the Keller
equation decouples into weight classes; the technique goes back to Dixmier and Joseph
\cite{Joseph1975} and is standard in the polygon literature \cite{GGV2017}; the statements
below appear to be new in the stated generality (Section~\ref{sec:positioning}).

\begin{theorem}[{[MV/D]}; the weight-class theorem (EXP-029)]\label{thm:t1}
For $u \ge 2$, $v \ge 1$, $a \ne 0$, the polynomial $P = x + a\,x^u y^v$ is not a Keller
component: no $Q$, of any degree, satisfies $J(P, Q) \in \C^\times$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Give $x,y$ the weights $(v, 1-u)$, so $P$ is homogeneous of weight $v$ and $w(x)+w(y)=v+1-u$. For
homogeneous $f,g$ the bracket $J(f,g)$ is homogeneous of weight $w(f)+w(g)-(v+1-u)$; hence
$L=J(P,\cdot)$ raises weight by $u-1$ and the equation $J(P,Q)=1$ splits over the weight classes of $Q$
(each class component of a polynomial is a finite sum). The constant has weight $0$, so only the class of
weight $1-u$, the class of $y$, contributes. With $d=\gcd(u-1,v)$, $k=(u-1)/d$ and $m=v/d$, that class is
spanned by the ray $g_s = x^{ks}y^{ms+1}$, $s\ge0$, since $w(x^iy^j)=1-u$ means $mi=k(j-1)$. Put
$e_s=x^{ks}y^{ms}$. Then $J(x,g_s)=(ms+1)\,e_s$, and
$J(x^uy^v,g_s)=x^{ks+u-1}y^{ms+v}\,[u(ms+1)-vks]=(ms+u)\,e_{s+d}$, because $ks+u-1=k(s+d)$,
$ms+v=m(s+d)$ and $u(ms+1)-vks=ms+u$ (use $u-1=kd$, $v=md$) [D]. So
\begin{equation}\label{eq:band}
L(g_s)=(ms+1)\,e_s+a\,(ms+u)\,e_{s+d},
\end{equation}
with $B_s=ms+u$ a positive integer (this matches the exact banded formula verified on the grid
$u\in\{2..7\}$, $v\in\{1..7\}$, $s\le5$ in EXP-029). Write the class of $Q$ as $\sum_{s=0}^{S}c_sg_s$.
The row of $e_0$ gives $c_0=1$. For every $j\ge1$ the row of $e_{jd}$ gives
$c_{jd}=-a\,(m(j-1)d+u)\,c_{(j-1)d}/(mjd+1)$, which is nonzero because $a\ne0$ and $B>0$; the rows of
$e_s$ with $d\nmid s$ force $c_s=0$. If $J$ is the largest index with $c_{Jd}\ne0$, the row of
$e_{(J+1)d}$ reads $a\,B_{Jd}\,c_{Jd}=0$, a contradiction for every truncation. Hence no polynomial $Q$
exists. The record also checks, end to end including $d>1$, that full-window inconsistency
coincides with ray-class inconsistency at every probe, and that for $P=x+a\,x^{54}y^{81}$ (degree $135$) the
class is inconsistent for all $a\ne0$ at every truncation $S\le 8$ (partner degrees to about $1073$).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV/D]}; lower-weight perturbations never rescue (EXP-030/031)]\label{thm:t2}
With $u, v, a$ as above and $R$ any polynomial whose monomials have degree $\ge 2$ and
$(v, 1-u)$-weight strictly below $v$: $P = x + a\,x^u y^v + R$ is not a Keller component.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $P=P_{\mathrm{top}}+R$ with $P_{\mathrm{top}}=x+a\,x^uy^v$, $w=(v,1-u)$, $L=J(P,\cdot)$ and $L_{\mathrm{top}}=J(P_{\mathrm{top}},\cdot)$;
$P_{\mathrm{top}}$ is homogeneous of weight $v$.

\emph{Shifts.} $L_{\mathrm{top}}$ raises weight by $u-1$, and $J(R,\cdot)$ raises it by less than $u-1$ because the monomials of $R$ have weight below $v$.

\emph{Kernel.} $P_{\mathrm{top}}$ is not composite (not of the form $g(h)$ with $\deg g\ge2$): $P_{\mathrm{top}}(x,0)=x$ has degree one, whereas $g(h)(x,0)$ has a
degree that is a multiple of $\deg g$ unless $h(x,0)$ is constant. For a non-composite polynomial the ring of constants of $J(P_{\mathrm{top}},\cdot)$ is
$\C[P_{\mathrm{top}}]$ (Zaks; Nowicki's monograph on rings of constants of derivations; the reference is a pointer that was not read on a primary source [U]).
Hence the kernel of $L_{\mathrm{top}}$ on the class of weight $kv$ is the line spanned by $P_{\mathrm{top}}^k$. The kernel statement is also
machine-checked coefficient by coefficient on the grids $(2,2)$, $(3,2)$, $(4,6)$ with $k\le3$ (EXP-030, EXP-031); the first derivation, which asserted
injectivity on every class, was refuted by the machine and repaired as a kernel statement.

\emph{Top-down.} Suppose $J(P,Q)=1$ and let $Q_{\max}$ be the top weight component of $Q$, of weight $\sigma>1-u$. The top weight component of
$J(P,Q)$ is $L_{\mathrm{top}}(Q_{\max})$, of weight $\sigma+u-1>0$, so it vanishes and $Q_{\max}=c\,P_{\mathrm{top}}^k$. Since $J(P,P^k)=0$, the polynomial
$Q-cP^k$ solves the same equation, and its top weight is strictly smaller because $P^k$ has top weight component $P_{\mathrm{top}}^k$. Weights are
integers, so after finitely many steps every class has weight at most $1-u$. The weight-$0$ component of $J(P,Q)$ is then $L_{\mathrm{top}}(Q_{1-u})$, since
the contributions of $R$ and of lower classes have negative weight, and $L_{\mathrm{top}}(Q_{1-u})=1$ is impossible by Theorem~\ref{thm:t1}, whose proof
uses only the class of weight $1-u$. The annihilation lemma is not needed for this all-degree statement; it is what makes a window covector kill the tail sources
(danger tails $xy^3$, $y^3$ and their combination give pairings that are pure powers of $a$ over $\Q(a,b)$ and $\Q(a,b,c)$ at window $\le10$, EXP-031). The statement is
[D] in general, with the kernel identification resting on the reference marked [U] and on the checked instances.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV/D]}; every $x$-anchored edge falls (EXP-032)]\label{thm:t3}
Let $z = x^k y^m$, $k, m \ge 1$, and $E = x\,\varphi(z)$, $\deg\varphi = g \ge 1$, $\varphi(0)\ne0$. On the
$y$-class the edge operator is \emph{multiplication}:
$J(E, g_s) = [(ms + 1)\varphi + k z \varphi'] z^s$, and the top coefficient
$(mD + kg + 1)\varphi_g$ of the class ODE kills every polynomial candidate. Hence $E$ is
never the leading form of a Keller component, and $P = E + R$ (any strictly lower-weight
tail) is never a Keller component.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $z$ primitive (otherwise write $z=z_0^e$ with $z_0$ primitive and apply the argument to $\varphi(z_0^e)$). Grade by $(m,-k)$, so that $E$ is homogeneous and the class of $y$ is spanned by $y\,z^s$. A direct computation
gives $J(E,y)=\varphi+kz\varphi'$ and $J(E,z)=mz\varphi/y$, hence $J(E,z^sy)=[(ms+1)\varphi+kz\varphi']z^s$,
the formula the record verifies in symbolic coefficients for all $k,m,g\le3$ [MV]. For a class component
$y\,f(z)$ of $Q$ the equation $J(E,yf)=1$ reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classode}
m\,z\,\varphi\,f'+(\varphi+k\,z\,\varphi')\,f=1 .
\end{equation}
If $f=\sum_{i\le D}f_iz^i$ with $f_D\ne0$, the coefficient of $z^{D+g}$ on the left is
$(mD+kg+1)\,\varphi_g\,f_D$; every factor is nonzero and $D+g\ge1$, while the right side has degree $0$, so
$f_D=0$, a contradiction; thus $f=0$ and the left side is $0\ne1$. The univariate kill is confirmed on $12$ configurations,
with certificates that are pure powers of the top coefficient, and full windows are empty for binomial and
trinomial edges including perfect-square and perfect-cube $\varphi$. The statement with a tail $R$ of strictly lower weight follows from the top-down argument in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:t2} with the
weights $(m,-k)$: $J(E,\cdot)$ raises weight by $k\ge1$ and $J(R,\cdot)$ by less than $k$; the ring of constants of $J(E,\cdot)$ is $\C[E]$ because $E$ is
not composite ($E(x,0)=\varphi(0)\,x$ has degree one); each step subtracts $c\,P^j$; and the weight-$0$ component is $J(E,Q_{-k})=1$, excluded by the
computation above. Instances: the kernels on the classes of weight $jm$ are exactly the powers $E^j$ (checked, EXP-032).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV/D]}; the vertex dichotomy (EXP-033)]\label{thm:t4}
If the monomial $x$ is a vertex of the Newton polygon $N(P)$ (convex hull of the support
with the origin) of a Keller component with linear part $x$, then $P = x + f(y)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $(i,j)$ be the other endpoint of the boundary edge of $N(P)$ that leaves the vertex $(1,0)$ away from the segment to the origin. The case $j=0$ is impossible,
since then $(i,0)$ with $i\ge2$ would make $x$ a non-vertex. If $i\ge2$, every lattice point of the edge is divisible by $x$, so the edge is an $x$-anchored edge
$x\varphi(z)$ with interior direction $(i-1,j)$, $i-1,j\ge1$, and it is the top edge for the weight $(j,-(i-1))$; Theorem~\ref{thm:t3} excludes $P$ [D, elementary polygon
combinatorics]. If $i=0$, the edge joins $(1,0)$ to a pure-$y$ vertex $(0,d)$, contains no further lattice points, and every other support point $(a,b)$ lies on the
origin side of that edge, which for $a\ge1$ forces $(a,b)=(1,0)$; hence the support is $\{x\}\cup\{y^b\}$ and $P=x+f(y)$. If $i=1$, the support lies in $\{i\le1\}$, so
$P=x\,\varphi(y)+f(y)$ with $\deg\varphi\ge1$. Grade by the $x$-degree. For $Q$ with top $x$-degree $s\ge1$ and top coefficient $q_s(y)$, the component of $J(P,Q)$
of $x$-degree $s$ is $x^s(\varphi q_s'-s\varphi'q_s)=x^s\varphi^{s+1}(q_s/\varphi^s)'$, because the part $f(y)$ of $P$ lowers the $x$-degree. If $J(P,Q)=1$ this component
vanishes, so $q_s=c\,\varphi^s$, and $Q-cP^s$ solves the same equation with smaller top $x$-degree, since $P^s=x^s\varphi^s+(\text{lower }x\text{-degree})$. At $s=0$ the equation
is $J(P,q(y))=\varphi\,q'=1$, which has no polynomial solution for $\deg\varphi\ge1$. So this case does not occur. Machine verification on all three sides: samples with vertex
$x$ and mixed support are all inconsistent (the certificate for $x(1+c_1y)$ pairs to $-c_1^6$, EXP-033); triangular samples are consistent (they are components);
$x$-swallowed samples such as $x+(x+y)^2$ and $x+(x+2y)^3$ are consistent, which is the escape route the next section studies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Annihilation (EXP-036)]\label{lem:annihilation}
For any polynomial $P$, $J(m, P^k) = -k\,L(P^{k-1} m)$ with $L = J(P, \cdot)$ (Leibniz). Any
covector annihilating the image of $L$ on a space containing the preimage therefore kills the
source; the certificate covectors used throughout are left-null on the completion matrix,
whose columns are exactly $L(m)$, so the tail steps of
Theorems~\ref{thm:t2}--\ref{thm:t4} are unconditional. The finite-window subtlety (spurious
pairings for out-of-window preimages) reproduces the computed values exactly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $J(f,f)=0$ one has $J(f,f^{k-1}g)=f^{k-1}J(f,g)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leibniz}
J(m,P^k)=kP^{k-1}J(m,P)=-kP^{k-1}J(P,m)=-kJ(P,P^{k-1}m)=-kL(P^{k-1}m).
\end{equation}
A left-null covector of the completion matrix
kills every $L(m')$ with $m'$ in the ansatz, hence every source whose preimage $P^{k-1}m$ lies in it. The pairing of a
source is zero exactly when $\deg(P^{k-1}m)\le N$; the nonzero pairings of a first run with fixed windows ($15$, $30a$, $30$, $45$
at window $6$, and $945a^2$ at window $10$) are reproduced entry by entry by out-of-window preimages. The operator in play is
$L=J(P,\cdot)$ of the \emph{full} $P$, not of the top form: a first derivation that used $L_{\mathrm{top}}$ was refuted by the machine.
\end{proof}

The dichotomy's other side is real: $x + (x+y)^2$ and $x + (x+2y)^3$ are components in which
$x$ is \emph{swallowed} inside the polygon (equivalently: a pure power $x^d$, $d \ge 2$,
appears in the support). Every non-triangular component is of that kind. The residual open
core is therefore precise: components whose polygon swallows $x$ with a mixed staircase.
\subsection{The corner is diagonal; the staircase transport}\label{sec:staircase}

\begin{proposition}[{[MV]}; the diagonal corner (EXP-035)]
For $B = x^i y^j$, $i, j \ge 1$:
$J(B^p, x^a y^b) = p (ib - ja)\, x^{ip + a - 1} y^{jp + b - 1}$, with kernel exactly the
powers of $B$ when $B$ is primitive; no monomial gives $J(B^p, \cdot)$ a constant term. The mixed corner is clean;
the obstruction lives on the staircase of Newton vertices joining the swallowed linear
vertex to the corner.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $B=x^iy^j$, $J(B^p,x^ay^b)=pB^{p-1}J(B,x^ay^b)=p\,x^{i(p-1)}y^{j(p-1)}\bigl(ib-ja\bigr)x^{i+a-1}y^{j+b-1}$, which is the stated monomial. It is zero exactly when $(a,b)$ is parallel to $(i,j)$. The output exponents $ip+a-1$ and $jp+b-1$ are never both $0$ when $i,j\ge1$ and $p\ge1$, so no constant term arises.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV]}; block triangularity and the transport chain (EXP-037)]
\label{thm:transport}
Fix the $x$-edge grading $w = (v, 1-u)$ and let $s_0$ be the minimal $w$-class of $P$. In
the completion window (unknown $Q$ of bounded degree), every nonzero matrix entry sits at a
class offset $s - \sigma$ for a $P$-class $s$ ($\sigma = v + 1 - u$), and the offset
$s_0 - \sigma$ is minimal: ordering $Q$-classes by $w$-class, the system is block
triangular. Classwise elimination (solve the diagonal block, inject its kernel parameters,
emit its cokernel obstructions) is therefore well-defined; it reproduces the outcome of the
monolithic consistency test on every tested sample, including a consistent positive control. On the
pure above-weight staircase family the diagonal blocks are the banded operators of
Theorem~\ref{thm:t1}, and on all $8$ grid samples the first inconsistent transport equation
sits at the \emph{constant's} class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV]}; the fifth exclusion, window form (EXP-042)]\label{thm:t5}
Let $P = x + a\,x^u y^v + b\,x^d$ ($u \ge 2$, $v, d \ge 1$; the minimal shape swallowing
$x$). For every grid point $(u, v, d) \in \{2,3\} \times \{1,2\} \times \{2,3\}$ and window
$N = 7$, a cleared certificate covector with polynomial entries satisfies $c^T M = 0$
identically and pairs with the right-hand side to a \emph{monomial}:
\[
-13860\,a^7,\quad -32\,a^4,\quad -630\,a^4,\quad -2730\,a^7,\quad -1155\,a^3,\quad
-165\,a^4,\quad -4620\,a^7 b,\quad -81\,b^4,
\]
so the completion window is empty for \emph{all} parameter values with $a \ne 0$ (the
$b$-factors vanish only where Theorem~\ref{thm:t1} applies anyway). At $(u, v, d) =
(2, 1, 2)$ the pairing at window $N$ is $-c_N\,a^N$ with $c_N \in \{420, 1260, 13860,
180180, \dots\}$ for $N = 5..9$: a nonzero monomial in $a$ alone at every certified window.
The annihilation mechanism of Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} transfers verbatim (it is
$P$-agnostic), and in-window sources pair to zero exactly as the window criterion demands.
A three-parameter tail certificate ($-27720\,a^7 c_3^4$) extends the family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{lemma}[{[MV/D]}; the tower lemma (EXP-044/046)]\label{lem:tower}
Let $P = x + R$ ($R$ without constant or linear part), $M_N$ the completion-window
matrix at window $N \ge \deg P - 1$, and $T$ the total-degree top form of $P$. Suppose that
$T$ is not a proper power, so that the kernel of $J(T,\cdot)$ on forms of degree $M$ is zero unless
$\deg T \mid M$ and is then the line spanned by $T^{M/\deg T}$ (this holds in particular when $T$ is a primitive
monomial, or when $T=P-x$ is not a proper power). Then any left-null certificate of $M_N$ with
nonzero pairing extends to $M_{N+1}$ with the same (scaled) pairing: window
inconsistency propagates to every larger window.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Four steps, each machine-verified at the instances named, with the derivations stated.

\emph{Bookkeeping.} For a monomial $m$ of degree $\le N$ the column $L(m)=J(P,m)$ has degree at most $N+\deg P-2$.
The rows that $M_{N+1}$ adds to $M_N$ have degree $N+\deg P-1$, so the old columns vanish on the new rows, and
any covector $c$ left-null on $M_N$, extended by arbitrary entries on the new rows, stays left-null on the old columns.

\emph{The top block.} For a form $g$ of degree $N+1$ the new-row part of $J(P,g)$ is $J(T,g)$, since the lower parts of
$P$ contribute in lower degree. The new block is therefore the action of $J(T,\cdot)$ on forms of degree $N+1$. By the
hypothesis its kernel is zero unless $\deg T\mid N+1$ and is then spanned by the resonance $\kappa=\lambda T^{k}$,
$k\deg T=N+1$ (the dependence of binary forms; at $(2,1,2)$ the single resonance is $(x^2y)^3$ at degree $9$).

\emph{The key identity.} Since $T=P-\text{low}$, where low is the sub-top part of $P$, the difference
$\kappa-\lambda P^{k}$ has degree at most $N$. As $L(P^k)=0$, one gets $L(\kappa)=L(\kappa-\lambda P^k)$, the
$L$-image of an in-window polynomial; it lies entirely in the old rows, and every left-null covector of $M_N$
annihilates it (checked exactly in the three regimes $d<u+v$, $d=u+v$ and the $B$-power cases, with a rank test that
places $L(\kappa)$ in the old column space).

\emph{The extension.} A new row vector $c'$ with $c'^TB=-c^TC$ ($B$ the top block, $C$ the old-row entries of the new
columns) exists iff the right side vanishes on $\ker B$; on the resonance this is the key identity, and elsewhere $B$ is
injective. The right-hand side of the Keller equation, the constant, sits in an old row, so the pairing does not change.
Across the active resonance step $8\to9$ at $(2,1,2)$ the window-$9$ certificate restricts to the window-$8$ one on all $52$
common rows with a single ratio $2$ and pairing ratio $2$ (EXP-046); the measured window law, with pairings $-c_Na^N$ and
ratios $2N-3$ up to the clearing content (a prime at $N=7,8,10,11$), is this tower seen through the clearing normalization (EXP-044).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The hypothesis excludes a pure power $T=b\,x^d$. The kernel of $J(x^d,\cdot)$ contains every $x^M$, not only the powers of
$T$; for $d\nmid M$ the form $x^M$ is not of the form $\lambda P^k+\text{lower}$, so the key identity does not apply.
No proof of the case $T=b\,x^d$ is recorded (the instances of EXP-046 have $d\le3\le u+v$), and none is claimed. The same
holds when $T=P-x$ is a proper power, for example $P=x+(x+y)^2$. The proper-power tops are the subject of the half-plane
tower below.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV/D]}; the fifth exclusion at all partner degrees (EXP-042/044/046)]
\label{thm:t5all}
Let $(u,v,d)$ be one of the six grid shapes $(2,1,2)$, $(2,1,3)$, $(3,1,2)$, $(3,1,3)$, $(3,2,2)$, $(3,2,3)$,
and $a\ne0$, $b$ arbitrary (at $(2,1,3)$ the pairing contains a factor $b$ that vanishes only where
Theorem~\ref{thm:t1} applies). Then $P = x + a\,x^u y^v + b\,x^d$ is not a Keller component at \emph{any} partner
degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Base: the cleared certificates of Theorem~\ref{thm:t5} at window $N=7$, with pairings
$-13860\,a^7$, $-4620\,a^7b$, $-630\,a^4$, $-2730\,a^7$, $-1155\,a^3$, $-165\,a^4$ at the six shapes in the order listed
(EXP-042, part A of the output). Induction: Lemma~\ref{lem:tower}, whose hypothesis holds because the top form is the primitive
monomial $a\,x^uy^v$ or, at $(u,v,d)=(2,1,3)$, equals $P-x=x^2(ay+bx)$, which is not a proper power for $a\ne0$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a statement on these six shapes only. The scope was fixed by the base: cleared certificates exist on the
eight grid points $\{2,3\}\times\{1,2\}\times\{2,3\}$, and no closed-form covector for general $(u,v,d)$ is recorded
(EXP-042 states the missing piece as the closed form of the transport chain). Outside the grid no base certificate is
available: Theorem~\ref{thm:t6} turns one certificate per shape into an all-degree exclusion but does not supply it. For the
two grid points with a proper-power top, $(u,v)=(2,2)$, the theorem makes no claim: the odd resonances do not reduce mod
$\C[P]$, and the certificates kill them in every tested instance (for example $(xy)^5$ at window $9$) without a derivation of
that kill. Whether the statement extends beyond the grid is a conjecture [C].

\begin{theorem}[{[MV/D]}; the universal tower (EXP-049)]\label{thm:t6}
Let $P = x + R$ ($R$ without constant or linear part) whose total-degree top form
$T = P_D$ is \emph{not} a proper power. If the completion window is inconsistent with a
certificate at any single $N \ge \deg P - 1$, then $P$ is not a Keller component at \emph{any}
partner degree. (The tower argument with $T$ the full top form: $\ker J(T, \cdot)$ on
degree-$M$ forms is zero unless $\deg T \mid M$ and then the span of $T^{M/\deg T}$;
and $T = P - \text{lower}$ by definition, so $T^k - P^k$ has degree $\le k \deg P - 1$:
every resonance reduces in-window modulo $\ker L$.) One cleared window solve, seconds
of computation, yields an all-degree exclusion per shape; the shapes excluded in this way
include multi-edge staircases such as $x + a x^2 y + b x^2 + c x^3 y^2$ (pairing
$-4620\,c^4$). For proper-power tops the measured mechanism is the \emph{half-plane}
certificate \mbox{(EXP-048)}: a pairing-nonzero certificate supported in the $y$-heavy half-plane,
disjoint from every $x$-heavy resonance source; its general construction is the remaining
structural gap, and it is exactly what the $B = 16$ frontier shapes (proper-power tops)
require. Certificates also exist at the open pair's degrees themselves:
sampled degree-72 shapes with the GGHV corner $(16, 56)$ have empty filtered windows at
$N = 108$ in about one second each (EXP-050).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[{[MV]}; half-plane certificates at the sampled windows (EXP-051)]\label{thm:halfplane}
Let $P = x + R$ have a total-degree top form that attains $\min\{p - q\}$ over the support
(the swallowed-staircase geometry), and let $H$ be the $y$-heavy half-plane of rows
$\{i \le j\}$. The $H$-restricted window systems carry certificates with nonzero pairing at the following samples and
windows: $(u,v,d)=(2,2,2)$ at $N=7,\dots,10$ (pairings $-32a^4$, $-64a^4$, $-64a^5$, $-512a^5$); the degree-$32$ sample
$x+R_0(1)^2+x^2$ of $B=16$ flavor at $N=14$ ($380$ $H$-rows, pairing $-256$); and the degree-$72$ sample
$x+2(x^2y^7)^8+x^2$ with the corner $(16,56)$ at $N=12$ ($135$ $H$-rows, pairing $-32$). Each certificate excludes the partners of the sample
of degree at most $N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{conjecture}[{[C]}; propagation of $H$-certificates]\label{conj:halfplane}
An $H$-certificate at one window $N\ge\deg P-1$ excludes $P$ at every partner degree, proper-power tops included.
\end{conjecture}

The recorded argument for this propagation (EXP-051) rests on three ingredients: the resonances that are powers of $T$ reduce by the universal
identity, the columns whose $T$-output leaves $H$ vanish, and the kernel resonances that are not powers of $T$ have $x$-heavy sources with
empty $H$-part. The last ingredient holds for $T=a(xy)^2$ (the sources are $x^sy^{s-1}$) but not for the two frontier samples: for the degree-$32$ sample, with top
$(xy^3)^8$, the resonances $(xy^3)^s$, $s=4,\dots,7$, lie in the kernel of $J(T,\cdot)$ and their sources $L(\kappa)$ lie entirely in $H$ [D]; for the degree-$72$
sample $J(x,(x^2y^7)^s)=7s\,x^{2s}y^{7s-1}$ also lies in $H$ [D]. The degree-$32$ and degree-$72$ samples are therefore excluded
only at the windows computed, and the propagation is open; the odd-resonance derivation for proper-power tops is open problem (5) of Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

Two instrument-level results complete the toolkit. The \emph{matched-pair law} (EXP-038):
for cornered pairs $P = \alpha B^p + P_{\mathrm{sub}}$, $Q = \beta B^q + Q_{\mathrm{sub}}$,
the second class of $J(P, Q)$ is exactly
$\alpha p (ib - ja) q_{a,b} - \beta q (id - jc) p_{c,d}$ over outputs matched by
$(a, b) = (c, d) + (q - p)(i, j)$; imposing the pair shape does not change the obstruction
depth on any tested sample, but it halves the window unknowns: an efficiency tool, not a
second obstruction source. And the \emph{$x^m$-anchored edge operator} (EXP-043): for
$E = x^m \varphi(z)$, $z = x^k y^l$,
\[
J\left(x^m \varphi(z),\; x^{\alpha} y^{\beta}\right)
= x^{m + \alpha - 1}\, y^{\beta - 1}\,
\bigl[\beta m\, \varphi(z) + (\beta k - \alpha l)\, z\varphi'(z)\bigr],
\]
verified identically in symbolic edge coefficients; Theorem~\ref{thm:t3}'s operator is the
case $m = 1$.

\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design}

Every statement marked [MV] comes from an archived computation that was specified before it was run. The specification
fixes the question, the falsifiable predictions, the method, the exact objects compared and the time limit; it is committed to
the repository before the run, so a prediction that fails is reported as a failed prediction. Several results below are
refutations of this kind: the injectivity of the top operator on every class (EXP-030), the localization of the first transport
obstruction at the vertex-matching classes (EXP-037, where the first failure sits at the constant's class on all $8$ grid
samples), the pairing form that was expected to be the same at every window (EXP-042, replaced by the law $-c_Na^N$), and the
bound of at most $24$ columns on the largest cyclic block of the quotient system (EXP-116, where the block has $51$).

\emph{Hypotheses.} The planar machine is tested against the claim that every consistent stratum it meets can be inverted
(EXP-020 to EXP-022). The exclusion theorems are tested against the claim that a nonzero monomial pairing exists at every grid shape
and window (EXP-042) and that it persists from one window to the next (EXP-044, EXP-046). The chart atlas is tested against
the claim that each residual left by one chart is finite after a section is chosen on it (EXP-101 to EXP-135). The Case 1
certificate is tested against the claim that the system reduces to the unit ideal over the number field (EXP-139 to EXP-143).

\emph{Baselines and controls.} Three baselines are run by the product itself. The monolithic consistency test of the window
matrix is the baseline of the classwise transport; the two agree on every tested sample (EXP-037). The descent inverter on a library of eight
composed Keller maps is the baseline of the machine and inverts all eight, with exact composition in both orders (EXP-022).
Direct $125\times125$ and $124\times124$ determinants at rational points are the baseline of every blockwise determinant of
Table~\ref{tab:atlas} (typically four or five controls per record). Positive controls (the quasi-triangular map $x+(x+y)^2$ stays
consistent with $5$ kernel parameters; $x(1+c_1y)$ pairs to $-c_1^6$), negative controls (every corruption of one certificate or
generator coefficient breaks its identity; an extra vertex changes the band equations, EXP-137) and planted certificates
(EXP-141: a planted system with a known certificate is recovered through the same pipeline, and the planted test caught a
Chinese-remainder representative error before production) are part of every run.

\emph{Statistics.} The results are exact; no random sampling decides a statement. Sampling enters in two places and is labelled
where it does: the sampled shapes at the open degree pair (EXP-050, EXP-051) and the sampled points on residual curves (EXP-125, EXP-132),
which select a chart and never certify it; the certificate is the exact computation that follows. Modular statements carry their
caveat in the text: an infeasible system modulo a prime $p$ excludes over $\Q$ every solution whose denominators are prime to $p$, and
it excludes all rational solutions when $p$ is good for the coefficient matrix (equal rank over $\mathbb F_p$ and over $\Q$); a statement over $\Q$
from modular vanishing needs a height argument, which is what Algorithm~\ref{alg:crt} supplies.

\emph{Time limits and outcomes.} An exact step that does not finish inside its time limit is reported as unknown, never as a
result: the third chart of EXP-102 stopped at $304$ s, an exact standard basis over $\Q$ with the field polynomial adjoined stopped at $374$ minutes
(EXP-139), the quintic-field run stopped after $23{,}611$ s (EXP-140) and the certificate runs stopped at their prime budgets
(Table~\ref{tab:cost}). A selection rule is also fixed before a run: the atlas selects bases deterministically by modular pivoting
and reports when a deterministic choice fails (for example, no single sampled basis covers all three blocks of $F_7$, EXP-129).

\emph{Scope of every exclusion.} A window certificate is a statement about one shape. Table~\ref{tab:exclusions} lists, for
each exclusion result, the scope the computation verified, the window, the pairing or certificate and the record, so that a
theorem and its verified scope are read together.

\subsection{The frontier, recalibrated from primary sources}\label{sec:frontier}

The statements about the literature in this section were checked against the cited
sources as of 2026-07-22.

\emph{Verified gcd coverage.} For a counterexample pair, $B := \gcd(\deg P, \deg Q)$
satisfies: $B \ge 9$ is forced by the classical interval results ($B = 1$: Magnus
\cite{Magnus1955}; $B \le 2$: Nakai--Baba; $B \le 8$ or prime: Appelgate--Onishi
\cite{AppelgateOnishi1985} and Nagata \cite{Nagata1989}); $B \ne p$ \cite{Abhyankar2008};
$B \ge 16$ (Heitmann \cite{Heitmann1990}); $B \ne 2p$ and $B = 16$ or $B > 20$
\cite{GGV2017}. Zoladek's claimed $B > 33$ has a documented gap \cite{GGV2017} and is not
used. Consequently our machine certificates at $\gcd = 2, 9, 12, 18$ (EXP-024..028) are
\emph{replications} of literature-covered territory: independent verifications, not new
frontier results.

\emph{Verified degree floor.} Moh's classical analysis discards $\max\deg \le 100$
\cite{Moh1983}, with complete published detail only for the smallest case ($64$); the
four exceptional cases correspond to six configurations in the modern corner terminology,
which the algorithmic re-examination by Guccione--Guccione--Horruitiner--Valqui enumerates
and discards \cite{GGHV2017, GGHV2022}. The current floor is: any counterexample has
$\max\deg \ge 125$, or $(\deg P, \deg Q) \in \{(72, 108), (108, 72)\}$ \cite{GGHV2022};
that single pair was left open explicitly for compute reasons.

\emph{The open targets.} (i) The $B = 16$ case comes with a forced direction set and
leading forms $l_{4,-1}(P) = \lambda_P R_0^m$, $R_0 = x (x y^4 - \lambda_0)^3$, and a
reduced normal form \cite{GGV2017}. We verify [MV] that $R_0$ is $(4,-1)$-homogeneous with
collinear $x$-anchored support, and, by the $x^m$-anchored operator above, that for
$m \ge 2$ \emph{no monomial reaches the Keller constant from the $R_0^m$ edge}: the
$B = 16$ problem is a staircase transport problem, the exact object of
Section~\ref{sec:staircase}. The pure $m = 1$ shape has $x$ as a Newton vertex and is
excluded outright by Theorem~\ref{thm:t4} (window replication empty); swallowed variants
land in Theorem~\ref{thm:t5}'s territory (sampled windows empty). (ii) The similarity filter
(partner supported in the scaled polygon $\tfrac{\deg Q}{\deg P} N^0(P)$; sound by the
similarity theorem since the scaled polygons are nested in the degree) cuts the
$(48, 64)$ full-window system from $2142$ to $111$ unknowns: three degree-48 samples of the
$F_1$ flavor (the pure shape $x + R_0(1)^3$ and two swallowed variants) have \emph{empty}
filtered windows at $N = 64$ in $2.4$ to $3.6$ seconds each, excluding all partners of
degree $\le 64$ per sample (the classical case-64 degrees: a sampled replication of Moh and
Heitmann that also measures the computational cost at this scale). (iii) For the $(72, 108)$ system the histograms give $5992$ unknowns
in $535$ classes with largest diagonal block $22$ before filtering: sampled shapes at the open degrees cost seconds to minutes per certificate with the filter and
the transport (EXP-050); the open territory is the twenty-three configurations of maximal degree in $[125,150]$ below, the larger
degrees, and $(72, 108)$ itself, whose cost for the whole family is measured in Section~\ref{sec:helali}.

\subsection{The reduction of Proposition 4.3: Cases 1 and 2 and the three branches}\label{sec:prop43}

\subsection{The truncation ladder for the simultaneous certificate}\label{sec:ladder}

The certified statement above reduces raising the floor to one object: a covector
$\Lambda(\varepsilon)$, polynomial in the interior coefficients $\varepsilon$,
that closes the reduced system for \emph{all} interior values at once. The
computations below decide this object degree by degree; every step is
exact, and every modular reduction of a rational uses $\mathrm{num}\cdot
\mathrm{den}^{-1} \bmod p$.

\emph{Solvability is automatic (the dual annihilation identity, EXP-068).} The
reduced system $M_0$ has corank one; its cokernel covector $\Lambda_0$ satisfies
$\Lambda_0 M_0 = 0$ and, more, it annihilates every Jacobian-bracket image that
arises in the corrector recursion. Hence the consistency (solvability) conditions
of the ladder hold at every order, and the only obstruction to a finite covector
is the top-order \emph{vanishing} condition. This is the dual form of the
annihilation lemma $J(m, P^k) = -k\,L(P^{k-1} m)$ of Section~\ref{sec:staircase}.

\emph{The tower, decided through degree three.} A polynomial covector of
$\varepsilon$-degree $d$ exists iff a finite linear system over the interior
coefficients is feasible. We find [MV]: \textbf{no covector of degree $1$}
(EXP-067; eight ``diagonal'' operators obstruct, the full system infeasible modulo
the prime $2147483629$; over $\mathbb{Q}$ this excludes every covector whose denominators are prime
to it, and no good-prime argument is given for the rest); and \textbf{no covector of
degree $2$} (EXP-072; the support $\{(0,1),(1,0),(3,5)\}$ obstructs, again
conclusive modulo two primes). At degree $3$ no obstruction is found through the
pair and all triple supports (EXP-071, EXP-073), but the quadruple support
$\{(0,3),(1,0),(3,4),(4,7)\}$ makes the degree-three necessary subsystem infeasible modulo both
primes, the conclusive direction of a support test with the same modular caveat: \textbf{no
covector of degree $3$} (EXP-075, [MV] modulo two primes). The
obstruction \emph{persists} rather than migrating: the degree-two obstructing
support $\{(0,1),(1,0),(3,5)\}$ contains two of the eight operators that already
obstruct at degree one, and what grows is the support size needed to expose it
(single operators at degree one; at degree two every pair support is feasible,
EXP-071, and a triple obstructs).

\emph{One structural reduction, and no degree bound.} The rational-covector
relaxation adds nothing (EXP-074): since $\Lambda_0 M_0 = 0$ the certificate
condition is homogeneous, so any rational covector clears its denominator to a
polynomial one. The dimension of the Krylov closure of $\Lambda_0$ is \emph{not}
known to bound the degree of a covector (EXP-078): the derivation of such a finite
ceiling presupposes that the pinned corrector ladder terminates, and the ladder does
\emph{not} terminate (EXP-064: the descending chain stabilizes nonzero at dimension
$39$). Since the pinning carries a large gauge freedom, neither
the ceiling nor its negation follows; \emph{no} a priori degree bound is known,
effective or otherwise, the chain of solvable degrees is controlled only by
Noetherianity, which is not effective, and the degree-by-degree search is not known
to be a finite decision procedure.

\emph{The natural $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ premise is refuted (EXP-080).} A route through a Lie-algebra grading
would need one grading of the forced polynomial $P_T=y^8(xy-1)^8+x$ that splits the $51$ lower operators into single-degree
raising and lowering parts. No nonzero monomial weight makes $P_T$ homogeneous: its support points $(0,8),(1,9),(1,0)$ give
$a+b=0$ and $a-8b=0$ with coefficient determinant $-9$, so only $(a,b)=(0,0)$ works [MV]. The phrase ``the $(v,1-u)$ weight'' does not select one grading on the
nine-monomial edge $(k,8+k)$, $0\le k\le 8$, which gives nine distinct candidates; each of the nine vertex-derived gradings mixes several positive or
several negative shift classes, and even the canonical edge-normal grading $(1,-1)$ gives raw shifts $-7,\dots,1$ while $P_T$ itself has degrees $-8$ and $1$ [MV]. This refutes the
natural construction and its premise; it is not a no-go statement for every certificate construction. The proposed full cubic solve (EXP-093) has an empty target, because degree three is excluded by EXP-075, and is cancelled.

What does hold is weaker but exact. The set of degrees at which a covector exists
is \emph{upward closed} (a degree-$d$ covector is a degree-$(d+1)$ covector with
vanishing top coefficients), so either none exists at any degree, or there is a
minimal $d_0$; with degrees $1$, $2$ and $3$ closed, $d_0 \ge 4$. And the
support-restricted sweeps are \emph{one-sided}: they test necessary conditions, so
an infeasible support decides a degree negatively, while all supports passing does
\emph{not} establish existence. Settling a degree $d \ge 4$ positively therefore
requires constructing a covector, not sweeping. Both outcomes remain open: a
covector would be an exclusion proof, and a proof that none exists at any degree
would be the first step of a counterexample.

\emph{A lead toward a degree-uniform statement.} Written as a connection, the
annihilation lemma makes the covector obstruction a first cohomology class, and
termination of the corrector ladder becomes the regularity type of that connection
(regular singular: the ladder terminates and a structural degree bound follows;
irregular: no such bound, matching the measured non-termination of the pinned
ladder). Whether this decides $(72,108)$ in one stroke is open; we state it as a
question, not a result.

In summary, the ladder reads as follows. Degrees $1$, $2$ and $3$ are closed (EXP-067, EXP-072, EXP-075), so $d_0\ge4$ if a covector exists. The pinned corrector ladder does not terminate
(EXP-064, dimension $39$), the finite ceiling derived from its termination is not established (EXP-078), and the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ premise is refuted (EXP-080). Degree $4$ and above are open, and
settling any of them positively requires constructing a covector.

stabilizes at a nonzero space and the ladder does not terminate.

\subsection{Necessary conditions from the original pair (EXP-095/096)}\label{sec:necessary}

The reduced Proposition~4.3 system is a Laurent pair with bracket $x^2$, so
theorems requiring a polynomial Keller pair with bracket one cannot be applied
to it directly. Returning instead to the hypothetical original pair gives an
exact applicability bridge [MV]. Its degree-$72$ component has
\[
  2A_0=(16,56),\qquad 2A'_0=(2,0),\qquad
  2A_1=(11/2,14),
\]
which is precisely the first retained $D=72$ branch in the Newton-resolution
list of Makar-Limanov--Trakhtenberg \cite{MakarLimanovTrakhtenberg2024}.
Thus that independent classification retains the GGHV branch; it does not
exclude it.

Two sharper necessary conditions do result. First, instantiating
Lee--Li's inner-polynomial region \cite{LeeLi2024} with
\[
 (a,b,m,n)=(2,3,16,56),\qquad \mathfrak m=\frac{139}{39},
\]
gives [MV] exactly seven possible northeastern vertices whenever the inner or
innermost polynomial is nonzero:
\[
 (1,3),(2,7),(3,10),(4,14),(5,17),(6,21),(7,24).
\]
The zero-inner-polynomial alternative remains. Nor does the stronger diagonal
corollary apply, since
\[
 \frac ba=\frac32\not>\frac{n-m}{a}-1=19.
\]

Second, the approximate-root formulas of
Guccione--Guccione--Horruitiner--Valqui \cite{GGHVApproxRoots} become an exact
intersection invariant [MV]. For the complete chain
\[
 (m,n)=(3,2),\qquad A_1=(11/4,7),\qquad k=1,\qquad l=4,
\]
the forced fourth-power edge supplies four final major classes. Each has
$|D_\tau^P|=3\cdot7=21$ and $\lambda_\tau^Q=1/4$, hence
\[
 I(P,Q)=\deg_x\operatorname{Res}_y(P,Q)
       =4\cdot21\cdot\frac14=21.
\]
The degree-$108$ component therefore has $84$ major and $24$ minor roots.
These are necessary conditions on the original pair, independent of sampling the
$51$ interior coefficients of the reduced system. They do not prove that any of
the seven vertices is realizable, transport either invariant through the
Laurent normalization, close $(72,108)$, or raise the degree floor. In
particular, the approximate-root paper proves only an inequality for the
minor-root expression where equality would be needed for the proposed family
exclusion; that route is not used here.

\emph{Transport to the reduced system (EXP-097).} These original-pair invariants
cannot be imposed directly on the $51$ final Laurent coefficients without
additional boundary data. If $R(x)=\operatorname{Res}_y(F,G)$ and
\(\phi_c(x,y)=(x^{-1},x^cy)\), then [D; exact symbolic controls]
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_y(\phi_cF,\phi_cG)=x^{cpq}R(x^{-1}),
\]
where $p=\deg_yF$ and $q=\deg_yG$. For the final polygons
$(p,q,c)=(16,24,4)$, so $cpq=1536$: an input exponent interval
$[\nu,21]$ becomes $[1515,1536-\nu]$, of width $21-\nu$.
The reduction first swaps the selected coordinate eliminant and then
localizes $x$, making $x^\nu$ a unit; Proposition~4.3 does not retain the
missing $x=0$ divisor order $\nu$. Thus absolute degree $21$ remains a
necessary condition for reconstructing an original polynomial pair, not a direct
equation on the reduced coefficients. Laurent exponent width is a conditional
replacement only after proving $\nu=0$ and swap compatibility. A complete account
of the boundary divisors through these steps could supply that missing theorem;
none is claimed here.

\subsubsection{Constructible certificate strata and the first exact chart transition}

Finite chart covers by localized certificates need a commutative-algebra
qualification \mbox{(EXP-098)}. Suppose principal opens \(D(s_i)\)
cover the parameter space and each carries a localized lift \(c_i\) with
\[
 c_i^TM=0,\qquad c_i^Tb=1.
\]
Clearing denominators gives global syzygies pairing to powers \(s_i^{N_i}\).
Those powers generate the unit ideal, so a polynomial combination is one
global covector pairing to \(1\). A pure principal-open cover of lifted
syzygies is therefore not stronger than the global-covector target.

The strict generalization is a \emph{constructible rank stratification}: after
certifying a generic open, quotient by the residual closed-locus ideal and
recompute the kernel. New syzygies can appear after this non-flat
specialization and need not lift. EXP-098 verifies the distinction on an exact
universally inconsistent control whose global pairing ideal is the proper
ideal \((x)\), while a new certificate appears on \(V(x)\).

This mechanism applies to the actual GGHV matrix (EXP-099--101). Common-flag
shortcuts fail, but they isolate the first nontrivial interior interaction:
\[
 s=\varepsilon_{(0,1)},\qquad t=\varepsilon_{(1,7)}.
\]
For the selected \(125\times125\) augmented minor, a Sylvester reduction gives
the exact factorization [MV]
\[
 \frac{\det A(s,t)}{\det A_0}
 =
 \frac{(st-8)^6\bigl(2^{15}s^9-(st-8)^7\bigr)}{2^{39}}.
\]
The mixed coefficient also follows independently from
\[
 \operatorname{tr}B_s=\operatorname{tr}B_t=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(B_sB_t)=\frac{13}{8}.
\]
At \((s,t)=(-8,-1)\), the first minor vanishes, but exact ranks remain
\[
 \operatorname{rank}M=124,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}[M\mid b]=125.
\]
Pivot extraction gives an alternative right-hand-side-containing minor
nonzero there. The first two minors cover the component \(st=8\) and leave
only
\[
 2^{15}s^9=(st-8)^7,
\]
with normalization
\[
 s=8u^7,\qquad t=\frac{8u^9+1}{u^7},\qquad u\ne0.
\]
At \(u=1\), both previous minors vanish but a third exact augmented minor is
nonzero \mbox{(EXP-102)}. Its dense pullback has combined direction rank \(121\) and
exceeded a five-minute time limit. \mbox{EXP-103} instead evaluates at roots of unity
and interpolates the complete maximal-minor polynomials by NTT. An endpoint
check detects an 81-degree cancellation that a modular gcd alone would miss.

EXP-104 settles this cancellation over characteristic zero [MV]. The third determinant
is evaluated exactly at 100 integer values. Its assignment bounds are
\([1547,1646]\); after division by \(u^{1547}\), exact degree-99 interpolation
shows that coefficients \(0,\ldots,80\) vanish and coefficient \(81\) does
not. Thus its exact support is \([1628,1646]\). A second row chart has exact
support \([777,903]\).

The support graphs of both charts admit connected row/column weights modulo
9 \mbox{(EXP-105)}. With \(z=u^9\), removal of the proved Laurent monomials and
integer contents gives the primitive determinants
\[
 F(z)=21-96z-1024z^2,\qquad G(z)=(8z+1)^{14}.
\]
The only zero of \(G\) is \(z=-1/8\), where \(F(-1/8)=17\). More strongly,
EXP-105 exhibits integer polynomials \(A,B\) satisfying
\[
 A(z)F(z)+B(z)G(z)=17^{14}.
\]
Therefore the two normalized maximal minors generate the unit ideal over
\(\mathbb{Q}[u,u^{-1}]\). Together with the first two charts, this proves
rank \(125\) for the augmented matrix at every point of the
\(\{(0,1),(1,7)\}\) coefficient slice [MV].

The grading is not confined to that slice. For every nonconstant lower
monomial \(x^py^q\) tested in the 26-parameter family, EXP-106
finds the exact variable-weight law
\[
 w_{p,q}\equiv q-p+1\pmod9.
\]
All 23 remaining nonconstant directions preserve both connected chart
gradings; the constant direction is bracket-zero. This supplies a coordinate
system that scales to further directions, not a full-family proof. The next
direction, \((0,7)\), has selected ranks 53 and 41; after setting
\(z=u^9\) and \(y=v/u\), both determinant charts have \(z\)-width 14.

EXP-107 reconstructs both bivariate minors on independent 16-by-64 NTT grids
and verifies them by off-grid determinant evaluations [MV]. The endpoint-safe
minor is independent of \(y\), so it remains \(G(z)=(8z+1)^{14}\). The other
minor restricts at \(z=-1/8\) to a squarefree degree-12 polynomial \(Q(y)\).
Thus the first two charts leave only twelve geometric points in that fiber,
not a curve.

EXP-108 selects a third 125-row chart at the residual fiber [MV]. Removing
the connected mod-9 row/column weights turns both fiber matrices into exact
rational matrices. Interpolation under structural degree bounds 14 and 13,
using 29 exact determinants and two independent controls, gives primitive
polynomials \(Q,H\in\mathbb Z[y]\). The polynomial \(Q\) is irreducible over
\(\mathbb Q\), while, up to sign,
\[
\begin{split}
H(y)={}&y(49y^2+168y+192)(2401y^4+1568y^2+1024)\\
&\quad\cdot(117649y^6-403368y^5+921984y^4
-1053696y^3+786432).
\end{split}
\]
Their exact gcd is one. The explicit integer B\'ezout identity has nonzero
constant
\[
3036277895244878564727703434378848855121939007419328699039744.
\]
Since every common zero with \(G\) must lie over \(z=-1/8\), the three minors
prove rank 125 throughout the
\(\{(0,1),(1,7),(0,7)\}\) coefficient slice.

These results establish the first complete constructible chart cover on an
actual three-coefficient GGHV slice and expose a global lower-family grading.
They do not cover simultaneous variation of the other coefficients, exclude
the full \((72,108)\) family, or raise the degree floor.

\begin{proposition}[Ambient transverse inertness of the EXP-124 section]
\label{prop:exp135-inert}
Let
\[
 H(A,B,C)=I+(A-1)K_{(0,1)}+B K_{(0,5)}+C K_{(2,9)}
\]
be the normalized 33-by-33 cyclic core of the EXP-124 section, and
write \(K_T=K_{(2,8)}\). Then
\[
 \det\!\bigl(H(A,B,C)+T K_T\bigr)=\det H(A,B,C)
 \quad\text{in }\Q[A,B,C,T].
\]
The remaining 86 singleton blocks are also \(T\)-independent, so the complete
selected normalized maximal minor is unchanged by \((2,8)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact row reduction gives
\(\operatorname{rank}K_T=7\),
\(\operatorname{rank}K_T^2=3\), and \(K_T^3=0\). Fix a rank factorization
\(K_T=UV^{\mathsf T}\). The ranks of the four coefficient matrices in
\((A,B,C,T)\) are respectively \((25,24,6,7)\), so these are separate degree
bounds for the determinant difference. At each point of the tensor grid
\[
 A\in\{1,\ldots,26\},\qquad B\in\{0,\ldots,24\},\qquad
 C\in\{0,\ldots,6\},
\]
and at each of 30 distinct primes, exact modular elimination finds \(H\)
invertible and verifies
\[
 \bigl(V^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}U\bigr)^2=0.
\]
The matrix determinant lemma therefore makes
\(\det(I_7+T V^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}U)=1\) as a polynomial in \(T\) at every
grid point. Tensor interpolation proves that every coefficient of the
cleared determinant difference is zero modulo every listed prime.

For the characteristic-zero lift, the common entry denominator is
\(D=5{,}241{,}600\). The product of the rowwise coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norms
of \(D(H+T K_T)\) bounds the determinant coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm.
Including the subtraction at \(T=0\) gives an explicit 885-bit absolute
coefficient bound. The 30-prime product has 897 bits and exceeds twice that
bound. A nonzero integer coefficient cannot therefore vanish modulo their
product. Every coefficient is zero, proving the identity over \(\Q\) [MV/D].
The exact singleton calculation is EXP-134.
\end{proof}

The proof changes the computational viewpoint. It avoids both a dense
33-by-33 symbolic determinant and graph-function-field inversion: rank gives
the interpolation degrees, low-rank determinant and Woodbury lemmas control
each finite evaluation, and a height bound turns modular vanishing into an
exact rational identity. The same route applies to the EXP-129 section, which is
observed to be inert; sections that are affine or quadratic in \(T\) require their
actual \(T\)-coefficients, since inertness is not inherited.

map: it is the coefficient map of the product of a linear and a quadratic binary
form, restricted to a normalized slice. Concretely, on
$V_1\times V_2\to V_3\times\mathbb{A}^1$, $(L,Q)\mapsto(LQ,\operatorname{Res}(L,Q))$,
the slice $X_H=\{(L,Q):\operatorname{Res}(L,Q)=1,\ [LQ]_{\text{next}}=1\}$ carries the
projection $(L,Q)\mapsto LQ$, which is \'etale on the coprime (nonzero-resultant) locus
by a one-line argument: multiplication forgets exactly the relative scaling
$(L,Q)\mapsto(\lambda L,\lambda^{-1}Q)$, and the resultant, of weight $\deg Q-\deg L$
under that scaling, detects it. The projection is generically $n$-to-one (the choices
of linear factor), hence not injective. EXP-086 verifies [MV] that the three-dimensional construction
reproduces exactly (the Jacobian determinant of the coordinate change is $-1$, the map factors through the coefficient map of
$(a+bt)(c+dt+et^2)$ and the slice constraints equal $1$): the
displayed counterexample equals this coefficient map up to linear normalization, with
$X_H\cong\mathbb{A}^3$. So, for this family, the entire Jacobian question reduces to a
single \emph{recognition of affine space}: is $X_H\cong\mathbb{A}^n$?

In dimension three the recognition holds by a toric coincidence: the residual
$\mathbb{G}_m$-grading has weights $(1,-1,-2)$, and the associated semigroup generators
form a unimodular lattice basis (determinant $1$), so $X_3\cong\mathbb{A}^3$ (EXP-088) [MV]. In the plane it \emph{fails},
and we can say why in one invariant (EXP-090) [MV]. The controlling quantity is
$n-2=\deg Q-\deg L$, the resultant weight under the relative scaling; it is
simultaneously the degree of the Danielewski core
$\{x^{\,n-2}W=B^{\,n-2}-1\}$ and the number of boundary components (EXP-088) [MV]. For $n=2$ it is
$0$: the resultant becomes scaling-invariant, unable to rigidify the normalization,
and the slice degenerates. Two exact statements make this precise (EXP-089) [MV]:
with both forms linear, the two normalizations force $x\delta=1$ and $\beta\gamma=0$, so the source is reducible (two components),
hence not $\mathbb{A}^2$; and in the finite-root chart the source is $\mathbb{A}^2$ minus the discriminant hyperbola, whose
equation is a nonconstant unit, while $\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^2)^*=\C^*$. For $n=3$ the weight is $1$, the unique value that is both
nonzero and linear, giving $X_n\cong\mathbb{A}^n$; for $n\ge4$ it is $\ge2$, the core has $n-2$ boundary components and the
class group is obstructed (rank about $n-3$ in the record, derived [D] from the component count). Thus the exact structure of the known
counterexample cannot occur in the plane [D], a conclusion consistent with the
$\mathbb{G}_m$-equivariant rigidity theorem of the companion paper \cite{SLfoundational},
which forbids the torus-graded planar
analog independently. This excludes the incidence/root-selection class, the structure
of the known counterexample family; it is strong structural
evidence for $JC(2)$, not a proof, since a planar counterexample of some entirely
different structure is not addressed. A naive lowering to two dimensions (a linear form times a linear form) is also degenerate
for the same reason: it forces $ad=1$, $bc=0$, a singular coordinate hyperplane (EXP-086).

\section{Ablation: what each ingredient contributes}\label{sec:ablation}

The methods of Sections~\ref{sec:methods} to~\ref{sec:results} are built from separable ingredients, and the records measure what each
one buys when the others are kept.

\emph{The similarity filter.} Restricting the partner to the scaled polygon $\tfrac{\deg Q}{\deg P}N^0(P)$ cuts the $(48,64)$ full-window
system from $2142$ to $111$ unknowns, and the three degree-$48$ samples then close in $2.4$ to $3.6$ seconds each (EXP-045, EXP-047). The unfiltered count is in EXP-043. At the open
pair the unfiltered histograms give $5992$ unknowns in $535$ classes with largest diagonal block $22$, while the filtered windows at $N=108$ have $143$
unknowns and about $320$ equations and close in $1.2$ to $1.4$ seconds (EXP-050).

\emph{The pair shape.} Imposing the matched-pair shape on $Q$ does not change the obstruction depth: the first inconsistent window is the same ($N=3$) on all
four cornered samples with $Q$ free or constrained. It only halves the unknowns, from $7$ to $3$--$4$ (EXP-038). It is an efficiency tool, not a second source of
obstruction.

\emph{Classwise transport against the monolithic test.} The two give the same outcome on every tested sample. The ingredient that makes the sweep triangular is the choice of
the minimal $P$-class as the diagonal block: genuine components carry classes below the $x$-class, and with that choice the sweep is triangular for every $P$ with linear part $x$ (EXP-037).

\emph{The tower against window growth.} A window certificate settles one window. The tower lemma turns it into an all-degree statement, but only when the top form is not a
proper power. For a proper-power top, the left-null space at window $9$ of $(2,2,2)$ has dimension $20$, two certificates have nonzero pairing, and only one kills the odd resonance; the
certificate supported in the half-plane does, which is why half-plane certificates are reported separately (EXP-048, EXP-051).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:positioning}

A search of the literature (the sources of Section~\ref{sec:frontier}) found no prior
statement of Theorems~\ref{thm:t1} or \ref{thm:t2}. For the equivariant rigidity theorem of
the companion paper \cite{SLfoundational}, Shaska \cite{Shaska2026}, in work submitted on
2026-07-22, treats the same question by the signature of the weights; the body of that paper was not read for the zero-weight case [U].
Same-sign actions may give nonlinear triangular automorphisms, one zero weight gives affine
maps, and opposite-sign actions give a linear map. No priority or novelty claim is made for that classification.
Theorem~\ref{thm:t3} was not found as stated, but its
operator identity is within the standard bracket calculus \cite{Joseph1975, GGV2017}: the
statement appears new, the method is classical. Theorem~\ref{thm:t4}'s counterexample half
is essentially contained in \cite{GGV2017} (Prop.~4.1) with van den Essen's subrectangular
normalization \cite{vdEssen2000}; we claim only the sharp dichotomy form. All proofs are
elementary once seen, and some of these statements may be folklore; the primary sources
consulted, including the items that could not be verified, are listed in the repository.

The exclusions and the frontier results answer different questions. The exclusions describe which
single shapes cannot be Keller components at any partner degree; they close the shapes with a top edge anchored at the linear vertex and,
through the tower, six staircase shapes, and they identify the open core, the staircase that swallows $x$ with a proper-power top. They are certificates about
shapes, not about degrees, and the shapes of the open degree pair carry free coefficients that no single certificate covers. This is why the paper moves, in
Section~\ref{sec:results}, from shapes to the system of Proposition~4.3, where the question is whether a polynomial system in $51$ interior coefficients has a common solution.

On that system the three outputs are of different strength. The chart atlas is exact and partial: a four-coefficient restriction is closed with explicit identities, and each
closure is a statement a reader can re-derive from a determinant and a Bezout identity. The independent equations and checks are total and modular for Case 1: the five-prime unit ideal is
evidence over the whole system, not a proof. The attempt at an exact certificate fixes a cost: the certificate that Case 1 is empty has coordinates of more than about $94{,}000$ digits,
which excludes a direct approach and points to a different formulation. The exclusion of $(72,108)$ has therefore an independent proof of Case 2 and independent evidence for Case 1,
and priority belongs to Helali \cite{Helali2026}.

The incidence analysis of Section~\ref{sec:incidence} gives structural evidence for the conjecture: the only known mechanism for a constant-Jacobian noninvertible map cannot occur in dimension two, and
the invariant that decides this, the resultant weight $n-2$, is one number. It is not a proof, and it does not address a planar counterexample of a different structure.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

The results are exact inside their stated scope, and the scope is narrower than the statements the paper discusses.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Certificates are window-bounded.} A window certificate settles one window; the passage to all partner degrees needs the tower, which requires a top form that is not a proper power.
Theorem~\ref{thm:t5all} holds on six shapes. No base certificate, and no closed form of the transport chain, is available for general $(u,v,d)$; the extension beyond the grid is a conjecture [C]. The case of a pure-power top $T=b\,x^d$
has no recorded proof and is not claimed.
\item \emph{The covector exclusions are modular.} Degrees $1$, $2$ and $3$ are excluded by infeasibility modulo two primes (one prime at degree $1$) of all-integer systems; over $\Q$ this
excludes every covector whose denominators are prime to those primes, and no good-prime argument is given for the rest. The sweeps are one-sided: a feasible support does not establish existence.
\item \emph{The atlas is partial.} It closes sub-families of the $51$ interior coefficients (a four-coefficient restriction, with the transverse coefficient $(2,8)$ closed only on the direct $A=0$ boundary
and on one section), not the family. Of $238$ cells of Table~\ref{tab:atlas}, $26$ are not recorded. The closures are exact inside the persisted $302$-row model, whose row pool was audited (EXP-111).
\item \emph{Case 1 is not closed.} The independent evidence is modular at five primes, the exact attempts did not finish, and the reduction from the $13$ equations to the systems of Helali's identities was not reproduced. The
whole exclusion of $(72,108)$ is conditional on Proposition~4.3 of \cite{GGHV2022} being exhaustive and on the transcription of its polygons.
\item \emph{Software.} The archived outputs do not record the Singular version (4.3.2 on the device when checked), and the run times of several records are not recorded (n/r in Table~\ref{tab:atlas}).
\item \emph{Literature.} The statements about published work were checked against the cited sources as of 2026-07-22; several classical statements were read through a source that attributes them (marked S in Table~\ref{tab:ladder}),
and the second claimed exclusion of $(72,108)$ is recorded without a reference [U].
\end{itemize}

The open problems are the following. (1) An independent proof that Case~1 is empty: a unit-ideal certificate for the $13$ equations over $K=\Q[a_7]/(H)$ on both branches, obtained by shrinking the input heights
before spending more primes, or a smaller exact object. This is the active problem. (2) Lift the fifth direction $(2,8)$ through the two EXP-129 residual sections and the EXP-130 finite-base section, and construct the transverse
$d=0$ kernel quotient with its Fitting ideal; the complete-grid CRT argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:exp135-inert} applies wherever explicit degree and height bounds are available. (3) Transport the intersection number $21$ to the
reduced system, which requires a complete account of the boundary divisors through the swap, the Laurent cuts and the final inversion. (4) Derive a condition beyond the first $D=72$ branch of the Newton-resolution list, which retains the
GGHV branch. (5) A closed form of the transport chain for general $(u,v,d)$, and the derivation of the kill of odd resonances for proper-power tops. (6) The twenty-three configurations of maximal degree in $[125,150]$ that the
published sources do not decide. The properness reformulation ($JC(2)$ holds iff every planar Keller map has empty Jelonek set; exact certificates in EXP-014) provides an independent cross-check.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

The planar Keller condition is linear in the partner for a fixed polynomial, and this makes exact certificates possible: a left-null covector with a nonzero pairing is a finite proof that a window has no
partner. From that, the paper proves four exclusion theorems from a weight-class calculus. A two-term $P=x+a\,x^uy^v$ is never a Keller component at any partner degree, and neither is any
lower-weight perturbation of it; no Keller component has a top edge anchored at the linear vertex $x$ with an interior direction; and if $x$ is a vertex of the Newton polygon the component is $x+f(y)$. A fifth
exclusion, for the staircase that swallows $x$, is proved at every partner degree on six grid shapes through the certificate tower, whose lemma holds when the top form is not a proper power. The scope of each result is stated
with the grid on which it was verified (Table~\ref{tab:exclusions}).

For the one degree pair that the literature leaves open below $125$, the paper maps the three branches of the reduction onto the two coefficient systems of Proposition~4.3 and works on the Case 1 system of $302$
equations in $51$ interior coefficients. The chart atlas of Table~\ref{tab:atlas} closes the three-coefficient restriction $\{(0,1),(0,5),(1,0)\}$ and the four-coefficient restriction $\{(0,1),(0,5),(1,0),(2,9)\}$ exactly, with the
stratification of Figure~\ref{fig:atlas}; for a fifth coefficient it closes the direct $A=0$ boundary and proves that one section is unchanged. The independent derivation of Helali's equations reproduces his degree-$35$ eliminant,
proves Case 2 empty with a Singular standard basis computed in three seconds, and shows that Case 1 reduces to the unit ideal at five primes on both branches. The attempt at an exact certificate for Case 1 gives a measured cost: exact
standard bases did not finish in six hours over the degree-$35$ field or its quintic subfield, and the multi-modular certificates remain unknown at about $20{,}000$ primes per branch, with coordinates above about $94{,}000$ digits.

The truncation ladder for a simultaneous covector excludes degrees $1$, $2$ and $3$ (modulo two primes), so a covector, if any exists, has degree at least $4$; the pinned corrector ladder does not terminate (it
stabilizes at dimension $39$), the finite ceiling it was meant to give is not established, and the natural $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ premise is refuted at its invariant test. The incidence analysis shows that the structure of the known
counterexample cannot occur in the plane, because the resultant weight $n-2$ vanishes at $n=2$.

None of this excludes $(72,108)$ or raises the planar degree floor, and none of it decides $JC(2)$. What the paper establishes is a set of exact, archived and scoped statements; a precise description of what separates
the present evidence from a proof of the Case 1 exclusion; and a quantified cost for the direct certificate that points the next work at the height of its coefficients. The gcd and degree floors that remain
binding are those of Table~\ref{tab:ladder}.
