\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design}

The computations of Sections~\ref{sec:construction} and~\ref{sec:results6} are decision problems, not samples: every
satisfiability question has a definite answer that is checked, so the quantities that vary between runs are only the solver times.
Each computation was designed around predictions that were recorded before the runs they govern, with a stated way for each
to fail; Table~\ref{tab:preds} lists them with their outcomes, including the predictions that were refuted or left undecided.

\paragraph{Sources of 6-poles.} The 6-poles are cut from the Petersen graph $\PG$, the flower snarks $J_5$ and $J_7$ and the
dodecahedron, each cyclically 5-edge-connected (exhaustive searches find no cycle-separating cut with at most four edges), and from
$\Gfty$, which is not and serves as a control and for the charge spectrum of its critical pairs. Shape~(a) removes two
non-adjacent vertices up to automorphisms of the source; shape~(b) removes three pairwise disjoint edges and is run for $\PG$, $J_5$
and the dodecahedron with every split of the six ends into two triples. For each 6-pole, split and orbit one formula fixes the class of the first connector
to the orbit representative, which gives \nFormulasSix{} formulas for the \nSplits{} splits of the four colorable sources and
$6 \cdot 212 = 1{,}272$ for $\Gfty$; no formula reached the cap of 120 seconds.

\paragraph{Blocks and families.} A block is a 6-pole with an ordered pair of connectors. The 81 blocks are the claw $Y$,
the Petersen superedge $S$, the 40 shape-(b) 6-poles of $\PG$ with their ordered splits ($\mathrm{Pb}$), the 14 shape-(a) 6-poles of $J_5$ ($\mathrm{Ja5}$) and the 21
of $J_7$ ($\mathrm{Ja7}$), and the 4 of the dodecahedron ($\mathrm{Da}$). The five families are $F_1 = \{Y\}$, $F_2 = \{S\}$,
$F_3 = \{Y, S\}$, $F_4$, the 40 blocks $\mathrm{Pb}$ with \nGenFfour{} distinct generators, and $F_5$, all 81 blocks with \nGenFfive{} distinct generators.
The claw and the superedge are the blocks whose rings contain the flower snarks and the odd rings of Petersen superedges, and the larger
families ask whether any block of the sources measured above has a ring without Petersen coloring.

\paragraph{Baselines and independent checks.} The product runs the following checks on its own results, each independent of the
computation it tests. A satisfiable answer is accepted only by a program that tests the star condition from the definition and reads only the graph and the
labels. An unsatisfiable answer is accepted only with a DRAT proof checked by \texttt{drat-trim}. The sector matrices of the claw and the superedge are recomputed by
exhaustive enumeration and by a second encoding with a second solver, and 46 of the 81 blocks by the second encoding. The nonempty sectors of the 6-poles of $\PG$, $J_5$,
$J_7$ and the dodecahedron that appear in both computations coincide with the conducted orbits that EXP-013 measured by a
different method, formulas that constrain the class instead of enumerating models. The certificates are checked by Algorithm~\ref{alg:check}. The cut searches are compared with an
exhaustive enumeration on three graphs of known behaviour (Table~\ref{tab:rings}).

\paragraph{Randomness and statistics.} The only random choices are the ten sampled pairs of $R_3$ (five inside one block, five across two; a fixed seed),
the 200 random words of the cross-check (a fixed seed), and the randomized growth that finds the 6-edge cut of $\Gfty$ (a fixed seed). Solver times are reported as
minimum to maximum, and as mean and sample standard deviation where a set of runs is summarized; they size the work and carry no claim about the speed of the
methods, and the parallel runs shared one machine among several solvers. No statistical test is used, because no claim rests on a sample: the 203 random rings are a
check, and their agreement is reported as a count with the one direction it tests (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
