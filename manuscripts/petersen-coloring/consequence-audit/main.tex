\begin{abstract}
Jaeger's Petersen coloring conjecture, the strongest known sufficient condition for the
Berge--Fulkerson and 5-cycle double cover conjectures, was refuted in 2026 by cubic graphs on 112
and 52 vertices. We determine, by proof-carrying computation, what survives on the five
retrievable counterexamples, of orders 52, 52, 68, 112 and 112. Independent encodings refute
Petersen colorings and normal 5-edge-colorings of all five, with proofs checked by
\texttt{drat-trim}. Explicit witnesses give each graph a Berge--Fulkerson cover, perfect matching
index exactly 4, a Fan--Raspaud triple, a 5-cycle double cover and a nowhere-zero 5-flow; none has
a nowhere-zero 4-flow. Oddness and resistance are 4 and 3 on the two 112-vertex graphs and 2 and 2
on the others, and the normal chromatic index is exactly 6. We prove that no edge map into the
Petersen graph has exactly one bad vertex, and all five graphs have Petersen defect 2 with every
vertex pair critical (17{,}362 witnesses). The defect is at most the least number of abnormal
edges, and both equal 2. Rings and frames assembled from counterexamples need one bad vertex per
block, so no sublinear function bounds the abnormal edges of 2-connected or 3-connected cubic
graphs, and two statements of a conjecture of Mattiolo, Mazzuoccolo and Mkrtchyan are false. A
fifth statement is equivalent to a defect bound of 2, conditionally on a proposition of that paper
stated without proof. Every computation is propositional; encodings,
counts and commands are given, and each proof is reproduced by re-running its
command.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and problem formulation}

\subsection{The conjecture and its ladder}

Let $\PG$ denote the Petersen graph. For a cubic graph $G$, a \emph{Petersen coloring} is a map
$\sigma : E(G) \to E(\PG)$ such that for every vertex $v$ of $G$ there is a vertex $w$ of $\PG$
with $\sigma(\partial_G(v)) = \partial_\PG(w)$, where $\partial(\cdot)$ denotes the set of edges
at a vertex; equivalently, the three edges at $v$ map bijectively onto the three edges at $w$.
A proper 5-edge-coloring is \emph{normal} if every edge $uv$ is \emph{poor}
($|c(\partial u) \cup c(\partial v)| = 3$) or \emph{rich} ($= 5$); the \emph{normal chromatic
index} $\chi'_N(G)$ is the least $k$ admitting a normal $k$-edge-coloring.

\begin{conjecture}[Jaeger 1988~\cite{Jaeger1988}]
Every bridgeless cubic graph admits a Petersen coloring.
\end{conjecture}

Jaeger~\cite{Jaeger1985} proved that a cubic graph has a Petersen coloring iff it has a normal
5-edge-coloring (an explicit bijective form is Proposition~2.1 of~\cite{Putman2026}). The
conjecture implies the Berge--Fulkerson conjecture (six perfect matchings covering every edge
exactly twice) and the 5-cycle double cover conjecture~\cite{GJMMMU2026, MazzMkrt2020}; the
Berge conjecture (perfect matching index at most~5) and the Fan--Raspaud conjecture (three
perfect matchings with empty intersection) follow from Berge--Fulkerson. Every 3-edge-colorable
cubic graph is trivially Petersen colorable, so the whole ladder concerns snarks. Exhaustive
generation had verified the conjecture for every snark on at most 36 vertices~\cite{BGHM2013};
the case of the weak snarks of girth 4 on 36 vertices was completed in~\cite{GMS2019}, as reported
in~\cite{GJMMM2026}. A smallest counterexample has between 40 and 52 vertices (Observation~10
of~\cite{GJMMMU2026}).

\subsection{The disproof}

On 6 August 2026 Putman~\cite{Putman2026} published a simple bridgeless cubic graph $\Gmain$ on
112 vertices and 168 edges with no Petersen coloring and no normal 5-edge-coloring, certified by
CaDiCaL proofs checked with \texttt{drat-trim}, together with a nonisomorphic $D_3$-symmetric
counterexample $\Gsym$; both have girth~5 and edge- and vertex-connectivity~3. The construction
composes copies of the 4-pole $F$ (the Petersen graph minus the endpoints of one edge) with claw
six-poles: $L = 4F + C$ and $\Gmain = 3L + C$. Jooken~\cite{GJMMMU2026} gave a human-checkable
proof through the Petersen-coloring sets of $F$ and $L$ and a 4-clique obstruction in the line
graph of $\PG$. Goedgebeur, Jooken, M\'a\v{c}ajov\'a, Mattiolo and Mazzuoccolo~\cite{GJMMM2026}
then gave a cyclically 4-edge-connected girth-5 counterexample $\Gfty$ on 52 vertices and infinite
cyclically 4-edge-connected families. The extended account by the same authors and
Ulyanov~\cite{GJMMMU2026} gives a second counterexample $\Gftyb$ on 52 vertices, a purely
theoretical proof for both, cyclically 4-edge-connected counterexamples of girth 5 of every even
order at least 60 (Theorem~8 there), and the bounds
$40 \le n_{\min} \le 52$ for the order of a smallest counterexample. It leaves open whether every
bridgeless cubic graph has a normal 6-edge-coloring (Conjecture~12 there, attributed to
\v{S}\'amal) and whether cyclically 5-edge-connected counterexamples exist (Problem~11). A further
counterexample $\Gsix$ on 68 vertices, credited there to an announcement on a social network, is
available with the other four from the House of Graphs~\cite{HoG}. The Jooken note and the
extended account are two revisions of one arXiv record. For these five graphs~\cite{GJMMMU2026}
reports Berge--Fulkerson covers, perfect matching index at most~4, 5-cycle double covers, strong
normal 6-edge-colorings, and proper 5-edge-colorings with exactly two abnormal edges; the
corresponding statements below were obtained independently.

\subsection{What is computed and proved}

The five counterexamples $\Gmain$, $\Gsym$, $\Gfty$, $\Gftyb$, $\Gsix$ are the first bridgeless
cubic graphs for which the Petersen route to a Berge--Fulkerson cover or a 5-cycle double cover is
closed. For each of them we ask which conjectures at the top of the ladder still hold, and how far
the graph is from being colorable. Every answer is exact: a positive answer is an explicit witness
re-verified by a checker that reads only the graph, and a negative answer is a DRAT proof checked
by \texttt{drat-trim}. The results are collected in Table~\ref{tab:invariants}. On top of the
finite computations, three theorems are proved: the number of bad vertices of an edge map into the
Petersen graph is never exactly one (Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}); rings and frames assembled from
counterexamples force one bad vertex in every block (Theorems~\ref{thm:rings}
and~\ref{thm:frames}); and statement~(e) of the conjecture of Mattiolo, Mazzuoccolo and
Mkrtchyan is, conditionally on a proposition of that paper stated without proof, a statement about two bad
vertices (Theorem~\ref{thm:twodefect}).

encodings, their sizes, the solver settings and the checkers. Section~\ref{sec:certify} reports

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Origin and equivalent forms.} Jaeger~\cite{Jaeger1988} stated the conjecture in 1988; the
statement that a cubic graph has a Petersen coloring iff it has a normal 5-edge-coloring is
due to~\cite{Jaeger1985}, and Putman~\cite{Putman2026} proves a bijective form in Kneser notation.
Both Jaeger sources are cited here through the 2026 papers; their statements are not re-derived.
Version~4 of~\cite{GJMMMU2026} also presents Petersen colorings as cycle-continuous maps to the
Petersen graph and connects them to spin models~\cite{Beaudin2010}, matroids and the apex-graph form of Tutte's
3-edge-coloring conjecture~\cite{Inoue2026}.

\paragraph{Verification frontier.} Brinkmann, Goedgebeur, H\"agglund and
Markstr\"om~\cite{BGHM2013} generated all snarks on at most 36 vertices and report that strong forms
of the cycle double cover conjecture and the Petersen coloring conjecture hold for them. Their
snarks, in the terminology reported in~\cite{GJMMM2026}, are cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graphs of girth at least 5 that are not 3-edge-colorable.
Goedgebeur, Ma\v{c}ajov\'a and \v{S}koviera~\cite{GMS2019} prove that the smallest snark with cyclic
connectivity 4 and oddness at least 4 has 44 vertices; in that paper a snark is any connected cubic
graph that is not 3-edge-colorable, and the theorem does not exclude cyclically 5-edge-connected
snarks of oddness at least 4 below 44 vertices. The claim that the weak snarks of order 36 were
completed there is taken from~\cite{GJMMM2026}, not from~\cite{GMS2019}. H\"agglund and
Steffen~\cite{HS2014} show that several families of snarks are Petersen colorable, Ferrarini,
Mazzuoccolo and Mkrtchyan~\cite{FMM2019} that a family of Loupekhine snarks has normal
5-edge-colorings, and Sedlar and \v{S}krekovski~\cite{SS2024} the same for snarks superpositioned by
flower snarks; normal 5-edge-colorings and Petersen colorings are equivalent~\cite{Jaeger1985}. These
families are cited through the reference list of~\cite{Putman2026} and their statements are not
re-derived here.

\paragraph{The counterexamples.} Putman's two 112-vertex graphs~\cite{Putman2026} assume nothing
beyond cubic and bridgeless and are certified by two SAT encodings and DRAT proofs. The proof
of~\cite{GJMMMU2026} for $\Gmain$ computes the Petersen-coloring sets of the 4-poles $F$ and $L$
and concludes through a 4-clique in the line graph of a triangle-free cubic graph. Goedgebeur
et al.~\cite{GJMMM2026} give a 52-vertex graph and show that substituting longer 4-poles with the
same coloring set yields infinitely many cyclically 4-edge-connected counterexamples; the extended
account~\cite{GJMMMU2026} adds a second 52-vertex graph. All known counterexamples contain the
4-pole $F$. Putman also derives infinitely many counterexamples from one by the closure
theorems of Ma, Mattiolo, Steffen and Wolf~\cite{MMSW2025}.

\paragraph{Abnormal edges and normal colorings.} Mazzuoccolo and Mkrtchyan~\cite{MazzMkrt2020}
introduce normal edge-colorings, prove that every simple cubic graph has a normal 7-edge-coloring
and ask whether 6 colors suffice for bridgeless cubic graphs; the same authors study normal
6-edge-colorings of special bridgeless cubic graphs~\cite{MM2020b}. Fabrici, Lu\v{z}ar, Sot\'ak
and \v{S}vecov\'a~\cite{FLSS2025} show that every cubic graph with oddness 2 has a normal
6-edge-coloring. The graphs $\Gfty$, $\Gftyb$ and $\Gsix$ have oddness 2 (Section~\ref{sec:cdc}),
so their normal chromatic index is at most 6 also by that theorem; the computation here is
independent and also gives strong normal colorings. Mattiolo, Mazzuoccolo and
Mkrtchyan~\cite{MMM2021} prove that no proper 5-edge-coloring has exactly one abnormal edge, show
that the sublinear approximation of the Petersen coloring conjecture is equivalent to it for
bridgeless graphs and to boundedness of the abnormal edges by 5, 7 and 9 on three smaller classes,
and conjecture that five statements (a) to (e) are equivalent. Their Proposition~2 (the cyclic
joins used below are cyclically 4-edge-connected) is stated without proof, and their proof of the
statement for the last class (cyclically 4-edge-connected graphs, bound 9) uses it.

\paragraph{Companion results.} The preprint on unbounded defect~\cite{UD2026} constructs cyclically
4-edge-connected cubic graphs of Petersen defect at least $t$ on $102t$ vertices and shows that
every ring of claws and Petersen superedges is Petersen colorable. The colorable-only-by-itself
preprint~\cite{COBI2026} shows, conditionally on Observation~10 of~\cite{GJMMMU2026}, that the two
52-vertex counterexamples are colorable only by themselves. Adjacent work on other targets
(Sylvester colorings~\cite{FM2026}) and on perfect matching conjectures for $r$-graphs~\cite{MSWZ2026}
makes no statement on Petersen colorings of the counterexamples. The solvers are described
in~\cite{CaDiCaL2024, DRATtrim2014} (the solver run here is CaDiCaL 1.7.3, not the 2.0 release of
that reference), and the open problem is archived in~\cite{OPG}.

\section{Method: encodings and certification}\label{sec:encodings}

\subsection{Conventions}

A graph $G$ is simple, cubic, with $n$ vertices and $m = 3n/2$ edges, the edges indexed in the
sorted order of the edge list. $\PG$ is the Kneser graph $K(5,2)$ with 15 edges; two edges of $\PG$
are adjacent when they share a vertex, and every edge of $\PG$ has exactly four adjacent edges. The star of a vertex is the set of the three
edges at it. Every formula is built by a small builder with named variables, pairwise ``exactly one'' blocks and
the sequential counter of Sinz for cardinality bounds. For $N$ literals and a bound $k$ with
$0 < k < N$ the counter adds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinz}
\mathrm{sinz}(N,k) = \bigl(Nk \text{ variables},\; k + (N-1)(2k+1) \text{ clauses}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and for $k=0$ it adds $N$ unit clauses. The encodings use edge variables only, and the Petersen
encoding shares no variable scheme with the vertex-witness encodings of~\cite{Putman2026}, so that
an error in one scheme is not repeated in the other. Table~\ref{tab:notation} lists the notation. Every instance is a propositional formula in
conjunctive normal form; its status is satisfiable, with a model that is decoded and checked, or
unsatisfiable, with a DRAT proof that is checked, or undecided at the cap of its computation.

\subsection{The encodings}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Petersen edge-image encoding (computations EXP-001, EXP-004, EXP-006)}\label{alg:petersen}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State variables $y_{e,f}$ for every edge $e$ of $G$ and every edge $f$ of $\PG$
\For{each edge $e$ of $G$} \State add the clause $\bigvee_f y_{e,f}$ and $\lnot y_{e,f} \lor \lnot y_{e,f'}$ for $f \ne f'$ \EndFor
\For{each vertex $v$, each pair $a,b$ of the three edges at $v$}
 \State for every $(s,t)$ with $s = t$ or $s,t$ not adjacent in $\PG$, add $\lnot y_{a,s} \lor \lnot y_{b,t}$
\EndFor
\State symmetry units: fix $y_{e_0,f_0}$ for $e_0$ the first edge, and $y_{e_1,f_1}$ for $e_1$ the other
edge at the first end of $e_0$ and $f_1$ the first edge adjacent to $f_0$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The encoding is exact. Three pairwise adjacent, pairwise distinct edges of a triangle-free graph
share a common vertex, since otherwise they would form a triangle; $\PG$ is triangle-free, and the
clauses of the third line forbid precisely the equal or non-adjacent image pairs. Hence the
images of the three edges at $v$ are the three edges of one vertex star of $\PG$. The two units use
that $\PG$ is edge-transitive and that the stabilizer of an edge is transitive on its four adjacent
edges; the three archived refutations without the units (Table~\ref{tab:certify}) show that the
status does not change.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Normal 5-edge-coloring encoding (computations EXP-001, EXP-004, EXP-006)}\label{alg:normal}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State variables $x_{e,c}$, $c \in \{0,\dots,4\}$; exactly one color per edge; $\lnot x_{a,c} \lor \lnot x_{b,c}$ for adjacent edges $a,b$
\For{each edge $e=uv$ with the two other edges $a_1,a_2$ at $u$ and $b_1,b_2$ at $v$}
 \State variables $p_{e,c}$, $q_{e,c}$, $r_e$ with $p_{e,c} \leftrightarrow x_{a_1,c} \lor x_{a_2,c}$ and $q_{e,c} \leftrightarrow x_{b_1,c} \lor x_{b_2,c}$
 \State for every color $c$: $\lnot r_e \lor \lnot p_{e,c} \lor \lnot q_{e,c}$ (rich), $r_e \lor \lnot p_{e,c} \lor q_{e,c}$ and $r_e \lor p_{e,c} \lor \lnot q_{e,c}$ (poor)
\EndFor
\State strong normal colorings: add the unit $r_e$ for every edge; $k$ colors: replace 5 by $k$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

An edge is rich exactly when the two sides carry disjoint color sets, which the first clause
expresses, and poor exactly when they carry equal sets, which the other two clauses express.
Normal $k$-edge-colorings use the same clauses with $k$ colors, and the Petersen and normal
defects are obtained by guarding the clauses of one edge or vertex with an indicator and bounding
the indicators (Algorithm~\ref{alg:defect}).

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Perfect matching covers, cycle double covers and flows (EXP-002, EXP-003, EXP-008)}\label{alg:covers}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State matchings: variables $m_{e,i}$ for $i \le k$, exactly one edge of $m_{\cdot,i}$ at every vertex
\State Berge--Fulkerson ($k=6$): every edge in at least two and at most two matchings; units $m_{0,0}$, $m_{0,1}$
\State Berge cover by $k$ matchings: every edge in at least one; unit $m_{0,0}$; Fan--Raspaud ($k=3$): no edge in all three, with the unit $m_{0,0}$ (included in the encoder and in the counts; it only restricts the satisfiable instances, since a triple need not contain the first edge, so a satisfiable answer is unaffected)
\State cycle double cover: variables $z_{e,i}$, $i \le 5$; at every vertex forbid degrees 1 and 3 in each $z_{\cdot,i}$; every edge in exactly two; units $z_{0,0}$, $z_{0,1}$
\State flow over $\mathbb Z_k$: variables $w_{e,a}$, $a \in \{1,\dots,k-1\}$, edges oriented from the smaller end; exactly one value per edge; at every vertex forbid each value triple with signed sum not $0 \bmod k$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Three perfect matchings of a cubic graph cover every edge only if they are pairwise disjoint at every
vertex, that is, if they form a proper 3-edge-coloring~\dd{}; so the perfect matching index of a
graph that is not 3-edge-colorable is at least 4, and the encoding of three matchings is satisfiable
exactly when the graph is 3-edge-colorable.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Oddness and resistance (EXP-003, EXP-008)}\label{alg:oddres}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State oddness bound $b$: variables $m_e$ (a perfect matching $M$, exactly one at each vertex), $\mathrm{col}_v$, $\mathrm{mono}_e$
\State for every edge $e=uv$: $m_e \lor \mathrm{mono}_e \lor \mathrm{col}_u \lor \mathrm{col}_v$ and $m_e \lor \mathrm{mono}_e \lor \lnot\mathrm{col}_u \lor \lnot\mathrm{col}_v$
\State at most $b$ of the $\mathrm{mono}_e$ are true; unit $\mathrm{col}_0$
\State resistance bound $b$: variables $x_{e,c}$, $c \in \{0,1,2\}$, and $\mathrm{del}_e$; each edge has a color or is deleted, never two colors; adjacent non-deleted edges differ; at most $b$ deletions
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{lemma}[\dd]\label{lem:oddness}
Let $M$ be a perfect matching of a bridgeless cubic graph. The minimum, over vertex 2-colorings
$\mathrm{col}$, of the number of edges of the 2-factor $E \setminus M$ whose ends have equal color is the
number of odd cycles of that 2-factor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The claim is the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oddness}
\min_{\mathrm{col}} \bigl|\{uv \in E \setminus M : \mathrm{col}(u) = \mathrm{col}(v)\}\bigr| = \#\{\text{odd cycles of } E \setminus M\}.
\end{equation}
The cycles of the 2-factor are disjoint and the count is additive. A cycle of even length admits an
alternating coloring with no monochromatic edge. A cycle of odd length cannot be properly 2-colored,
so it has at least one monochromatic edge, and the alternating coloring with one adjacent equal pair
has exactly one.
\end{proof}

Consequently the least bound $b$ for which the oddness formula is satisfiable is the oddness of the
graph. The resistance formula is satisfiable for $b$ exactly when some set of at most $b$ edges
leaves a 3-edge-colorable graph.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Petersen defect by cardinality and by designated relaxation (EXP-004, EXP-006, EXP-008)}\label{alg:defect}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State cardinality bound $d$: Algorithm~\ref{alg:petersen} without the symmetry units; add an indicator $\mathrm{bad}_v$ to the pair clauses of every vertex $v$; at most $d$ indicators true
\State relaxation at a set $U$ of vertices: Algorithm~\ref{alg:petersen} with the pair clauses of every vertex of $U$ omitted; symmetry units on an edge with no end in $U$ and an adjacent edge
\State a satisfying assignment is decoded to an edge map; the checker counts the vertices whose star is not mapped onto a star, and the instance is a witness only if that set equals $U$
\State relaxation at one edge for normal 5-edge-colorings: the $r$, $p$, $q$ clauses of that edge are omitted
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Experimental design: solvers, caps and checkers}

Formulas are written in DIMACS and solved by CaDiCaL 1.7.3 under WSL (\texttt{cadical -q f.cnf
f.drat}), which writes a DRAT proof when the formula is unsatisfiable; the proof is checked by
\texttt{drat-trim} (\texttt{drat-trim f.cnf f.drat}, accepted only when it prints \texttt{s
VERIFIED}). The manifests record the \texttt{drat-trim} usage banner but not a version string, which
is therefore not stated. Every call has a wall-clock cap: 1{,}800 s for the certification, cover,
flow, oddness, resistance, normal-6 and cardinality-defect instances and for the ring and frame
instances; 600 s for single-vertex and pair relaxations and for the 4-pole instances; 1{,}800 s for
single-edge relaxations of $\Gfty$ and 900 s for those of the later graphs; and 3{,}600 s for a
proof check. An instance that reaches its cap is reported as undecided and is used for nothing.

A satisfying assignment is decoded and verified by a checker written in the Python standard library
that reads only the edge list and the decoded object. The checkers test that a set is a perfect
matching and count the coverage of each edge; that a set is an even subgraph and count the double
cover; that a flow takes nonzero values and conserves modulo $k$; that a coloring is proper and
count its abnormal edges; that a map into $E(\PG)$ is counted at the vertices whose star is not a
star; that a 2-factor has the stated number of odd cycles; and that the non-deleted edges are
properly 3-edge-colored. No floating-point arithmetic is used.

\subsection{The graphs}

edge list with $u < v$. The five graphs are
$\Gmain$ (digest \texttt{dc16cc18\ldots{}e8b}), $\Gsym$ (digest \texttt{0f2d8858\ldots{}35c7}),

\begin{proposition}[\mv]\label{prop:structure}

\end{proposition}

\section{Perfect matching covers}\label{sec:covers}

\begin{theorem}[\mv]\label{thm:covers}
Each of the five graphs admits a Berge--Fulkerson cover, a Berge cover by five perfect
matchings, three perfect matchings with empty intersection, and a cover by four perfect
matchings. Consequently their perfect matching index equals~4.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The matchings are explicit (the repository files of computations EXP-002 and EXP-008
list them as edge index sets) and were re-verified by a checker that tests each set to be a perfect
matching and then counts the coverage of every edge. The index is at least~4 because the graphs
are not 3-edge-colorable (Theorem~\ref{thm:certify}, the trivial direction of the conjecture, and
Algorithm~\ref{alg:covers}): three perfect matchings covering the edges would be a 3-edge-coloring.
For $\Gftyb$ and $\Gsix$ the instance of three matchings is also refuted directly with a checked
proof (1{,}262 bytes for $\Gftyb$).
\end{proof}

The cover instances are small (Table~\ref{tab:encvalues}): each Berge--Fulkerson instance has
$9n$ variables and $63n+2$ clauses, that is 1{,}008 variables and 7{,}058 clauses on the 112-vertex
graphs and 468 variables and 3{,}278 clauses on $\Gfty$ and $\Gftyb$. They were solved in at most
\CoverMaxA{} s for the first three graphs and \CoverMaxB{} s for the other two. The value~4 is one
below the Petersen graph's own perfect matching index~5, which our control recovers with a verified
refutation of the four-matching instance (452 bytes of proof) and a satisfiable five-matching
instance. Thus, on the first counterexamples, the Berge--Fulkerson, Berge and Fan--Raspaud
conjectures all hold, and the graphs are in this sense better behaved than the Petersen graph. The covers exist although the
Petersen route to them is closed.

Explicitly, a Berge--Fulkerson cover is a family of six perfect matchings $M_1,\dots,M_6$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bf}
\sum_{i=1}^{6} \mathbf 1_{M_i} = 2\cdot \mathbf 1_{E(G)},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf 1_X$ is the indicator of an edge set $X$. The other covers of Theorem~\ref{thm:covers}
that are weaker than~\eqref{eq:bf} follow from it~\dd{}: every edge lies in exactly two of the six
matchings, so deleting any one matching leaves every edge covered, which gives the Berge cover by
five matchings, and no three matchings share an edge, which gives the Fan--Raspaud triple. The cover
by four matchings does not follow from~\eqref{eq:bf} and is a separate computation. A perfect matching
of a graph of order $n$ has $n/2$ edges, so each cover of a 112-vertex graph consists of six sets of
56 edges and each cover of a 52-vertex graph of six sets of 26 edges, which is the count $6 \cdot n/2 =
2m$ that~\eqref{eq:bf} requires. These witnesses are stored as edge index sets, and the checker
recomputes the coverage of every edge from the graph.

\section{Cycle double covers, flows, oddness and resistance}\label{sec:cdc}

\begin{theorem}[\mv]\label{thm:cdc}
Each of the five graphs admits a 5-cycle double cover (five even subgraphs covering
every edge exactly twice) and a nowhere-zero $\Zfive$-flow, and none admits a nowhere-zero
$\mathbb{Z}_4$-flow. The oddness and resistance are
\[
\mathrm{odd}(\Gmain) = \mathrm{odd}(\Gsym) = 4,\qquad \mathrm{res}(\Gmain) = \mathrm{res}(\Gsym) = 3,
\]
\[
\mathrm{odd} = \mathrm{res} = 2 \quad\text{for } \Gfty,\ \Gftyb \text{ and } \Gsix .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Double covers and flows are explicit witnesses; the 4-flow refutations carry checked proofs.
Oddness is decided from both sides with Algorithm~\ref{alg:oddres}: by Lemma~\ref{lem:oddness} the
least satisfiable bound $b$ is the oddness. Bounds $1, 2, 3$ are refuted with checked proofs for the
112-vertex graphs and bound~$4$ is satisfied by a perfect matching whose 2-factor has exactly four
odd cycles (counted by the checker); for $\Gfty$, $\Gftyb$ and $\Gsix$ bound~$1$ is refuted and
bound~$2$ satisfied. Resistance uses a deletion indicator per edge and a proper 3-edge-coloring of
the remaining edges, with the same two-sided protocol: bounds 1 and 2 are refuted and bound 3 is
satisfied on the 112-vertex graphs, and bound 1 is refuted and bound 2 satisfied on the others.
\end{proof}

The ladders of Table~\ref{tab:ladders} are short and every instance of the first three graphs

A 5-cycle double cover is a family of five even subgraphs $Z_1,\dots,Z_5$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cdc}
\sum_{i=1}^{5} \mathbf 1_{Z_i} = 2\cdot \mathbf 1_{E(G)},
\end{equation}
so that the five subgraphs carry $2m = 3n$ edge incidences; each is the edge set of a union of cycles
because every vertex has degree 0 or 2 in it. A nowhere-zero $\mathbb Z_5$-flow is stored as one value
in $\{1,2,3,4\}$ per edge, with the edges oriented from the smaller end, and the checker verifies the
conservation modulo 5 at every vertex. The perfect matching that realizes the oddness has $n/2$ edges
and leaves a 2-factor of $n$ edges; on the 112-vertex graphs that 2-factor has exactly four odd
cycles, which the checker counts by traversing it. These three kinds of witness are independent of
each other, and each is re-verified from the edge list alone.

\section{Normal 6-edge-colorings and the Petersen defect}\label{sec:normal}

\subsection{Normal chromatic index}

Mazzuoccolo and Mkrtchyan proved that every simple cubic graph admits a normal 7-edge-coloring
and asked whether 6 colors always suffice for bridgeless cubic graphs~\cite{MazzMkrt2020}; the
question is restated as a conjecture in~\cite{GJMMM2026, GJMMMU2026}, where strong normal
6-edge-colorings (every edge rich) of the five graphs are reported.

\begin{theorem}[\mv]\label{thm:normal6}
Each of the five graphs admits a normal 6-edge-coloring and a strong normal
6-edge-coloring. Since none admits a normal 5-edge-coloring (Theorem~\ref{thm:certify}), their
normal chromatic index is exactly~6.
\end{theorem}

The colorings are explicit witnesses re-verified by a checker that recomputes, for every edge,
the number of colors on its two end stars. The ten instances of Algorithm~\ref{alg:normal} with $k = 6$
(with and without the strong units) solved in at most \NsixMaxA{} s on the first three graphs and at
most \NsixMaxB{} s on the other two. A normal coloring is proper and has, at every edge $uv$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal}
\bigl|c(\partial u) \cup c(\partial v)\bigr| \in \{3, 5\},
\end{equation}
and it is strong when the value is 5 at every edge. The lower bound $\chi'_N \ge 6$ is the
unsatisfiability of the 5-color instance, which carries a checked proof (Table~\ref{tab:certify}), and
the upper bound is the witness; the equality is therefore exact for each of the five graphs. For
$\Gfty$, $\Gftyb$ and $\Gsix$, whose oddness is 2, the upper bound also follows from the theorem
of~\cite{FLSS2025}, which the witnesses here confirm without using.

\subsection{How far from colorable: the Petersen defect}

Define the \emph{Petersen defect} of a cubic graph $G$ as the least number of vertices at which
the star condition fails, over all maps $E(G) \to E(\PG)$ (equivalently, the least number of
vertices whose star constraints must be dropped for a coloring to exist); a Petersen coloring is
a map of defect~0. Encoding the defect through a cardinality constraint over all vertices
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:defect}, computation EXP-004) proved hard for the solver. The bound-0 instances
are refuted with checked proofs (262 and 279 s on the 112-vertex graphs, 50 s on $\Gfty$), the
bound-1 instances of $\Gmain$ and $\Gsym$ reached the 1{,}800 s cap without a decision, and the
bound-1 instance of $\Gfty$ was refuted in $532.4$ seconds with a verified proof of
548{,}686{,}502 bytes, checked in 578 s. The bound-2 instance of $\Gfty$ is satisfiable in $0.09$
seconds and its witness has exactly two bad vertices. We therefore decided the defect by
\emph{designated relaxation}: for each vertex $v$, a formula whose star
constraints are dropped at $v$ only; and for each pair $\{u, v\}$, a formula relaxed at both.
The two routes agree on $\Gfty$: the cardinality route (bound 1 refuted in 532.4 s, bound 2
satisfied) and the relaxation route (52 single vertices refuted, 1{,}326 pairs satisfied) give the
same value from two different formulas (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).

\begin{theorem}[\mv]\label{thm:defect52}
The Petersen defect of $\Gfty$ is exactly~2. For every one of its $52$ vertices, the map relaxed
at that vertex alone does not exist (DRAT proofs checked by \texttt{drat-trim}, each solved in 0.8
to 5.4 seconds), while every one of the $1{,}326$ vertex pairs admits a map whose star condition
fails exactly at the two relaxed vertices (explicit witnesses; the checker reports exactly those
two bad vertices; the slowest instance took $0.73$ seconds).
\end{theorem}

Thus the obstruction in the smallest known counterexample is not localized: no single vertex,
in particular none of the four vertices outside the six disjoint copies of $F$ (measured in
Section~\ref{sec:compositions}), can absorb it, whereas every pair can. The first half is not
an accident of $\Gfty$:

\begin{theorem}[\dd]\label{thm:parity}
Let $G$ be a cubic graph and $\sigma : E(G) \to E(\PG)$ any map. Then the number of vertices of
$G$ at which $\sigma$ fails the star condition is never exactly one. Hence the Petersen defect
of a cubic graph is $0$ or at least $2$, and $\Gfty$ attains the minimum possible defect of a
counterexample.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a vertex $v$ let $\chi_v \in \mathbb F_2^{E(\PG)}$ be the indicator of the multiset of the
three labels at $v$ (repeated labels cancel). Every edge of $G$ contributes its label to both
endpoints, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss}
\sum_{v \in V(G)} \chi_v = 0 .
\end{equation}
If $u$ satisfies the star condition then $\chi_u$ is the
indicator of a vertex star of $\PG$, an edge cut. Suppose $v$ is the only bad vertex; then
$\chi_v = \sum_{u \ne v} \chi_u$ is a sum of cuts, hence a cut of $\PG$, of odd weight $1$ or
$3$. The Petersen graph is bridgeless, so the weight is $3$ and the three labels at $v$ are
distinct and form a 3-edge cut $\partial(S)$. Since $\PG$ is cyclically 5-edge-connected, one
side of the cut, say $S$, induces a forest with $c \ge 1$ components, so $3 = |\partial(S)| =
3|S| - 2(|S| - c) = |S| + 2c \ge |S| + 2$ and $|S| = 1$: the cut is a star, and $v$ satisfies
the star condition after all, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}, the relaxation of one vertex of any counterexample is unsatisfiable:
a map with at most one bad vertex has none, and a counterexample has no map without bad vertices.
The single-vertex instances are therefore instances of a theorem. Two of them were nevertheless
refuted for $\Gmain$ (vertices 8 and 9, in 121 and 160 seconds), and the 52 of $\Gfty$ as stated.
The argument gives more: for any map, the sum of $\chi_v$ over the bad vertices lies in the cut
space of $\PG$. For the 112-vertex graphs the theorem already gives defect at least~2; whether it
equals~2 is decided by pair relaxations (computation EXP-006). The $120$ pairs of vertices

\section{Theory of abnormal edges: rings, frames and sublinear approximations}\label{sec:abnormal}

For a proper 5-edge-coloring $c$ of a cubic graph $G$, an edge is \emph{abnormal} if it is neither
poor nor rich, and $\ab(G)$ denotes the least number of abnormal edges over all proper
5-edge-colorings of $G$; write $\pd(G)$ for the Petersen defect. Mattiolo, Mazzuoccolo and
Mkrtchyan~\cite{MMM2021} proved that no proper 5-edge-coloring of a cubic graph has exactly one
abnormal edge and asked whether $\ab(G) \le 2$ implies that $G$ has a normal 5-edge-coloring; the
five counterexamples have colorings with exactly two abnormal edges and answer the question in the
negative~\cite{GJMMMU2026}. The two defects are related as follows.

\begin{lemma}[\dd]\label{lem:pdab}
$\pd(G) \le \ab(G)$ for every cubic graph $G$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
View $\PG$ as the Kneser graph on the 2-subsets of $\{1,\dots,5\}$: two subsets are adjacent when
disjoint, and the \emph{color} of the edge $\{A, B\}$ is the element outside $A \cup B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kneser}
\mathrm{col}(\{A,B\}) = \{1,\dots,5\} \setminus (A \cup B),
\end{equation}
so that the three edges at $A$ carry the three colors outside $A$. Let $c$ be a proper 5-edge-coloring with
set of abnormal edges $N$, and for a vertex $v$ let $A_v$ be the complement of $c(\partial(v))$.
For an edge $e = uv$, the \emph{view from $u$} is the edge of $\PG$ at $A_u$ of color $c(e)$. If
$e$ is poor then $A_u = A_v$; if $e$ is rich then $A_u$ and $A_v$ are disjoint and avoid $c(e)$; in
both cases the two views coincide. Let $\sigma(e)$ be the common view if $e$ is normal and the view
from one chosen end if $e$ is abnormal. A vertex all of whose edges carry its own view satisfies
the star condition, so every bad vertex is the end not chosen of an abnormal edge, and there are
at most $|N|$ of them.
\end{proof}

If $c$ has a single abnormal edge $uv$ and the view from $u$ is chosen, then $v$ is bad: the view
from $u$ is an edge at $A_v$ only if $A_u = A_v$ or $A_u \cap A_v = \emptyset$, that is, only if
$uv$ is poor or rich. The map $\sigma$ would then have exactly one bad vertex, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:parity} implies the proposition of~\cite{MMM2021} quoted above. In particular
$\ab(G) \ge \pd(G) \ge 2$ for every cubic graph without a Petersen coloring. All five
counterexamples have explicit proper 5-edge-colorings with exactly two abnormal
edges (computations EXP-006 and EXP-008, Table~\ref{tab:invariants}), hence
\[
\pd = \ab = 2 \quad\text{for each of } \Gmain,\ \Gsym,\ \Gfty,\ \Gftyb,\ \Gsix .
\]
For $\Gfty$ the lower bound $\ab \ge 2$ was also confirmed directly: the 42 single-edge
relaxations refuted with checked proofs meet all 14 edge orbits of its automorphism group of
order~6 (the six automorphisms are re-checked from the edge list).

\begin{theorem}[\dd]\label{thm:rings}
Let $G_1, \dots, G_t$, $t \ge 2$, be cubic graphs without a Petersen coloring, $e_i = a_ib_i$ an
edge of $G_i$, and let $C$ be obtained from the disjoint union of the graphs $G_i - e_i$ by adding
the edges $a_ib_{i+1}$, indices modulo $t$. Then every map $E(C) \to E(\PG)$ has a bad vertex in
every $V(G_i)$; hence $\ab(C) \ge \pd(C) \ge t$. If every $G_i$ is bridgeless, so is $C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose every vertex of $V(G_i)$ satisfies the star condition under $\sigma$. With the notation of
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}, for the two edges $f_1, f_2$ leaving $V(G_i)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flux}
\sum_{v \in V(G_i)} \chi_v = \mathbf 1_{\sigma(f_1)} + \mathbf 1_{\sigma(f_2)},
\end{equation}
because an edge inside $V(G_i)$ is counted twice. Each $\chi_v$ is the indicator of a star, so the sum is a cut of $\PG$ with at most two edges, hence
empty, since $\PG$ is 3-edge-connected. The two leaving edges therefore carry the same label $x$,
and giving $e_i$ the label $x$ turns the restriction of $\sigma$ into a Petersen coloring of
$G_i$, a contradiction. If $f$ is an edge of $G_i - e_i$, any two vertices of $V(G_i)$ are joined
in $G_i - f$ by a path, in which $e_i$ can be replaced by the detour around the ring; the added
edges lie on a cycle through all the blocks. So $C$ is bridgeless.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\dd]\label{thm:frames}
Let $K$ be a cubic graph with $t$ vertices and, for each vertex $x$ of $K$, let $G_x$ be a cubic
graph without a Petersen coloring and $v_x$ a vertex of $G_x$. Replace every vertex $x$ of $K$ by
$G_x - v_x$, attaching the three edges of $K$ at $x$ to the three neighbours of $v_x$. Every map
from the edges of the resulting cubic graph to $E(\PG)$ has a bad vertex in every
$V(G_x - v_x)$; hence $\ab \ge \pd \ge t$. If $K$ and all $G_x$ are 3-connected, so is the result.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If all vertices of $V(G_x - v_x)$ satisfy the star condition, the sum of their vectors $\chi_v$ is
a cut of $\PG$ equal to the sum of the indicators of the labels of the three leaving edges. It has
odd weight, since $|V(G_x)| - 1$ is odd. Weight~1 is impossible in a bridgeless graph, so the
three labels are distinct and form a 3-edge cut of $\PG$, which is a star by the argument in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}. Restoring $v_x$ with these labels on its edges gives a Petersen
coloring of $G_x$. The connectivity statement is Lemma~\ref{lem:frame3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\dd]\label{lem:frame3}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:frames}, if $K$ and every $G_x$ are 3-connected, the frame $F$ is
3-connected.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use two facts on a simple cubic graph $H$ with at least four vertices. First, if $H$ is
3-connected then it is 3-edge-connected, because the vertex connectivity never exceeds the edge
connectivity and the latter never exceeds the minimum degree~3. Second, if $H$ is 3-edge-connected
then it is 3-connected. Indeed, let $S$ be a minimum vertex cut with $|S| \le 2$ and let $A$ be a
component of $H - S$ and $B$ the union of the others. Every $s \in S$ has a neighbour in $A$ and one
in $B$ by minimality, so $s$ has at most one neighbour on one of the two sides; putting $s$ on the
side where it has two neighbours, the edge cut between the two enlarged sides has exactly one edge
per vertex of $S$ and at most two edges, which is impossible. (If $S = \{s,s'\}$ and $s,s'$ are
adjacent, each of them has one neighbour in $A$, one in $B$ and one in $S$, and no side gives it two
neighbours; then put both on the side of $A$, which leaves the edges from $s$ and $s'$ to $B$, at most
two edges again.) So it remains to show that $F$ is
3-edge-connected. $F$ is simple, because $K$ is simple and each $G_x$ is.

Write $B_x = V(G_x) \setminus \{v_x\}$, $H_x = G_x - v_x$ and $A_x = \{a^x_1,a^x_2,a^x_3\}$ for the
neighbours of $v_x$, the vertex $a^x_j$ carrying the edge of $K$ to the $j$-th neighbour $y_j$ of
$x$. Let $S$ be a nonempty proper subset of $V(F)$, write $S_x = S \cap B_x$, and call a block
\emph{full} if $S_x = B_x$, \emph{empty} if $S_x = \emptyset$ and \emph{mixed} otherwise. For a mixed
block let $i_x$ be the number of edges of $H_x$ between $S_x$ and $B_x \setminus S_x$ and
$s_x = |S_x \cap A_x|$. Since $G_x$ is 3-edge-connected and $S_x$ is a nonempty proper vertex set of
$G_x$, the edge cut of $S_x$ in $G_x$ has $i_x + s_x \ge 3$ edges; the same applied to
$B_x \setminus S_x$ gives $i_x + (3 - s_x) \ge 3$. Hence $i_x \ge \max(3 - s_x, s_x) \ge 2$ for every
mixed block.

If no block is mixed, $S$ is a union of full blocks and its edge cut in $F$ is the edge cut in $K$ of the
corresponding vertex set, which has at least 3 edges since $K$ is 3-edge-connected. If at least two
blocks are mixed, the cut has at least $2 + 2$ edges. Let exactly one block $x$ be mixed, let $T$ be the set of full blocks
and $f$ the number of neighbours $y_j$ of $x$ that are in $T$. The cut of $S$ consists of the $i_x$
edges inside $H_x$, of $d$ edges of $K$ at $x$, namely those with $a^x_j \in S$ and $y_j$ empty or
$a^x_j \notin S$ and $y_j$ full, and of $e'$ edges of $K$ between full and empty blocks not at $x$.
In all, the cut has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutcount}
|\partial_F(S)| = i_x + d + e'
\end{equation}
edges. If $T = \emptyset$ then $d = s_x$ and $e' = 0$, so the cut has $i_x + s_x \ge 3$ edges. If $T$ contains
every other block then $d = 3 - s_x$, $e' = 0$, and the cut has $i_x + 3 - s_x \ge 3$ edges. Otherwise
$T$ is a nonempty proper subset of $V(K)$ not containing $x$, so the cut of $T$ in $K$ has at least 3
edges, of which $f$ are at $x$, whence $e' \ge 3 - f$ and the cut of $S$ has at least $i_x + d + 3 - f$
edges. If $f \le 2$ this is at least $2 + 3 - 2 = 3$. If $f = 3$ then $d = 3 - s_x$ and
$i_x + d \ge \max(3 - s_x, s_x) + 3 - s_x \ge 3$ for $s_x \in \{0,1,2,3\}$, and the cut again has at least 3 edges.
So every cut of $F$ has at least 3 edges.
\end{proof}

In~\cite{MMM2021} it is conjectured that five statements are equivalent: (a) the Petersen
coloring conjecture, and the existence of a sublinear function $f$ such that
$\ab(G) \le f(|V(G)|)$ for (b) all bridgeless, (c) all 2-connected, (d) all 3-connected,
respectively (e) all cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graphs $G$. It is proved there that the second statement is equivalent to the first, and
that on the last three classes a sublinear bound exists if and only if $\ab$ is bounded by 5, 7
and 9 respectively (the proof for the last class uses Proposition~2 of~\cite{MMM2021}, which is stated
there without proof). The rings of Theorem~\ref{thm:rings} built from $\Gfty$ are 2-connected with
$\ab \ge |V|/52$, and the graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:frames} built from $\Gfty$ over 3-connected
cubic graphs $K$ are 3-connected with $\ab \ge |V|/51$, because the orders are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orders}
|V(C)| = \sum_{i=1}^{t} |V(G_i)|, \qquad |V(F)| = \sum_{x \in V(K)} \bigl(|V(G_x)| - 1\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[\dd]\label{thm:sublinear}
On the class of 2-connected cubic graphs, and on the class of 3-connected cubic graphs, there is
no sublinear function $f$ with $\ab(G) \le f(|V(G)|)$ for all $G$ in the class. More precisely, on
either class such a function exists if and only if every graph of the class has a normal
5-edge-coloring.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If every graph of the class has a normal 5-edge-coloring, the zero function is a bound. Otherwise
let $G$ be a graph of the class without one. The rings of $t$ copies of $G$ are bridgeless by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rings}, hence 2-connected (a cut vertex of a cubic graph is incident with a bridge), and
the graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:frames} over the prisms with $t$ vertices, $t \ge 6$ even, with
every $G_x = G$, are 3-connected when $G$ is (Lemma~\ref{lem:frame3}); both have $\ab \ge t$ and at
most $t\,|V(G)|$ vertices, so $\ab$ is at least linear in the order along these sequences. The graph
$\Gfty$ is 3-connected without a normal 5-edge-coloring (Theorem~\ref{thm:certify}).
\end{proof}

Thus statements (c) and (d) of~\cite{MMM2021} are false, and their conjectured equivalence now
amounts to the falsity of (e): by their Theorem~4, whose proof uses their Proposition~2 (stated without
proof), to the existence of a cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph with $\ab \ge 10$. All five retrievable counterexamples are
cyclically 4-edge-connected with $\ab = 2$. A much smaller defect would suffice.

\begin{proposition}[\dd; conditional on Proposition~2 of~\cite{MMM2021}]\label{prop:threshold}
Assume that the cyclic joins of copies of a cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph minus two edges are
cyclically 4-edge-connected (Proposition~2 of~\cite{MMM2021}, stated there without proof). If some
cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph $G^*$ has $\pd(G^*) \ge 3$, then $\pd$ and $\ab$
are unbounded on the class of cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graphs. Hence on that class either
$\pd \le 2$ everywhere, or no sublinear function bounds $\ab$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $e_1 = ab$ and $e_2 = cd$ be the end-edges of a path of length three of $G^*$, and let $M$ be
the 4-pole obtained from $G^* - e_1 - e_2$ by attaching a pendant edge at each of $a, b, c, d$.
If $M$ had a map to $E(\PG)$ satisfying the star condition at every vertex, then giving $e_1$ the
label of the pendant edge at $a$ and $e_2$ that of the pendant edge at $c$ would produce a map on
$G^*$ whose only possible bad vertices are $b$ and $d$, so $\pd(G^*) \le 2$. Hence $M$ has no such
map. Join $t$ copies of $G^* - e_1 - e_2$ cyclically as in~\cite{MMM2021}; the result is
cyclically 4-edge-connected~\cite[Proposition~2]{MMM2021}, and every map from its edges to
$E(\PG)$ has a bad vertex in every copy, since a copy without bad vertices would restrict to a map
on $M$. So its defect is at least $t$.
\end{proof}

The dichotomy is in fact an equivalence, and it identifies statement~(e) with a statement about
two bad vertices.

\begin{theorem}[\dd; conditional on Proposition~2 of~\cite{MMM2021}]\label{thm:twodefect}
Suppose that the cyclic joins of copies of a cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph minus two
edges are cyclically 4-edge-connected (Proposition~2 of~\cite{MMM2021}, whose proof is omitted
there). Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] some sublinear function $f$ satisfies $\ab(G) \le f(|V(G)|)$ for every
cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph $G$;
\item[\textup{(ii)}] $\ab(G) \le 18$ for every cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph $G$;
\item[\textup{(iii)}] $\pd(G) \le 2$ for every cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph $G$;
\item[\textup{(iv)}] for every cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph $G$ and every path $abcd$ in
$G$, the 4-pole $G - ab - cd$ has a Petersen coloring.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(iii) implies (ii). Let $\sigma$ be a map whose bad vertices lie in $\{u, v\}$, and color every
edge by the color of its image in the Kneser model of $\PG$ (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:pdab}). The
three colors at a good vertex are distinct, and an edge with two good ends is poor or rich,
because its two views coincide. Recolor the three edges at $u$: each has at most two colors
forbidden at its other end, so distinct admissible colors can be chosen greedily; then recolor the
edges at $v$ in the same way (if $uv$ is an edge, two and one admissible colors remain). The
result is a proper 5-edge-coloring in which only the edges with an end in $u$, in $v$, or in one
of their neighbors can be abnormal, at most $9 + 9$ edges. (ii) implies (i) trivially. (i)
implies (iii) by Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}. (iv) implies (iii) by the relabeling in the
proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}. (iii) implies (iv): if some $G - ab - cd$ had no
Petersen coloring, the cyclic join of three copies of it would be cyclically 4-edge-connected
with defect at least~3.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\dd; conditional on Proposition~2 of~\cite{MMM2021}]\label{cor:mmm}
Conjecture~3 of~\cite{MMM2021}, the equivalence of statements (a) to (e), holds if and only if
some cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph has Petersen defect at least~3 (under the assumption
of Theorem~\ref{thm:twodefect}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Statement (a) is false, (b) is equivalent to it by~\cite[Theorem~1]{MMM2021}, and (c) and (d)
are false by Theorem~\ref{thm:sublinear}. So the five statements are equivalent exactly when (e),
which is statement (i) of Theorem~\ref{thm:twodefect}, is false.
\end{proof}

The condition of Corollary~\ref{cor:mmm} is met: for every $t \ge 2$ there is a cyclically
4-edge-connected cubic graph of girth~5 on $102t$ vertices with Petersen defect at least
$t$~\cite{UD2026}. These graphs are rings alternating two 4-poles cut from $\Gfty$, one obtained by
deleting two independent edges and one by deleting two adjacent vertices; modulo the cut space of
$\PG$ each 4-pole transmits a single, different class between its two connectors, so no two
consecutive blocks can both be free of bad vertices. Their proof does not use the assumption of
Theorem~\ref{thm:twodefect}. Hence statement (e) is false, all five statements of~\cite{MMM2021}
are false, and the conjectured equivalence holds.

cap), and $\ab(C_3)$, $\ab(C_4)$ and $\ab(K_4[\Gfty])$ are at least the number of copies with the
next two bounds undecided; these are open. As a check of Theorem~\ref{thm:rings}, twenty
relaxations of $C_2$ at two vertices of the same copy were refuted with checked proofs (mean
128 s), and the controls $C_2$, $C_3$ and $K_4$ built from $J_5$ are Petersen colorable.

\section{Four-poles of the counterexamples}\label{sec:poles}

The cyclic joining of copies of $G - e_1 - e_2$ used in~\cite{MMM2021} for the class of cyclically
4-edge-connected cubic graphs does not force bad vertices by itself. For $\Gfty$, $\Gftyb$ and
$\Gsix$, every 4-pole obtained by deleting two independent edges has a Petersen coloring (one
representative per orbit of edge pairs: 482, 482 and 4{,}947 4-poles, computation EXP-010,
Table~\ref{tab:poles}), and the boundary labels of the colorings found are of two kinds only, as
the cut space predicts: the \emph{crossed} pattern, with two distinct labels among the four, or the
\emph{four-distinct} pattern, the four edges adjacent to one edge of $\PG$. The latter identification
is forced by the cut space: the sum of the four pendant labels is a cut of $\PG$ of weight 4; since $\PG$
is cyclically 5-edge-connected one side $S$ of that cut induces a forest with $c$ components, so
$4 = |S| + 2c$ with $|S| \ge 1$ gives $|S| = 2$ and $c = 1$, an edge, and the cut consists of the four edges
adjacent to it. The \emph{restorable}
pattern, in which the two pendant labels at the ends of each deleted edge are equal, never occurs,
and no other pattern occurs. A 4-pole with a crossed pattern can be chained around
a ring with an even number of copies, because any two edges of $\PG$ are exchanged by an
automorphism, so these cyclic joins are Petersen colorable~\dd{}.

\section{Compositions of copies of the Petersen 4-pole}\label{sec:compositions}

All known counterexamples contain the 4-pole $F$ (Figure~\ref{fig:fpole})~\cite{GJMMM2026}. A packing
computation (exhaustive enumeration of the 8-vertex sets with a 4-edge boundary that induce $F$, followed
by an exact maximum disjoint packing) shows that $\Gfty$ contains exactly six disjoint copies of $F$
and four further vertices (the vertices $1, 18, 35, 51$), and that each 112-vertex graph contains twelve
disjoint copies and sixteen further vertices. This suggests the class $\mathcal C(k, m)$ of simple cubic
bridgeless graphs made of $k$ disjoint copies of $F$ and $m$ free vertices, the $4k + 3m$ semi-edges
being joined by a perfect matching. The class is defined by the packing and not by the way the known
graphs were built: the 112-vertex graphs belong to it only through $k = 12$ and $m = 16$, and the
52-vertex graph through $k = 6$ and $m = 4$. For $\Gfty$ the count is $6 \cdot 8 + 4 = 52$ vertices and
$(24 + 12)/2 = 18$ joining edges, and $6 \cdot 10 + 18 = 78$ edges.

\begin{proposition}[\dd; \mv{} for the instances]\label{prop:pure}
Every graph in $\mathcal C(k, 0)$ has a Petersen coloring. Hence no counterexample consists
solely of copies of $F$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$F$ admits a Petersen coloring in which its four semi-edges carry the same edge $x$ of $\PG$
(the distance-zero case of Lemma~2.1 of the Jooken note, revision v2 of~\cite{GJMMMU2026}; an explicit such coloring is
recorded in the repository). Color every copy by it, with the same $x$. Every join edge then
carries $x$ at both ends, and every vertex star is a star of a copy of $F$, hence a star of
$\PG$.
\end{proof}

The counterexample-guided search (computation EXP-005: join formula for the matching; structural
check; Petersen-colorability of each candidate; sound blocking clauses learned from every
coloring found, each recorded with its witness) exhausted $\mathcal C(5,0)$ and $\mathcal C(6,0)$
(40 and 48 vertices) in 3.2 and 5.5 seconds by exactly such a universal coloring, but did not converge
on classes with free vertices: the 26-vertex control $\mathcal C(3,2)$ stopped at a 15-minute time limit
after $1{,}059$ iterations and $29{,}052$ learned clauses (reported in the archived summary of the
computation, not in its manifest), and $\mathcal C(5,2)$ (42 vertices) stopped
at a two-hour time limit after $1{,}992$ iterations and $80{,}832$ learned clauses, as did $\mathcal C(6,2)$
(50 vertices) after $1{,}498$ iterations and $75{,}286$ learned clauses, all without finding a
counterexample and without exhausting. We do not claim anything about classes the search did not
exhaust~\cj{}; a symmetry-broken enumeration is a natural next step.

\subsection{Ablation of the symmetry units and of the defect encoding}

\paragraph{Removing the symmetry units.} The three Petersen refutations without the two symmetry
units (Table~\ref{tab:certify}) have 2 fewer clauses, are 4 to 15 times slower and have proofs
about 5 to 21 times larger, and remain unsatisfiable with checked proofs, so the units change the cost
and not the answer. The comparison is the ablation of the only non-structural clauses of the encoding:
the 15 images of the first edge are interchangeable under the automorphisms of $\PG$, and fixing one of
them removes a factor that the solver would otherwise rediscover through every branch.

\paragraph{Replacing the cardinality bound by designated relaxation.} The same defect question is
posed in two forms. With a cardinality bound over all $n$ vertices (Algorithm~\ref{alg:defect}, first
line) the bound-1 instances of the three graphs of EXP-004 were undecided at the cap of 1{,}800 s on
two graphs and cost 532.4 s on $\Gfty$. With a relaxation at one designated vertex or pair the
instances have the same variables and 495 or 990 fewer clauses than the unrelaxed formula, and all
1{,}326 pairs of $\Gfty$ were decided in at most 0.73 s. Fixing which vertices carry the defect, instead of counting it, is what
makes the sweeps of Table~\ref{tab:pairs} possible, and the parity theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}) is what makes the single-vertex half of the sweep unnecessary.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Three observations organize the results. The first concerns what the closed Petersen route does not
close. On all five counterexamples every consequence of the conjecture tested here still holds: a
Berge--Fulkerson cover, perfect matching index 4, a Fan--Raspaud triple, a 5-cycle double cover and a
nowhere-zero 5-flow. The graphs are in this sense better behaved than the Petersen graph, whose
perfect matching index is 5. The two classical distances from 3-edge-colorability disagree about which
graphs are far: oddness 4 and resistance 3 on the 112-vertex graphs, and 2 and 2 on the three smaller
ones, while the normal chromatic index (6), the Petersen defect (2) and the abnormal-edge number (2)
are the same on all five. The structure that makes the graphs counterexamples is therefore invisible to
oddness and resistance and visible to the defect.

The second observation concerns the defect. The parity theorem explains why the defect of a
counterexample is at least 2, since one bad vertex would carry a charge with nothing to cancel it, and
it explains that the single-vertex sweeps cannot succeed. It does not explain the stronger fact that
every one of the 17{,}362 pairs is critical. The distribution of the cut-space classes
(Table~\ref{tab:classes}) shows that the pair witnesses put a charge of the class of a single edge of the
Petersen graph on the two bad vertices in 96.5 percent of the cases of $\Gfty$ and in between 90.6
percent ($\Gsix$) and 96.5 percent on the five graphs, with the rarer classes $D_2$, $D_3$ and $T_2$,
and one witness of class $T_1$ on $\Gftyb$, filling the rest; the
pattern is measured, not derived.

The third observation concerns sampling. Fifteen cyclically 4-edge-connected counterexamples, the five
public ones and ten dot products, all have every adjacent pair critical, and for three of them every 4-pole obtained by deleting
two independent edges is colorable. A reader who extrapolated from them would conclude that
statement~(e) is true. The rings of~\cite{UD2026} show that it is false: they alternate two kinds of
4-poles cut from the same graph, and the property that fails is a property of the pair of blocks, not of
either block. Evidence from the known counterexamples therefore says little about the class, which is the
reason this paper separates its finite results from its theorems.

On method, the cost is dominated by the normal-5 refutations, whose proofs reach \MaxProofGB{} GB and
whose checks reach 4{,}492.7 s (Table~\ref{tab:certify}), while the Petersen encoding with two symmetry
units refutes the same graphs 18.5 to 190 times faster and is the practical route for any further search
for counterexamples. The designated relaxation turns a defect question that the cardinality encoding
could not decide on the 112-vertex graphs into thousands of instances of a few seconds each.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

The results are finite statements about five graphs. Universal 2-criticality (Theorem~\ref{thm:defect})
holds for these graphs and for the ten dot products of Section~\ref{sec:poles}, and nothing in the
parity argument explains it~\cj{}. The perfect matching covers, flows and oddness values are values of
these graphs, not statements about the Berge--Fulkerson, cycle double cover or 5-flow conjectures in
general.

Several computations are not decisive and are used only as stated. The cardinality encodings of the
defect reached the cap on the 112-vertex graphs, so those bounds come from the parity theorem; the
normal-5 defect bound 1 was undecided on the first three graphs and its value follows from the
parity-type proposition of~\cite{MMM2021} together with the two-abnormal-edge witnesses; the
abnormal-edge numbers of the rings and the frame are bounded below only; the 4-pole sweep of $\Gsym$
is 23 percent complete and that of $\Gmain$ was not run; 403 of the 949 distance sets of $\Gfty$ are
undecided; and the counterexample-guided search of Section~\ref{sec:compositions} did not converge on
the classes with free vertices. The edge-orbit sweeps planned for $\Gftyb$ and $\Gsix$ were not
completed (five instances reached their 900 s limit), so for these graphs, as for the 112-vertex
graphs, the lower bound $\ab \ge 2$ rests on the parity theorem and Lemma~\ref{lem:pdab} and not on a
sweep. Every time quoted is the wall-clock time of a single run, several of which shared the machine with
other computations; the spread over instances is shown in Table~\ref{tab:pairs}, and no instance was
timed repeatedly.

Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}, Theorem~\ref{thm:twodefect} and Corollary~\ref{cor:mmm} are conditional on Proposition~2
of~\cite{MMM2021}, whose proof is omitted there. The statement that the conjectured equivalence
holds rests on the unconditional result of~\cite{UD2026}, not on that proposition. The cited
background results (Jaeger's two papers, the generation of snarks, Hagglund and Steffen, the
statements of the 2026 papers) are taken from the cited sources and are not
re-derived.

The trust base is the two tools: a bug common to the encoders and the checkers would not be caught by
the positive controls alone, which is why the 112-vertex refutations were also checked against
Putman's independent formulas (Section~\ref{sec:validation}); \texttt{drat-trim} is the only proof
checker used and no second checker was run. Finally, the formulas and DRAT proofs are not part of a
public evidence record: they are regenerated by the commands of Section~\ref{sec:data}, the
formulas are identified by the digests of Table~\ref{tab:digests}, and a reader checks a
refutation by re-running its command and comparing the digests.

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

Nothing here bears on the general Berge--Fulkerson, cycle double cover, 5-flow or normal
6-edge-coloring conjectures: the computations concern five graphs. The questions left open by the
disproof, the order of a smallest counterexample in $[40, 52]$ (Problem~9 of~\cite{GJMMMU2026}),
cyclically 5-edge-connected counterexamples (Problem~11), and the normal 6-edge-coloring
conjecture (Conjecture~12), remain open. The question raised by Section~\ref{sec:abnormal}, whether
some cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph has Petersen defect at least~3, is answered
in~\cite{UD2026}. The same work shows that every ring of claws and of Petersen superedges is
Petersen colorable, which includes an infinite family of cyclically 5-edge-connected snarks of
girth~5; whether the defect is bounded on cyclically 5-edge-connected cubic graphs is open.

The two 52-vertex counterexamples are colorable only by themselves~\cite{COBI2026}, conditionally on
Observation~10 of~\cite{GJMMMU2026}. Is every pair of vertices critical in every counterexample of
minimum order, as it is in the five graphs of Section~\ref{sec:certify}~\cj{}? What are the exact values
$\ab(C_2)$, $\ab(C_3)$, $\ab(C_4)$ and $\ab(K_4[\Gfty])$, given $2 \le \ab(C_2) \le 4$ and the lower
bounds of Table~\ref{tab:rings}? Does every counterexample contain the 4-pole $F$, and does a
counterexample exist in a class $\mathcal C(k,m)$ with $m > 0$ below 52 vertices? The 403 undecided
distance sets and the unrun sweeps of the 4-poles of $\Gmain$ and of the rest of $\Gsym$ are finite
computations that would complete Section~\ref{sec:poles}. Finally, the abstract question behind the
class $\mathcal C(k,m)$ is whether the oddness-2 graphs $\Gfty$, $\Gftyb$, $\Gsix$ and the
oddness-4 graphs $\Gmain$, $\Gsym$ belong to different families of counterexamples, which the
present data neither suggest nor exclude.

The cut-space classes of Table~\ref{tab:classes} pose a question of their own: why the class $E$ of a
single edge carries between 90.6 and 96.5 percent of the pair witnesses, and whether the class $T_1$,
which occurs once among the 1{,}326 witnesses of $\Gftyb$ and nowhere else, is an exception or a
rare member of a regular distribution~\cj{}.

Two further questions come from the 4-pole computations. A single adjacent pair of vertices that is
not critical in some counterexample would give a 4-pole without a Petersen coloring; none exists among
the fifteen graphs examined, and whether one exists in another counterexample is not known. And the
least order of a cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph of Petersen defect at least 3 is not known:
the graphs of~\cite{UD2026} have $102t$ vertices and defect at least $t$, which gives 306 vertices for
$t = 3$, and the rings $C_t$ of Section~\ref{sec:abnormal} reach defect 3 on 156 vertices but are only
2-connected. A smaller cyclically 4-edge-connected graph of defect 3 would also sharpen the
threshold of Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

The five retrievable counterexamples to the Petersen coloring conjecture, of orders 52, 52, 68, 112 and 112,
have been audited with encodings that share no variable scheme with the published ones. The results
are of three kinds. Machine-verified, with checked proofs or re-verified witnesses: none of the five
graphs has a Petersen coloring or a normal 5-edge-coloring; each has a Berge--Fulkerson cover, perfect
matching index exactly 4, a Fan--Raspaud triple, a 5-cycle double cover and a nowhere-zero 5-flow but no
nowhere-zero 4-flow; the oddness and resistance are 4 and 3 on the 112-vertex graphs and 2 and 2 on the
others; the normal chromatic index is exactly 6; and the Petersen defect is exactly 2, with all 17{,}362
vertex pairs critical. Derived, with proofs written here: no edge map into the Petersen graph has
exactly one bad vertex; the Petersen defect is at most the least number of abnormal edges; rings and
frames assembled from counterexamples have one bad vertex in every block, so no sublinear function
bounds the abnormal edges of 2-connected or 3-connected cubic graphs; and statement~(e) of a conjecture
of Mattiolo, Mazzuoccolo and Mkrtchyan is equivalent to a defect bound of 2, conditionally on one
proposition of that paper.

The finite computations also give measurements that no theorem predicts. On the smallest instances the
lower bounds of the ring and frame theorems are attained, with defects 2, 3 and 4 on rings of two, three
and four copies of $\Gfty$ and defect 4 on the frame over $K_4$. The 4-poles obtained from three of the
graphs by deleting two independent edges are all colorable, and so are the 4-poles obtained from fifteen
graphs by deleting two adjacent vertices; these examples do not represent the class of cyclically
4-edge-connected cubic graphs, as the rings of~\cite{UD2026} show. No counterexample is made only of
copies of the Petersen 4-pole, and the search for counterexamples with free vertices below 52 vertices
did not converge.

The paper decides nothing about the Berge--Fulkerson, cycle double cover, 5-flow or normal
6-edge-coloring conjectures in general, and nothing about cyclically 5-edge-connected counterexamples.
Its open computations are listed in Section~\ref{sec:open}, with the abnormal-edge numbers of the rings
and the frame first among them. Every table of the paper is regenerated from the archived manifests by a
script that also asserts the encoding sizes, so that a later computation extends the tables without
retyping them, and each proof is reproduced by re-running the command given for it.
