\begin{abstract}
Multiscale layer constructions produce finite-time blowup for several fluid equations: for the
incompressible porous media equation, for the two-dimensional Boussinesq system, for
three-dimensional Euler, and, with fractional dissipation $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ of order
$\alpha<(22-8\sqrt7)/9$, for the forced Navier-Stokes equations. The exponent in the last of these is
small, and it is natural to ask what limits it. Exponents in this abstract are in the $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$
convention, in which classical viscosity is $\alpha=2$.

We give two upper bounds on the dissipation exponent such constructions can carry. The first is
independent of every design constant: a cascade whose layers grow at rate $\sqrt A$ on a background
gradient $A$ cannot exceed $\alpha=1/2$, and one whose layers grow at rate $A$ cannot exceed
$\alpha=1$. Neither mechanism reaches classical viscosity, by factors of four and two respectively. The second
bound applies to the smooth-forcing designs used for the inviscid Boussinesq and Euler results:
both are of the pendulum class, and transcribing the exponent content of their force estimates, with
the equation and page of every estimate, we show that they certify dissipation only up to
$\alpha\le5.3\times10^{-4}$ (Boussinesq) and
$\alpha\le6\times10^{-8}$ (Euler) at the published choices, and only up to
$\alpha\le9.0\times10^{-3}$ and $\alpha\le7.5\times10^{-3}$ when every constant of the design is
retuned in its favour, at least ten times below the exponent already achieved with a force of
finite regularity. In every reading the ceiling is highest when the
fewest derivatives of the force are controlled.

Both bounds follow from the amplitude system of the construction together with one additional
ingredient, the effect of fractional dissipation on a single layer, which we derive and then test
against direct simulation of the nonlinear equations, including the effect of the spatial
localization the constructions use. The simulations meet most of their criteria and miss three, which
are reported with their numbers: the local growth rate in a two-scale background has a slope $1.36$
times the prediction; the handoff from a steered layer to the next reaches a correlation of $0.808$
against a criterion of $0.9$, and $0.509$ at the largest scale separation; and the run at the first
chosen parameters grows $726$-fold and is refuted by the instability of its own background. We also
reconstruct the exponent budget behind the published hypodissipative threshold, identify the
constraint that determines it, and show that the admissible frequency ratios form an interval that
closes exactly at that threshold, so that cascades with geometrically growing frequencies are
excluded at every positive dissipation.

The machine-checked status of the two announced results is reported at the level a kernel replay
establishes: the statements and the integrity of the formal proof, not that the formal proof follows
the written argument, which Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen report it does not. The mechanism is due to
C\'ordoba and Mart\'inez-Zoroa, extended to smooth forcing by Alp\"oge and Buckmaster; nothing here
establishes blowup for any equation or improves any published threshold.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}\label{sec:question}

Let $\theta$ and $u$ solve the forced two-dimensional Boussinesq system, or let $u$ solve the forced
three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations with fractional dissipation. Multiscale constructions in the
style introduced by C\'ordoba and Mart\'inez-Zoroa build blowup by stacking oscillatory layers: each
layer grows on the gradient deposited by its predecessors and hands a larger gradient to its
successor, with infinitely many stages compressed into finite time.

Dissipation opposes this directly. A layer of frequency $\lambda$ is damped at rate
$\nu\lambda^{2\alpha}$ in the convention $-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$, while its growth rate is set by the
background gradient alone. Frequency therefore buys gradient but not growth, and the question of how
much dissipation a cascade tolerates becomes a question about the exponents relating amplitude,
frequency and gradient. This paper answers it at two levels: for the mechanism classes
(Section~\ref{sec:class}) and for two published designs (Section~\ref{sec:design}).

\subsection{Related work: the claims examined, their hypotheses and conclusions}\label{sec:related}

The paper sits between a body of blowup constructions and the formal and numerical checks that can be
run on them without refereeing them. This subsection states, for each work the paper depends on, the
hypotheses and the conclusion as its abstract or main theorem gives them; the page or theorem is cited,
and a work known only through another source is marked \ev{U}.

\paragraph{The problem and the classical results.} The question is the Millennium problem in the form of
Fefferman~\cite{fefferman}. The OpenAI announcement states that its Theorem~1.1 establishes alternative~(C),
a forced solution on $\mathbb R^{3}$ that starts from rest, keeps its kinetic energy bounded and has
unbounded velocity at a finite time, and that compact support of the force gives alternative~(D) on
$\mathbb T^{3}$ through its Corollary~10.6~\cite{openai-ns} \ev{V} (page~1). The classical results around the
question, quoted from the introduction of that paper (page~2), are the global finite-energy weak solutions of
Leray~\cite{leray}, the zero one-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure of the singular set of a suitable weak
solution of Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg~\cite{ckn}, and the regularity under a bounded scale-invariant
$L^{\infty}_{t}L^{3}_{x}$ norm of Escauriaza, Seregin and \v{S}ver\'ak~\cite{ess} \ev{U}. For the
hyperdissipative equation, Lions~\cite{lions} proved global regularity at order $5/2$ and above in the
$|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ convention, as Section~1 of~\cite{cmz} states \ev{U}; with classical viscosity at $2$,
the constructions reach $0.0927$ and regularity is proved from $5/2$, and the exponents in between are open.

\paragraph{Averaged and dyadic models.} Tao~\cite{tao} replaces the nonlinearity of the Navier-Stokes
equation by an averaged bilinear operator, built from rotations, dilations and Fourier multipliers of order
zero, that keeps the cancellation $\langle\widetilde B(u,u),u\rangle=0$ and therefore the energy identity, and
constructs a smooth solution of the averaged equation that blows up in finite time \ev{V} (abstract, page~1).
The conclusion drawn there is that any proof of global regularity has to use finer structure of the
nonlinearity than harmonic analysis estimates and the energy identity provide, and the paper proposes a
program for adapting the construction to the true equations. Blowup in dyadic models was shown earlier for
the Euler case by Katz and Pavlovi\'c~\cite{kp} and for the Navier-Stokes case by Cheskidov~\cite{cheskidov},
both listed in Section~1 of~\cite{cmz} \ev{U}. None of these is a statement about the equation itself, which
is why they bound the methods that can succeed and do not bound the answer.

\paragraph{Non-uniqueness.} Buckmaster and Vicol~\cite{bv} used convex integration to prove non-uniqueness
among finite-energy weak solutions \ev{U} (introduction of~\cite{openai-ns}, page~2). Albritton, Bru\'e and
Colombo~\cite{abc} work with the forced equations in three dimensions, zero initial velocity and a body force
in $L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{x}$; they exhibit two distinct Leray solutions with that datum and force, built from a
background solution that is unstable in similarity variables, whose profile is a vortex ring modified from
Vishik's two-dimensional unstable vortex, and a second solution on its unstable manifold \ev{V} (abstract,
page~1); the force is singular at the initial time \ev{V} (introduction of~\cite{openai-ns}, page~2).
Hou, Wang and Yang~\cite{hwy} prove, with a computer-assisted proof, that the unforced equation has
infinitely many suitable Leray-Hopf solutions from one compactly supported initial velocity that is smooth
away from the origin and in $L^{q}$ for every $q<3$ \ev{V} (Theorem~1, page~1). These results concern the
uniqueness of weak solutions; none produces a smooth solution that loses smoothness, and they are context
for the claims examined here, not part of them.

\paragraph{Euler and Boussinesq blowup from regular data.} The local theory of the Euler equations
with H\"older continuous gradient is classical, and Elgindi~\cite{elgindi} shows that these solutions can
develop singularities in finite time, for $C^{1,\alpha}$ velocities and not for smooth data \ev{V}
(abstract, page~1). Smooth solutions continue past a time $T$ whenever
$\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{curl}\,u\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt$ is finite, by the criterion of Beale, Kato and
Majda~\cite{bkm}, as the OpenAI Euler paper recalls on its page~1 \ev{V}; breakdown therefore requires that
integral to diverge, and the Alp\"oge-Buckmaster Euler theorem states that it does. Chen and Hou~\cite{chenhou}
prove stable, nearly self-similar finite-time blowup of the two-dimensional Boussinesq and the
three-dimensional axisymmetric Euler equations from smooth data of finite energy on a domain with boundary,
with weighted $L^{\infty}$ and $C^{1/2}$ estimates and a finite-rank decomposition of the linearized
operator \ev{V} (abstract, page~1). Wang and co-authors~\cite{dm} compute new unstable self-similar
solutions of the porous media equation and of the three-dimensional Euler equations with boundary, with
machine-learning architectures and a high-precision Gauss-Newton optimizer, and report an empirical
asymptotic formula relating the blowup rate to the order of instability; their stated motivation is that
stable singularities are not expected in the boundary-free Euler and Navier-Stokes problems, where unstable
ones are hypothesized to matter \ev{V} (abstract, page~1). These works follow regular or self-similar blowup; the layer-cascade line below is a different route, and
the two do not share a mechanism.

\paragraph{The layer-cascade program.} C\'ordoba and Mart\'inez-Zoroa~\cite{cmzi} prove that, for the
two-dimensional porous media equation, a compactly supported smooth source in $L^{\infty}_{t}C^{\infty}_{x}$
and smooth compactly supported data give a classical solution on $[0,1)$ whose $C^{1}$ norm diverges at $t=1$
\ev{V} (Theorem and Remark~1, page~1); Remark~1 states that the time regularity was chosen for simplicity,
that $C^{1}_{t}C^{\infty}_{x}$ follows with extra work and that $C^{\infty}_{t}C^{\infty}_{x}$ should be
reachable. For the two-dimensional Boussinesq system, C\'ordoba, La\'in-Sanclemente and
Mart\'inez-Zoroa~\cite{clsmz} obtain blowup with a force that is uniformly
$C^{1,\sqrt{4/3}-1-\epsilon}$ and $L^{2}$, through an accumulated hysteresis effect of an infinite chain of
degenerate pendula \ev{V} (abstract). For the hypodissipative Navier-Stokes equations, C\'ordoba,
Mart\'inez-Zoroa and Zheng~\cite{cmz} prove that for every dissipation order below
$\alpha_{0}=(22-8\sqrt7)/9$ in the $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ convention there are classical finite-energy solutions
on $\mathbb R^{3}\times[0,T]$, with a force in $L^{1}_{t}C^{1,\epsilon}_{x}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}$,
that blow up \ev{V} (abstract and Theorem~1). C\'ordoba, Dom\'inguez, Lucas-Manch\'on and
Mart\'inez-Zoroa~\cite{cdlm} extend the mechanism to the forced generalized surface quasigeostrophic equation
in the singular velocity regime: a smooth compactly supported datum and a force in $L^{1}([0,1];H^{s})$ for an
interval of exponents $s$ give a classical finite-energy solution on $[0,1)$ that loses Sobolev regularity at
$t=1$ \ev{V} (abstract, page~1). The hypotheses of all four are a regularity class for the force, and the
conclusion in each is a loss of regularity at a finite time with the force still in its class; the
dissipation order is the only parameter among them that this paper varies.

\paragraph{The claims of September 2026.} Alp\"oge and Buckmaster~\cite{ab-boussinesq} construct blowup for
the inviscid Boussinesq system on $\mathbb R^{2}$ with forces in $C^{\infty}(\mathbb R^{2}\times[0,T])$ in both
equations and supported in one fixed spatial ball, from a smooth compactly supported temperature and zero
velocity; the temperature stays bounded while its gradient norm tends to infinity and the vorticity norm has
infinite limit superior, both in $L^{\infty}$ \ev{V} (abstract, page~1). Their Euler paper~\cite{ab-euler}
treats three dimensions with an axisymmetric force and initial velocity supported in one fixed solid torus
and smooth in space and time through the blowup time: the circulation and the meridional velocity stay
bounded, the $L^{\infty}$ norms of the circulation gradient and of the vorticity tend to infinity, the time
integral of the vorticity norm diverges, and on every closed interval before blowup the solution is smooth
and unique in a finite-energy, locally Lipschitz class \ev{V} (abstract, page~1). Alp\"oge, Buckmaster and
Coiculescu~\cite{ab-ipm} (arXiv:2609.16470) prove the corresponding statement for the porous media equation
on $\mathbb T^{2}$: a smooth odd density, a smooth odd force $F\in C^{\infty}([0,1]\times\mathbb T^{2})$ and a
classical solution on $[0,1)$ whose density and velocity gradients diverge in $L^{\infty}$ while the density
converges in $C^{\eta}$ for every $\eta<1$ \ev{V} (abstract, page~1; the arXiv listing states 57 pages and a
Lean formalized proof). The OpenAI Navier-Stokes manuscript~\cite{openai-ns} states, for every $\nu>0$, a
smooth force of compact support in space and time and smooth fields supported in a compact set $K$, with the
velocity starting from rest, bounded in $L^{2}$ and unbounded in $L^{\infty}$ as $t\uparrow1$ \ev{V} (Theorem~1.1,
page~1). Its mechanism is a self-similar vortex whose radial width shrinks faster than its axial length, with
oscillatory pulses whose nonlinear momentum fluxes cancel the singular part of the momentum residual
\ev{V} (Section~2, pages~3--4), and the introduction states that its amplified disturbances play a different
role from those of the cascade line (pages~2--3). The OpenAI Euler manuscript~\cite{openai-euler} is for the
unforced equation: smooth compactly supported divergence-free data, $0<T<\infty$, and both
$\limsup\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}=\infty$ and $\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{curl}\,u\|_{L^{\infty}}dt=\infty$
\ev{V} (Theorem~1.1, page~1). Buckmaster's public statement~\cite{abstat} records that the three
Alp\"oge-Buckmaster results were released together, that the program is due to C\'ordoba and
Mart\'inez-Zoroa, that large language models were used, and that a hypodissipative Navier-Stokes result was
believed but not released while its Lean verification was unfinished \ev{V} (pages~1--2). The independent
analysis of the Lean development by Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen~\cite{bch} is summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:status}.

\paragraph{Where this paper stands.} Every construction above is either a forced blowup, with the force in a
stated regularity class, or an unforced blowup or non-uniqueness result whose proof is not a layer cascade.
No source states a ceiling on the dissipation a layer cascade can carry \ev{U}; the nearest quantitative
value is the threshold of~\cite{cmz}, and the paper's contribution is the bounds of Sections~\ref{sec:class}
and~\ref{sec:design}, the reconstruction of that threshold in Section~\ref{sec:budget}, and the record of how
the dissipative extension behaves in the equations in Section~\ref{sec:validation}.

\subsection{Conventions and evidence marks}

\paragraph{Conventions.} Two conventions for the dissipation exponent occur. Ours writes the
dissipation as $-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$, so classical viscosity is $\alpha=1$. The sources of the
hypodissipative constructions write it as $\nu|\nabla|^{\alpha}$, so classical viscosity is
$\alpha=2$, and every exponent is twice ours: $\alpha_{|\nabla|^{\alpha}}=2\,\alpha_{\mathrm{ours}}$.
The hypodissipative threshold $(22-8\sqrt7)/9=0.09267$ of~\cite{cmz} is stated in the
$|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ convention; in ours it is $0.04633$, half of it. Every exponent in this paper
carries its convention. Sections~\ref{sec:mechanism} to~\ref{sec:design} and~\ref{sec:method}
to~\ref{sec:status} use ours unless a number is tagged ``($|\nabla|^{\alpha}$)''; Section~\ref{sec:budget}
transcribes the budget of the source and uses $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ throughout; the abstract uses
$|\nabla|^{\alpha}$.

\paragraph{Evidence marks.} Each claim carries one of five marks. \ev{V}: read on a primary source, with
the equation or page cited; the read depth of each source is recorded in the repository index of
references, and a source read only in part is not marked \ev{V} for the parts not read. \ev{D}: derived here
from stated hypotheses, with the derivation given or a calculation that can be repeated by hand.
\ev{MV}: checked by machine, meaning a kernel replay of a formal development, an exact symbolic
computation, or a numerical run whose recorded values are pinned by a repository test; a numerical run
supports a statement and does not prove it, and the text says which kind of check is meant.
\ev{U}: unverified, stated but not checked on a primary source or by machine. \ev{C}: conjecture, an
interpretation or an expectation that the paper does not establish. Marks follow the statement they
qualify; a theorem is marked by the kind of argument that proves it.

\section{The layer mechanism and its dissipative extension}\label{sec:mechanism}

On a background that is affine near the origin, $u_{\mathrm{old}}=D(t)x$ and
$\theta_{\mathrm{old}}=G(t)\cdot x$, a temperature wave and a vorticity wave sharing one phase
$s=\lambda\zeta(t)\cdot x$, with $\vartheta=\Theta F(s)$, $\varpi=\Omega F'(s)$ and
$\psi=\Omega P(s)/(\lambda^{2}|\zeta|^{2})$, satisfy a closed system of amplitude equations,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mod}
\dot\zeta=-D^{T}\zeta,\qquad
\dot\Theta=-\frac{J\zeta\cdot G}{\lambda|\zeta|^{2}}\,\Omega,\qquad
\dot\Omega=\lambda\,\zeta_{1}\,\Theta ,
\end{equation}
where $J(x_{1},x_{2})=(-x_{2},x_{1})$ \ev{V}: Lemma~3.1 and equation~(3.2) of~\cite{ab-boussinesq},
page~8, for a general odd periodic profile $F$ and its even mean-zero primitive $P$, with the wave
written as a sine and a cosine in Subsection~1.2, pages~3--4. The wave velocity is perpendicular to
$\zeta$ while both wave gradients are parallel to it, so the wave does not advect itself and the ansatz
is exact where the envelope is one and the profile is linear. In the frozen configuration $D=0$,
$G=-Ae_{2}$, $\zeta=re(\varphi)$, the growth rate is $\sqrt A\sin\varphi$: it carries no $\lambda$,
because the frequency in $\dot\Omega$ cancels the reciprocal frequency in $\dot\Theta$ \ev{V}
(page~4). The gradient the layer deposits is governed by the amplification identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:amp}
\nabla\vartheta(0,t)=\lambda\Theta\zeta ,
\end{equation}
which is why a short wave can carry a small amplitude and a large gradient, and is what makes the
cascade possible \ev{V} (page~4, with $F(s)=s$ near the origin, equation~(4.1), page~37).

\paragraph{Dissipation.} Adding $-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$ to both equations leaves the two geometric
cancellations untouched, because the operator commutes with them. On a single Fourier mode it is
diagonal on the phase, exactly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode}
(-\Delta)^{\alpha}e^{is}=(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}e^{is},\qquad s=\lambda\zeta\cdot x ,
\end{equation}
\ev{D}. For a general periodic profile $F=\sum_{n}\hat F_{n}e^{ins}$ the identity holds for the
fundamental, $n=\pm1$, and the $n$-th harmonic is damped at the larger rate
$\nu\,n^{2\alpha}(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}$ \ev{D}. We therefore apply the dissipation to the
fundamental harmonic of the profile, for which the amplitude system acquires one damping term per
amplitude,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diss}
\dot\Theta=-\frac{J\zeta\cdot G}{\lambda|\zeta|^{2}}\,\Omega-\nu(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}\Theta,
\qquad
\dot\Omega=\lambda\zeta_{1}\Theta-\nu(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}\Omega ,
\end{equation}
\ev{D}, with eigenvalues $-\nu(\lambda r)^{2\alpha}\pm\sqrt A\sin\varphi$ in the frozen configuration.
Equation~\eqref{eq:diss} is exact for a single mode and is the equation tested in
Section~\ref{sec:validation}, where the wave is a single mode. Growth at frequency $\lambda$ on
background gradient $A$ therefore requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
\nu(\lambda r)^{2\alpha}<\sqrt A\,\sin\varphi .
\end{equation}
For a profile with several harmonics we use~\eqref{eq:growth} as a necessary condition: the
harmonics sit at higher frequency and are damped more, which can only make growth harder. That the
fundamental carries the growth of a multi-harmonic layer, whose nonlinear self-interaction the
amplitude system does not control, is not proved here \ev{C}. Equation~\eqref{eq:diss} is the one
ingredient of this paper that does not appear in the constructions themselves;
Section~\ref{sec:validation} reports its test against direct simulation of the nonlinear equations
\ev{MV}.

\paragraph{Two growth laws.} The Boussinesq layers above grow at $\sqrt A$, a pendulum law: the
amplitude pair obeys a second-order equation whose coefficient is the background gradient \ev{V}
(the growth scale of the next layer satisfies $\sigma_{q+1}^{2}=|G_{<q+1}(t^{b}_{q})|$, item~(iv) of the
stage protocol, page~10). The vortex layers of the hypodissipative Navier-Stokes construction instead grow at rate
$A_{n-1}$, the background gradient itself, which is why its dissipation constraint reads $a=\alpha R$
in the exponent notation of Section~\ref{sec:budget} rather than carrying a square root \ev{V}
(Subsections~1.2.3 and~1.2.4 of~\cite{cmz}, page~5: the stretching of layer $n$ by the outer layers
is of magnitude $A_{n-1}$, equation~(9), and must beat the dissipation of the layer; saturated, this is
constraint \textbf{D}). The swirl layers of the axisymmetric
Euler construction obey the pendulum law, with the circulation gradient in place of the temperature
gradient (Section~\ref{sec:euler}) \ev{V}. Both laws are treated below.

\section{A bound independent of design constants}\label{sec:class}

\begin{theorem}[\ev{D}; numerical support \ev{MV} from the cascade model of Section~\ref{sec:validation}]\label{thm:class}
Consider a layer cascade with the following structure:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A0)] the background gradients are unbounded, $A_{q}\to\infty$, as the divergence of the
gradient at blowup requires;
\item[(A1)] each layer stops at amplitude $\lambda_{q}^{-1+\delta}$ with $\delta<1$;
\item[(A2)] it deposits a background gradient $A_{q+1}=\lambda_{q}^{\delta}$, by~\eqref{eq:amp};
\item[(A3)] frequencies and background gradients are related by $\lambda_{q}=A_{q}^{p}$ for a fixed $p$;
\item[(A4)] layers grow at rate $A^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma=1/2$ or $\gamma=1$;
\item[(A5)] a layer of frequency $\lambda$ is damped at $\nu\lambda^{2\alpha}$, as in~\eqref{eq:diss}.
\end{enumerate}
Then $p\delta>1$, and growth at every stage requires
\[
\alpha\le\frac{\gamma}{2p}<\frac{\gamma\,\delta}{2}<\frac{\gamma}{2},
\]
that is $\alpha<1/4$ for $\gamma=1/2$ and $\alpha<1/2$ for $\gamma=1$. In the $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$
convention these ceilings are $1/2$ and $1$, against a classical viscosity of $2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By (A2) and (A3), $A_{q+1}=\lambda_{q}^{\delta}=A_{q}^{p\delta}$. If $p\delta\le1$ then
$A_{q+1}\le A_{q}$ whenever $A_{q}>1$, and the gradients would stay bounded, against (A0); hence
$p\delta>1$. With $\delta<1$ from (A1), which is required because the field itself stays bounded
while its gradient diverges, this gives $p>1/\delta>1$.

By (A5) and (A4), stage $q$ grows only while $\nu\lambda_{q}^{2\alpha}<c\,A_{q}^{\gamma}$ for a
constant $c$ independent of $q$. Taking logarithms and using (A3),
\[
\log\nu+2\alpha p\log A_{q}<\log c+\gamma\log A_{q},
\]
and since $A_{q}\to\infty$ by (A0) the inequality can hold for all large $q$ only if
$2\alpha p\le\gamma$, that is $\alpha\le\gamma/(2p)$. At equality the condition reduces to $\nu<c$,
which a small viscosity satisfies, so this step is not strict; strictness of the final bounds comes
from $p>1/\delta$, which gives $\alpha\le\gamma/(2p)<\gamma\delta/2<\gamma/2$.
\end{proof}

Hypothesis (A0) is the divergence of the gradient that the blowup statements assert, in the
unbounded-gradient form of~\cite{ab-boussinesq}, Theorem~1.1, equation~(1.5), page~3 \ev{V}.
Hypothesis (A3) takes $p$ and the constant $c$ of the proof independent of the stage, as in the
simplified model of Section~\ref{sec:validation}; a stage-dependent exponent is treated next.

\begin{remark}[stage-dependent frequency exponent, \ev{D}]\label{rem:pq}
Let $\lambda_{q}=A_{q}^{p_{q}}$ with $p_{q}$ depending on the stage and the other hypotheses
unchanged. Growth at stage $q$ requires $(2\alpha p_{q}-\gamma)\log A_{q}<\log(c/\nu)$. If
$\limsup_{q}p_{q}>\gamma/(2\alpha)$, then $2\alpha p_{q}-\gamma$ is bounded below by a positive
constant along a subsequence while $\log A_{q}\to\infty$, and the inequality fails. Hence the
conclusion becomes $\alpha\le\gamma/(2\limsup_{q}p_{q})$, and when $p_{q}\to\infty$ no positive
exponent is admissible at every stage. This is the situation of the axisymmetric Euler design, whose
frequency ratios $Q_{i}$ are unbounded; Theorem~\ref{thm:euler}(i) states it in the variables of that
design.
\end{remark}

In the variables of Section~\ref{sec:design}, where frequencies satisfy
$\lambda_{q}=\lambda_{q-1}^{Q}$, the exponents are related by $Q=p\delta$, and the bound
$\alpha\le\gamma/(2p)$ reads $\alpha\le\gamma\delta/(2Q)$, which for $\gamma=1/2$ is the constraint
$\alpha\le m/(4Q)$ used there; the margin $\delta$ of (A1) is written $m$ in that section, where
$\delta$ denotes the per-level parameter of the correction hierarchy.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:classical}
No cascade of either type reaches classical viscosity. The pendulum mechanism falls short by a factor
of four and the vortex-stretching mechanism by a factor of two, uniformly in every constant of the
design \ev{D}.
\end{corollary}

The two ceilings differ by exactly the growth law: a layer that grows at the background gradient,
rather than at its square root, gains a factor of two and nothing else in the argument changes. The
published constructions sit well below their own class ceilings, the hypodissipative vortex-layer
threshold by a factor $10.8$ and the Boussinesq design of Section~\ref{sec:boussinesq}, at its
published choices, by a factor $945$ \ev{MV}. To our knowledge no source states this class ceiling
\ev{U}; the nearest quantitative value in the literature is the threshold of~\cite{cmz}.

\begin{remark}
Corollary~\ref{cor:classical} is consistent with a choice visible in the literature: the construction
aimed at the viscous case does not use a layer cascade, but a self-similar core whose radial Reynolds
number stays of order one while the angular one grows without bound \ev{V} (Section~2 of~\cite{openai-ns},
page~4).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Boussinesq design}\label{sec:boussinesq}

The smooth-forcing Boussinesq construction~\cite{ab-boussinesq} controls the force of each layer in
$C^{k}_{x,t}$ for $k\le d=k_{q}$ through a finite hierarchy of corrections, and its estimates are
powers of the layer frequency $\lambda=\lambda_{q}$. The inputs are: the amplitude $Y\le C\lambda^{-(1-m)}$, with margin $m=1/8$
(Definition~7.1(iii), equation~(7.6), page~58); the frequency ratio
$\lambda_{q}=\lambda_{q-1}^{Q}$ and the coefficient scale $\Pi\le\lambda^{5/Q}$ (equations~(3.7), page~9,
and~(7.4), page~58); the per-level small parameter $\delta=\Pi^{5}Y$ (Subsection~7.1.1, page~59, and
page~69); $J=2d+8$ correction levels, so that the uncancelled remainder begins at grade $J+1$
((3.8), page~9, and page~57); a cost of $\lambda\Pi^{2}$ per physical derivative of an oscillatory
profile and of $\Pi^{2}$ per derivative of a phase-independent one ((7.19), page~62); and coefficient
derivatives through order $9d+41$, which are available uniformly only through order $Q-1$ because the
sums over future stages converge only in that range ((7.15), page~61, Theorem~5.5, page~49, and
Lemma~5.4, page~48) \ev{V}. The force of one layer at order $k\le d$ then consists of the following
contributions (page~64).

Each must be at most $\lambda^{-\tau}$ for some $\tau>0$ for the force series to converge. The
construction takes $\tau=3/2$, which absorbs its constants into a logarithm ((7.26), page~65),
together with the sufficient rules $Q\ge201$ and $120d\le Q$ ((7.4), page~58) \ev{V}.

\begin{theorem}[\ev{D} from transcribed estimates \ev{V}; arithmetic \ev{MV}]\label{thm:design}
Write $\pi=5/Q$. The estimates behind Table~\ref{tab:contrib} hold uniformly only if $Q\ge9d+42$, and its bounds
are at most $\lambda^{-\tau}$ for all large $\lambda$ only if
\[
m\le\frac{d+9-2d\pi-5\pi(2d+9)-\tau}{2d+10},\qquad
m\le1-\frac{(2d+3)\pi+\tau}{2},\qquad
m\le\frac{4-\tau-(2d+18)\pi}{5},
\]
and carrying dissipation $-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$ requires in addition $\alpha\le m/(4Q)$.
Consequently, in our convention:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] at the published choices $\tau=3/2$, $Q\ge\max(201,120d)$, the exponent satisfies
$\alpha\le2.65\times10^{-4}$, attained at $d=0$, $Q=201$, where the leading phase means bind;
\item[(ii)] with every free choice released, $\tau\to0$ and only $Q\ge9d+42$, it satisfies
$\alpha\le2.22\times10^{-3}$, attained at $d=0$, $Q=45$;
\item[(iii)] even with the coefficient scale reduced to the previous wavelength, $\Pi\le\lambda^{1/Q}$,
and the per-level parameter to $\Pi^{3}Y$, the least the correction estimates tolerate, it satisfies
$\alpha\le4.48\times10^{-3}$, attained at $d=0$, $Q=42$.
\end{enumerate}
In each case the exponent is largest when the fewest derivatives are controlled, and it is at least
ten times below the threshold $0.04633$ already reached with a force of finite regularity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taking the exponent of $\lambda$ in each row and requiring it to be at most $-\tau$ gives one linear
inequality in $m$ per row; every exponent increases with $k$, so $k=d$ is the binding order. The
remainder row gives the first display, the leading means the second, the vorticity higher means the
third; the scalar higher means and the requirement that each level gain, $m<1-25/Q$, are weaker in
every case considered. The activation row is met by the choice of seed and constrains nothing
else. The condition $Q\ge9d+42$ is the requirement that order $9d+41$ lie within the range $Q-1$. The
dissipation condition is~\eqref{eq:growth} with $A_{q}=\lambda_{q-1}^{m}$ and an insertion angle of
order one, the most favourable case, which is Theorem~\ref{thm:class} with $p=Q/m$ and $\gamma=1/2$.
The three consequences follow by maximizing $m/(4Q)$ over $d\ge0$ and integer $Q$ above each floor.
As a check on the transcription, the published margin $m=1/8$ satisfies all three displayed
inequalities at the published choices for every $d$, and at $\tau=3/2$ the second inequality is the
construction's own condition $\Pi^{2d+3}\le\lambda^{1/4}$ ((7.25), page~64). The inequalities of
case (iii) are displayed below and their arithmetic is reproduced.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Euler design}\label{sec:euler}

In the axisymmetric Euler construction~\cite{ab-euler}, written in the volume coordinates
$y=(z,r^{2}/2)$, the circulation $\Gamma=ru^{\varphi}$ takes the place of the temperature. The
principal amplitudes of a layer of frequency $N$ obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler-amp}
\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}T\\ \widehat\Omega\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&-d/(\sigma\kappa)\\ \sigma c\,\zeta_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}T\\ \widehat\Omega\end{pmatrix},
\qquad d=(J\zeta)\cdot\nabla_{y}\Gamma_{<},\qquad c=2W\Gamma_{<},
\end{equation}
equation~(3.4) of~\cite{ab-euler}, page~7, with $c$ bounded above and below on the support of the layer
by~(2.12), page~5 \ev{V}. The growth rate is the square root of the product of the off-diagonal
entries and scales as $|\nabla\Gamma|^{1/2}$: the Euler layers belong to the pendulum class of
Theorem~\ref{thm:class}, with $\gamma=1/2$ in the variable $\nabla\Gamma$ whose norm diverges, which
is the blowup variable of equation~(1.3), page~2 \ev{D}. On the constructed sequence the growth scale is
$\sigma_{i}\asymp N_{i}^{\beta/2}$ against a deposited circulation gradient of order $N_{i}^{\beta}$,
in agreement with this law (page~97, where these are prescribed scalar intervals) \ev{V}.

The force estimates of this construction are stated with explicit exponents in Section~12. Write
$\beta$ for the amplitude margin (amplitude $N^{-(1-\beta)}$), $h=1-\beta$, $N_{i}=N_{i-1}^{Q}$,
$\Pi\le N^{v}$ with $v=p/Q$ for the coefficient scale, $\delta=\Pi^{\mathsf d}N^{-h}$ for the
per-level parameter, $h_{*}=h-\mathsf dv$, $J=2k+8$ correction levels and $u\ge kv$ (equations~(12.1)
to~(12.3), page~95, and page~104). The activation, remainder and mean contributions to the force of
one layer at order $k$ then carry the exponents
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler-exp}
1-e+2u+v,\qquad -(2h_{*}-1)k+\beta-9h_{*}+2u+v,\qquad -2h+3u+6v
\end{equation}
of $N$, where $e$ is the exponent of the seed (equation~(12.15), page~104) \ev{V}. The estimates
further require the following (Appendix~\ref{app:force}, Table~\ref{tab:euler-quot}):
thirteen coefficient quotients of the form $\Pi^{A-B\mathsf d}N^{-n\beta}$ to be at most one,
equation~(12.13), page~102; the localization exponent $a$ of $\ell=N_{i-1}^{-a}$ to satisfy
$a>5\beta/2$, the second row of~(12.7), page~98, where the positive number $c=a+h-1-3\beta/2=a-5\beta/2$
is defined on page~95, with $p\ge a$ because $\Pi$ dominates $\ell^{-1}$; the centre condition
$2h_{*}>\beta/2$ and the row $2h_{*}>v$ of~(12.7); and coefficient derivatives through the orders the
correction recursion consumes, which are available uniformly only when $9k+51\le Q$ (page~101) \ev{V}.
The construction chooses $\beta=1/8$, $\mathsf d=16$, $p=8$, $a=4$ and $Q_{i}=q_{0}+i$ with
$q_{0}\ge2^{20}$ (equations~(12.1) and~(12.2), page~95); with these choices the three exponents are
$-7.00$, $-10.75$ and $-1.75$, within its targets $-6$, $-6$ and $-3/2$ (page~104), and every side
condition holds \ev{MV}.

\begin{theorem}[\ev{D} from transcribed estimates \ev{V}; arithmetic \ev{MV}]\label{thm:euler}
Under the Euler design, carrying dissipation $-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$ at an insertion angle of order
one requires $\alpha\le\beta/(4Q)$. Consequently, in our convention:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] at the published choices $\alpha\le2.98\times10^{-8}$ at the first stage, and no positive
exponent is admissible at every stage, since $Q_{i}$ is unbounded;
\item[(ii)] with every free choice released, that is, force exponents only required to be negative,
$\mathsf d$ at the least value the coefficient quotients permit, the localization exponent $a$ at the
larger of $1$ and the limit $5\beta/2$ of its row, and $Q\ge9k+51$, the exponent
satisfies $\alpha\le3.64\times10^{-3}$, attained at $k=0$, $Q=51$, $\beta=0.742$, where the centre
condition binds;
\item[(iii)] with the localization at the previous wavelength, $a=1$, and the rows of~(12.7) that
involve $a$ not imposed, it satisfies $\alpha\le3.77\times10^{-3}$, attained at $k=0$, $Q=51$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Growth at frequency $N_{i}$ on the circulation gradient deposited by the previous layer requires
$\nu N_{i}^{2\alpha}<\sigma_{i-1}\sin s_{i}$ with $\sigma_{i-1}\asymp N_{i-1}^{\beta/2}$ and
$N_{i}=N_{i-1}^{Q}$, which is $\alpha<\beta/(4Q)$; this is Theorem~\ref{thm:class} with $p=Q/\beta$
and $\gamma=1/2$, and, with Remark~\ref{rem:pq}, no positive exponent survives $Q_{i}\to\infty$. The first
consequence is the bound at $Q=2^{20}+1$ and $\beta=1/8$. For the
others, each listed condition is an inequality in $\beta$ at fixed $k$ and $Q$; $\mathsf d$ is taken
as the least value for which every coefficient quotient is at most one, which is $\mathsf d=2$ in the
maximizing range because the quotients carrying no power of $N$ are $\Pi^{2-\mathsf d}$; and
$\beta/(4Q)$ is maximized over $k\ge0$ and integer $Q\ge9k+51$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The inequalities of cases (ii) and (iii).} With $a$, $p=a$, $v=p/Q$, $u=kv$ and $\mathsf d$
as above, $h=1-\beta$ and $h_{*}=h-\mathsf dv$, the conditions imposed are
\begin{gather}
-(2h_{*}-1)k+\beta-9h_{*}+2u+v<0,\qquad -2h+3u+6v<0,\qquad h_{*}>0,\label{eq:eu1}\\
2h_{*}>\beta/2,\qquad 2h_{*}>v,\qquad (A-B\mathsf d)v-n\beta\le0\ \text{for the thirteen rows},\qquad 9k+51\le Q,\label{eq:eu2}
\end{gather}
and, in (ii) only, the rows of~(12.7) that involve $a$, namely $a-2\beta$, $a-5\beta/2$, $a-3\beta/2$ and
$a-\beta/2$, positive, with $a=\max(1,5\beta/2)$. At the optimum of (ii), $k=0$, $Q=51$, $\beta=0.742$,
$a=1.855$, $v=0.0364$, $\mathsf d=2$ and $h_{*}=0.1855$, so that $2h_{*}-\beta/2=0$ to the precision
of the search and the row $a-5\beta/2$ is zero by the choice of $a$; every other row of~(12.7) is
positive. At the optimum of (iii), $\beta=0.7685$ and $a=1$, the rows $a-2\beta$, $a-5\beta/2$ and
$a-3\beta/2$ are negative and are not imposed, which is what makes (iii) a bound on retuning and not a
design; $2h_{*}-\beta/2=3\times10^{-4}$ binds. The values $3.64\times10^{-3}$ and $3.77\times10^{-3}$,
and $3.12\times10^{-3}$ at $k=1$ below, were reproduced from~\eqref{eq:eu1} and~\eqref{eq:eu2} with
the thirteen rows of Table~\ref{tab:euler-quot}, independently of the repository code \ev{MV}.

In the $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ convention the ceilings of (ii) and (iii) are $7.27\times10^{-3}$ and
$7.53\times10^{-3}$, below the proved threshold $0.0927$ ($|\nabla|^{\alpha}$)
by factors of $12.7$ and $12.3$; with one controlled derivative of the force, $k=1$, (ii) gives
$3.12\times10^{-3}$ in our convention, $6.25\times10^{-3}$ in the $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ convention. The two smooth-forcing designs
therefore behave alike: both are of the pendulum class, both tie the frequency ratio to the
regularity of the force through the range of their coefficient bounds, $Q\ge9d+42$ and $Q\ge9k+51$,
and both certify at most a few thousandths, more than ten times below the rough-force threshold. For
a Navier-Stokes version of the Euler design the damping of a localized swirl layer is taken at its
leading symbol $\nu(N|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}$; the test of Section~\ref{sec:validation} concerns
the Boussinesq system, and the lower-order geometric terms of the swirl case were not measured \ev{U}.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:levers}
A dissipative version of either design must therefore change one of three things: the range of
uniform coefficient bounds, which is what forces $Q$ to grow with $d$; the treatment of the
nonoscillatory phase means, which no number of correction levels reduces; or the growth law, since
the factor four in $\alpha\le m/(4Q)$ comes from the rate $\sqrt A$ and becomes a factor two for
vortex stretching. The bounds concern what the estimates of each design can certify, not the
existence of any solution: a different correction scheme is not covered, and Theorem~\ref{thm:class}
is the only statement here independent of the designs \ev{D}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[\ev{D}; discriminant identity \ev{MV}]\label{prop:threshold}
With \textbf{D} and \textbf{S} saturated, \textbf{O} reduces to $4s<2+3\alpha-7\alpha R-2/R$, whose
maximum over the frequency ratio is at $\alpha R^{2}=2/7$. At that optimum
$s(\alpha)=\bigl(2+3\alpha-2\sqrt{14\alpha}\bigr)/4$, and $s>0$ is equivalent to
$9\alpha^{2}-44\alpha+4>0$, whose smaller root is $(22-8\sqrt7)/9$.
\end{proposition}

The optimizing ratio $R=\sqrt{2/(7\alpha)}$ is the one the construction uses, and no coefficient in
the chain is fitted. At the threshold the localization constraint is inactive, with margin
$\tfrac12(\sqrt{2\alpha/7}-\alpha)=0.0350$ ($|\nabla|^{\alpha}$ convention, $\alpha=0.09267$);
a budget that saturates \textbf{L} instead and omits
\textbf{O} yields $\alpha R^{2}=1/3$ and the weaker bound $5-2\sqrt6=0.10102$ ($|\nabla|^{\alpha}$), the value given by an
order-of-magnitude estimate in the source (Subsection~1.2.4). The difference between the two is precisely which
constraint is active.

\begin{proposition}[\ev{D}; discriminant identity and the case $R=1$ \ev{MV}]\label{prop:ratios}
A positive force margin requires $7\alpha R^{2}-(2+3\alpha)R+2<0$, so the admissible ratios form the
interval with endpoints
$R_{\pm}=\bigl[(2+3\alpha)\pm\sqrt{9\alpha^{2}-44\alpha+4}\bigr]/(14\alpha)$. The discriminant is the
polynomial of Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}, so the interval closes to a single point exactly at the
threshold, and that point is $\sqrt{2/(7\alpha)}$. Moreover $R_{-}>1$ for every $\alpha>0$, and at
$R=1$ the margin equals $-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[\ev{D}]
Cascades with geometrically growing frequencies, the case $R=1$, are inadmissible at every positive
dissipation: localization requires $b\ge a+1+s$ while self-interaction permits only $b\le1-a-s$,
forcing $\alpha+s\le0$. Super-geometric frequency growth is therefore necessary, and more of it is
necessary as the dissipation rises, until at the threshold a single ratio remains.
\end{corollary}

Subsection~1.2.2 of~\cite{cmz} (page~5) states in words that the exponential separation of
frequencies used for the inviscid construction produces no meaningful growth in the presence of
diffusion of any order \ev{V}; we read this as the case $R=1$ of the corollary \ev{C}. Propositions~\ref{prop:threshold}
and~\ref{prop:ratios} are verified in exact arithmetic by computer
algebra in the accompanying repository (\fp{tests/test_navier_stokes_threshold.py}), and
numerically in \fp{nslib/cmz_budget.py} with its tests, in continuous integration \ev{MV}.

\section{Numerical methods and reproducibility}\label{sec:method}

Every simulation in Section~\ref{sec:validation} and every number it quotes was produced by the code in
\fp{problems/analysis-pde/navier-stokes/code/} of the repository, from the settings stored in the
\fp{args} block of each result file.

\paragraph{Equations and discretization.} The nonlinear control is the two-dimensional Boussinesq system
on the periodic box $[0,2\pi)^{2}$,
\[
\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\theta,\qquad
\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\partial_{1}\theta-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\omega,\qquad
u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi,\ \Delta\psi=\omega ,
\]
with $\nabla^{\perp}=J\nabla$, in our convention (module \fp{nslib/boussinesq.py}). Space is
discretized by a Fourier pseudospectral method on $N\times N$ points with the two-thirds dealiasing
rule applied to every product and after every step, so that a wave above $N/3$ is annihilated;
the runners refuse such configurations, and for the two-layer runs they also refuse a configuration in
which the demodulation window, and not only the wave, reaches past $N/3$.

\paragraph{Time integration and precision.} Time is advanced by fourth-order Runge-Kutta in the
integrating-factor form: the linear dissipative part $-\nu|k|^{2\alpha}$ is solved exactly in Fourier
space and the Runge-Kutta stages act on the nonlinear terms only, so the scheme is stable when
$\nu|k|^{2\alpha}$ is large at the grid scale. The step $\Delta t$ is fixed per run
(Table~\ref{tab:runs}). Arithmetic is IEEE double precision (\fp{float64}) on an
NVIDIA RTX~4070 Laptop GPU with 8~GB of memory, through PyTorch; the PyTorch version is not recorded in
the result files \ev{U}.

\paragraph{Steering.} The control that returns a grown layer to rest is applied in the co-rotating frame
(module \fp{nslib/corotating.py}): in two dimensions the common rotation of the background is
equivalent, on the torus, to a rotating gravity direction $g(t)$, because the Coriolis and
centrifugal accelerations are gradients absorbed by the pressure and the Euler acceleration shifts only
the uniform mean vorticity. The prescribed stratification is held by a low-frequency force that cancels
its buoyancy torque. The control law is the transcription of Subsections~3.2, 3.3 and~3.6.2
of~\cite{ab-boussinesq} (module \fp{nslib/steering.py}), integrated in the reduced model by
fourth-order Runge-Kutta at $\Delta t=2\times10^{-4}$. The background of the repaired runs is
$\theta=-(A/\lambda_{0})\bigl(\sin\lambda_{0}x_{2}-\tfrac18\sin2\lambda_{0}x_{2}\bigr)$, whose gradient is
constant to fourth order at the origin, at three quarters of $A$; the other background is
$\theta=-(A/\lambda_{0})\sin\lambda_{0}x_{2}$. Both are functions of $x_{2}$ alone and hence exact steady
states of the unforced system with vertical gravity.

\paragraph{Local growth rate.} The local growth rate of a layer is read by demodulation: the spectrum is
shifted so that the layer's wavevector lies at the origin, low-passed at a cutoff radius, and the
modulus of the result is the slowly varying amplitude. At every point the logarithm of the modulus is
fitted by least squares in time over about $30$ samples (the stride is $\lfloor n/30\rfloor$ steps, which stores $34$ in the frozen run of Algorithm~\ref{alg:rate}), after a settling interval that is evolved and
discarded so that the transient of a wave injected off its growing eigenmode does not inflate the
rate. The fitted rate field is compared with $\sigma(x)=\sqrt{\max(0,-\zeta_{1}\,J\zeta\cdot G)}$
evaluated on the low-pass filtered gradient (the total two-scale gradient) and, as the control, on the
base stratification alone; the comparison is restricted to the points where both predictions exceed a
fraction (the mask fraction) of their maximum, and reports the correlation, the regression slope of
the measured on the predicted rate, and the root-mean-square difference.

\paragraph{Cascade model.} The cascade bookkeeping of Theorem~\ref{thm:class} is evaluated in
logarithmic variables, because the quantities are exponentially large or small
(module \fp{nslib/cascade.py}): the stage gradients are $A_{q}=A_{0}e^{gq}$, the frequencies
$\lambda_{q}=c_{\lambda}A_{q}^{p}$ and the amplitudes $\Theta_{q}=A_{q+1}/\lambda_{q}$, with four
constraints (summable amplitudes, positive growth, finite total time, survival of the deposited gradient
under the damping of the holds). The critical exponent is found by bisection on $\alpha$.

\paragraph{The procedures as algorithms.} Five procedures carry every number of Sections~\ref{sec:design}
and~\ref{sec:validation}. Each is written below from the code that runs it, with its parameters as they

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{One integrating-factor Runge-Kutta step of the Boussinesq system (\fp{Boussinesq2D.step}).}\label{alg:ifrk4}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Fourier coefficients $\hat\theta,\hat\omega$ on an $N\times N$ grid, step $\Delta t$, $\nu$, $\alpha$
\State $M\gets\mathbf 1[\,|k_{1}|\le N/3,\ |k_{2}|\le N/3\,]$;\ \ $D\gets\nu|k|^{2\alpha}$;\ \ $E_{1}\gets e^{-D\Delta t/2}$, $E_{2}\gets E_{1}^{2}$
\Function{Nonlinear}{$\hat\theta,\hat\omega$}
  \State $\hat\psi\gets-\hat\omega/|k|^{2}$ (mean mode set to $0$);\ \ $u\gets\mathcal F^{-1}[\,(-ik_{2}\hat\psi,\ ik_{1}\hat\psi)\,]$
  \State $\hat a\gets-M\,\mathcal F[\,u\cdot\mathcal F^{-1}(ik\hat\theta)\,]$;\ \ $\hat b\gets-M\,\mathcal F[\,u\cdot\mathcal F^{-1}(ik\hat\omega)\,]+ik_{1}\hat\theta$
  \State \Return $(\hat a,\hat b)$
\EndFunction
\State $(a_{1},b_{1})\gets$ \Call{Nonlinear}{$\hat\theta,\hat\omega$}
\State $(a_{2},b_{2})\gets$ \Call{Nonlinear}{$E_{1}(\hat\theta+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}a_{1}),\ E_{1}(\hat\omega+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}b_{1})$}
\State $(a_{3},b_{3})\gets$ \Call{Nonlinear}{$E_{1}\hat\theta+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}a_{2},\ E_{1}\hat\omega+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}b_{2}$}
\State $(a_{4},b_{4})\gets$ \Call{Nonlinear}{$E_{2}\hat\theta+\Delta t\,E_{1}a_{3},\ E_{2}\hat\omega+\Delta t\,E_{1}b_{3}$}
\State $\hat\theta'\gets M\bigl[E_{2}\hat\theta+\tfrac{\Delta t}{6}(E_{2}a_{1}+2E_{1}(a_{2}+a_{3})+a_{4})\bigr]$;\ \ $\hat\omega'$ likewise from $b_{i}$
\Ensure $(\hat\theta',\hat\omega')$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ifrk4} is the solver of every simulation of Section~\ref{sec:validation}. The linear
dissipative part never enters a Runge-Kutta stage; it is applied through the exact factors $E_{1}$ and
$E_{2}$, so the step is stable for any $\nu|k|^{2\alpha}\Delta t$, and the two-thirds mask $M$ is applied to
every product and to the result. One evaluation of \textsc{Nonlinear} costs eight two-dimensional fast
Fourier transforms (two for the velocity and three for each of the two advected fields), so a step costs
$32$ transforms of $N^{2}$ points, $O(N^{2}\log N)$ per step and $O(N^{2})$ memory; the
$N=2048$ run of EXP-005 part D, with $\Delta t=2\times10^{-3}$, took $1304.6$~s, that is $22$ minutes
(Table~\ref{tab:runs}). The step is fourth order in $\Delta t$ for the nonlinear terms, and the
step-halving test of EXP-005 shifts the steering gain by $8.7\times10^{-4}$ on the flattened background, the
number gated at $10^{-3}$ \ev{MV}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Local growth-rate field of a wave and its comparison with the two predictions (EXP-004, EXP-005).}\label{alg:rate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require state $(\hat\theta,\hat\omega)$, wavevector $k$ of the layer, step $\Delta t$, settling steps $s$, measuring steps $n$, samples $S=30$, demodulation radius $r$, gradient cutoff $\rho$, mask fraction $f$
\For{$j=1,\dots,s$} \State advance one step (Algorithm~\ref{alg:ifrk4}) \EndFor \Comment{discarded: the settling interval}
\For{$i=0,\dots,n$}
  \If{$i\bmod\lceil n/S\rceil=0$} \State store $t_{m}\gets i\Delta t$ and $y_{m}(x)\gets\log\bigl|\mathcal F^{-1}[\,\mathrm{roll}(\hat\theta,-k)\cdot\mathbf 1(|\kappa|\le r)\,]\bigr|$ \EndIf
  \State advance one step
\EndFor
\State $\sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}(x)\gets\sum_{m}(t_{m}-\bar t)\bigl(y_{m}(x)-\bar y(x)\bigr)\big/\sum_{m}(t_{m}-\bar t)^{2}$
\State $G\gets\nabla(\theta\text{ low-passed at radius }\rho)$;\ \ $\sigma_{\mathrm{pred}}(x)\gets\sqrt{\max(0,\,-\zeta_{1}\,J\zeta\cdot G)}$; $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}$ likewise with the base stratification
\State mask $\gets\{x:\sigma_{\mathrm{pred}},\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}>f\max\}$
\State \Return correlation, regression slope $\mathrm{cov}(\sigma_{\mathrm{meas}},\sigma_{\mathrm{pred}})/\mathrm{var}(\sigma_{\mathrm{pred}})$ and RMS difference of $\sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}$ against each prediction on the mask
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:rate} is the measurement behind Figure~\ref{fig:handoff}. In the frozen run of EXP-004
the settling interval is $400$ steps, the measuring interval $200$ steps at $\Delta t=4\times10^{-4}$, the
demodulation radius $r=1.5\lambda_{1b}$, the gradient cutoff $\rho=55.4$ and the mask fraction $0.35$, which keeps $302{,}080$ points
(\fp{result-frozen.json}); the cost is the $600$ steps of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ifrk4} plus one transform per stored sample, so the time
stepping dominates. The stride is $\lfloor n/S\rfloor=6$ steps, which stores $34$ samples and not $30$. The slope here is the regression of the measured on the
predicted rate, which is why a prediction that is correct in pattern and biased in scale gives correlation
near one and slope away from one, as the frozen run does. Both predictions use the same mask, so the control
is compared with the prediction on identical points \ev{D}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Test of the plane-wave damping law on a localized wave (EXP-007).}\label{alg:loc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require grid $N=4096$, wavevector $k=(0,\lambda)$, envelope radius $\ell$, exponent $\alpha$, mask level $0.5$
\State $\mathrm{env}(x)\gets\exp\bigl(1-1/(1-u)\bigr)$ with $u=\min(1,|x-x_{c}|^{2}/\ell^{2})$, and $0$ for $u=1$;\ \ $\mathrm{field}\gets\mathrm{env}\,\sin(k\cdot x)$
\If{$\lambda+3/\ell>N/3$} \State \Return refuse: past the two-thirds limit \EndIf
\State $\mathrm{exact}\gets\mathcal F^{-1}[\,|\kappa|^{2\alpha}\mathcal F(\mathrm{field})\,]$;\ \ $\mathrm{plane}\gets|k|^{2\alpha}\,\mathrm{field}$
\State core $\gets\{x:\mathrm{env}>0.5\max\mathrm{env}\}$
\State \Return $\max_{\mathrm{core}}|\mathrm{exact}-\mathrm{plane}|\ /\ \max_{\mathrm{core}}|\mathrm{plane}|$ and $\ell\lambda$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:loc} needs two transforms per configuration and no time stepping. The scan runs twelve
configurations ($\lambda=64,128,256,512$ with $\ell=0.4,0.8,1.6$), five exponents at $\lambda=256$ and
$\ell=0.8$, and one control, and the slope of $\log$ error against $\log(\ell\lambda)$ is fitted by least squares
over the twelve. The core restriction is what keeps the error from being a ratio of two numbers that are both
zero in the tails, and the refusal in line~2 is the guard against a wave whose envelope reaches the dealiasing
limit \ev{D}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Critical exponent of the cascade model by bisection (EXP-008; EXP-003 evaluates the same constraints on a GPU batch).}\label{alg:bisect}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require growth law $\gamma$, frequency exponent $p$, $\nu$, $g$, stages $Q$, tolerance, $60$ iterations
\State $\mathrm{closes}(\alpha)$: in logarithms, $\log A_{q}=gq$, $\log\lambda_{q}=p\log A_{q}$, $\log\Theta_{q}=\log A_{q+1}-\log\lambda_{q}$; true iff (C1) $p>1$, (C2) $\gamma\log A_{q}>\log\nu+2\alpha\log\lambda_{q}$ for every $q<Q$, at $\phi=\pi/2$, (C3) the total stage time is finite, and (C4) $\log(\nu\lambda_{q}^{2\alpha}R_{q})\le6.9$ at $25$ equally spaced stages
\State $\mathrm{lo}\gets10^{-8}$, $\mathrm{hi}\gets2$; \If{not closes(lo)} \Return \textsc{nan} \EndIf
\For{$60$ iterations} \State $\mathrm{mid}\gets(\mathrm{lo}+\mathrm{hi})/2$;\ \ \textbf{if} closes(mid) \textbf{then} lo $\gets$ mid \textbf{else} hi $\gets$ mid \EndFor
\State \Return lo, to be compared with $\bigl[\gamma/2+\log(1/\nu)/(2g(Q-1))\bigr]/p$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:bisect} is evaluated in log space because $A_{q}$ and $\lambda_{q}$ overflow in a few dozen
stages. One evaluation of $\mathrm{closes}$ costs $O(Q)$ operations for the first three constraints and
$O(Q)$ for each of the $25$ survival checks, so the bisection is $60\times O(25\,Q)$, about $3\times10^{5}$
elementary operations at $Q=200$ with the standard library alone, which is why EXP-008 records no wall time.
The reference value in the last line is the finite-horizon correction: a run of $Q$ stages cannot see a
schedule that stalls after stage $Q-1$ \ev{D}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Largest certified exponent of the Boussinesq design by exhaustive search (\fp{nslib/ab_force_budget.py}).}\label{alg:design}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require architecture $(\tau,c_{\Pi},e)$, derivative orders $d=0,\dots,d_{\max}$, search span $4000$
\For{$d=0,\dots,d_{\max}$}
  \State $Q_{\min}\gets9d+42$ (and $\max(201,120d)$ at the published choices)
  \For{$Q=Q_{\min},\dots,Q_{\min}+3999$}
    \State $\pi\gets c_{\Pi}/Q$;\ \ $m(Q)\gets\min$ of the five bounds of~\eqref{eq:rowsA}, \eqref{eq:rowsB} and the level gain $1-e\pi$
    \State $\alpha(Q)\gets\max(0,m(Q))/(4Q)$
  \EndFor
  \State record $\arg\max_{Q}\alpha(Q)$, the binding row and $\alpha$
\EndFor
\State \Return the table of $(d,Q,m,\text{binding row},\alpha)$ and its maximum over $d$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:design} is the computation behind Table~\ref{tab:boussinesq}, Figure~\ref{fig:design} and

optimizer is needed; the search is $(d_{\max}+1)\times4000$ evaluations of five closed-form expressions, $84{,}000$ at

\section{Verification of the dissipative extension}\label{sec:validation}

Theorems~\ref{thm:class}, \ref{thm:design} and~\ref{thm:euler} rest on~\eqref{eq:diss}, which is derived rather than
taken from the constructions. It was tested against direct pseudospectral simulation of the nonlinear
two-dimensional Boussinesq system (Section~\ref{sec:method}). Table~\ref{tab:gates} lists every
criterion of every run, the ones met and the ones not met, with the numbers and what follows from each;
the paragraphs after it give the details that do not fit in a row. All numbers are read from the
result files (\ev{MV}).

\subsection{Experimental design: the question, the prediction and the criterion of each run}\label{sec:expdesign}

Every experiment fixes its hypothesis and its pass criteria before the run, in the \fp{hypothesis.md} of its
folder or in the docstring of its runner, and the criteria below are quoted from those texts \ev{V}. The runs
are single deterministic evaluations of the equations or of the model, so no confidence interval is attached
to a measured value, and the uncertainty a number carries is the resolution stated with it (a relative error
against a prediction, a step-halving shift, a spread across frequencies) \ev{D}. The rerun harness reproduced
every numeric leaf of the stored results of EXP-002, EXP-003 and EXP-004 on 2026-09-17, after the refactors of
the library \ev{V}. The one sampled ensemble, the $10^{6}$ schedules of EXP-003, draws its parameters from a
generator seeded with $20260912$ in the runner, so the draw is repeatable.

\paragraph{EXP-002, one layer on a stratified background.} The question is whether the amplitude
system~\eqref{eq:mod}, with the damping term of~\eqref{eq:diss}, predicts the nonlinear equations for a single
layer. The background is $\theta=-(A/\lambda_{0})\sin\lambda_{0}x_{2}$ with zero vorticity, an exact steady
state of the unforced system, so any motion is the wave. Three predictions are committed. The local rate is
$\sqrt{A\cos\lambda_{0}x_{2}}\,\sin\varphi-\nu|k|^{2\alpha}$, checked where $\cos\lambda_{0}x_{2}\ge0.9$, with a
pass criterion of $2\%$ relative error; where the cosine is negative the wave must oscillate, and the criterion
is that the stable-band envelope never exceeds twice its initial value (the committed text adds that the
unstable band grows by orders of magnitude, whereas the horizon of $0.6$ time units gives the factor $3.32$ of
Table~\ref{tab:gates}, which meets the criterion on its stable-band bound); and the peak rate must not depend on $\lambda$, with a spread of at most $2\%$. Three corrupted
models, dropping $\zeta_{1}$, dropping the inverse norm and flipping the sign, must fail the rate criterion, and
a failure of the rate or band prediction would void every statement built on the reduction. The outcomes are in
Table~\ref{tab:gates}; the first attempt of the corrupted-model control was run at $\varphi=\pi/2$, where
$\zeta_{1}=1$ and the control cannot discriminate, and was rerun at $\varphi=\pi/4$.

\paragraph{EXP-003, the cascade model against the published threshold.} The question is whether the
bookkeeping of Section~\ref{sec:method} predicts $\alpha_{c}=1/(4p)$ and the published threshold. Three
predictions. First, the batched evaluation reproduces $\alpha_{c}(p)$ over $p\in[1.5,12]$ once the finite-horizon
bias is applied, to within $1\%$, and the measured value decreases toward $1/(4p)$ as $Q$ doubles. Second, growth
positivity (C2) and survival under the damping of the holds (C4) give the same exponent: the stage at which
C4 first binds is not earlier than the stage at which C2 does, which would have lowered the threshold had it
failed, since the first-pass estimate omitted both the time budget and the hold damping and was expected to
be pushed down by them. Third, inverting the relation at the published threshold gives $p=11/4+\sqrt7$
exactly, a consistency statement and not a derivation. The ensemble draws $p\in[1.5,12]$, $\alpha\in[0.002,0.302]$,
$g\in[0.5,2]$ and $\nu\in[10^{-14},10^{-4}]$.

\paragraph{EXP-004, the handoff on a frozen background.} The premise of the cascade is that the growth rate of
a layer is set by the total low-frequency gradient, including what earlier layers deposited, and not by the
base stratification. Prediction H1: the local rate of the new layer, measured by demodulation, follows
$\sigma(x)$ computed from the low-passed total gradient. Control H2: the same prediction from the base
stratification alone must fit worse; if it fits equally well the experiment says nothing about the handoff.
The criterion is correlation above $0.9$ with a regression slope within $15\%$ of one, and the control doing
measurably worse on both, so the slope is part of the committed criterion and its failure is reported as a
failure. Two runs were designed: a dynamical one, in which the first layer is grown and the second is
injected while the first is still deforming, and a frozen one, in which the first layer is replaced by a
vertical sinusoid of amplitude $A_{1}/\lambda_{1b}$ that keeps the background an exact steady state.

\paragraph{EXP-005, steering, holding and the two-layer handoff.} The control that returns a grown layer to
rest is transcribed from Lemmas~3.7 and~3.8 of~\cite{ab-boussinesq} and run against the equations in the
co-rotating frame. Gate A: every assertion of Lemma~3.7 holds on a fine grid and on a $200$-point grid of
pulse amplitudes at $c_{p}=3$, $\Lambda=12$; a failure means the transcription is wrong and nothing
downstream runs. Gate B, inviscid cycle: the landing $|\Omega(t_{b})|/\max|\Omega|<2\times10^{-2}$ and the
steering gain within $2\%$ of the reduced model; the endpoint map within $0.02$ at three pulse amplitudes
including a deliberately mis-set one; the hold drifting by less than $1\%$ in amplitude and below $3\times10^{-2}$
in vorticity; step halving moving the gain by less than $10^{-3}$; and two controls, the cycle without the pulse
and without steering, that must fail the landing. Gate C, dissipation: the inviscid pulse still lands, the hold
decays at the predicted rate $d=\nu\lambda^{2\alpha}$ within $2\%$, and $e^{dt}$ times the viscous run matches
the inviscid run within $2\%$. Gate D, two layers: the first layer must land below $5\times10^{-2}$ at a deposit
comparable to the background, and the rate of the second layer must have correlation above $0.9$ with the total
gradient (D1) while the base-only control is lower by more than $0.3$ with a larger RMS difference (D2); the
slope is reported and not gated, since EXP-004 had already shown a systematic in it. A further gate, B5, added after
the first run, rejects a run whose neighbouring modes take over: at the landing time the largest horizontal
neighbour must stay below $10^{-2}$ of the wave, and at the end the field maximum below $1.05$ of the
background maximum.

\paragraph{EXP-001 and EXP-006, the formal certificate.} EXP-001 asks whether the repository of the two
announcements builds and whether the two comparator challenges accept the solution modules against the
reference statements; its hypothesis states the asymmetry that a green build is weak positive evidence and a red build is first of all evidence about
the machine used. EXP-006 asks the stronger question whether the kernel, and not only the elaborator, accepts
the development. Its gates: E1, every module replays with exit status zero; E2, the number of modules replayed
equals the number of object files, because a checker run on nothing also exits zero; E3, a negative control in
which a damaged object file must make the checker fail, stating that this does not show the
checker would detect an unsound proof; E4, the four headline theorems are inside the replayed set. The first
attempt, a sweep of all $2{,}659$ modules, was stopped after $80$ minutes with $144$ seconds of processor time and
$11.6$~GB resident and no module finished, because each module task loads its own import closure and the work is
input-bound; it was replaced by replays of the two root modules from an empty environment, which are processor
bound and make the count question moot \ev{V}.

\paragraph{EXP-007 and EXP-008, localization and the growth law.} EXP-007 asks whether the damping law
survives the spatial localization the constructions use. H1: the error is first order in the bandwidth ratio,
a slope of $-1$ within $0.1$ in $\log(\ell\lambda)$. H2: the coefficient stays of order one over
$\alpha\in[0.05,1]$ (the gate accepts values between $0.01$ and $10$). H3, the control: with an envelope as wide
as the wave the error must be of order one, here above $0.1$, since a small error there would mean the
measurement does not feel localization at all. H4: at the construction's own separations the error is
$\lambda_{q-1}^{3-Q_{q}}$. EXP-008 asks whether the critical exponent follows $\gamma/(2p)$ for both growth
laws. The prediction of Theorem~\ref{thm:class} was committed before the run, and the
gates are agreement within a tolerance of $10^{-5}$ for the pendulum law, the same for the stretching law, and an
exact ratio of two between them at equal $p$.

the measured peak growth rate matches $\sqrt A\sin\varphi$ to $8.6\times10^{-5}$ relative at

\paragraph{The cascade model and its finite horizon.} The cascade model reports a critical exponent found by
bisection on a run of $Q$ stages, and a supercritical schedule does not stall at once: its growth rate stays
positive until stage $q^{*}=\log(1/\nu)/\bigl(g(2\alpha p-\tfrac12)\bigr)$, so a run of $Q$ stages reports a
threshold too high by the factor $1+2\log(1/\nu)/\bigl(g(Q-1)\bigr)$ \ev{D}. Figure~\ref{fig:horizon} shows
the consequence in the results of EXP-003: the largest gap between the measured critical exponent and the closed
form $1/(4p)$, over $p\in[1.5,12]$, is $0.009618$, $0.004803$ and $0.002400$ at $400$, $800$ and $1600$ stages,
a halving at each doubling (ratios $2.00$ and $2.00$), which is the $1/Q$ law and the evidence that the excess
is a horizon artefact and not a different exponent. Against the horizon-corrected prediction the largest
relative error is $2.59\times10^{-6}$, and the million-schedule ensemble shows no violation of the bound, the
tightest closing schedule lying $2.7\times10^{-6}$ below it. The bound was first written with $Q$ in place of
$Q-1$, which left $47$ apparent violations and a residual of $2.56\times10^{-4}$; the correction, which is the
last stage inspected, removed them. The figure does not show that the model's exponent is the exponent of the
partial differential equations: it measures the bookkeeping and nothing else \ev{MV}.

\paragraph{The cascade premise.} On a two-scale background that is an exact steady state, with drift
$1.45\times10^{-15}$, the local growth rate of a fine wave follows the total gradient at correlation
$0.99945$ and the base stratification alone at $0.212$: a layer responds to the accumulated

still evolving does not discriminate between the two predictions (correlations $0.274$ and $0.222$)

\paragraph{The growth, steering and holding cycle.} The control that returns a grown layer to rest
rotates the background gradient and the phase direction together, so that the laboratory component
$\zeta_{1}$ changes sign while $J\zeta\cdot G$ and $|\zeta|$ are preserved. In the co-rotating frame it
becomes a rotating gravity direction, with the stratification held by a low-frequency force. At the
first chosen parameters the cycle fails: the amplitude grows $726$-fold during the hold, because the
background is Rayleigh-Taylor unstable at rate $\sqrt A$ and the layer grows at the smaller rate
$\sqrt A\sin s$ (Table~\ref{tab:gates}). On the repaired schedule and on a background whose gradient
is affine to fourth order the cycle reproduces the amplitude system to
$3.06\times10^{-6}$ in the steering gain and returns the vorticity amplitude to $3.67\times10^{-4}$ of
its peak, where it holds to $0.36\%$ over three growth times; on a plain sinusoidal background the
same quantities are $1.80\times10^{-2}$ and $1.57\times10^{-2}$, the difference being the curvature of
the background over the layer's support. Under dissipation the cycle factorizes as $e^{-dt}$ times the
inviscid cycle with $d=\nu\lambda^{2\alpha}$: a holding interval decays at $d$ to within $0.97\%$, and
the rescaled viscous run reproduces the inviscid one to $0.24\%$ \ev{MV}. The dynamical handoff with
steering, a layer grown, steered to rest and then used as the background of the next, reaches a
correlation $0.808$ with the total gradient at best, against the $0.9$ the criterion required, and
$0.509$ at the largest separation tried; it is not met, and the control discriminates in three of the
five runs \ev{MV}.

\paragraph{Localization.} Since the constructions localize each layer, \eqref{eq:diss} is applied to
a band rather than to a single frequency. For an envelope of radius $\ell$ on a wave of frequency
$\lambda$ the relative error of the damping law is
\[
\frac{\bigl\lVert(-\Delta)^{\alpha}(g\,e^{i\lambda\zeta\cdot x})-(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}g\,e^{i\lambda\zeta\cdot x}\bigr\rVert}{\bigl\lVert(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}g\bigr\rVert}
\;\approx\;\frac{3.6\,\alpha}{\ell\lambda},
\]
first order in the bandwidth ratio, with measured slope $-0.9863$, and depending on that ratio alone:
configurations sharing $\ell\lambda$ but splitting it differently between frequency and envelope agree
to four digits. The coefficient is linear in $\alpha$, as the symbol expansion predicts; the factor
$1.80$ that remains is the shape constant of the one envelope used, a compactly supported
$C^{\infty}$ bump. At the separations the constructions use, $\ell_{q}^{-1}=\lambda_{q-1}^{3}$ against
$\lambda_{q}=\lambda_{q-1}^{Q_{q}}$ with $Q_{q}\ge201$, the error is $\lambda_{q-1}^{3-Q_{q}}$, so
localization does not affect the bounds above \ev{MV}. The test concerns the damping term and not the
inviscid residual of a localized ansatz, which is what the force estimates of the designs control. A
construction that shrinks the separation between envelope and wave is not covered: at $\ell\lambda$ of
order ten the law is already wrong by tens of per cent, and at $\ell\lambda=1.4$ by $190\%$.

\paragraph{A property of the mechanism in an unforced periodic setting.} The stratification these
layers grow on is Rayleigh-Taylor unstable at rate $\sqrt A$, while a layer inserted at angle $s$
grows at $\sqrt A\sin s$. Perturbations at more favourable angles therefore gain $1/\sin s$ times as
many e-foldings as the layer. This is the cause of the refuted run of Table~\ref{tab:gates}, and the
effect is measured and not only estimated: neighbouring modes grow from $1.0\times10^{-7}$ to
$3.3\times10^{-2}$ during the run. The constructions are unaffected, since each layer is localized and the
force controls the exterior, but any realization without that control must keep its schedule short
enough for the layer to stay ahead \ev{MV}.

\section{Scope}\label{sec:scope}

The bound of Section~\ref{sec:class} is a necessary condition for growth in the layer mechanism,
derived from the amplitude system, the amplification identity and the dissipative
term~\eqref{eq:diss}, under hypotheses (A0) to (A5). The bounds of Section~\ref{sec:design} concern what the estimates of two designs
can certify: they track each design's corrections through the exponents of its published estimates,
but not constants or logarithmic factors, and they assume the most favourable insertion angle. Neither
bound affects the published inviscid results, which carry no dissipation. The mechanism is due to C\'ordoba
and Mart\'inez-Zoroa~\cite{clsmz,cmz}, extended to smooth forcing by Alp\"oge and
Buckmaster~\cite{ab-boussinesq,ab-euler,ab-ipm}; nothing in this paper
establishes blowup for any equation or improves any published threshold.

Evaluating the relation $\alpha_{c}=1/(4p)$ at the published threshold gives $p=11/4+\sqrt7$, in our
convention with $\alpha_{c}=0.04633$. This
value carries no information about the threshold: the relation is constraint \textbf{D} saturated,
which the construction saturates at every $\alpha$, so the identity holds along the whole family,
and its algebraic form is automatic because $\alpha_{0}\in\Q(\sqrt7)$ and the norm of
$22-8\sqrt7$ is a perfect square \ev{D}. The value $0.04633$ in this relation is our convention's form of the value
$0.09267$ of Section~\ref{sec:budget}; the two differ by the factor $2$ of Section~\ref{sec:question}.
