\begin{abstract}
In September 2026 two groups announced finite-time blowup for fluid equations with
machine-checked certificates: Alpog\'e and Buckmaster for the two-dimensional inviscid Boussinesq
system and three-dimensional Euler with \emph{smooth} forcing, and OpenAI for the
three-dimensional
Navier-Stokes at every positive viscosity together with unforced Euler. Both extend the multiscale
layer program of C\'ordoba and Mart\'inez-Zoroa, whose hypodissipative branch already proves blowup
for every exponent below $(22-8\sqrt7)/9$ with a merely $C^{1,\epsilon}$ force.

\textbf{Result.} The smooth-forcing design cannot be pushed into that regime. Its own correction
hierarchy, seed rule and frequency-ratio rule, combined with the dissipative growth condition for its
own amplitude system, cap the dissipation exponent it can carry at
\[
\alpha\le 5.38\times10^{-4}\quad\bigl(1.08\times10^{-3}\text{ in the }|\nabla|^{\alpha}\text{ convention}\bigr),
\]
attained at exactly one controlled derivative of the force. That is $86$ times below the exponent
already proved with a rough force, and the gap is structural rather than a matter of constants: the
amplitude margin saturates at $1/2$ however many derivatives are demanded, while the frequency ratio
those derivatives cost grows at least linearly, and the exponent is the margin divided by four times
the ratio.

\textbf{Evidence.} The bound rests on one ingredient that is ours rather than transcribed, the
dissipative extension of their amplitude system, so the paper reports the audit that makes it
credible: the Lean certificate rebuilt and then replayed through the kernel from an empty
environment, both halves; the amplitude system validated against direct pseudospectral simulation,
including the growth, steering and holding cycle that makes the cascade iterable; the cost of
localization to the damping law measured as $3.6\alpha/(\ell\lambda)$ and negligible at the
separations in use; and the published threshold recovered exactly from its own exponent budget, which
also shows that the admissible frequency ratios close at that threshold and exclude geometric
cascades outright.

Statements are labelled machine-verified \MV, derived \Der, or conjectural \Con. Two claims from an
earlier round are retracted in place, and this version supersedes v0.01, which reported the same
audit without stating a conclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result}\label{sec:result}

Write $\lambda_q$ for the frequency of layer $q$, $\delta$ for its \emph{amplitude margin}, meaning
that a layer stops at temperature amplitude $\lambda_q^{-1+\delta}$ and therefore deposits a gradient
$\lambda_q^{\delta}$ through the amplification identity $\nabla\vartheta(0)=\lambda\Theta\zeta$, and
$Q$ for the frequency ratio, $\lambda_q=\lambda_{q-1}^{Q}$. The published smooth-forcing design fixes
$\delta=1/8$ and $Q\ge200$, controls $k$ derivatives of the force with $120k\le Q$, and corrects the
localization error through $J=2k+8$ levels, each gaining a factor $\Pi^{5}\lambda^{-(1-\delta)}$ with
$\Pi\le\lambda^{5/Q}$; its seed is chosen so that the remaining force sits below $\lambda^{-6}$.

\begin{theorem}[\Der]\label{thm:ceiling}
Let a layer cascade of this design carry fractional dissipation $-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$. Then the
force budget forces
\[
J\Bigl[(1-\delta)-\frac5Q\Bigr]\ \ge\ k+6,
\]
and the dissipative growth condition forces $\alpha<\delta/(4Q)$. With $Q=120k$, the smallest ratio
the design's own rule allows, these give
\[
\delta\le 1-\frac{k+6}{2k+8}-\frac{1}{24k},
\qquad
\alpha\le\frac{1}{4\cdot120k}\Bigl(1-\frac{k+6}{2k+8}-\frac{1}{24k}\Bigr),
\]
whose maximum over $k\ge1$ is attained at $k=1$ and equals
\[
\boxed{\ \alpha_{\max}=5.38\times10^{-4}\ \ (\text{ours}),\qquad 1.08\times10^{-3}\ \ (\text{theirs}),\ }
\]
with margin $\delta\le0.2583$ at $Q=120$.
\end{theorem}

The two halves of the trade-off pull against each other and neither wins: raising $k$ buys margin,
since more correction levels gain more, but the margin saturates,
\[
\delta\le1-\frac{k+6}{2k+8}-\frac1{24k}\ \longrightarrow\ \frac12\qquad(k\to\infty),
\]
while the ratio those derivatives cost grows at least linearly, and the ratio divides. The exponent
therefore falls monotonically in $k$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrr}
\toprule
$k$ & $J=2k+8$ & $Q=120k$ & $\delta\le$ & $\alpha\le$ \\
\midrule
$1$ & $10$ & $120$ & $0.2583$ & $5.38\times10^{-4}$ \\
$2$ & $12$ & $240$ & $0.3125$ & $3.26\times10^{-4}$ \\
$3$ & $14$ & $360$ & $0.3433$ & $2.38\times10^{-4}$ \\
$10$ & $28$ & $1200$ & $0.4244$ & $8.84\times10^{-5}$ \\
$1000$ & $2008$ & $120000$ & $0.4990$ & $1.04\times10^{-6}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}[the comparison that makes this a conclusion]
Blowup is already proved for every exponent below $0.0463$ in our convention with a force in
$L^{1}_{t}C^{1,\epsilon}_{x}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling} puts the
smooth-forcing design $86$ times below that, and the separation does not close by retuning: at the
proved threshold the ratio rule cannot buy even one derivative. \emph{Smoothness of the force and
fractional dissipation are bought from one budget in this mechanism, and the published design spends
essentially all of it on smoothness.}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what would move it]
Exactly three levers appear in the proof, so a hypodissipative version of this mechanism must use one
of them: decouple the correction levels from the ratio, so that $Q$ need not grow with $k$; raise the
margin past $1/2$, which the hierarchy as stated forbids; or change the growth law, since the factor
$4$ in $\alpha<\delta/(4Q)$ comes from a rate $\sqrt A$, and a mechanism whose layers grow at the
background gradient itself, as the vortex-layer construction does, pays a different price. This is
the dated prediction of Section~\ref{sec:tradeoff}, now with a number attached.
\end{remark}

The ingredient of Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling} that is not transcribed from the published design is the
dissipative growth condition, and Sections~\ref{sec:mv} to~\ref{sec:budget} are the audit that makes
it credible: the certificate is machine-checked to the kernel, the amplitude system is validated
against the equations it reduces, the localization correction is measured, and the one published
threshold that can be recovered independently is recovered exactly.

\section{Scope, and what is not claimed}

The Clay Millennium problem asks for one of four alternatives in Fefferman's official statement; the
forced alternatives (C) and (D) are among them. Nothing in this paper bears on whether any announced
proof is correct as mathematics. What is offered is an audit that a third party can run, a validated
reduced model, and exponent bookkeeping whose every constant comes from a displayed formula in a
published paper.

The layer mechanism is due to Diego C\'ordoba and Luis Mart\'inez-Zoroa, pushed to smooth forcing by
Levent Alpog\'e and Tristan Buckmaster, with a different mechanism used by OpenAI for the viscous
case. Nothing here is our mechanism. Throughout, our dissipation convention is
$-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$, so classical viscosity is $\alpha=1$; C\'ordoba, Mart\'inez-Zoroa and Zheng
write $|\nabla|^{\alpha}$, so their exponent is twice ours and their threshold
$(22-8\sqrt7)/9=0.09267$ is $0.04633$ in our convention.

\section{The reduced model, and what it costs to reduce}\label{sec:reduction}

\subsection{The amplitude system and its dissipative extension \Der}

On a background that is affine near the origin, $u_{\mathrm{old}}=D(t)x$ and
$\theta_{\mathrm{old}}=G(t)\cdot x$, a temperature wave and a vorticity wave sharing one phase
$s=\lambda\zeta(t)\cdot x$ satisfy a closed system,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mod}
\dot\zeta=-D^{T}\zeta,\qquad
\dot\Theta=-\frac{J\zeta\cdot G}{\lambda|\zeta|^{2}}\,\Omega,\qquad
\dot\Omega=\lambda\,\zeta_{1}\,\Theta,
\end{equation}
exact where the envelope is one and the profile is linear. In the frozen configuration $D=0$,
$G=-Ae_{2}$, $\zeta=re(\varphi)$ the growth rate is $\sqrt A\sin\varphi$, which carries no $\lambda$:
the frequency in $\dot\Omega$ cancels the reciprocal frequency in $\dot\Theta$. Frequency buys
gradient, not growth, through the amplification identity $\nabla\vartheta(0)=\lambda\Theta\zeta$.

Adding $-\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$ to both equations is diagonal on the phase, so the whole effect is one
damping term per amplitude,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diss}
\dot\Theta=\cdots-\nu(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}\Theta,\qquad
\dot\Omega=\cdots-\nu(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}\Omega,
\end{equation}
with eigenvalues $-\nu(\lambda r)^{2\alpha}\pm\sqrt A\sin\varphi$ and therefore the frequency cap
$\lambda\lesssim A^{1/(4\alpha)}\nu^{-1/(2\alpha)}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:diss} is derived here; it is
the one analytic ingredient of this paper that is not transcribed.

\subsection{Validation against the equations \MV}

Direct pseudospectral simulation of the nonlinear two-dimensional Boussinesq system, in double
precision with two-thirds dealiasing, reproduces the reduced model: peak growth rate to
$4\times10^{-5}$ relative at two insertion angles, frequency independence to $2.7\times10^{-4}$ across
an eightfold range of $\lambda$, and the derived dissipative term of~\eqref{eq:diss} tracking the
measured rate to $2.6\times10^{-4}$ absolute across $\alpha\in\{1/4,1/2,1\}$ and
$\nu\in\{10^{-5},10^{-4}\}$. Three negative controls fail as required, after a first attempt was found
vacuous at an angle where the corruption changed nothing.

The cascade premise, that a layer responds to the \emph{total} accumulated low-frequency gradient
rather than to the base stratification, was tested on a frozen two-scale background that is an exact
steady state (measured drift $1.45\times10^{-15}$): the local rate follows the total gradient at
correlation $0.99945$ and the base alone at $0.212$.

\subsection{The steering cycle, realized on a torus \MV}

The mechanism is iterable only because a grown layer can be returned to rest: growth leaves both the
temperature gradient the next layer needs and a shear with coefficient $\Omega$ that would keep
deforming it. The construction removes the shear by rotating $G$ and $\zeta$ \emph{together}, which
preserves $J\zeta\cdot G$ and $|\zeta|$ but moves the laboratory component $\zeta_{1}$ that multiplies
$\Theta$ in $\dot\Omega$. In normalized time the control is a smooth ramp of $\zeta_1$ to zero
followed by a short negative pulse whose amplitude is \emph{selected} so that the vorticity amplitude
lands on zero.

A rigid rotation of the background does not fit on a torus. In two dimensions it does not have to: in
the co-rotating frame the Coriolis and centrifugal accelerations are gradients and are absorbed by the
pressure, and the Euler acceleration shifts only the spatially uniform mean vorticity, which induces
no velocity on a torus. What remains is the Boussinesq system with a \emph{rotating gravity
direction}, in which every wavevector stays an exact grid mode and the buoyancy torque reproduces the
transcribed coefficient exactly. A low-frequency force holds the prescribed stratification, which is
what the forcing in the theorem being modelled does anyway.

On a background flattened so that its gradient is affine to fourth order at the origin, the full
cycle reproduces the reduced model:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & sinusoidal background & flattened background \\
\midrule
vorticity landing, $|\Omega(t_b)|/\max|\Omega|$ & $1.57\times10^{-2}$ & $3.67\times10^{-4}$ \\
steering gain against the reduced model & $1.80\times10^{-2}$ & $3.06\times10^{-6}$ \\
hold: drift over three growth times & $14.7\%$ & $0.36\%$ \\
endpoint map, worst gap of three pulses & $0.196$ & $4.7\times10^{-4}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The two columns differ only in the curvature of the background over the layer's support, so that
curvature is the entire residual: the reduction assumes an affine background, which the construction
arranges by localizing each layer where its background is affine. Both negative controls fail as they
must: without the pulse the vorticity is still at $0.99985$ of its peak, and with gravity frozen it
never turns.

With equal dissipation on both fields the damping enters both amplitude equations on the diagonal, so
the whole cycle factorizes as $e^{-dt}$ times the inviscid cycle with $d=\nu\lambda^{2\alpha}$. Three
consequences were tested and hold: the \emph{inviscid} pulse amplitude still lands the vorticity at
zero (measured $4.1\times10^{-4}$), a holding interval decays at exactly $d$ (measured to $0.97\%$),
and rescaling the viscous run by $e^{dt}$ reproduces the inviscid one to $0.24\%$.

\subsection{A property of the mechanism that the bookkeeping cannot see \MV}

The first attempt at the cycle, at a small insertion angle, was destroyed by its own background. The
stratification the mechanism grows on is Rayleigh-Taylor unstable at rate $\sqrt A$, while a layer
inserted at angle $s$ grows at $\sqrt A\sin s$. Every mode at a more favourable angle therefore gains
$1/\sin s$ times as many e-folds as the layer does; over the schedule that run needed, about $72$
e-folds from round-off, and the measured amplitude grew by a factor $726$ in a holding interval that
should have been stationary, with $\max|\theta|$ going from $4.0$ to $10.0$.

The construction is not vulnerable to this, because each layer is localized and the force controls
what happens elsewhere. But it is worth stating plainly: \emph{the cascade always runs against a
faster instability of its own background, and what keeps it alive is localization, not speed.}

\subsection{What localization costs the dissipative reduction \MV}\label{sec:loc}

Equation~\eqref{eq:diss} is exact for a plane wave, and every layer of the construction is localized,
so the damping law is applied to a band of frequencies. Measuring the residual of a compactly
supported smooth envelope of radius $\ell$ on a wave of frequency $\lambda$ gives a relative error
first order in the bandwidth ratio, with slope $-0.9863$ against a predicted $-1$, and depending on
that ratio alone: cases sharing $\ell\lambda=102.4$ but splitting it differently between frequency and
envelope agree to four digits. The coefficient is exactly linear in the exponent, $3.606\,\alpha$,
which is what the symbol expansion gives, leaving $1.80$ as the shape constant of this envelope:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loc}
\frac{\bigl\lVert(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\bigl(g\,e^{i\lambda\zeta\cdot x}\bigr)
-(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}g\,e^{i\lambda\zeta\cdot x}\bigr\rVert}
{\bigl\lVert(\lambda|\zeta|)^{2\alpha}g\bigr\rVert}\;\approx\;\frac{3.6\,\alpha}{\ell\lambda}.
\end{equation}
A control with an envelope as wide as the wave is wrong by $190\%$, so the measurement can see the
failure it tests for. At the separations the published construction uses, $\ell_q^{-1}=\lambda_{q-1}^{3}$
and $\lambda_q=\lambda_{q-1}^{Q_q}$ with $Q_q\ge201$, the error is $\lambda_{q-1}^{3-Q_q}$:
localization does not obstruct the dissipative reduction and cannot be what sets a hypodissipative
threshold.

\section{The published threshold, recovered from its own budget}\label{sec:budget}

C\'ordoba, Mart\'inez-Zoroa and Zheng prove blowup for the forced fractional Navier-Stokes equations
for every exponent below $\alpha_0=(22-8\sqrt7)/9$, with a force in
$L^{1}_{t}C^{1,\epsilon}_{x}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}$. Their introduction gives a
back-of-the-envelope value $5-2\sqrt6=0.10102$ and says plainly that the real calculation has other
error sources. This section identifies which one.

\subsection{Four constraints \Der}

Every quantity in their construction is a power of one base: the frequency of layer $n$ is
$M_n=N^{R^{n}}$, the amplitude $A_n=N^{aR^{n}}$, the localization scale $L_n=N^{bR^{n}}$, and $s>0$ is
the H\"older margin of the force. Their Section 4.3 gives four requirements:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
 & source & constraint \\
\midrule
\textbf{D} dissipation & 4.3.5 & $a=\alpha R$ \\
\textbf{S} self-interaction & 4.3.2 & $2a-a/R+b+s-1\le0$ \\
\textbf{L} localization & 4.3.3 & $b\ge a+1/R+s$ \\
\textbf{O} outer velocity on the inner layer & 4.3.4 & $a+2/R+s+1-3b\le0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{proposition}[\Der]\label{prop:threshold}
With \textbf{D} and \textbf{S} saturated, \textbf{O} reduces to
\[
4s<2+3\alpha-7\alpha R-\frac2R,
\]
whose maximum over the frequency ratio is at $\alpha R^{2}=2/7$, which is exactly the value the paper
chooses. At that optimum $s(\alpha)=\bigl(2+3\alpha-2\sqrt{14\alpha}\bigr)/4$, and $s>0$ is
$9\alpha^{2}-44\alpha+4>0$, whose smaller root is $\alpha_0=(22-8\sqrt7)/9$.
\end{proposition}

No coefficient in that chain is fitted; each comes from a displayed exponent. At the published point
the localization constraint is \emph{not} active, with margin
$\tfrac12\bigl(\sqrt{2\alpha/7}-\alpha\bigr)=0.0350$ at $\alpha_0$. The introduction's heuristic keeps
\textbf{D}, \textbf{S} and \textbf{L} and does not contain \textbf{O}; saturating \textbf{L} instead
and optimizing the same way gives $\alpha R^{2}=1/3$, $s(\alpha)=(1+\alpha)/2-\sqrt{3\alpha}$ and the
bound $5-2\sqrt6$. \emph{The entire gap between the heuristic and the proved threshold is a swap of
which constraint binds.}

\subsection{The admissible frequency ratios \Der}\label{sec:ratios}

Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} is a quadratic in the frequency ratio, and reading it that way gives
a statement we have not seen made explicit.

\begin{proposition}[\Der]\label{prop:ratios}
A positive force margin requires $7\alpha R^{2}-(2+3\alpha)R+2<0$, so the admissible ratios form the
interval with endpoints
\[
R_{\pm}=\frac{(2+3\alpha)\pm\sqrt{9\alpha^{2}-44\alpha+4}}{14\alpha}.
\]
The discriminant is exactly the polynomial whose root is $\alpha_0$, so the interval closes to a
single point precisely at the published threshold, and that point is the paper's own
$R=\sqrt{2/(7\alpha)}$. Moreover $R_{-}>1$ for every $\alpha>0$, and at $R=1$ the margin is exactly
$-\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
$\alpha$ & admissible $R$ & the paper's choice \\
\midrule
$0.001$ & $(1.002,\ 285.1)$ & $16.90$ \\
$0.01$ & $(1.021,\ 27.98)$ & $5.345$ \\
$0.05$ & $(1.143,\ 5.000)$ & $2.390$ \\
$\alpha_0=0.09267$ & $\{1.7559\}$ & $1.7559$ \\
$0.10$ & empty & none \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The consequence is structural: a cascade whose frequencies grow geometrically, so that $\log M_n$ is
linear in $n$, is the case $R=1$, and it is inadmissible at every positive dissipation. Read off the
constraints directly, $R=1$ forces $b\ge a+1+s$ from localization against $b\le1-a-s$ from
self-interaction, hence $\alpha+s\le0$. \emph{Super-geometric frequency growth is forced, not
preferred, and more of it is forced as the dissipation rises.}

Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} and Proposition~\ref{prop:ratios} are verified in exact arithmetic,
by computer algebra, in the public repository's continuous integration.

\section{A reduced cascade model, and the retraction of one of its claims}

\subsection{The model and its threshold \Der}

With a geometric background $A_q=A_0e^{gq}$, a frequency schedule $\lambda_q=cA_q^{p}$ and amplitudes
fixed by the amplification identity, four constraints govern the cascade: summable amplitudes,
positive growth at every stage, a finite total time, and survival of each layer's gradient to the
blowup time. The time budget turns out not to bind, because stage times decay geometrically, and layer
survival coincides with growth positivity, because the remaining time shrinks at exactly the rate the
growth rate rises. The threshold is $\alpha_c=1/(4p)$, measured against a horizon-corrected prediction
to $2.59\times10^{-6}$ over $10^{6}$ schedules with zero violations \MV.

\subsection{A retraction \Der}

An earlier round of this work inverted $\alpha_c=1/(4p)$ at the published threshold, obtained
$p=11/4+\sqrt7$ exactly, and argued that this was informative because the relation came from
constraints not tuned to hit a number and because the resulting $p$ is a clean algebraic number.
\emph{Both arguments are withdrawn.} The relation is constraint \textbf{D} saturated, and the
published family saturates \textbf{D} at every $\alpha$, so $2p=1/\alpha$ holds identically along it:
evaluating at $\alpha_0$ says nothing about $\alpha_0$. And the clean form is automatic, since
$\alpha_0\in\Q(\sqrt7)$, inverses stay in $\Q(\sqrt7)$, and the result is tidy only because the norm
of $22-8\sqrt7$ is $36$, a perfect square. The retraction is kept here because it is part of the
record.

\subsection{The cap is unreachable \Der}

Allowing any schedule $\log\lambda_{q+1}=R\log\lambda_q$ moves only the amplitude constraint, which
becomes $R<p$; the threshold $\alpha_c=1/(4p)$ stands. Importing nothing but
Proposition~\ref{prop:ratios} then gives $\alpha_c\le1/(4R_{-}(\alpha))$, and above the published
threshold there is no admissible ratio at all. So the cap of $1/4$ that the model measures is correct
for the model and vacuous as a bound on the real problem: the schedules that approach it are excluded
by the force budget before the dissipation constraint ever binds.

\section{Force regularity against dissipation, and a dated prediction}\label{sec:tradeoff}

\subsection{The exchange rate \Der}

The smooth-forcing construction fixes, in its Subsection 3.3, a frequency ratio
$\lambda_q=\lambda_{q-1}^{Q_q}$ with $Q_q=Q^{*}+q$ and $Q^{*}\ge200$, a derivative order
$k_q=\max\{k:120k\le Q_q,\dots\}$, and a seed and gain whose stated consequence is that a layer stops
at temperature amplitude $\lambda_q^{-7/8}$. Two facts follow immediately. By the amplification
identity the layer deposits a gradient $\lambda_q^{1/8}$, so the \emph{amplitude margin} is
$\delta=1/8$. And the rule $120k_q\le Q_q$ ties the smoothness of the force to the frequency ratio:
each further derivative costs another $120$ in the ratio, and a $C^{\infty}$ force needs
$k_q\to\infty$, which is why $Q_q$ grows with the stage.

Applying~\eqref{eq:diss} stage by stage, growth requires
$\nu\lambda_q^{2\alpha}<\sqrt{A_q}\sin s_q$ with $A_q=\lambda_{q-1}^{\delta}$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tradeoff}
\boxed{\ \alpha<\frac{\delta}{4Q_q}\ }
\end{equation}
with an insertion angle of order one, and a factor $Q_{q-1}$ tighter with the construction's own
angle, which is itself a negative power of the frequency. In the notation of the reduced model this is
nothing new: $\lambda_q=A_q^{p}$ with $p=Q_q/\delta$, and~\eqref{eq:tradeoff} is growth positivity.

\begin{claimbox}[\Der]
Since $Q_q=Q^{*}+q$ is unbounded, no positive $\alpha$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:tradeoff} at every stage: a
cascade with this schedule and any fixed dissipation runs finitely many stages and stops, and a finite
cascade produces a finite gradient.
\end{claimbox}

Read the other way, $120k\le Q$ with~\eqref{eq:tradeoff} gives $\alpha<\delta/(480k)$, so even at the
most generous margin the amplification identity allows, $\delta\to1$, controlling a single derivative
caps the exponent at $1/480=0.00208$: twenty-two times below the threshold already proved for a
$C^{1,\epsilon}$ force, and one hundred and seventy-eight times below it at $\delta=1/8$. The
obstruction is therefore not the published constants, which are design choices, but the exchange rate
of $120$ units of frequency ratio per derivative.

Our own cap of $1/4$ is the corner $\delta\to1$, $Q\to1$ of the same trade-off; the smooth-forcing
design sits at $\delta=1/8$, $Q\ge200$. Section~\ref{sec:ratios} shows the other corner is closed too,
since $R=1$ is inadmissible.

\subsection{The prediction \Con}

\begin{claimbox}[\Con, recorded 2026-09-16]\label{claim:prediction}
A hypodissipative version of the smooth-forcing mechanism must do at least one of: bound the frequency
ratio $Q$, which by $120k\le Q$ bounds the derivatives controlled and therefore gives a force of
finite regularity rather than $C^{\infty}$; raise the amplitude margin $\delta$ toward $1$; or change
the growth law to one whose rate is the background gradient itself rather than its square root, which
is what the vortex-layer construction uses. Its threshold should scale like $\delta/(4Q)$.
\end{claimbox}

The prediction is wrong if a hypodissipative result appears with a positive threshold while keeping a
stage-dependent unbounded $Q_q$, an amplitude margin near $1/8$, and the same square-root growth law.
It is also wrong, in a more interesting way, if the dissipative analysis does not damp a layer at
$\nu\lambda^{2\alpha}$, which is the one ingredient here that is ours rather than transcribed, and is
the ingredient Section~\ref{sec:reduction} tested. The sequel had not appeared when this was recorded,
checked against the preprint listings on 2026-09-16 and again on 2026-09-17.

\section{Method notes}

Every experiment in this program commits its hypothesis and its pass criteria before the run, keeps
its raw output, and ends in a verdict that may be CONFIRMED, REFUTED, INCONCLUSIVE or DECIDED IN PART.
Two consequences are visible in this paper: a gate that was not met is reported as not met, and the
run at the parameters committed for the steering cycle is reported as refuted, with the reason.

Several defects were found by construction rather than by luck, and all are of one family, a check
that cannot see what it claims to test: a negative control evaluated where the corruption changed
nothing; a slope fitted where the amplitude crosses zero; a horizon too short to see the effect;
a wave placed above the dealiasing limit; a measurement window reaching past that limit while the
wave itself passed the check; a guard asserting that a background drifts in temperature when the
runaway is entirely in the vorticity; and a schedule demanding a phase component of $1.07$, which no
unit vector has, followed by the reduced model but not by the simulation.

All results reproduce from their own records: each result file stores the settings it was run with,
and a harness rebuilds the command line from that block rather than from prose, then diffs every
numeric leaf. That distinction is not cosmetic. Rerunning one experiment from its documented command
gave a correlation of $0.9920$ against a recorded $0.9995$, purely because two settings of the
recorded run differed from the runner's defaults, which is indistinguishable from a regression.

\section{Conclusion}

An audit is only worth its DOI if it concludes something. This one concludes a number:
Theorem~\ref{thm:ceiling}. The smooth-forcing mechanism of the September 2026 Boussinesq result,
taken with its own correction hierarchy and its own ratio rule, cannot carry a dissipation exponent
above $5.38\times10^{-4}$, which is $86$ times below what a rough-forcing construction already
achieves. Everything else reported here is the evidence that the one non-transcribed ingredient of
that bound is sound.

\section{What is not claimed}

This paper does not prove finite-time blowup for any equation; does not verify the mathematics of
either announced proof beyond what a kernel replay of a formalization establishes; does not improve
any published threshold; and does not claim the layer mechanism, which belongs to C\'ordoba and
Mart\'inez-Zoroa. The threshold reconstruction of Section~\ref{sec:budget} is exponent bookkeeping
with $\delta\to0$, logarithms dropped and constants ignored, reproducing the authors' own stated
choices; the trade-off of Section~\ref{sec:tradeoff} applies a reduction we derived to a schedule
designed for an inviscid theorem, which is a statement about what that design costs in a setting it
was not built for, not a claim that it fails.
