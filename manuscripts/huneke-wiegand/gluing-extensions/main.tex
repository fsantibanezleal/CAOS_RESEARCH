\begin{abstract}
Let $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ be a gluing of numerical semigroups, $\kk$ a field, and
$A=a_1A_1+a_2A_2$ the extension of relative ideals $A_i$ of $\Gamma_i$. With $y$ acting as $t^{a_2}$ on
$\kk[\Gamma_1]$ and as $t^{a_1}$ on $\kk[\Gamma_2]$, we prove $I_A\otimes I_{A^*}\cong T_1\otimes_{\kk[y]}T_2$,
where $T_i=I_{A_i}\otimes I_{A_i^*}$. The torsion lengths satisfy
$\len(\tor(T))=a_1\len(\tor(T_1))+a_2\len(\tor(T_2))+\sum_{j\ge1}b_1(j)b_2(j)$, with $b_i(j)$ the number of Jordan
blocks of size at least $j$ of $y$ on $\tor(T_i)$; the numbers of minimal generators multiply, and $A$ has the
Huneke--Wiegand property exactly when a factor is a non-principal ideal with it. This re-proves two propositions of
Garc\'{\i}a-S\'anchez and Leamer and settles the case they leave open. Because the conjecture is false in
dimension one, it yields counterexample ideals with $2^r$ minimal generators over symmetric numerical semigroup
rings, among them four-generated examples with Frobenius numbers $23645$ and $53599$. Exact computation, by the
definition of the tensor product and by Leamer's partition criterion, confirms the law on \NSeeded{} seeded
gluings and on \NHard{} gluings with large factors, \NHardCE{} of them with a counterexample factor and \NHardCEItself{} with an extension
that is itself a counterexample; an independent implementation agrees on every audited case. The two four-generated examples rest
on the proof and the partition criterion, since their direct torsion is not computed. The results concern
monomial ideals.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $R$ be a one-dimensional Gorenstein local domain. Huneke and Wiegand asked whether a finitely generated
torsion-free $R$-module $M$ with $M\otimes_R\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$ torsion-free must be free
\cite{HunekeWiegand1994}. Over a one-dimensional integrally closed local domain the answer is yes, by
Auslander's result \cite[3.3]{Auslander1961}, as cited in \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, and by the structure of modules over a principal ideal domain.
For numerical semigroup rings, Leamer reduced the question for monomial ideals to arithmetic of relative ideals
\cite{Leamer2012}. Garc\'{\i}a-S\'anchez and Leamer then proved it for monomial ideals of free numerical semigroup
rings and for two-generated monomial ideals over complete intersections \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}. Their
method rests on gluings $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ of numerical semigroups: an extension $a_1A_1+a_2A_2$ of
relative ideals inherits the Huneke--Wiegand property from a non-principal factor that has it (their Proposition~9),
and $a_1A_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ has the property exactly when $A_1$ does (their Proposition~10). In their final section they
ask whether the property for ideals with more than two generators is invariant under gluing, and whether their
Proposition~9 extends to rings of the form $(R_1\otimes_\kk R_2)/(f\otimes1-1\otimes g)$.

The conjecture is now known to be false in dimension one. Christensen, Gerko and Iyengar gave a non-principal
rigid ideal in a one-dimensional Gorenstein domain \cite{ChristensenGerkoIyengar2026}, and Pham gave a
two-generated monomial counterexample over a symmetric numerical semigroup of multiplicity $56$, embedding
dimension $26$ and Frobenius number $181$ \cite{Pham2026}. An infinite family of two-generated counterexamples of
multiplicity $24p$, $p\ge4$, is constructed in \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}. In the presence of counterexamples the
case of an extension whose two factors are both non-principal and both fail the property is no longer vacuous,
and the propositions of \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} do not cover it. Two questions follow. What is the torsion
of the tensor product $I_A\otimes I_{A^*}$ of an extension in terms of its factors, and does a computation that
reaches factors with counterexample ideals agree with that answer?

\paragraph{The method in one paragraph.}
For a gluing, the semigroup ring $\kk[\Gamma]$ is the tensor product $\kk[\Gamma_1]\otimes_{\kk[y]}\kk[\Gamma_2]$
over a polynomial ring in one variable, and an extension ideal is the tensor product of its factors over the same
ring (Proposition~\ref{prop:iso}). Tensor products of the form $I\otimes I^{*}$ then factor over $\kk[y]$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor}). Over a principal ideal domain the factors split into a free part and a torsion
part, and the torsion of the product is read from the Jordan types of $y$ on the torsion of the factors. The
computational check uses two routes that share no code: the torsion is computed from the definition of the tensor
product, degree by degree, and the Huneke--Wiegand property is decided by Leamer's partition criterion.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A tensor factorization, a torsion-length law, multiplicativity of minimal generators and a characterization
of the Huneke--Wiegand property for every extension ideal of a gluing (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), with proofs.
\item The case left open by Proposition~9 of \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, both factors counterexamples, decided in
favour of a counterexample (Corollary~\ref{cor:open}), and counterexample ideals with $2^r$ minimal generators over
symmetric numerical semigroup rings (Corollary~\ref{cor:many}), including two explicit four-generated examples.
\item A derivation of the Frobenius number and of symmetry for the gluings used, from the Ap\'ery decomposition
of Proposition~\ref{prop:iso} (Corollary~\ref{cor:frobsym}), and a proof that the two-parameter family obtained from the
family of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026} has embedding dimension $11p$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:embdim}).
\item Algorithms with a proof of the degree bound that makes the direct torsion computation finite
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bound}), their cost, and an independent implementation.
\item Exact validation on \NSeeded{} seeded gluings and on \NHard{} gluings with large factors, \NHardCE{} of which have a counterexample
ideal as a factor, the case the theorem is about, together with controls that remove each hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ be a gluing, $A_i$ relative ideals of $\Gamma_i$, and
$A=a_1A_1+a_2A_2$. Write $T=I_A\otimes I_{A^*}$ and $T_i=I_{A_i}\otimes I_{A_i^*}$, and let $y$ act on
$\kk[\Gamma_1]$ as $t^{a_2}$ and on $\kk[\Gamma_2]$ as $t^{a_1}$. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T\cong T_1\otimes_{\kk[y]}T_2$;
\item $\len(\tor(T))=a_1\len(\tor(T_1))+a_2\len(\tor(T_2))+\sum_{j\ge1}b_1(j)b_2(j)$, where $b_i(j)$ is
the number of Jordan blocks of size at least $j$ of $y$ acting on $\tor(T_i)$;
\item $\mu(A)=\mu(A_1)\mu(A_2)$;
\item $A$ has the Huneke--Wiegand property if and only if $A_1$ or $A_2$ is a non-principal ideal with
that property.
\end{enumerate}
\mD{}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:many}
If $A_1$ and $A_2$ are two-generated counterexample ideals over symmetric numerical semigroups
$\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$, and $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ is a gluing, then $a_1A_1+a_2A_2$ is a
four-generated counterexample ideal over the symmetric semigroup $\Gamma$. Iterating, for every $r\ge1$
there are counterexample ideals with $2^r$ minimal generators over symmetric numerical semigroup
rings. \mD{}
\end{corollary}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The literature around the Huneke--Wiegand conjecture falls into four groups: positive results for classes of
ideals and rings, the recent counterexamples, the combinatorics of numerical semigroups and of gluing, and the
Kunz-cone literature that organizes numerical semigroups by multiplicity. Each group is characterised below by what it
assumes and what it gives, and the last paragraph says where the factorization stands.

\subsection{Positive results}

The conjecture was posed in \cite{HunekeWiegand1994}. Celikbas and Takahashi showed that the version for
modules over one-dimensional Gorenstein local domains implies the version for Gorenstein domains of any
dimension, as quoted in \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}; the equivalence is their Proposition~5.6, which the
present paper takes from the quotations in \cite{Leamer2012} and does not re-read \cite{CelikbasTakahashi2011}. Over rings of small
multiplicity, Goto, Takahashi, Taniguchi and Truong prove that a faithful ideal with
$I\otimes I^{\vee}$ torsion-free is isomorphic to the ring or its canonical module when the multiplicity is at
most six, by a change of rings to rings between $R$ and $I:I$ \cite{GotoTakahashiTaniguchiTruong2013}.
Celikbas, Goto, Takahashi and Taniguchi establish the ideal case for a large class of ideals over arbitrary
one-dimensional local domains \cite{CelikbasGotoTakahashiTaniguchi2017}. Huneke, Iyengar and Wiegand study rigid
ideals, those with $\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(I,I)=0$, in one-dimensional Gorenstein local rings
\cite{HunekeIyengarWiegand2018}, and their colon criterion is the tool through which Pham's example is analysed
\cite{Pham2026}. Dey and Kobayashi introduce Burch and weakly $\mathfrak m$-full submodules and obtain a new class
of rings over which the conjecture holds \cite{DeyKobayashi2022}. Celikbas, Le, Matsui and Sadeghi prove the
conjecture for two periodic modules through Hochster's theta invariant \cite{CelikbasLeMatsuiSadeghi2022}, and Dey
and Lyle give criteria, in terms of centers of endomorphism rings, that force a nonzero free summand
\cite{DeyLyle2025}. These results hypothesize a property of the ideal or of the ring (a bound on multiplicity, a
rigidity or Burch condition, periodicity). None of them factors the torsion of a tensor product across a
decomposition of the ring, and an ideal built by gluing two counterexamples satisfies none of the hypotheses in
general.

\subsection{Counterexamples}

Pham's counterexample lives over a symmetric numerical semigroup with $I=(t^{56},t^{70})$ in
$\kk[t^{\Gamma}]_{\mathfrak m}$ for every field $\kk$, persists under completion, and is accompanied by exact
verifiers \cite{Pham2026}. Christensen, Gerko and Iyengar construct a rigid non-principal ideal by a field
extension in a Gorenstein domain of dimension one and prove the conjecture when the ring is equicharacteristic and
its embedding dimension is at most three \cite{ChristensenGerkoIyengar2026}. That positive result bounds where a
counterexample factor can occur: the semigroup rings used here have embedding dimension $26$, $44$ and $55$, far
above three. The infinite family of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026} provides the second factor of the mixed example below. All
three constructions give two-generated ideals; the factorization of this paper produces counterexamples with four,
eight and in general $2^r$ generators from them. The three constructions are also of different kinds: the first is an
explicit semigroup found by search and verified by a finite sumset identity, the second is a field-theoretic criterion, and the
third is a parametric family whose members share a common structure and are indexed by one integer. A factor from either
kind can be glued to a factor from another, which is what the mixed example below does.

\subsection{Numerical semigroups, ideals and gluing}

Leamer shows that torsion in $I\otimes J$ for monomial ideals is detected by a partition criterion on the
generators of $I$, and computes the torsion degree by degree in terms of a semigroup tensor product
\cite{Leamer2012}. Garc\'{\i}a-S\'anchez and Leamer introduce extensions of relative ideals along a gluing, prove
Propositions~9 and~10, and verify by computer algebra that two-generated ideals of symmetric numerical semigroups
with Frobenius number below $69$ have the property, using the \texttt{numericalsgps} package
\cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013,DelgadoGarciaSanchezMorais}. Landeros, O'Neill, Pelayo, Pe\~na, Ren and Wissman use that
approach to prove the conjecture for two-generated monomial ideals when the semigroup is generated by a generalized
arithmetic sequence \cite{Landeros2024}. Roy treats one-dimensional graded complete intersections satisfying degree conditions that hold whenever the ring is a numerical semigroup ring of embedding dimension at least
three, through Auslander--Reiten sequences whose middle term has at least two non-free summands \cite{Roy2018}. Herzinger
proved the conjecture for two-generated ideals whose dual is also two-generated
\cite{Herzinger1999}. For free numerical semigroups in the sense of Bertin and Carbonne \cite{BertinCarbonne1977}
the conjecture holds for monomial ideals \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}. Gluing was introduced by Rosales \cite[Chapter~8]{RosalesGarciaSanchez2009}, inspired by Delorme's characterisation of complete intersection
numerical semigroups as iterated gluings \cite{Delorme1976}. Gimenez and Srinivasan study gluing for semigroups in
$\N^n$, where two semigroups can be glued only under conditions, in contrast to $n=1$ \cite{GimenezSrinivasan2022}.
Casabella, D'Anna and Garc\'{\i}a-S\'anchez relate ideals of a numerical semigroup with smallest element $0$ to
Ap\'ery sets and Kunz coordinates \cite{CasabellaDAnnaGarciaSanchez2023}.

\subsection{Kunz cones and the place of the present result}

A numerical semigroup of multiplicity $m$ is a point of the Kunz cone $\mathcal C_m$, and points in one face
share properties such as the number of minimal generators \cite{Borevitz2023}; the faces are walked and organized
by the Kunz fan in \cite{BrowerMcDonoughONeill2024}. The family of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026} has multiplicity
$24p$, so its members lie in different cones and no single face contains the family. The simple gluing of
Section~\ref{sec:theory} produces the two-parameter family with $24p$ fixed and the second parameter $q$ free.

None of the sources above states the factorization $T\cong T_1\otimes_{\kk[y]}T_2$, the length law, or the
decision of the case left open by Proposition~9 of \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}; the sources searched for
these statements were \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013,GotoTakahashiTaniguchiTruong2013,Leamer2012,Roy2018}. This
is a negative search and not a proof of novelty. The present result lies at the intersection of the second and
third groups: it takes the counterexamples of the second as inputs and the extension calculus of the third as its
tool, and it proves what happens to the Huneke--Wiegand property when they are combined.

\section{Preliminaries and problem formulation}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Semigroups, relative ideals and duals}

A numerical semigroup $\Lambda\subseteq\N$ is a submonoid with finite complement; $F(\Lambda)$ is its Frobenius
number, the largest integer not in $\Lambda$ (so $F(\N)=-1$), and $\cond(\Lambda)=F(\Lambda)+1$ is its conductor. A
relative ideal is a non-empty set $B\subseteq\Z$, bounded below, with $B+\Lambda\subseteq B$; it has a unique
minimal generating set, and every relative ideal is a finite union $\bigcup_i(x_i+\Lambda)$
\cite[Section~1]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}. Its dual is $B^*=\Lambda-B=\{z\in\Z:z+B\subseteq\Lambda\}$, and $B$ is
principal if $B=x+\Lambda$. The fractional ideal $I_B$ of $\kk[\Lambda]$ is spanned by the monomials $t^b$,
$b\in B$. We write $\cond(B)$ for the least integer $c$ with $[c,\infty)\subseteq B$, $\mu(B)$ for the number of
minimal generators, and $\Ap(B,c)=\{b\in B:b-c\notin B\}$ for $c\in\Lambda$, $c>0$, as in
\cite[Section~2]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}. The sum and the intersection of relative ideals are relative ideals, and the sum of ideals
generated by $X$ and $Y$ is generated by the sums $x+y$; these elementary facts are listed in \cite[Section~1]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} and are used without comment.

The monomial ideal $I_{B^*}$ is the inverse $(\kk[\Lambda]:_KI_B)$ in the field of fractions $K$, and for a
domain $R$ and a finitely generated submodule $M$ of $K^n$ the inverse $M^{-1}$ is naturally isomorphic to
$\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$ \cite[Definition~1.6 and Lemma~1.8]{Leamer2012}. Hence
$I_{B^*}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\kk[\Lambda]}(I_B,\kk[\Lambda])$. This is the statement that
\cite[Corollary~7]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} uses with the pointer to Remark~1.7 of \cite{Leamer2012}; in the
numbering of arXiv:1211.2896v1 that remark concerns inverses of sums and the duality is Lemma~1.8.

\subsection{The Huneke--Wiegand property and its criterion}

Following \cite[Section~1]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, a relative ideal $A$ is \emph{Huneke--Wiegand} if it is
principal or if there are relative ideals $P,Q$ with $P\cup Q=A$ and $(P+A^*)\cap(Q+A^*)\ne(P\cap Q)+A^*$. For a
non-principal $A$, \cite[Theorem~6]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} (which restates \cite[Theorem~1.4]{Leamer2012})
says that $I_A\otimes I_{A^*}$ has non-zero torsion if and only if $A$ is Huneke--Wiegand. In this paper the
\emph{Huneke--Wiegand property} is that torsion condition for a non-principal ideal, and a \emph{counterexample
ideal} is a non-principal $A$ without it, so that $I_A\otimes I_{A^*}$ is torsion-free.

The criterion used by the computations is the generator form. By \cite[Remark~3]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, if
$A=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ with the $x_i$ its minimal generators, then $A$ is Huneke--Wiegand if and only if there is a
partition $\{S,S'\}$ of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $P=(x_i:i\in S)$ and $Q=(x_j:j\in S')$ such that
$(P+A^*)\cap(Q+A^*)\ne(P\cap Q)+A^*$. It is the equivalence of conditions (1) and (3) of
\cite[Theorem~1.4]{Leamer2012} with $J=I_{A^*}$: condition (3) is the equality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition}
\bigl((a_i:i\in S)\cap(a_j:j\notin S)\bigr)J=(a_i:i\in S)J\cap(a_j:j\notin S)J
\end{equation}
for every subset $S$ of the generators, and the torsion vanishes exactly when it holds for all $S$.
The ideal-level criterion over all pairs $P,Q$ with $P+Q=I$ is condition (2) of the same theorem, and the
generator form is the one the algorithms use. The hypotheses of that theorem include the graded ring $\kk[\Gamma]$, as a graded subalgebra of a polynomial ring; the
passage to the localization and the completion is made in Section~\ref{sec:theory} by flat base change.

\subsection{Gluings and extensions}

Following \cite[Section~2]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, $\Gamma$ is the \emph{gluing} of numerical semigroups
$\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ if $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ with $a_1\in\Gamma_2$ and $a_2\in\Gamma_1$. Because
$\N\setminus\Gamma$ is finite, $\gcd(a_1,a_2)=1$ follows. The definition does not require $a_1,a_2$ to be non-generators:
in Example~\ref{ex:pham} below $a_1=56$ and $a_2=57$ are minimal generators of $\Gamma_P$. For any numerical
semigroups $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ gluing parameters exist: take any positive $a_1\in\Gamma_2$ and any $a_2\in\Gamma_1$
with $a_2\equiv1\pmod{a_1}$, which exists because $\Gamma_1$ contains every integer above its conductor. The Frobenius number and symmetry of
a gluing are derived in Corollary~\ref{cor:frobsym}; they are not taken from the literature, so that they cover the definition as stated, including gluings with $a_1,a_2$ minimal generators such as the examples below. The extension of ideals, written next, is the construction on which the whole paper rests, and
it is defined for every pair of relative ideals, principal or not.

The \emph{extension} of $A_i\subseteq\Gamma_i$ is $A=a_1A_1+a_2A_2$; it is a relative ideal of $\Gamma$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extension}
A=\bigl(a_1x+a_2z:\ x\in X_1,\ z\in X_2\bigr),\qquad A^*=a_1A_1^*+a_2A_2^*,
\end{equation}
where $X_i$ generates $A_i$ \cite[Lemma~8(1) and Lemma~8(9)]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}. Throughout, $R_i=\kk[\Gamma_i]$,
$R=\kk[\Gamma]$ and $S=\kk[y]$, with $y\mapsto t^{a_2}$ in $R_1$ and $y\mapsto t^{a_1}$ in $R_2$. All rings and
modules are graded by the exponent, and for graded modules of finite length, $\len=\dim_\kk$.

\subsection{Problem and assumptions}

\emph{Problem.} For a gluing $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ and relative ideals $A_i$ of $\Gamma_i$, determine the
length of the torsion submodule of $T=I_A\otimes_{\kk[\Gamma]}I_{A^*}$, the number $\mu(A)$ and the
Huneke--Wiegand status of $A$ from invariants of $(\Gamma_1,A_1)$ and $(\Gamma_2,A_2)$ alone, and decide the status
of the extension when both factors are counterexample ideals. \emph{Assumptions.} The ideals are monomial (relative
ideals), the field $\kk$ is arbitrary, and the rings are graded by the exponent. Only extension ideals are treated;
relative ideals of $\Gamma$ that are not extensions are outside the problem. The answer is sought as a formula, so that it can be checked against a direct computation that does not use it:
the torsion length of the extension must be a function of the torsion lengths of the factors and of the action of $y$ on them, and the
number of generators and the Huneke--Wiegand status must be functions of those of the factors. The problem is posed for relative
ideals and not for arbitrary modules, because the duality used in Section~\ref{sec:prelim} identifies $I_{A^*}$ with the Hom module only for monomial ideals
with the pointers given there. The notation is collected in
Table~\ref{tab:notation}.

\section{Method: extensions are tensor products}\label{sec:factor}

The method has three steps: an extension ideal is a tensor product over $S$ of its factors, tensor products of the
form $I\otimes I^*$ then factor over $S$, and torsion over $S$ is computed from the structure of finitely generated
modules over a principal ideal domain. The first step rests on the Ap\'ery decomposition.
Throughout this section $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ is a gluing and $A_i$ are relative ideals of $\Gamma_i$; no assumption of symmetry or of the
Huneke--Wiegand property is made until the consequences of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The statements are proved for graded modules over the semigroup rings, with
every module spanned by monomials, and each proof is a short computation on bases: the content is that the bases of the two sides are indexed by the same set.
The three results are used in sequence, and none of them uses the next. The proofs are given in full because they are short and because the theorem is stated for every field and for every pair of relative ideals, so that no step is delegated to a citation. Lemma~\ref{lem:apery} gives a basis of a fractional monomial ideal over a polynomial subring, Proposition~\ref{prop:iso} compares the two bases through
the exponents, and Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor} transports the comparison to the tensor products of ideals with their duals.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:apery}
Let $B$ be a relative ideal of $\Lambda$ and $c\in\Lambda$, $c>0$. Then $I_B$ is a free
$\kk[t^c]$-module with basis $\{t^b:b\in\Ap(B,c)\}$, and $\Ap(B,c)$ has exactly one element in each
residue class modulo $c$. \mD{}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each residue class modulo $c$ meets $B$ in a non-empty set that is bounded below and closed under
adding $c$. With $b_r$ its least element, the class is $b_r+c\N$, so $I_B=\bigoplus_r t^{b_r}\kk[t^c]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:iso}
The assignment $t^x\otimes t^z\mapsto t^{a_1x+a_2z}$ defines an isomorphism of
$R_1\otimes_SR_2$-modules $I_{A_1}\otimes_SI_{A_2}\to I_A$. For $A_i=\Gamma_i$ it is a ring isomorphism
$R_1\otimes_SR_2\cong R$, and $I_{A^*}\cong I_{A_1^*}\otimes_SI_{A_2^*}$. \mD{}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The map is $S$-balanced, since $(t^{a_2}t^x)\otimes t^z$ and $t^x\otimes(t^{a_1}t^z)$ both go to
$t^{a_1x+a_1a_2+a_2z}$, and it is compatible with both actions. By Lemma~\ref{lem:apery} the source is
$S$-free on $t^x\otimes t^z$ with $x\in\Ap(A_1,a_2)$ and $z\in\Ap(A_2,a_1)$, so it has the $\kk$-basis
$y^n(t^x\otimes t^z)$, sent to $t^{a_1x+a_2z+na_1a_2}$. Every element of $A$ is $a_1x'+a_2z'$ with
$x'=x+ia_2$ and $z'=z+ja_1$ for Ap\'ery elements $x,z$ and $i,j\ge0$, hence equals
$a_1x+a_2z+(i+j)a_1a_2$; so the map is onto. If $a_1x+a_2z+na_1a_2=a_1x'+a_2z'+n'a_1a_2$, reduction
modulo $a_1$ gives $z\equiv z'$, hence $z=z'$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:apery}; likewise $x=x'$, and then
$n=n'$. Distinct basis vectors therefore go to distinct monomials. The last statement follows from
$A^*=a_1A_1^*+a_2A_2^*$ \cite[Lemma~8(9)]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tensor}
$T\cong T_1\otimes_ST_2$ as modules over $R\cong R_1\otimes_SR_2$. \mD{}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For commutative $S$-algebras $R_i$ and $R_i$-modules $M_i,N_i$ the assignment
$(m_1\otimes m_2)\otimes(n_1\otimes n_2)\mapsto(m_1\otimes n_1)\otimes(m_2\otimes n_2)$ gives an
isomorphism
\[
(M_1\otimes_SM_2)\otimes_{R_1\otimes_SR_2}(N_1\otimes_SN_2)\cong(M_1\otimes_{R_1}N_1)\otimes_S(M_2\otimes_{R_2}N_2):
\]
both sides are the quotient of $M_1\otimes_SM_2\otimes_SN_1\otimes_SN_2$ by the relations
$r_im_i\otimes n_i=m_i\otimes r_in_i$, and the inverse assignment is well defined on them. Apply it with
$M_i=I_{A_i}$, $N_i=I_{A_i^*}$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:iso}.
\end{proof}

\section{The torsion-length law}\label{sec:length}

The law is a statement about lengths of finite graded modules, and it is derived from the decomposition of $T$ over the principal ideal domain $S$.
The only input beyond Section~\ref{sec:factor} is that torsion over $R$ and torsion over $S$ coincide, which is Lemma~\ref{lem:torsion}; the rest is the structure theorem
for finitely generated graded modules over $\kk[y]$ and the tensor product of cyclic modules over it. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} follows the lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:torsion}
$S$ embeds in $R$ by $y\mapsto t^{a_1a_2}$, $R$ is a finite $S$-module, and $R_1$ and $R_2$ are free over
$S$ of ranks $a_2$ and $a_1$. For a finitely generated module over $R$, $R_1$ or $R_2$, the torsion
submodule equals the $S$-torsion submodule. \mD{}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The ranks come from Lemma~\ref{lem:apery}. A non-zero element of $S$ stays non-zero in each domain, so
$S$-torsion is torsion. Conversely, if $rm=0$ with $r\ne0$, take a monic relation
$r^d+s_{d-1}r^{d-1}+\cdots+s_0=0$ over $S$ of least degree; then $s_0\ne0$ and
$s_0m=-(r^{d-1}+\cdots+s_1)rm=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Part~(1) is Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor}. For (2), each $T_i$ is finitely generated over $S$ and has
rank one over $R_i$, so over the graded principal ideal domain $S$ we have
$T_1\cong S^{a_2}\oplus\tor_1$ and $T_2\cong S^{a_1}\oplus\tor_2$ with $\tor_i=\tor(T_i)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:torsion}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
T\cong S^{a_1a_2}\oplus\tor_1^{\,a_1}\oplus\tor_2^{\,a_2}\oplus(\tor_1\otimes_S\tor_2).
\end{equation}
The last three summands are $S$-torsion and the first is free, so their sum is $\tor(T)$. Each $\tor_i$
has finite length and $y$ raises degree, so $y$ is nilpotent on it:
$\tor_1\cong\bigoplus S/(y^{e_i})$ and $\tor_2\cong\bigoplus S/(y^{f_j})$ up to shifts. Since
$S/(y^e)\otimes_SS/(y^f)\cong S/(y^{\min(e,f)})$, the length of $\tor_1\otimes_S\tor_2$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross}
\sum_{i,j}\min(e_i,f_j)=\sum_{j\ge1}b_1(j)b_2(j).
\end{equation}
The identity in~\eqref{eq:cross} holds because $\min(e,f)$ counts the integers $j\ge1$ with $j\le e$ and $j\le f$,
so each pair of blocks contributes once for every $j$ at which both have size at least $j$.

For (3), let $\mathfrak m$ be the graded maximal ideal. Then $R/\mathfrak m=\kk=(R_1/\mathfrak m_1)\otimes_S(R_2/\mathfrak m_2)$
with $y$ acting by zero, and the isomorphism of Proposition~\ref{prop:tensor} applied to $N_i=\kk$ gives
$I_A\otimes_R\kk\cong(I_{A_1}\otimes_{R_1}\kk)\otimes_\kk(I_{A_2}\otimes_{R_2}\kk)$, of dimension
$\mu(A_1)\mu(A_2)$.

For (4), a principal factor has $T_i\cong R_i$, which is torsion-free, and a non-principal factor has
$\tor(T_i)\ne0$ exactly when it has the Huneke--Wiegand property. Since $a_1,a_2\ge1$, part~(2) gives
$\tor(T)\ne0$ iff $\tor(T_1)\ne0$ or $\tor(T_2)\ne0$, and part~(3) gives that $A$ is non-principal iff a
factor is.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} shows the decomposition~\eqref{eq:decomp} and where each term of the length law
comes from. The cross term vanishes when either factor is torsion-free, which is the case for a counterexample
factor, and it is the only term that depends on the action of $y$ and not only on the lengths.

\section{Algorithms and implementation}\label{sec:algorithms}

The theorem is proved, and the computations of Sections~\ref{sec:design} and~\ref{sec:results} test it and its
transcription. This section states the algorithms the computations use, with the proof that makes the direct
torsion computation finite, their cost, and the code that implements them.

\subsection{Degree-wise component count}

For a relative ideal $A$ of a numerical semigroup $\Lambda$ with a generating set $G$ of $\Lambda$, in degree $d$ the
module $T=I_A\otimes_{\kk[\Lambda]}I_{A^*}$ is spanned by the symbols $e_x\otimes e_z$ with $x\in A$, $z\in A^*$ and
$x+z=d$, subject to the relations $e_{x+g}\otimes e_z=e_x\otimes e_{z+g}$ for $g\in G$ (for every $g\in\Lambda$ the
relation holds, because $t^g$ acts on both factors, and the generators suffice: for $g=g_1+\dots+g_k$ with generators $g_i$ the intermediate pairs $(x+g_1+\dots+g_i,\,z+g_{i+1}+\dots+g_k)$ lie in $A\times A^*$, so the relations of the generators imply it). Every relation identifies two basis
symbols, so a basis of $T_d$ is indexed by the connected components of the graph on the pairs $(x,z)$ with $x+z=d$ whose
edges join $(x,z)$ to $(x-g,z+g)$ when $x-g\in A$. Let $\pi\colon T\to K$ be the multiplication map into the field
of fractions. Its image is the product ideal $I_AI_{A^*}\subseteq K$, which is torsion-free, so $\tor(T)\subseteq\ker\pi$;
conversely $T$ has rank one, the image has rank one, so $\ker\pi$ has rank zero and is torsion. Hence
$\tor(T)=\ker\pi$, in agreement with \cite[Section~1]{Leamer2012}, and the image in degree $d$ is the line spanned by $t^d$
whenever the degree has a pair. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:components}
\dim_\kk\tor(T)_d=\max\{0,\,\kappa_d-1\},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_d$ is the number of connected components in degree $d$; this is the torsion number of
\cite[Definition~2.5 and Remark~2.6]{Leamer2012}. The remaining question is where to stop.

\begin{lemma}[degree bound]\label{lem:bound}
Let $x_1,\dots,x_\mu$ generate $A$ and put $M=\max_ix_i+\cond(\Lambda)+\cond(A^*)$. For every degree $d\ge M$ all pairs
$(x,z)\in A\times A^*$ with $x+z=d$ lie in one connected component, so $\tor(T)_d=0$. \mD{}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(x,z)$ be a pair in degree $d$. Since $A=\bigcup_i(x_i+\Lambda)$ we have $x=x_i+\gamma$ with $\gamma\in\Lambda$, and
$z+\gamma\in A^*$ because $A^*+\Lambda\subseteq A^*$. The relation with $g=\gamma$ gives
$e_x\otimes e_z=e_{x_i}\otimes e_{z+\gamma}$, so every pair is joined to an anchor $(x_i,d-x_i)$. Put
$w=\max_jx_j+\cond(\Lambda)$. Then $w-x_i\ge\cond(\Lambda)$, so $g_i=w-x_i\in\Lambda$ and $w=x_i+g_i\in A$. Moreover
$d-w\ge\cond(A^*)$, so $d-w\in A^*$. The relation with $g=g_i$ gives
\[
e_{x_i}\otimes e_{d-x_i}=e_{x_i}\otimes e_{(d-w)+g_i}=e_{x_i+g_i}\otimes e_{d-w}=e_w\otimes e_{d-w},
\]
so every anchor is joined to the pair $(w,d-w)$. All pairs of degree $d$ lie in one component.
\end{proof}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:bound} and equation~\eqref{eq:components}, only the degrees from $a_{\min}+s_{\min}$ up to $M$ need to be scanned (the lemma shows that degree $M$ contributes nothing, and the implementation includes it),
where $a_{\min}$ and $s_{\min}$ are the least elements of $A$ and $A^*$. This is the bound maxgen$(A)+\cond(\Lambda)+\cond(A^*)$
that the implementation scans up to. Algorithm~\ref{alg:components} implements it with a union--find structure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Degree-wise torsion length of $I_A\otimes I_{A^*}$ from the definition.}\label{alg:components}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require generators $G$ of $\Lambda$; generators $X=\{x_1,\dots,x_\mu\}$ of $A$
\State compute $A^*=\bigcap_{x\in X}(\Lambda-x)$, $\cond(\Lambda)$, $\cond(A^*)$, $a_{\min}$, $s_{\min}$; $M\gets\max X+\cond(\Lambda)+\cond(A^*)$
\State $\text{length}\gets0$
\For{$d=a_{\min}+s_{\min},\dots,M$}
  \State $P_d\gets\{(x,d-x):x\in A,\ d-x\in A^*\}$; make each pair of $P_d$ a singleton class
  \For{$(x,z)\in P_d$ and $g\in G$}
    \If{$x-g\in A$} unite the classes of $(x,z)$ and $(x-g,z+g)$ \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State $\text{length}\gets\text{length}+\max\{0,\#\text{classes of }P_d-1\}$
\EndFor
\State \Return length
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The cost is governed by the number of pairs. If $N_{\mathrm{pairs}}$ is the number of $(x,z)\in A\times A^*$ with $x+z\le M$,
the algorithm performs at most $e\,N_{\mathrm{pairs}}$ membership tests and unions, where $e=|G|$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
N_{\mathrm{pairs}}=\#\{(x,z)\in A\times A^*:\ x+z\le M\},\qquad \text{work}\le e\,N_{\mathrm{pairs}}.
\end{equation}
The pairs grow
quadratically with $M$, and Section~\ref{sec:results} measures $N_{\mathrm{pairs}}/M^2$ between \HardRatioMin{} and
\HardRatioMax{} on \NHard{} instances. Membership is a bit test in a mask over a window $[\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi})$ in which every
integer $\ge\mathrm{hi}$ is a member; the window is checked to contain a full tail of the semigroup and of $A^*$, and the
implementation raises an error otherwise, so an upper window that is too small cannot produce a silent wrong value; the lower end is set below every generator by the drivers.

\subsection{Jordan type of \texorpdfstring{$y$}{y}}

The cross term of the law needs the numbers $b_i(j)$. Let $r_k=\dim_\kk y^k\tor(T_i)$. A Jordan block of size $s$ contributes
$\max\{0,s-k\}$ to $r_k$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jordan}
b_i(j)=r_{j-1}-r_j,\qquad r_0=\len(\tor(T_i)).
\end{equation}
In degree $d$ the torsion is spanned by the differences of the component classes. Multiplication by $y^k$ sends a class
$[(x,z)]$ in degree $d$ to the class $[(x+ka,z)]$ in degree $d+ka$, where $a$ is the degree of $y$ ($a_2$ on $R_1$,
$a_1$ on $R_2$), and the image of the differences in degree $d+ka$ has dimension (number of distinct image classes)
$-1$ when that is positive. Summing over the degrees at most $M$ gives $r_k$ and Algorithm~\ref{alg:jordan} stops when $r_k=0$. The restriction to degrees at most $M$ is justified by Lemma~\ref{lem:bound}: the
torsion vanishes in every degree $d\ge M$, so the images in those degrees contribute nothing. The recursion terminates because $y$ is nilpotent on a module of finite length, with at most as many
steps as the length of the longest block. The block sizes are recovered from the differences of the numbers $b(j)$, and equation~\eqref{eq:cross} uses only these numbers.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Jordan type of $y$ on $\tor(I_A\otimes I_{A^*})$ from the classes of Algorithm~\ref{alg:components}.}\label{alg:jordan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the classes of every degree $d\le M$, and the degree $a$ of $y$
\State $r_0\gets$ length from Algorithm~\ref{alg:components}; $k\gets0$
\While{$r_k>0$}
  \State $k\gets k+1$; $r_k\gets0$
  \For{each degree $d$ with at least two classes and $d+ka\le M$}
    \State $I\gets\{\text{class of }(x+ka,z):(x,z)\text{ a representative of a class of degree }d\}$
    \State $r_k\gets r_k+|I|-1$
  \EndFor
\EndWhile
\State \Return $b(j)=r_{j-1}-r_j$ for $j=1,\dots,k$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{The partition criterion and its cost}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:partition} implements the generator form of the criterion of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. A non-principal
ideal has $\mu\ge2$ minimal generators; fixing $x_1$ in the first block removes the symmetry between $S$ and $S'$, and the
proper subsets of the remaining generators that can join it number $2^{\mu-1}-1$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Generator form of the Huneke--Wiegand criterion for a non-principal relative ideal $A=(x_1,\dots,x_\mu)$.}\label{alg:partition}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require generators $G$ of $\Lambda$; minimal generators $x_1<\dots<x_\mu$ of $A$, $\mu\ge2$
\State compute $A^*$
\For{each proper subset $C\subsetneq\{x_2,\dots,x_\mu\}$}
  \State $P\gets\{x_1\}\cup C$; $Q\gets\{x_2,\dots,x_\mu\}\setminus C$
  \State $L\gets(P+A^*)\cap(Q+A^*)$ with $P,Q$ read as the ideals they generate
  \State $\text{meet}\gets$ minimal generators of the ideal $(P)\cap(Q)$; $L'\gets\text{meet}+A^*$
  \If{$L\ne L'$} \Return \textsc{true} \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return \textsc{false}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{lemma}[cost of the partition criterion]\label{lem:cost}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:partition} runs through at most $2^{\mu-1}-1$ splits. Each split performs $O(\mu+e+m)$ shifts and unions of
window masks, where $e=|G|$ and $m$ is the multiplicity of $\Lambda$; the number of splits is exact when no split breaks the
equality. \mD{}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The subsets $C$ of $\{x_2,\dots,x_\mu\}$ other than the whole set number $2^{\mu-1}-1$. Per split, building the ideals $P$
and $Q$ and the two sums with $A^*$ takes $O(\mu)$ shifts, and the minimal generators of $(P)\cap(Q)$ take $O(e)$ shifts
to find; their number is at most $m$, because two minimal generators of a relative ideal cannot be congruent modulo $m$ (their
difference would be a positive multiple of $m$, which lies in $\Lambda$ and makes the larger one redundant), and the sum with
$A^*$ takes one shift per such generator.
\end{proof}

For the gluings of this paper the criterion is exponential only in $\mu$, and $\mu(A)=\mu(A_1)\mu(A_2)$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(3); the two-generated factors give $\mu=4$ and $2^{3}-1=7$ splits.

\section{Theory: consequences and properties}\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Frobenius number and symmetry of a gluing}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:frob}
Let $\Lambda$ be a numerical semigroup (possibly $\N$) and $c\in\Lambda$, $c>0$. Then $\max\Ap(\Lambda,c)=F(\Lambda)+c$. If
$\Lambda$ is symmetric, that is, $x\in\Lambda\iff F(\Lambda)-x\notin\Lambda$ for every integer $x$, then
$x\mapsto F(\Lambda)+c-x$ is an involution of $\Ap(\Lambda,c)$. \mD{}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:apery} with $B=\Lambda$, the class of $r$ modulo $c$ meets $\Lambda$ in $w_r+c\N$, so the integers of that class
outside $\Lambda$ are $w_r-jc$ with $j\ge1$, and the largest integer outside $\Lambda$ is $\max_rw_r-c$. For the second
claim let $x\in\Ap(\Lambda,c)$ and put $F=F(\Lambda)$. Symmetry gives $F-x\notin\Lambda$ because $x\in\Lambda$, and
$F+c-x\in\Lambda$ because $x-c\notin\Lambda$. Hence $F+c-x\in\Lambda$ and $(F+c-x)-c\notin\Lambda$, so it lies in $\Ap(\Lambda,c)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:frobsym}
Let $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ be a gluing. Every integer $n$ has a unique representation
$n=a_1x+a_2z+ka_1a_2$ with $x\in\Ap(\Gamma_1,a_2)$, $z\in\Ap(\Gamma_2,a_1)$ and $k\in\Z$, and $n\in\Gamma$ if and only if $k\ge0$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frob}
F(\Gamma)=a_1F(\Gamma_1)+a_2F(\Gamma_2)+a_1a_2,
\end{equation}
and $\Gamma$ is symmetric when $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ are. \mD{}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Given $n$, the residue of $x$ modulo $a_2$ is that of $n a_1^{-1}$, so $x$ is unique by Lemma~\ref{lem:apery}; likewise $z$ modulo $a_1$.
The integer $n-a_1x-a_2z$ is divisible by $a_1$ and by $a_2$, hence by $a_1a_2$, which defines $k$. If $n\in\Gamma$,
write $n=a_1\gamma_1+a_2\gamma_2$ and $\gamma_1=x'+ia_2$, $\gamma_2=z'+ja_1$ with Ap\'ery elements $x',z'$ and $i,j\ge0$; then
$n=a_1x'+a_2z'+(i+j)a_1a_2$, and uniqueness gives $k=i+j\ge0$. Conversely $k\ge0$ gives $n=a_1(x+ka_2)+a_2z\in\Gamma$.
The largest integer outside $\Gamma$ has $k=-1$ and maximal $x,z$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}
\[
F(\Gamma)=a_1(F(\Gamma_1)+a_2)+a_2(F(\Gamma_2)+a_1)-a_1a_2,
\]
which is~\eqref{eq:frob}. For symmetry write $x^*=F(\Gamma_1)+a_2$ and $z^*=F(\Gamma_2)+a_1$, so $F(\Gamma)=a_1x^*+a_2z^*-a_1a_2$. For $n=a_1x+a_2z+ka_1a_2$,
\[
F(\Gamma)-n=a_1(x^*-x)+a_2(z^*-z)+(-k-1)a_1a_2,
\]
and $x^*-x$ and $z^*-z$ are Ap\'ery elements by Lemma~\ref{lem:frob}. By uniqueness the coefficient of $F(\Gamma)-n$ is $-k-1$,
so $n\in\Gamma$ iff $k\ge0$ iff $-k-1<0$ iff $F(\Gamma)-n\notin\Gamma$.
\end{proof}

This covers the definition of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, which does not require $a_1,a_2$ to be non-generators. The converse (a
gluing is symmetric only when each factor is) is credited to Delorme in \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} and is not needed here.
The decomposition of Corollary~\ref{cor:frobsym} with $k=0$ is the equality $\Ap(\Gamma,a_1a_2)=a_1\Ap(\Gamma_1,a_2)+a_2\Ap(\Gamma_2,a_1)$,
recorded as Remark~18 of \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} with a pointer to \cite{RosalesGarciaSanchez2009} that was not consulted here.

\subsection{Consequences of the theorem}

\begin{corollary}[{\cite[Propositions 9 and 10]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}}]
If $A_1$ is a non-principal ideal with the Huneke--Wiegand property, every extension $a_1A_1+a_2B$ has
it. The extension $a_1A_1+a_2\Gamma_2$ has it if and only if $A_1$ has. \mD{}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:open}
If $A_1$ and $A_2$ are both counterexample ideals, then $a_1A_1+a_2A_2$ is a counterexample ideal with
$\mu(A_1)\mu(A_2)$ minimal generators. \mD{}
\end{corollary}

The first is parts (2) and (4) of the theorem with a non-principal factor that has the property, and with $A_2=\Gamma_2$
principal. The second is part (4) read for two factors without it, together with part~(3). Corollary~\ref{cor:many} follows
from Corollary~\ref{cor:open} and Corollary~\ref{cor:frobsym}. For the iteration, let $\Gamma_1$ be the semigroup obtained at
stage $r$ and $\Gamma_2=\Gamma_P$; gluing parameters exist by the remark in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, and the extension of the
stage-$r$ ideal with $2^r$ generators and a two-generated counterexample of $\Gamma_P$ has $2^{r+1}$ generators.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:invariance}
If every non-principal relative ideal of $\Gamma_1$ and of $\Gamma_2$ has the Huneke--Wiegand property,
then so does every non-principal extension ideal of $a_1\Gamma_1+a_2\Gamma_2$. \mD{}
\end{corollary}

This answers the invariance question of \cite[Section~4]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} for extension ideals. Relative ideals of
$\Gamma$ that are not extensions are not covered. For monomial ideals, the module
$(I_{A_1}\otimes_\kk I_{A_2})/(t^{a_2}\otimes1-1\otimes t^{a_1})$ of the ring-level question in
\cite[Section~4]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} is $I_{A_1}\otimes_SI_{A_2}=I_A$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} answers the special case $R_i=\kk[\Gamma_i]$, $f=t^{a_2}$, $g=t^{a_1}$ of that question (the gluing itself)
for monomial ideals. The general question, for arbitrary local domains $R_1,R_2$ and arbitrary elements $f,g$, stays open, as does a statement for arbitrary modules, which would need the dual of $M_1\otimes_SM_2$ to factor over $S$, which is
not established here.

\subsection{Hilbert series and the two explicit examples}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hilbert}
With $H_B(z)=\sum_{b\in B}z^b$, the Hilbert series of an extension satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert}
H_A(z)=H_{A_1}(z^{a_1})\,H_{A_2}(z^{a_2})\,(1-z^{a_1a_2}).
\end{equation}
\mD{}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:iso} the monomials $t^{a_1x+a_2z'+na_1a_2}$ with $x\in\Ap(A_1,a_2)$, $z'\in\Ap(A_2,a_1)$ and $n\ge0$ are
the monomials of $I_A$, each once. So $H_A(z)=\bigl(\sum z^{a_1x}\bigr)\bigl(\sum z^{a_2z'}\bigr)/(1-z^{a_1a_2})$, while
$H_{A_1}(z^{a_1})=\sum_{x\in\Ap(A_1,a_2)}z^{a_1x}/(1-z^{a_1a_2})$ and likewise for $A_2$. Dividing gives~\eqref{eq:hilbert}.
\end{proof}

Identity~\eqref{eq:hilbert} is the invariant that the computations of Section~\ref{sec:results} check coefficientwise and that
fails without the gluing hypothesis. It decides that an ideal has the extension structure, but not its torsion, so it complements and does not replace the length law.
The two examples that follow are the instances the theorem was built to reach.

\begin{example}\label{ex:pham}
Let $\Gamma_P$ be Pham's semigroup, minimally generated by $56$, $57$, $58$, $63$, $64$, the integers from $70$ to $83$, and
$87$, $89$, $90$, $93$, $95$, $96$, $97$ (multiplicity $56$, embedding dimension $26$, Frobenius number $181$), with the counterexample
ideal $(0,14)$ \cite{Pham2026}. For the gluing $\Gamma=56\Gamma_P+57\Gamma_P$, the extension $56(0,14)+57(0,14)$ is the relative ideal
with minimal generators $0,784,798,1582$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:frobsym}, $\Gamma$ is symmetric with Frobenius number
$56\cdot181+57\cdot181+56\cdot57=23645$, and by Corollary~\ref{cor:open} the extension is a four-generated counterexample. \mD{}
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:mixed}
Let $C_4$ be the member $p=4$ of the family of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}, of multiplicity $96$ and
Frobenius number $311$, with counterexample ideal $(0,24)$. For $\Gamma=96\Gamma_P+89C_4$, the extension
$96(0,14)+89(0,24)$ has minimal generators $0,1344,2136,3480$, and $\Gamma$ is symmetric with Frobenius
number $96\cdot181+89\cdot311+96\cdot89=53599$; by Corollary~\ref{cor:open} the extension is a four-generated counterexample over this symmetric semigroup. \mD{}
\end{example}

\subsection{The two-parameter family}

For the simple gluings $m\N+q\Gamma$ with $\gcd(q,m)=1$ and $m$ the multiplicity of $\Gamma$, the case $\Gamma_2=\N$, $A_2=\N$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, already given by \cite[Proposition~10]{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, turns the family of
\cite{SantibanezLeal2026} into a two-parameter family of symmetric counterexamples. The statement needs the embedding
dimension, which the following proposition proves.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:embdim}
Let $\Gamma_p$ be the symmetric semigroup of the family of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}, $m=24p$ its multiplicity, $p\ge4$, with embedding
dimension $11p$, Frobenius number $78p-1$ and the counterexample ideal $(0,6p)$, and let $q\ge1$ be coprime to $m$. Then $\Gamma_{p,q}=m\N+q\Gamma_p$ is a
symmetric numerical semigroup of multiplicity $24p$, embedding dimension $11p$ and Frobenius number $q(102p-1)-24p$, and
$(0,6pq)$ is a counterexample ideal of $\Gamma_{p,q}$. \mD{}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:frobsym} with $\Gamma_1=\Gamma_p$, $a_1=q$, $\Gamma_2=\N$, $a_2=m\in\Gamma_p$,
$A_1=(0,6p)$ and $A_2=\N$. Symmetry holds since $\N$ and $\Gamma_p$ are symmetric. With $F(\Gamma_p)=78p-1$ and $F(\N)=-1$,
equation~\eqref{eq:frob} gives $q(78p-1)-m+qm=q(102p-1)-24p$. The ideal is a counterexample by Proposition~10 of
\cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, since $A_2=\Gamma_2$. For the embedding dimension, $\Gamma_{p,q}$ is generated by $m$ and the
numbers $qg$ for the generators $g\ne m$ of $\Gamma_p$ (the multiple $qm$ is redundant). If some $qg_i$ were a combination of the others, then
$qg_i=cm+q\sum_{j\ne i}n_jg_j$ with $n_j\ge0$, and reducing modulo $q$ with $\gcd(q,m)=1$ gives $q\mid c$; dividing by $q$ gives
$g_i=(c/q)m+\sum_{j\ne i}n_jg_j$, a redundancy among the generators of $\Gamma_p$, which is impossible. The generator $m$ is not
redundant since every other generator is at least $qm>m$ when $q\ge2$, and for $q=1$ the semigroup is $\Gamma_p$.
\end{proof}

The argument for the embedding dimension is the one of the simple-gluing transfer in the CAOS Research programme (EXP-067, proof section~4). For every fixed
$p$ infinitely many $q$ are coprime to $24p$, so the family has two free parameters.

\subsection{Scope, completion and what is not covered}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:completion}
For the localization and for the completion of $\kk[\Gamma]$ at its graded maximal ideal, a monomial ideal $I_A$ is
Huneke--Wiegand (in the torsion sense) if and only if it is over $\kk[\Gamma]$. Hence parts~(3) and (4) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and the corollaries that depend on them, hold over the graded ring, its localization and its
completion. \mD{}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $R'$ be the localization or the completion of $R=\kk[\Gamma]$ at the graded maximal ideal; $R'$ is a flat $R$-algebra and a domain. Put $T=I_A\otimes_RI_{A^*}$ and $T'=T\otimes_RR'$. Since $I_A$ is finitely presented and $R'$ is flat, $\operatorname{Hom}_{R'}(I_AR',R')=I_{A^*}R'$, so $T'=I_AR'\otimes_{R'}\operatorname{Hom}_{R'}(I_AR',R')$ is the tensor product of the ideal with its dual over $R'$. The sequence $0\to\tor(T)\to T\to T/\tor(T)\to0$ stays exact after $\otimes_RR'$. The module $T/\tor(T)$ is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence embeds in a free $R$-module, and $(T/\tor(T))\otimes_RR'$ embeds in a free $R'$-module, so it is torsion-free. Therefore $\tor(T')=\tor(T)\otimes_RR'$. The module $\tor(T)$ is graded of finite length (Lemma~\ref{lem:bound}), supported at the maximal ideal, so $\tor(T)\otimes_RR'\cong\tor(T)$. Hence $\tor(T')\ne0$ if and only if $\tor(T)\ne0$. An ideal $I_A$ is principal over $R'$ if and only if it is over $R$, since both are decided by $\mu(A)=1$.
\end{proof}

The length law and part~(2) of the theorem are statements for the graded ring, where the torsion is a graded module of finite
length. Pham's counterexample persists under completion by \cite{Pham2026}. What is not covered is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}: relative ideals of $\Gamma$ that are not extensions, and arbitrary modules.

\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design}

The computations test the written proof and its transcription; they do not replace it. Four predictions and two controls are
stated for them.

\subsection{Predictions and controls}

\begin{itemize}
\item[P1] \emph{Length law.} On every case the torsion length of $T$ computed directly on the glued semigroup equals
$a_1\len(\tor(T_1))+a_2\len(\tor(T_2))+\sum_jb_1(j)b_2(j)$ computed from the factors alone; for a counterexample factor
$\len(\tor(T_i))=0$ and the prediction is $a_{3-i}\len(\tor(T_{3-i}))$.
\item[P2] \emph{Characterization.} The partition-criterion predicate of $A$ equals the factor rule of part~(4), agrees with
$\len(\tor(T))>0$ for non-principal $A$, and $\mu(A)=\mu(A_1)\mu(A_2)$. In particular a counterexample factor with a
principal partner ideal gives a counterexample extension, and a non-principal partner ideal with the property gives an
extension with the property.
\item[P3] \emph{Independent route.} The auditor of Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} agrees with the producer on the torsion
length, the predicate, $\mu(A)$ and the Frobenius number.
\item[P4] \emph{Hilbert identity.} Identity~\eqref{eq:hilbert} holds coefficientwise, and the Frobenius number measured on the glued semigroup
equals~\eqref{eq:frob}. This prediction checks the derivation of Corollary~\ref{cor:frobsym} on every case, in addition to the extension structure.
\item[C1] \emph{Not a gluing.} Replacing $a_1$ by a gap of $\Gamma_2$ coprime to $a_2$ breaks identity~\eqref{eq:hilbert}.
\item[C2] \emph{Not an extension.} A perturbed generator makes the ideal fail the extension test, and its Huneke--Wiegand
status is reported and not assumed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The seeded family}

The seeded family is the broad test: it samples many small gluings so that every combination of numbers of generators and every combination of torsion in the factors
occurs, and it is the only place where the cross term is exercised many times. Its factors are small on purpose, because the direct computation on the glued semigroup must finish for every case.
The seeded family has \NSeeded{} gluings drawn with seed \Seed{} by the function \texttt{small\_case} of the first experiment. Each semigroup has
two to four generators sampled from $\{3,\dots,13\}$ with $\gcd=1$ and Frobenius number at most $10$; the multipliers $a_1,a_2$ are
chosen among the integers from $2$ to $7$ lying in the other factor and coprime to each other (the recorded values lie between
\SeededAMin{} and \SeededAMax{}); each ideal is $(0)$ or $(0,u)$ or $(0,u,v)$ with $u,v\in\{1,\dots,10\}$, reduced to its minimal generators.
The sampled factors contain no counterexample ideal, as Section~\ref{sec:results} shows by direct classification; the family tests the law, the
characterization and the Jordan-type cross term where both factors carry torsion.

\subsection{The counterexample-factor family}

The second family is built from the published counterexamples. The large factors are Pham's semigroup $\Gamma_P$ with the ideal $(0,14)$
(B1, counterexample), the member $C_4$ of the family of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026} with the ideal $(0,24)$ (B2, counterexample, $44$ generators,
multiplicity \MultCfour, Frobenius number \FrobCfour), $\Gamma_P$ with the ideal $(0,1)$ (B3, a non-principal ideal with the property, the
control), and $C_5$ with the ideal $(0,30)$ (B4, counterexample, $55$ generators, multiplicity $120$, Frobenius number $389$). The small partners are
$\langle2,3\rangle$, $\langle2,5\rangle$, $\langle3,4\rangle$, $\langle3,5\rangle$, $\langle3,4,5\rangle$ and $\langle4,5,6,7\rangle$, each with the principal ideal $(0)$ and
with the ideal $(0,1)$, which has the property in each of the six partners. The gluing parameters are $a_1$ the least nonzero element of the
partner and $a_2$ the least element of the large factor coprime to $a_1$, so that, writing $\Gamma=a_1\Gamma_B+a_2\Gamma_S$ with $\Gamma_B$ the large factor and $\Gamma_S$ the partner, $a_1\in\Gamma_S$, $a_2\in\Gamma_B$ and $\gcd=1$. This gives $36$ cases for B1 to B3 and $12$
for B4. The status of every ideal, counterexample or not, is recomputed in the run, by the partition criterion and by the torsion length of the factor, and for B1 to B3 the run stops if a status differs from the one assumed for it; the status of B4 is recomputed and recorded. The Frobenius numbers and multiplicities of the large factors are recomputed in the same way.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}

Every comparison is an exact equality of integers or of Boolean values, so there is no sampling error and no confidence interval
applies to a pass. The seeded family fixes the seed, and the counterexample-factor family has no random choice. Run times vary between executions and are
reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation over the cases. The cost of the direct route is measured by the number of pairs $N_{\mathrm{pairs}}$ and by the
window $[\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi})$ of each case, so that the cost model of Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} can be checked against the measurement.
The comparisons made are of three kinds. The first is the equality of the directly computed torsion length with the law, case by case. The second is the agreement of two implementations that
share no code, on the same cases, for the torsion length, the predicate, the number of generators and the Frobenius number. The third is the failure of the controls, which must fail in the way the theorem
predicts and not merely fail. A control that passed would show a defect in the instrument, and one that failed in an unpredicted way would show a defect in the theorem.
No test of significance is applied: a single disagreement of the first kind would refute the law, and the number of cases measures only how many places it was looked for.

\section{Ablation of the hypotheses and of the law}\label{sec:ablation}

An ablation asks what each part of a result contributes. Here the parts are the hypotheses of the theorem, which the controls remove, and the terms of the length law, which are removed in turn from the formula and counted against the direct computation.
The controls of Table~\ref{tab:controls} remove the hypotheses of the theorem one at a time, and each fails in the way the theorem predicts: a gap in place of $a_1$ breaks identity~\eqref{eq:hilbert} in every one of the
$30+2$ cases, a perturbed generator is rejected as a non-extension, and a factor with the property makes the extension have the property. The second ablation removes parts of the length law and counts, over all
cases computed directly, how often the weakened formula still equals the direct length. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} gives the counts.

The ablation shows which parts of the law the data constrain; it does not show that a part the data do not constrain is absent from the truth, and the proof is what establishes the full law.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The factorization explains why the property travels along a gluing in one direction and why the counterexamples travel in the other. Over $S$ a torsion-free module is free, so the torsion of $T_1\otimes_ST_2$ is the torsion of one
factor tensored with a free module of the rank of the other, plus the tensor product of the two torsion parts. If neither factor has torsion there is nothing for either term to carry, and the extension is torsion-free. That is
the case left open by Proposition~9 of \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}, and it is the situation created by the counterexamples of \cite{Pham2026,ChristensenGerkoIyengar2026,SantibanezLeal2026}.

The results of Section~\ref{sec:results} bear on this in three ways. The seeded family, whose factors are all small, never contains a counterexample ideal (Table~\ref{tab:classes}), so by itself it could not have caught an error confined to
the case the paper settles. The second family closes that gap for one-factor counterexamples: the law holds on \NHardCE{} gluings with a counterexample factor, with the extension itself a counterexample in \NHardCEItself{} of them, by a direct
computation on the glued semigroup that does not use the theorem. What remains uncomputed directly is the case with two counterexample factors, the case of the two four-generated examples. There the evidence is the proof, together with the
partition criterion, which is a different characterization of the same property and agrees with the factor rule on all \NHard{} cases of the second family.

The failure cases are the controls and the ablation. Without the gluing hypothesis the identity that underlies Proposition~\ref{prop:iso} fails at once, and the extension test detects an ideal that is not an extension; the perturbed ideal $(0,784,798,1583)$ shows that
the Huneke--Wiegand status is not stable under such a perturbation, since it has the property while the true ideal does not. No negative result arose in the comparisons: no case disagrees with the law or with the factor rule, in either family, by either route.

The theorem implies that counterexamples with $2^r$ generators exist for every $r$, which is a statement about the structure of the set of counterexample ideals and not about the conjecture, which is already refuted. Whether
every counterexample ideal of a gluing of two semigroups whose non-principal ideals all have the property is an extension is open (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\emph{Internal validity.} The computations share two ingredients: the inputs of the two routes (the generators of both factors and the gluing parameters) and the bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:bound}. An error in the choice of inputs would
affect both routes, which is why the controls check that a violation of the gluing hypothesis is detected. The bound is proved, so an implementation fault in it is the remaining risk; the cost model is checked against a brute-force enumeration of the
loops by a unit test. A pass on \NSeeded{}$+$\NHard{} cases is evidence for the transcription and not a proof; the proof is Section~\ref{sec:length}.

\emph{External validity.} The factors of the second family are small partners, at most four generators, and four large factors, three of them counterexamples, with Frobenius numbers up to $389$; the cross term is exercised in only
six cases of that family and in \NSeededCross{} seeded cases, and the Jordan blocks of size larger than one matter in a single recorded case. Gluings with larger Frobenius numbers are not computed directly: the two explicit examples have
Frobenius numbers $23645$ and $53599$, and the cost model of Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} puts the scan beyond the cost of the recorded cases. At the ratio of \HardRatioMin{} the examples would need more than $7\cdot10^{7}$ pairs, since
the scan limit is at least $\max X+\cond(\Lambda)\ge25228$ for the first one; this is an extrapolation and not a measurement. A prior estimate, made before the second family was computed, put the scan for these instances at more than $10^{9}$ pairs; it was not computed either.

\emph{Construct validity.} The Huneke--Wiegand property is used in the torsion sense for non-principal ideals, and a counterexample ideal is a non-principal ideal whose tensor product with its dual is torsion-free. Under the definition
of \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013} a principal ideal also counts as Huneke--Wiegand; the two conventions differ only on principal ideals, which the theorem treats separately.

\emph{Scope.} The results concern monomial (relative) ideals of numerical semigroup rings over any field, graded, localized or completed (Proposition~\ref{prop:completion}). Theorem~\ref{thm:main} decides extension ideals of a gluing only. Whether the gluing of two
semigroups whose non-principal ideals all have the Huneke--Wiegand property can carry a counterexample ideal that is not an extension is open, as is a module version for arbitrary modules, for which the dual of $M_1\otimes_SM_2$ would need to factor over $S$.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

For a gluing of numerical semigroups, the tensor product $I_A\otimes I_{A^*}$ of an extension ideal factors over $\kk[y]$, its torsion has length $a_1\len(\tor(T_1))+a_2\len(\tor(T_2))+\sum_jb_1(j)b_2(j)$, the number of minimal generators multiplies, and the
Huneke--Wiegand property holds exactly when a factor is a non-principal ideal with it. The case both factors are counterexamples gives a counterexample, which yields counterexample ideals with $2^r$ minimal generators and two explicit four-generated examples with Frobenius numbers $23645$ and $53599$.
The law was checked directly on \NSeeded{} seeded gluings and on \NHard{} gluings with large factors, \NHardCE{} of them with a counterexample factor and \NHardCEItself{} with an extension that is a counterexample, by two routes that share no code, and no case disagrees. The next steps are the non-extension ideals of a gluing and a module version of the factorization.
