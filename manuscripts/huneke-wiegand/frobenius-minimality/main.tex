\begin{abstract}
Son Pham publicly identified a counterexample to the Huneke--Wiegand conjecture in the class of
two-generated monomial ideals over symmetric numerical semigroup rings, subsequently verified by
Professor Craig Huneke. We preserve that discovery priority and answer two different questions:
how small can such an example be, and does the same semigroup/ideal class contain an explicit
infinite family? We prove by proof-carrying exact computation that the least Frobenius number is
$181$, attained by Pham's semigroup at ideal shift $14$.
First, an implementation of the complete Blanco--Rosales tree exhausts all $48{,}954$ symmetric
semigroups with Frobenius number below $69$ and all $1{,}503{,}391$ associated gap cases. An
independent Boolean route checks $1{,}156$ fixed-pair UNSAT certificates for the same range. We then
introduce a one-hot shift-selector CNF that asks one existence question per Frobenius number. Its
DRAT certificates exclude every odd value from $69$ through $179$, while the formula at $181$ is
satisfiable and decodes exactly to the public semigroup and shift. A separately generated fixed-pair
formula returns the same model. An independent audit rehashes all $228$ files in the minimality
search, totaling $760{,}081{,}739$ bytes, rechecks both SAT models, and reproduces the strict-order
manifest with no mismatch. Complete theorem-tree enumeration at $F=69,71,73,75$ provides an
object-level cross-check on another $79{,}790$ semigroups and $2{,}933{,}163$ gap cases. A second
projected enumeration proves that shift $14$ is the only feasible shift at $F=181$ and that the
public membership vector is the only semigroup there. Both terminal DRAT proofs are freshly
rechecked in an independent 12-file audit. Thus Pham's pair is the unique normalized pair attaining
the Frobenius minimum in this class. Finally, for every integer $p\ge4$ we construct a symmetric
numerical semigroup $\Gamma_p$ of multiplicity $24p$, Frobenius number $78p-1$, conductor $78p$,
and embedding dimension $11p$, for which
$I_p=(t^{24p},t^{30p})$ is nonprincipal and rigid. This family theorem is deductive: seven exact
interval-sum identities prove closure, symmetry, and $D_s=E_s+E_s$; a finite sweep through
$p=300$ and an independently implemented audit are supporting checks. For every family member we
also determine the value semigroup of the endomorphism ring exactly. It has multiplicity $24p$,
Frobenius number $54p-1$, conductor $54p$, genus $38p-1$, and embedding dimension $12p$.
It is nonsymmetric, and the endomorphism ring is strictly larger than the original ring. Recent
endomorphism-center criteria then show uniformly that the ideal remains rigid over its
endomorphism ring but is not reflexive there, with the adjacent Ext and Tor obstruction groups
nonzero. We further prove that this endomorphism semigroup has Cohen--Macaulay type and reduced
type $10p$, hence maximal reduced type, and that its completed semigroup ring is not almost
Gorenstein. Finally, the traces of the ideal and its endomorphism ring both equal the conductor of
the finite birational extension. We determine that common value ideal exactly and prove the
identity
\[
\ell_{R_p}(R_p/(R_p:E_p))=\ell_{R_p}(E_p/R_p)=p+1.
\]
The equality of the two lengths is a general consequence of one-dimensional Gorenstein local
duality, while the exact common ideal and its value $p+1$ are the family-specific data. We further
prove that this conductor is nonstable and compute its complete one-step stability defect:
\[
\ell_{R_p}(T_p^2/t^{4s}T_p)=14p.
\]
The full reduction sequence is also exact: $t^{4s}R_p$ is a minimal reduction of $T_p$ with
reduction number four, successive Sally-quotient lengths
$23p-1,14p,2p,1,0$, and Hilbert--Samuel coefficients $e_0(T_p)=24p$ and $e_1(T_p)=39p$.
The associated graded ring of $T_p$ nevertheless has depth zero: its complete
Valabrega--Valla module is concentrated in one degree, where it has length $p$. We compute
the exact Hilbert series and show that its numerator has only positive coefficients despite this
failure of Cohen--Macaulayness. We then determine the complete zeroth local cohomology: it is
$k^p$, concentrated in degree zero and annihilated by the full homogeneous maximal ideal. Hence
the tangent cone is Buchsbaum but not Cohen--Macaulay, with unbounded Buchsbaum invariant $p$;
its quotient by the finite-length torsion is Cohen--Macaulay with an exact Hilbert series. Over the
polynomial Noether normalization induced by the minimal reduction, we determine the entire graded
module: the torsion consists of $p$ exponent-one cyclic summands, while the free quotient has rank
$24p$ and explicit shifts. This yields the complete minimal resolution, projective dimension one,
regularity four, top-local-cohomology $a$-invariant three, and the exact parameter-section identity
$\ell(G_p/x_pG_p)=25p=e_0(T_p)+I(G_p)$. Finally, the exact identity
$T_p^2=\mathfrak m_pT_p$ identifies the special fiber canonically with
$G_p/H^0(G_p)$. It is Cohen--Macaulay of type $10p+1$, but its Artinian socle lies in degrees two
and four, so it is neither level nor Gorenstein. We then determine its defining ideal: in the
polynomial ring on its $10p$ minimal degree-one generators, it has exactly $50p^2-17p$ minimal
quadrics and the single additional cubic $X_0^2X_{3p}-X_p^3$, with no other minimal equation.
Thus its relation type is three and it is not Koszul. The all-parameter component calculation is
an exact Presburger verification, supported by a separately encoded graph audit; its solver trust
boundary is stated explicitly. From the same exact h-vector, Artinian socle, and first Betti row,
we then determine the homological edges over the full presentation ring. Its projective dimension
is $10p-1$, its regularity is four,
\[
\beta_{2,3}=\frac{2p(500p^2-330p+31)}{3},
\]
the complete last row has ranks $10p$ in shift $c+2$ and one in shift $c+4$ for
$c=10p-1$, and $\beta_{c-1,c+3}=8p$. The canonical module has $10p$ minimal generators in
degree $-1$ and one in degree $-3$. We then determine the first previously unknown interior
strand. With the offset grading $\deg X_a=(1,a)$,
\[
\beta_{2,4}=8p,
\qquad
\beta_{3,4}=\frac{p(5p-1)(500p^2-440p+47)}{2},
\]
and the $8p$ second syzygies occur with multiplicity one exactly at offsets
$\{3p+a:a\in\mathcal E_{p,1},\ a\ge6p\}$. Relative squarefree-divisor chains and an integral
unit matching give the upper bound; an exact quadratic colon and minimal mapping cone give the
matching lower bound. We then complete the second Betti row. The only remaining possible entries
are
\[
\beta_{2,5}=p(2p-3),\qquad \beta_{2,6}=0.
\]
The degree-five support is the union of three explicit intervals, with pair-sum multiplicities on
the outer blocks and a plateau profile on the middle block. An integral offset matching gives a
unit Smith form, so the result again holds over every field. This completes one homological row,
  not the remaining Betti table. We next determine the degree-five third syzygies. If
  \(H_p=\{a\in\mathcal E_{p,1}:a\ge6p\}\), then
  \[
  \beta_{3,(5,b)}=\#\{\{a,c\}\subset H_p:a<c,\ a+c=b-3p\},
  \qquad \beta_{3,5}=4p(8p-1).
  \]
  The support is \([15p+1,39p-3]\setminus\{33p-1\}\). An integral normal form leaves a primitive
  free basis indexed by unordered pairs of the \(8p\) high cubic-colon variables. Combining this
  with the complete second row and the Hilbert numerator gives
  \[
  \beta_{4,5}=\frac{2p(5p-1)(10p-3)(100p^2-110p+13)}{3},
  \]
  completing internal degree five over every field. Finally, we identify the complete cubic-colon
  quotient with the canonical idealization of the $p$-th Veronese rational normal curve ring.
  Its multigraded Hilbert numerator and an integral relative normal form give
  \[
  \beta_{3,6}=\frac{8p(7p^2-12p+2)}{3}
  \]
  over every field, with support
  \([3p+4,29p-5]\setminus([6p-3,6p+1]\cup[9p-3,9p])\). In total degree seven,
  a zero-vertex Boolean matching leaves only triangles at the single residual hole $6p-1$.
  Adjoining a missing vertex from \(\{1,2,3,4\}\) gives each one a same-offset tetrahedron whose
  reduced boundary has that triangle as its unique critical face with coefficient \(\pm1\).
  Hence \(\beta_{3,7}=0\) integrally, and the complete third row over every field is
  \[
  \begin{aligned}
  \beta_{3,4}&=\frac{p(5p-1)(500p^2-440p+47)}2,&
  \beta_{3,5}&=4p(8p-1),\\
  \beta_{3,6}&=\frac{8p(7p^2-12p+2)}3,&
  \beta_{3,7}&=0.
  \end{aligned}
  \]
  with no other shifts. Its total rank is
  \(\beta_3=p(7500p^3-7988p^2+2025p-133)/6\).
  The same canonical idealization determines much more about the cubic-colon quotient
  \(D_p=P_p/(\mathfrak q_p:f_p)\). Put \(c=2p-2\), \(m=8p\), and
  \(\lambda_{c,a}=c\binom ca-\binom c{a+1}-\binom c{a-1}\). Its complete graded Betti polynomial is
  \[
  B_{D_p}^{P_p}(x,z)=(1+xz)^m
  \left(1+\sum_{a=1}^{c-1}\lambda_{c,a}x^az^{a+1}+x^cz^{c+2}\right).
  \]
  Hence every free-module rank and shift is known over every field, with projective dimension
  \(10p-2\) and regularity two. A colon-intersection pullback then proves that the quadratic
  quotient \(A_p=P_p/\mathfrak q_p\) has depth one and regularity two. The grading gap makes the
  cubic mapping cone minimal, so
  \[
  B_{C_p}^{P_p}(x,z)=B_{A_p}^{P_p}(x,z)+xz^3B_{D_p}^{P_p}(x,z).
  \]
  Consequently the complete regularity-three and regularity-four strands of the special fiber are
  explicit binomial convolutions of the displayed colon polynomial, over every field. This removes
  every comparison-rank ambiguity from the cubic cone. Finally, put
  \(\tau_p=8p-1+p(p+1)/2\). The Artinian reduction of the high-variable kernel is a two-layer
  incidence module. Its first missing offset gives a unique primitive cokernel cell, and a
  separate unit pivot proves that the connecting map from the colon quotient cannot hit it. Thus,
  over every field,
  \[
  \beta_{p,(p+2,\tau_p)}(K_p)
  =\beta_{p,(p+2,\tau_p)}(A_p)
  =\beta_{p,(p+2,\tau_p)}(C_p)=1.
  \]
  We then classify every primitive zero row of the incidence cokernel. If
  $R_b=\{g:b-g\in H_p\}$, the coordinate $e_F\otimes v_b$ is a zero row exactly when
  $R_b\subseteq F$, giving a closed free-summand count in every homological degree. The block
  $b=10p+t$, $2\le t\le p-2$, supplies consecutive kernel classes in degrees
  $p+1,\ldots,2p-3$. The first connecting cell does not repeat the unit-pivot mechanism, but its
  complete target quotient exposes characteristic dependence:
  \[
  \beta_{5,(7,87)}(A_4)=\beta_{5,(7,87)}(C_4)
  =\begin{cases}4,&\kfield=\mathbf F_2,\\3,&\kfield=\mathbf F_3.\end{cases}
  \]
  The corresponding integral kernel cokernel is
  $\mathbb Z^4\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. This determines exact lower-strand points and a
  characteristic-dependent multigraded cell, not the two complete lower strands, the
  differential matrices, or the full special-fiber resolution. The results remain confined
to two-generated monomial ideals over symmetric numerical semigroup rings and do not classify
arbitrary Gorenstein domains and modules.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $R$ be a one-dimensional Gorenstein local domain and let $M$ be a nonzero finitely generated
nonfree module. The Huneke--Wiegand conjecture predicted nontrivial torsion in
$M\otimes_R\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$ \cite{HunekeWiegand1994}. For a numerical semigroup
$\Gamma\subseteq\N$, the semigroup ring $\kfield[t^\Gamma]$ is Gorenstein exactly when $\Gamma$ is
symmetric. This specialization converts part of the module problem into finite additive
combinatorics. Garc\'ia-S\'anchez and Leamer proved broad positive classes and reported a
NumericalSgps check for every symmetric semigroup with Frobenius number below $69$
\cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}.

In 2026, Son Pham published a symmetric numerical semigroup and a two-generated monomial ideal for
which the predicted torsion is absent \cite{Pham2026}. Professor Craig Huneke supplied an
independent verification archived with the public candidate record. That example has Frobenius
number $181$. The discovery settles the original conjecture negatively, but does not by itself say
whether $181$ is small, minimal, or typical.

This paper reports computer-assisted classification theorems and a deductive family theorem.
Claims are labeled \textbf{[MV]} when
they are verified by exact machine computation and independently checked artifacts, and
\textbf{[D]} when derived in the text. No conjectural claim is needed for the main theorem.

\section{The finite semigroup problem}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{numerical semigroup} is a cofinite additive submonoid $\Gamma\subseteq\N$. Its Frobenius
number is $F(\Gamma)=\max(\N\setminus\Gamma)$. It is \emph{symmetric} when
\[
  n\in\Gamma \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(\Gamma)-n\notin\Gamma
  \qquad (0\le n\le F(\Gamma)).
\]
\end{definition}

Fix a symmetric semigroup $\Gamma$ with Frobenius number $F$ and a positive gap
$s\notin\Gamma$. Normalize the corresponding two-generated relative ideal to $(0,s)$. Define
\begin{align*}
E_s &= \{n\in\N:n,n+s\in\Gamma\},\\
D_s &= \{n\in\N:n,n+s,n+2s\in\Gamma\}.
\end{align*}
The Garc\'ia-S\'anchez--Leamer reduction identifies the counterexample condition with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigidity}
  D_s=E_s+E_s.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every length-two arithmetic sequence of step $s$ in $\Gamma$ decomposes as a sum of
two length-one sequences \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013,Landeros2024}.

\begin{lemma}[Automatic inclusion, {[D]}]\label{lem:auto}
For every numerical semigroup $\Gamma$ and every positive integer $s$,
$E_s+E_s\subseteq D_s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $a,b\in E_s$, then $a,a+s,b,b+s\in\Gamma$. Closure gives
$a+b\in\Gamma$, $a+b+s=a+(b+s)\in\Gamma$, and
$a+b+2s=(a+s)+(b+s)\in\Gamma$. Hence $a+b\in D_s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite window, {[D]}]\label{lem:window}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rigidity} holds if and only if
$D_s\cap[0,2F+1]\subseteq E_s+E_s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only the reverse inclusion needs checking by Lemma~\ref{lem:auto}. Every integer at least $F+1$
belongs to $\Gamma$, hence to $E_s$ and $D_s$. Let $m=\min E_s$; then $m\le F+1$. For every
$n\ge m+F+1$, both $m$ and $n-m$ lie in $E_s$, so $n\in E_s+E_s$. Since
$m+F+1\le2F+2$, every $n\ge2F+2$ is automatic. The displayed finite window is therefore complete.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Parity, {[D]}]\label{lem:parity}
The Frobenius number of a symmetric numerical semigroup is odd.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $F$ were even, symmetry at $n=F/2$ would require $F/2$ to belong to $\Gamma$ exactly when it
does not belong to $\Gamma$.
\end{proof}

\section{The public model and independent calibration}

Pham's public semigroup is
\begin{align*}
\Gamma=\langle&56,57,58,63,64,70,71,72,73,74,75,76,77,78,79,80,81,82,83,\\
&87,89,90,93,95,96,97\rangle,
\end{align*}
with the ideal generated by $t^{56}$ and $t^{70}$, hence normalized shift $s=14$
\cite{Pham2026}. An independent Singular/4ti2 calculation and a separate standard-library finite
route verify $F=181$, conductor $182$, genus $91$, symmetry, and equality~\eqref{eq:rigidity}
\textbf{[MV]}. The Singular route uses a $322$-generator toric standard basis; both reduced ideal
differences vanish. The hypersurface control $\langle4,5\rangle$ rejects equality with explicit
residues \textbf{[MV]}.

The endomorphism overring of the ideal has value semigroup
\[
  v(\operatorname{End}_R(I))=\Gamma\cup\{101,107,181\},
\]
with Frobenius number $125$, genus $88$, and type $24$ \textbf{[MV]}. This explains why positive
criteria that require a Gorenstein endomorphism ring do not apply, but it does not imply minimality.

\section{Complete reproduction below 69}

For fixed odd $F$, irreducible numerical semigroups are exactly the symmetric ones. Blanco and
Rosales construct all of them as a rooted tree \cite{BlancoRosales2013}. Starting from
\[
 C(F)=\{0,(F+1)/2,(F+1)/2+1,\ldots\}\setminus\{F\},
\]
a child replaces a suitable minimal generator $x>F/2$ by $F-x$. Their theorem supplies exact
child conditions, completeness, and a unique parent.

Our direct implementation validates every node for closure, symmetry, genus, minimal generators,
and its theorem parent. It then checks every gap by Lemma~\ref{lem:window} and retains a first
element of $D_s\setminus(E_s+E_s)$ whenever equality fails. The full range $F=1,3,\ldots,67$
contains $48{,}954$ semigroups and $1{,}503{,}391$ gap cases; none is rigid \textbf{[MV]}. At
$F=11$ the implementation returns the six nodes listed by Blanco--Rosales, while an invalid
mutation is rejected by closure at $(1,1)$.

An independent route generates one fixed-pair DIMACS formula for each $(F,s)$, including shifts
that are forced not to be gaps. CaDiCaL emits a DRAT proof for every UNSAT result, and DRAT-trim

\section{One selector formula per Frobenius value}

The fixed-pair route duplicates the same semigroup constraints once per shift. We instead introduce
Boolean membership variables $h_n$ for $0\le n\le F$ and one-hot variables $q_s$ for

\subsection{Semigroup and selector clauses}

The CNF contains units $h_0$ and $\neg h_F$, the symmetry clauses
\[
 (h_n\lor h_{F-n})\land(\neg h_n\lor\neg h_{F-n}),
\]
and closure clauses $\neg h_a\lor\neg h_b\lor h_{a+b}$ for $a+b\le F$. The selectors satisfy one
at-least-one clause, all pairwise at-most-one clauses, and
$q_s\Rightarrow\neg h_s$. Thus every model chooses exactly one gap.

For $0\le n\le2F+1$, guarded Tseitin clauses impose, under the selected $q_s$,
\begin{align*}
 e_n &\longleftrightarrow h_n\land h_{n+s},\\
 d_n &\longleftrightarrow h_n\land h_{n+s}\land h_{n+2s},
\end{align*}
where $h_j$ is the constant true for $j>F$. For each unordered decomposition $n=a+b$, a variable
$p_{n,a,b}$ is equivalent to $e_a\land e_b$. Finally,
\[
 d_n\Rightarrow\bigvee_{a+b=n}p_{n,a,b}.
\]
The automatic inclusion from Lemma~\ref{lem:auto} need not be duplicated in the formula.

\begin{proposition}[Selector equisatisfiability, {[D]}]\label{prop:cnf}
For positive odd $F$, the selector CNF is satisfiable if and only if there exist a symmetric
numerical semigroup $\Gamma$ with Frobenius number $F$ and a gap $s$ satisfying
$D_s=E_s+E_s$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The semigroup clauses decode exactly a symmetric semigroup of Frobenius number $F$, and the one-hot
clauses decode a unique gap. Because exactly one guard is true, all $e_n$ and $d_n$ equal the
definitions for that gap. The $p$ equivalences and final disjunctions impose
$D_s\subseteq E_s+E_s$ through $2F+1$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:auto} and~\ref{lem:window} give global
equality. Conversely, from a rigid pair assign $h$ and $q$ from the pair, assign $e,d$ by their
definitions, and assign each $p$ by its conjunction. Every clause is then true.
\end{proof}

$363{,}912$ clauses, selects $s=14$, and decodes exactly to the public membership vector. A newly
generated fixed-pair formula at $(181,14)$ returns the same vector.

\begin{lemma}[Projected blocker, {[D]}]\label{lem:blocker}

\[
 B(a)=\bigvee_{a_i=1}\neg x_i\;\lor\!\bigvee_{a_i=0}x_i

projection.
\end{lemma}

clause cannot affect either statement.
\end{proof}

positive coordinates would not have this property.

\subsection{The parametric blocks}

Fix an integer $p\ge4$ and put $s=6p$. All intervals below are integer intervals. Define subsets
of $[0,s-1]$ by
\begin{align*}
A_p&=[0,p]\cup[3p,4p-2],\\
B_p&=\bigl([p+1,3p-1]\setminus\{2p-1\}\bigr)
      \cup\{4p\}\cup[5p-1,6p-1],\\
C_p&=[0,2p]\cup[3p,5p-2].
\end{align*}
Define $\Gamma_p\subseteq\N$ by the membership blocks
\begin{equation}\label{eq:familyblocks}
\{0\},\quad 4s+A_p,\quad [5s,6s-1],\quad 6s+B_p,\quad
8s+C_p,\quad[9s,13s-2],\quad[13s,\infty),
\end{equation}
with every unlisted nonnegative integer below $13s$ a gap.

For subsets of $[0,s-1]$, let $\operatorname{low}(X+Y)$ denote the sums below $s$, and let
$\operatorname{carry}(X+Y)$ denote the sums at least $s$ after subtraction of $s$.

\begin{lemma}[Residue identities, {[D]}]\label{lem:residues}
For every $p\ge4$,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{low}(A_p+A_p)&=C_p,&
\operatorname{carry}(A_p+A_p)&=[0,2p-4],\\
\operatorname{low}(A_p+B_p)&=[p+1,6p-1],&
\operatorname{carry}(A_p+B_p)&=[0,4p-3],\\
\operatorname{low}(B_p+B_p)&=[2p+2,6p-2],&
\operatorname{carry}(B_p+B_p)&=[0,6p-2],\\
\operatorname{low}(A_p+C_p)&=[0,6p-2].&&
\end{align*}
Moreover,
\begin{align}
[0,2p-4]\cup[p+1,6p-1]&=[0,6p-1],\label{eq:join1}\\
[0,4p-3]\cup[2p+2,6p-2]&=[0,6p-2].\label{eq:join2}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split $A_p$ into its two displayed intervals and split the punctured component of $B_p$ at
$2p-1$. Pairwise interval addition, followed by separating sums below and above $s=6p$, gives the
seven displayed identities. The singleton $4p$ fills the endpoint removed from the middle of
$B_p$. All interval joins are immediate except~\eqref{eq:join1}: its two pieces meet exactly when
$2p-3\ge p+1$, equivalently $p\ge4$. For~\eqref{eq:join2}, the overlap condition is
$4p-2\ge2p+2$, which also holds for $p\ge4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Infinite family, {[D]}]\label{thm:family}
For every integer $p\ge4$, the set $\Gamma_p$ in~\eqref{eq:familyblocks} is a symmetric numerical
semigroup with multiplicity $24p$, Frobenius number $78p-1$, conductor $78p$, and embedding
dimension $11p$. Let
\[
R_p=\kfield[t^{\Gamma_p}]_{(t^n:n\in\Gamma_p\setminus\{0\})},\qquad
I_p=(t^{24p},t^{30p})R_p.
\]
Then $R_p$ is Gorenstein and $I_p$ is nonprincipal and rigid. Hence the pairs $(R_p,I_p)$ form an
infinite family of counterexamples in the two-generated monomial-ideal class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the semigroup assertions. The only sums of positive displayed members that can stay
below the conductor have starting levels among $4,5,6,8$. Lemma~\ref{lem:residues} handles the
non-full target blocks: $A_p+A_p$ produces $8s+C_p$ and part of level $9$;
$A_p+B_p$, $B_p+B_p$, and $A_p+C_p$ land exactly inside the declared high blocks and never produce
the Frobenius residue $s-1$ in level $12$. Sums involving the full level $5$ block either fill a
declared level or enter the conductor tail. Thus $\Gamma_p$ is closed.

Conversely, the lower three blocks generate every displayed member. The identity
$\operatorname{low}(A_p+A_p)=C_p$ generates level $8$. The presence of $0\in A_p$ makes
$(4s+A_p)+[5s,6s-1]$ fill level $9$, and two level-$5$ elements fill level $10$ and residues
$0,\ldots,s-2$ of level $11$; the residue $s-1\in B_p$ fills the remaining point. Three elements
from $4s+A_p$ generate residues $0,\ldots,3p$ of level $12$, while
$\operatorname{low}(B_p+B_p)=[2p+2,s-2]$ completes that level through $13s-2$.
Adding $4s$ to $[9s,13s-2]$ gives $[13s,17s-2]$, and
$(4s+1)+(13s-2)=17s-1$. These are $4s$ consecutive generated integers, so repeated addition of
the multiplicity gives the entire tail.

Therefore the multiplicity is $4s=24p$, the conductor is $13s=78p$, and the Frobenius number is
$13s-1=78p-1$. Every member of the three lower blocks lies below $7s$, whereas a sum of two
positive members is at least $8s$; all are minimal generators. Since
$|A_p|=2p$, $|B_p|=3p$, and the full block has size $6p$, the embedding dimension is $11p$.

Symmetry is blockwise about $F=13s-1$. The residue-reflection complement of $A_p$ is exactly
$C_p$. The full level-$5$ block reflects to the empty level-$7$ block. In $B_p$, exactly one
residue from each pair $\{r,s-1-r\}$ occurs: the puncture $2p-1$ pairs with the singleton $4p$,
and the remaining displayed intervals pair with their omitted intervals. The gaps below $4s$
reflect to the full range $[9s,13s-2]$, and $0$ reflects to the gap $F$. Hence $\Gamma_p$ is
symmetric, so $R_p$ is Gorenstein.

It remains to prove rigidity. Normalize $I_p$ to $J_p=(1,t^s)$ and use $E_s,D_s$ from
Section~2. Direct inspection of~\eqref{eq:familyblocks} gives the nontrivial residue layers
\begin{align*}
E_4&=A_p, & E_5&=B_p, & E_8&=C_p,\\
E_9=E_{10}&=[0,s-1], & E_{11}=E_{12}&=[0,s-2],\\
D_8&=C_p, & D_9&=[0,s-1],\\
D_{10}=D_{11}=D_{12}&=[0,s-2].
\end{align*}
All lower $D$ layers are empty; in particular $D_4$ is empty because $A_p\cap B_p=\varnothing$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:residues} and~\eqref{eq:join1}--\eqref{eq:join2} compute the five load-bearing
layers of $E_s+E_s$:
\[
D_8=\operatorname{low}(A_p+A_p),\quad
D_9=\operatorname{carry}(A_p+A_p)\cup\operatorname{low}(A_p+B_p),
\]
\[
D_{10}=\operatorname{carry}(A_p+B_p)\cup\operatorname{low}(B_p+B_p),\quad
D_{11}=\operatorname{carry}(B_p+B_p),\quad
D_{12}=\operatorname{low}(A_p+C_p).
\]
At levels $13$ and $14$, the zero residue in $A_p$ translates full high $E$ blocks. At levels
$15$ and $16$, those high blocks omit only $s-1$, and the residues $0,1\in A_p$ fill both the
interval and its final endpoint. Level $17$ is filled by $E_4+E_{13}$; levels $18$, $19$, and
$20$ by $E_9+E_9$, $E_9+E_{10}$, and $E_{10}+E_{10}$; and level $21$ by
$E_8+E_{13}$ because $0\in C_p$. Every level from $22$ onward is filled by
$E_9+E_{q}$ with $q\ge13$, where the conductor makes $E_q$ full. Thus
$D_s\subseteq E_s+E_s$; Lemma~\ref{lem:auto} gives equality.

Finally, $s$ is a gap. If $J_p$ were principal, write $J_p=xR_p$. Since $1\in J_p$, there is
$r\in R_p$ with $xr=1$, so $rJ_p=R_p$ and $(r,rt^s)=R_p$. In the local ring, one of these two
generators must be a unit. Positive valuation excludes $rt^s$, so $r$ is a unit and
$t^s\in R_p$, a contradiction. The ideal is therefore nonprincipal and rigid.
The multiplicities $24p$ are distinct, so the family contains infinitely many distinct rings.
\end{proof}

\section{Pseudo-Frobenius anatomy and reduced type}

For a numerical semigroup $H$, write
\[
\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{f\notin H:f+h\in H\text{ for every }h\in H\setminus\{0\}\}.
\]
Its Cohen--Macaulay type is $|\operatorname{PF}(H)|$. For complements inside $[0,s-1]$, define
\begin{align*}
B_p^c&=[0,p]\cup\{2p-1\}\cup[3p,4p-1]\cup[4p+1,5p-2],\\
Q_p^c&=[0,p]\cup\{2p-1\}\cup[2p+1,4p-1]\cup[4p+1,6p-1],\\
C_p^c&=[2p+1,3p-1]\cup[5p-1,6p-1].
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Type anatomy, {[D]}]\label{thm:type}
For every integer $p\ge4$,
\[
\operatorname{PF}(\Lambda_p)
=(6s+B_p^c)\cup(7s+Q_p^c)\cup(8s+C_p^c).
\]
Consequently the type and reduced type of $\kfield[[\Lambda_p]]$ are both $10p$. Thus the ring has
maximal reduced type. The semigroup is not almost symmetric, and $\kfield[[\Lambda_p]]$ is not
almost Gorenstein.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The multiplicity of $\Lambda_p$ is $4s$ and its conductor is $9s$. Every gap in the final
multiplicity window $[5s,9s-1]$ is therefore pseudo-Frobenius. Since level 5 is full, these gaps
are precisely the complements of $B_p,Q_p,C_p$ at levels 6, 7, and 8. Their cardinalities are
$3p,5p,2p$.

It remains to exclude a pseudo-Frobenius number below $5s$. The full level-5 generator block gives
explicit witnesses. For a gap $r$ at level 0 with $r>0$, add $5s+(s-r)$ to reach the level-6 gap
of residue zero. At level 1, use $5s$ when $r=0$ and $5s+(s-r)$ otherwise. At levels 2 and 3,
add $5s+(s-1-r)$ to reach residue $s-1$ in a missing later block.

For a level-4 gap $4s+r$, if $r\notin C_p$ add the generator $4s$. The remaining residues lie in
\[
C_p\setminus A_p=[p+1,2p]\cup[4p-1,5p-2].
\]
In the first interval add $4s+(2p+1-r)$; in the second add $4s+(5p-1-r)$. Both added residues lie
in $[1,p]\subset A_p$, while the resulting level-8 residue is a gap of $C_p$. Thus no lower gap
is pseudo-Frobenius, proving the formula.

Reduced type counts the gaps in the last multiplicity window, so it is also $10p$

\[
2g-(F+\operatorname{type})=12p-1>0.
\]
The almost-symmetric genus identity therefore fails, giving the final claims.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact trace and conductor ideals}

Let $E_p=\operatorname{End}_{R_p}(J_p)=\kfield[[\Lambda_p]]$ and define the reflected block
\[
H_p=(s-1)-Q_p=\{2p-1,4p-1\}\cup[4p+1,5p-2].
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact trace and conductor, {[D]}]\label{thm:trace}
For every integer $p\ge4$,
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{R_p}(J_p)=R_p:E_p=\operatorname{tr}_{R_p}(E_p)=T_p,
\]
where
\begin{align*}
v(T_p)={}&(4s+A_p)\cup(5s+(A_p\cup B_p))\cup(6s+B_p)\cup(8s+C_p)\\
&\cup[9s,13s-2]\cup[13s,\infty).
\end{align*}
Moreover,
\[
\ell_{R_p}(R_p/T_p)=\ell_{R_p}(E_p/R_p)=p+1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because $1\in J_p$,
\[
v(R_p:J_p)=W_p=\{n\in\Gamma_p:n+s\in\Gamma_p\}.
\]
Intersecting adjacent blocks of~\eqref{eq:familyblocks} gives
\[
(W_4,W_5,W_6,W_7,W_8)=(A_p,B_p,\varnothing,\varnothing,C_p),
\]
with full blocks at levels 9 and 10, blocks $[0,s-2]$ at levels 11 and 12, and full blocks from
level 13 onward. Since $W_p$ is a $\Gamma_p$-ideal,
\[
v(\operatorname{tr}_{R_p}(J_p))=W_p\cup(s+W_p),
\]
which is exactly the displayed set $v(T_p)$.

For the conductor $R_p:E_p$, only the values in
\[
\Lambda_p\setminus\Gamma_p=(7s+Q_p)\cup\{13s-1\}
\]
add constraints. The terminal singleton excludes $n=0$ and imposes no condition on positive
$n\in\Gamma_p$. Every value at level 4 passes the $7s+Q_p$ translates: a failure in the sole
level-12 gap would require $a+q=2s-1$, but $a+q\le8p-2<12p-1$. At level 5, residue $r$ fails
exactly when $r+q=s-1$ for some $q\in Q_p$, hence exactly when $r\in H_p$. All values from level 6
onward translate into the conductor tail. Since
\[
[0,s-1]\setminus H_p=A_p\cup B_p,
\]
this proves $v(R_p:E_p)=v(T_p)$. The conductor is an $E_p$-ideal, so
$\operatorname{tr}_{R_p}(E_p)=E_p(R_p:E_p)=R_p:E_p$.

Relative to $\Gamma_p$, the common ideal omits exactly $\{0\}\cup(5s+H_p)$, giving colength
$p+1$. The endomorphism formula gives exactly $p+1$ values in
$\Lambda_p\setminus\Gamma_p$, proving the balanced identity.
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\enspace}{}Exact conductor-stability defect}

Dey proves that for a finite birational extension $R\subseteq S$ of a one-dimensional
Gorenstein local ring, the conductor $R:S$ is stable if and only if $S$ is Gorenstein
\cite[Corollary~3.7]{Dey2022}. Theorem~\ref{thm:type} gives
$\operatorname{type}(E_p)=10p>1$, so this criterion already shows that $T_p=R_p:E_p$ is
nonstable. The following computation quantifies that failure.

\begin{theorem}[Exact stability defect, {[D]}]\label{thm:stability}
For every integer $p\ge4$,
\[
v(T_p^2)=(8s+C_p)\cup[9s,13s-2]\cup[13s,\infty).
\]
Moreover,
\begin{align*}
v(T_p^2)\setminus v(t^{4s}T_p)={}&
8s+\bigl([p+1,2p]\cup[4p-1,5p-2]\bigr)\\
&\cup9s+\bigl(\{2p-1,4p-1\}\cup[4p+1,5p-2]\bigr)\\
&\cup10s+\bigl([0,p]\cup\{2p-1\}\cup[3p,4p-1]
                 \cup[4p+1,5p-2]\bigr)\\
&\cup11s+[0,s-1]\\
&\cup12s+\bigl([2p+1,3p-1]\cup[5p-1,s-2]\bigr)\\
&\cup\{17s-1\}.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\[
\ell_{R_p}(T_p^2/t^{4s}T_p)=14p.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $D=[0,s-1]$, $D^-=[0,s-2]$, and $U_p=A_p\cup B_p$. For residue sets $X,Y\subseteq D$,
write $L(X,Y)$ for sums below $s$ and $H(X,Y)$ for sums at least $s$, translated by $-s$.
Expanding the defining sets into integer intervals and using
$[a,b]+[c,d]=[a+c,b+d]$ gives
\begin{align*}
L(A_p,A_p)&=C_p,\\
H(A_p,A_p)\cup L(A_p,U_p)&=D,\\
H(A_p,U_p)\cup L(A_p,B_p)\cup L(U_p,U_p)&=D,\\
H(A_p,B_p)\cup H(U_p,U_p)\cup L(U_p,B_p)&=D,\\
H(U_p,B_p)\cup L(B_p,B_p)\cup L(A_p,C_p)&=D^-.
\end{align*}
These five identities list all contributions, with carries, to levels $8$ through $12$ of
$T_p^2$. They give $(8s+C_p)\cup[9s,13s-2]$ and exclude the inherited gap $13s-1$.

\[

\]
fills the intervening endpoint. This proves the square formula.

displayed defect. The six block cardinalities are $2p,p,3p,6p,2p-1,1$, whose sum is $14p$.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\enspace}{}Conductor reduction number and Hilbert data}

For an ideal $I$, a subideal $Q$ is a \emph{reduction} when $I^{r+1}=QI^r$ for some $r$;
the least such $r$ is the reduction number with respect to $Q$ \cite{NorthcottRees1954}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact conductor reduction sequence, {\bf[D]}]\label{thm:reduction}
For every integer $p\ge4$, put $Q_p=t^{4s}R_p\subset T_p$. Then $Q_p$ is a minimal reduction of
$T_p$ with reduction number four. More precisely,
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
n&0&1&2&3&n\ge4\\ \hline
\ell_{R_p}(T_p^{n+1}/Q_pT_p^n)&23p-1&14p&2p&1&0.
\end{array}
\]
The Hilbert--Samuel function satisfies
\[
\ell_{R_p}(R_p/T_p^n)=24pn-39p\qquad(n\ge4),
\]
and consequently $e_0(T_p)=24p$ and $e_1(T_p)=39p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} supplies the square. The residue identity
$\operatorname{low}(A_p+C_p)=[0,s-2]$ then gives
\[
v(T_p^3)=[12s,13s-2]\cup[13s,\infty).
\]
Adding $4s+A_p$ fills $[16s,17s-2]$ and the tail from $17s$; because $1\in A_p$,
$(4s+1)+(13s-2)=17s-1$ fills the intervening endpoint. Hence
\[
v(T_p^4)=[16s,\infty),\qquad v(T_p^5)=[20s,\infty)=v(Q_pT_p^4).
\]
Multiplication proves the same equality for every later power. Subtracting the shifted square and
cubic profiles gives
\begin{align*}
v(T_p^3)\setminus v(Q_pT_p^2)
 &=12s+\bigl([2p+1,3p-1]\cup[5p-1,6p-2]\bigr)\cup\{17s-1\},\\
v(T_p^4)\setminus v(Q_pT_p^3)&=\{17s-1\}.
\end{align*}
Their cardinalities are $2p$ and $1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} gives $14p$ at the preceding
stage. Finally, subtracting $v(Q_p)=4s+\Gamma_p$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:trace} leaves eight
disjoint blocks of sizes
\[
2p-1,\quad5p,\quad3p,\quad2p,\quad3p,\quad6p,\quad2p-1,\quad1,
\]
whose sum is $23p-1$. The strict nonzero quotients and terminal equality prove reduction number
four. Since $T_p$ is maximal-ideal primary in dimension one, the one-generated reduction is
minimal.

The parameter ideal $Q_p$ has colength $4s=24p$. Multiplication by its nonzerodivisor generator
gives
\[
\ell(T_p^i/T_p^{i+1})=24p-\ell(T_p^{i+1}/Q_pT_p^i).
\]
Summing through $i=n-1$ and using
$(23p-1)+14p+2p+1=39p$ proves the Hilbert formula and coefficients.
\end{proof}

\section{The depth-zero conductor tangent cone}

Let
\[
G_p=\operatorname{gr}_{T_p}(R_p)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}T_p^n/T_p^{n+1}

and retain the minimal reduction $Q_p=(x)$, where $x=t^{4s}$. The
Valabrega--Valla criterion detects regularity of the initial form $x^*$ through the intersections
$Q_p\cap T_p^{n+1}$ \cite{ValabregaValla1978}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact tangent-cone defect, {\bf[D]}]\label{thm:tangent}
For every integer $p\ge4$, put
\[
K_p=\{2p-1,4p-1\}\cup[4p+1,5p-2].

\[
v\!\left(\frac{Q_p\cap T_p^2}{Q_pT_p}\right)=9s+K_p,
\qquad
\ell_{R_p}\!\left(\frac{Q_p\cap T_p^2}{Q_pT_p}\right)=p,

while
\[
Q_p\cap T_p^{n+1}=Q_pT_p^n
\qquad(n=0\ \text{and every }n\ge2).
\]
Consequently
\[
\dim G_p=1,\qquad \operatorname{depth}G_p=0,
\]
so $G_p$ is not Cohen--Macaulay. Its Hilbert series is
\[
H_{G_p}(z)=
\frac{(p+1)+(9p-1)z+12pz^2+(2p-1)z^3+z^4}{1-z}.
\]
In particular every coefficient of the numerator is positive.
\end{theorem}

\[

             \cup[13s,\infty),\\
v(Q_pT_p)={}&(8s+A_p)\cup(9s+U)\cup(10s+B_p)\cup(12s+C_p)
             \cup[13s,\infty),\\
v(T_p^2)={}&(8s+C_p)\cup\bigl([9s,\infty)\setminus\{13s-1\}\bigr).
\end{align*}
Since $A_p\subset C_p$ and $C_p\subset[0,s-2]$, intersection leaves exactly the additional
block $9s+(D\setminus U)=9s+K_p$ beyond $v(Q_pT_p)$. This proves the first formula and its

\subsection{Complete graded torsion and Buchsbaumness}

Let $\mathcal M_p=(\mathfrak m_p/T_p)\oplus G_{p,+}$ be the homogeneous maximal ideal of
$G_p$. The dimension-one local-cohomology criterion says that $G_p$ is Buchsbaum precisely when
$\mathcal M_pH^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)=0$ \cite{DAnnaMezzasalmaMicale2009,DAnnaMicaleSammartano2011}.
The cited semigroup formulas concern maximal-ideal filtrations; here we compute the local
cohomology directly for the conductor filtration.

\begin{theorem}[Buchsbaum tangent cones with unbounded invariant, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:buchsbaum}
For every integer $p\ge4$, the complete zeroth local cohomology is concentrated in degree zero:
\[
H^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)
=\operatorname{span}_{\kfield}
 \{t^{5s+h}+T_p:h\in K_p\}\cong \kfield^p.
\]
Moreover
\[
\mathcal M_pH^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)=0.
\]
Consequently $G_p$ is Buchsbaum but not Cohen--Macaulay, and its Buchsbaum invariant is
\[
I(G_p)=\ell(H^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p))=p.
\]
The quotient $G_p/H^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)$ is Cohen--Macaulay and has Hilbert series
\[
\frac{1+(10p-1)z+12pz^2+(2p-1)z^3+z^4}{1-z}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A homogeneous class represented by $a\in T_p^n$ lies in zeroth local cohomology exactly when
$aT_p^k\subseteq T_p^{n+k+1}$ for some $k$. Thus
\[
H^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)_n=
\bigcup_{k\ge1}
\frac{(T_p^{n+k+1}:T_p^k)\cap T_p^n}{T_p^{n+1}}.
\]
Here saturation by $G_{p,+}$ and by $\mathcal M_p$ agree because the maximal ideal of the
Artinian degree-zero ring $R_p/T_p$ is nilpotent.

Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} gives $v(T_p^k)=[4ks,\infty)$ for every $k\ge4$. Therefore, for a
monomial $t^v$ and any $k\ge4$,
\[
t^vT_p^k\subseteq T_p^{n+k+1}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v\ge4(n+1)s.
\]
Any containment at a smaller power persists after multiplication to a power at least four, so
this stable threshold is the complete colon saturation.

The exact profiles give
\[
v(R_p)\setminus v(T_p)=\{0\}\cup(5s+K_p).
\]
The degree-zero threshold $4s$ deletes the unit and retains $5s+K_p$. In degrees one, two, and
three, the consecutive power profiles agree from the respective thresholds $8s$, $12s$, and
$16s$ onward. For every $n\ge4$, stabilization makes them agree from $4(n+1)s$ onward. Hence the
displayed $p$ degree-zero classes are the complete local cohomology.

It remains to test the full homogeneous maximal ideal. If $r=5s+h$ with $h\in K_p$, every product
of $t^r$ with $\mathfrak m_p$ or $T_p$ has value at least $9s$. Both $T_p$ and $T_p^2$ contain
all such values except $13s-1$. Equality $r+a=13s-1$ would force $a=8s-1-h$, strictly between
levels $7s$ and $8s$, but the exact ring profile has no value there. Thus
\[
t^r\mathfrak m_p\subseteq T_p,
\qquad
t^rT_p\subseteq T_p^2.
\]
The degree-zero and positive parts of $\mathcal M_p$ both annihilate every torsion class. The
dimension-one criterion proves Buchsbaumness; Theorem~\ref{thm:tangent} proves depth zero. The
Buchsbaum invariant is therefore the displayed length $p$.

Quotienting by the full zeroth local cohomology leaves a one-dimensional ring of positive depth,
hence a Cohen--Macaulay ring. Since the torsion Hilbert series is the constant $p$, subtracting it
from Theorem~\ref{thm:tangent}'s Hilbert series gives the asserted numerator.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The complete module over the Noether normalization}

Put $F_p=\kfield[x_p]$, where $x_p=(t^{4s})^*$ has degree one. The reduction theorem makes
$F_p\to G_p$ a Noether normalization. This viewpoint follows the graded-module framework of
Cortadellas Benitez and Zarzuela \cite{CortadellasZarzuela2007,CortadellasZarzuela2009}, while the
conductor-filtration calculation below is derived directly from Theorems~\ref{thm:reduction},
\ref{thm:tangent}, and~\ref{thm:buchsbaum}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Noether-normalization module, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:module}
For every integer $p\ge4$, there is a graded $F_p$-module isomorphism
\begin{align*}
G_p\cong{}& (F_p/(x_p))^p\oplus F_p\oplus F_p(-1)^{10p-1}\oplus F_p(-2)^{12p}\\
&\oplus F_p(-3)^{2p-1}\oplus F_p(-4).
\end{align*}
Its minimal graded $F_p$-resolution is
\[
0\longrightarrow F_p(-1)^p\longrightarrow
F_p^{p+1}\oplus F_p(-1)^{10p-1}\oplus F_p(-2)^{12p}
\oplus F_p(-3)^{2p-1}\oplus F_p(-4)
\longrightarrow G_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{pd}_{F_p}(G_p)=1,
\qquad \operatorname{reg}_{F_p}(G_p)=4,
\qquad a(G_p)=3,
\]
and
\[
\ell(G_p/x_pG_p)=25p=e_0(T_p)+I(G_p).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:reduction} gives $T_p^{n+1}=t^{4s}T_p^n$ for $n\ge4$, so $G_p$ is finite over
$F_p$. Since $G_p/x_pG_p$ has finite length, $F_p$-torsion equals zeroth local cohomology.
Theorem~\ref{thm:buchsbaum} therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{tors}_{F_p}(G_p)=H^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)\cong(F_p/(x_p))^p,
\]
with all generators in degree zero. In particular, every torsion elementary divisor has exponent
one.

The quotient $C_p=G_p/H^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)$ is finite and torsion-free over the principal
ideal domain $F_p$, hence graded free. Its Hilbert series from Theorem~\ref{thm:buchsbaum} is
\[
H_{C_p}(z)=\frac{1+(10p-1)z+12pz^2+(2p-1)z^3+z^4}{1-z}.
\]
Since $H_{F_p(-d)}(z)=z^d/(1-z)$, the numerator uniquely gives the five displayed free shifts.
The quotient is projective, so the exact sequence with the torsion submodule splits after choosing

\subsection{The conductor special fiber}

Let
\[
\mathcal F_p=\mathcal F(T_p)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}T_p^n/\mathfrak m_pT_p^n
\]
be the special fiber. There is a natural graded-algebra surjection $G_p\to\mathcal F_p$ because
$T_p^{n+1}\subseteq\mathfrak m_pT_p^n$.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical fiber cone and exact type, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:fiber}
For every integer $p\ge4$,
\[
T_p^2=\mathfrak m_pT_p,
\qquad
T_p^{n+1}=\mathfrak m_pT_p^n\quad(n\ge1),
\]
and the natural map induces a graded-algebra isomorphism
\[
G_p/H^0_{\mathcal M_p}(G_p)\cong\mathcal F_p.
\]
The ring $\mathcal F_p$ is one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay, with
\begin{align*}
\mathcal F_p\cong{}&F_p\oplus F_p(-1)^{10p-1}\oplus F_p(-2)^{12p}\\
&\oplus F_p(-3)^{2p-1}\oplus F_p(-4)
\end{align*}
as a graded $F_p$-module. Its multiplicity is $24p$, its reduction number and regularity are four,
and $a(\mathcal F_p)=3$. The Artinian reduction $B_p=\mathcal F_p/x_p\mathcal F_p$ has
\[
h(B_p)=(1,10p-1,12p,2p-1,1)
\]
and graded socle dimensions
\[
(0,0,10p,0,1).
\]
Thus $\operatorname{type}(\mathcal F_p)=10p+1$, and $\mathcal F_p$ is neither level nor
Gorenstein.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Minkowski addition of the exact value blocks in Theorems~\ref{thm:trace}
and~\ref{thm:stability} gives
\[
v(\mathfrak m_pT_p)=(8s+C_p)\cup[9s,13s-2]\cup[13s,\infty)=v(T_p^2).
\]
Indeed, the lowest block follows from
$A_p+A_p=C_p\cup[6p,8p-4]$; the latter interval and all remaining block sums lie at level at least
$9s$ and fill every ring value there. The Frobenius value $13s-1$ remains absent. Equality of the
monomial value sets proves $T_p^2=\mathfrak m_pT_p$, and multiplication by $T_p^{n-1}$ proves the
higher identities.

The degree-zero kernel of $G_p\to\mathcal F_p$ is $\mathfrak m_p/T_p$, which is exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:buchsbaum}'s $H^0$. In degree $n\ge1$, the kernel is
$\mathfrak m_pT_p^n/T_p^{n+1}=0$. This proves the natural algebra isomorphism. Cohen--Macaulayness
and the displayed free module now follow from Theorems~\ref{thm:buchsbaum}
and~\ref{thm:module}; the Hilbert, multiplicity, reduction, regularity, and $a$-invariant claims
follow immediately.

For the type, reduce modulo the regular element $x_p$. Direct subtraction of
$\mathfrak m_pT_p^n+t^{4s}T_p^{n-1}$ from the exact power blocks gives the displayed $h$-vector.
The degree-two nonsocle offsets are precisely
\[

unique degree-four class. That class is socle because degree five vanishes. Hence the only socle
dimensions are $10p$ in degree two and one in degree four, proving the final claims.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The defining ideal of the special fiber}

We now refine Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber} from a module decomposition to a minimal algebra
presentation. All intervals in this subsection are integer intervals. Let
\(\mathcal E_{p,d}\) be the offset basis of \((\mathcal F_p)_d\), after subtracting the base
value \(4sd\). The exact power calculation gives
\begin{align}
\mathcal E_{p,1}={}&[0,p]\cup[3p,4p-2]\cup[6p,8p-2]\cup[8p,10p-2]
 \cup\{10p\}\notag\\
&\cup[11p-1,12p-1]\cup[13p+1,14p-2]\cup[14p,15p-1]
 \cup\{16p\}\cup[17p-1,18p-1],\label{eq:fiber-e1}\\
\mathcal E_{p,2}={}&[0,2p]\cup[3p,5p-2]\cup[6p,24p-1],\notag\\
\mathcal E_{p,3}={}&[0,24p-1]\setminus\{6p-1\},\qquad
\mathcal E_{p,4}=\mathcal E_{p,5}=[0,24p-1].\label{eq:fiber-cumulative}
\end{align}
In particular, \(|\mathcal E_{p,1}|=10p\). Set
\[
P_p=\kfield[X_a:a\in\mathcal E_{p,1}]
\]
and let \(\varphi_p:P_p\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal F_p\) send \(X_a\) to the class of
\(t^{4s+a}\). Write \(\mathfrak a_p=\ker\varphi_p\).

\begin{theorem}[One-cubic defining ideal, {\bf[MV]}]
\label{thm:presentation}
For every integer \(p\ge4\),
\[
\mathfrak a_p=\bigl((\mathfrak a_p)_2,
f_p\bigr),\qquad
f_p=X_0^2X_{3p}-X_p^3.
\]
Equivalently, the first graded Betti row over \(P_p\) is
\[
\beta^{P_p}_{1,2}(\mathcal F_p)=50p^2-17p,\qquad
\beta^{P_p}_{1,3}(\mathcal F_p)=1,\qquad
\beta^{P_p}_{1,j}(\mathcal F_p)=0\quad(j\ge4).
\]
Hence \(\mu(\mathfrak a_p)=50p^2-17p+1\), the relation type is three, and
\(\mathcal F_p\) is not Koszul.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The semigroup monomials are linearly independent. Consequently, a monomial in the variables
\(X_a\) is zero in \(\mathcal F_p\) exactly when its total offset is absent from the corresponding
\(\mathcal E_{p,d}\), and two nonzero monomials have the same image exactly when their total
offsets agree. There is no linear equation. Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber} gives
\(\dim_{\kfield}(\mathcal F_p)_2=22p\), so
\begin{equation}
\beta^{P_p}_{1,2}(\mathcal F_p)
=\binom{10p+1}{2}-22p=50p^2-17p.\label{eq:quadratic-count}
\end{equation}

The offsets \(0,p,3p\) belong to \(\mathcal E_{p,1}\), and
\(0+0+3p=p+p+p\), so \(f_p\in\mathfrak a_p\). It is not generated by quadrics. Indeed, the
only unordered pair in \(\mathcal E_{p,1}\) with sum \(3p\) is \(\{0,3p\}\), the only pair with
sum \(2p\) is \(\{p,p\}\), and the only pair with sum zero is \(\{0,0\}\). Thus neither
monomial in \(f_p\) admits a nontrivial quadratic replacement, and the two cannot be joined by a
chain of quadratic moves.

It remains to prove that this is the only higher equation. Suppose the complete kernel is known
through degree \(d-1\), where \(d\in\{3,4,5\}\). For a fixed total \(t\), form the graph
\[
V_{d,t}=\{a\in\mathcal E_{p,1}:t-a\in\mathcal E_{p,d-1}\}.
\]
Two vertices \(a,c\) are adjacent when
\[
t-a-c\in\mathcal E_{p,d-2}.
\]
This is exactly the commutative factorization move between \(X_a\) times the preceding class of
offset \(t-a\) and \(X_c\) times the class of offset \(t-c\). Add a zero vertex when the same
factorization makes the alternate degree-\((d-1)\) remainder invalid. Therefore the degree-\(d\)
quotient by all lower equations has one basis element for each nonzero graph component. It follows
that
\begin{equation}
\beta^{P_p}_{1,d}(\mathcal F_p)
=\#\{\text{nonzero state components in degree }d\}-|\mathcal E_{p,d}|.
\label{eq:component-defect}
\end{equation}

For degree three, the exact component reduction is

\subsection{Homological edges over the presentation ring}

The h-vector and socle in Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber}, together with the complete first Betti row in
Theorem~\ref{thm:presentation}, force more of the minimal resolution than the defining equations
alone suggest. Put
\[
N=10p,\qquad c=N-1=10p-1,
\]
and write $\beta_{i,j}=\beta^{P_p}_{i,j}(\mathcal F_p)$.

\begin{theorem}[Homological edge data, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:homological-edge}
For every integer $p\ge4$,
\[
\operatorname{pd}_{P_p}(\mathcal F_p)=c,
\qquad
\operatorname{reg}_{P_p}(\mathcal F_p)=4,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i,j}(-1)^i\beta_{i,j}z^j
=(1-z)^c\bigl(1+(10p-1)z+12pz^2+(2p-1)z^3+z^4\bigr).
\label{eq:betti-polynomial}
\end{equation}
The first linear-syzygy count is
\begin{equation}
\beta_{2,3}=\frac{2p(500p^2-330p+31)}{3}.
\label{eq:linear-first-syzygies}
\end{equation}
The complete last homological row and a penultimate extremal entry are
\begin{equation}
\beta_{c,c+2}=10p,\qquad
\beta_{c,c+4}=1,\qquad
\beta_{c,j}=0\ (j\notin\{c+2,c+4\}),\qquad
\beta_{c-1,c+3}=8p.
\label{eq:betti-edge}
\end{equation}
Finally, the canonical module $\omega_{\mathcal F_p}$ has $10p$ minimal generators in degree
$-1$ and one in degree $-3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial ring $P_p$ has depth $N$, whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber} gives
$\operatorname{depth}_{P_p}(\mathcal F_p)=\dim\mathcal F_p=1$. Auslander--Buchsbaum therefore
gives projective dimension $N-1=c$. The same theorem gives the h-polynomial
\[
h_p(z)=1+(10p-1)z+12pz^2+(2p-1)z^3+z^4.
\]
Since $\mathcal F_p$ is Cohen--Macaulay, its regularity is $\deg h_p=4$. In particular,
regularity over the full presentation ring is already determined, rather than a separate open
question. Taking Hilbert series in a minimal $P_p$-resolution gives
\[
\frac{h_p(z)}{1-z}
=\frac{\sum_{i,j}(-1)^i\beta_{i,j}z^j}{(1-z)^N},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:betti-polynomial}.

There is no linear equation, so minimality gives no shift below $i+1$ in homological degree $i$.
Consequently the coefficient of $z^3$ in \eqref{eq:betti-polynomial} is
$\beta_{2,3}-\beta_{1,3}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:presentation} gives $\beta_{1,3}=1$, and expansion
yields
\[
\beta_{2,3}
=1-\binom{c}{3}+c\binom{c}{2}-12pc+(2p-1)
=\frac{2p(500p^2-330p+31)}{3}.
\]
Independently, the degree-three part of the first free module has dimension
$N\beta_{1,2}+\beta_{1,3}$ and maps onto $(\mathfrak a_p)_3$. Since
\[
\dim(P_p)_3=\binom{N+2}{3},\qquad
\dim(\mathcal F_p)_3=24p-1,
\]
its kernel has dimension
\[
N(50p^2-17p)+1-\left(\binom{N+2}{3}-(24p-1)\right),
\]
which simplifies to the same value.

Now reduce modulo $X_0$, whose image $x_p$ is the regular parameter from
Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber}. Because $X_0$ is regular on both $P_p$ and $\mathcal F_p$, tensoring the
minimal resolution with $Q_p=P_p/(X_0)$ remains exact and minimal. Hence its Betti numbers equal
those of the Artinian reduction $B_p=\mathcal F_p/x_p\mathcal F_p$ over the polynomial ring
$Q_p$ in $c$ variables. Top Koszul homology identifies
\[
\operatorname{Tor}^{Q_p}_c(B_p,\kfield)_j
\cong \operatorname{Soc}(B_p)_{j-c}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:fiber} gives socle dimensions $10p$ in degree two, one in degree four, and zero
otherwise. This proves the complete last row in \eqref{eq:betti-edge}.

In internal degree $c+3$, regularity four kills every contribution in homological degree at most
$c-2$, while the last-row calculation kills $\beta_{c,c+3}$. Thus the coefficient of
$z^{c+3}$ in \eqref{eq:betti-polynomial} is
$(-1)^{c-1}\beta_{c-1,c+3}$. Only the last two terms of $h_p$ contribute, giving
\[
(-1)^c(2p-1)+(-1)^{c-1}c=(-1)^{c-1}8p.
\]
Therefore $\beta_{c-1,c+3}=8p$.

Finally,
\[
\omega_{\mathcal F_p}
=\operatorname{Ext}^{c}_{P_p}(\mathcal F_p,P_p(-N)).
\]
Minimality lets us read its minimal generators from the dual of the last free module. A summand
$P_p(-j)$ contributes in degree $N-j$. The $10p$ last summands with $j=c+2=N+1$ therefore give
degree $-1$, and the single summand with $j=c+4=N+3$ gives degree $-3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[First interior Betti strand, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:interior-strand}
For every integer \(p\ge4\) and every field \(\kfield\),
\begin{equation}

\qquad

\label{eq:first-interior}
\end{equation}
Moreover, \(\beta_{2,(4,b)}=1\) exactly for

and it is zero at every other offset. Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal S_p=\{3p+a:a\in\mathcal E_{p,1},\ a\ge6p\}.

\begin{proof}
We first identify the exact finite chain complexes. Put
\[
H_p=\langle(1,a):a\in\mathcal E_{p,1}\rangle\subseteq\N^2,
\qquad
M_p=(t^{(n,u)}:u\ge24p)\subseteq\mathbb Z[H_p].

The offset calculation~\eqref{eq:fiber-cumulative} identifies the special fiber with the base
change of \(\mathbb Z[H_p]/M_p\). For a bidegree \(w=(j,b)\), define the squarefree divisor pair
\begin{align*}
\Delta_w&=\{F\subseteq\mathcal E_{p,1}:w-(|F|,\sum F)\in H_p\},\\
\Gamma_w&=\{F\in\Delta_w:t^{w-(|F|,\sum F)}\in M_p\}.
\end{align*}
The relative squarefree-divisor construction is standard for affine semigroup quotients by
monomial ideals \cite{BrunsHerzog1997}; see also \cite{Stamate2018} for the semigroup-ring Betti
framework and its characteristic warning. Here the identification is immediate: the
bidegree-\(w\) Koszul chain group in homological degree \(r\) has one basis vector for every
\(r\)-subset \(F\) satisfying
\[
b-\sum F\in\mathcal E_{p,j-r},
\]
and signed deletion is exactly the relative simplicial boundary. Hence
\begin{equation}
\beta_{i,w}=\dim_{\kfield}\widetilde H_{i-1}(\Delta_w,\Gamma_w;\kfield).
\label{eq:relative-betti}
\end{equation}

We next give an integral upper bound in total degree four. Order
\(\mathcal E_{p,1}\) increasingly. In every offset block, scan the vertices in this order and
pair a still-unmatched cell \(F\) with \(F\cup\{v\}\) whenever both survive; within a scan use
increasing dimension and lexicographic order. Every pair is a unit boundary entry, and the pivot
order strictly increases along a reversed matched arrow, so this is an acyclic integral matching.
Substitution of~\eqref{eq:fiber-e1}--\eqref{eq:fiber-cumulative} leaves the following complete
critical-cell list in relative dimensions zero and one. In the middle row write \(b=6p+r\).

$6p\le b\le7p-3$ & $\{0\},\{r+2\}$ & $\{p,3p+r\}$\\
all other $b$ & immaterial & none\\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\]
In every remaining case write \(a=u+v\), with
\(u\in\mathcal E_{p,1}\) and \(v\in V_p\), and use
\[
(0,0,3p,a)\sim(0,3p,u,v)\sim(p,3p,u,v-p)
\sim(p,p,u,v+p)\sim(p,p,p,a).
\]
The block formula~\eqref{eq:fiber-e1} verifies that these cases cover all high offsets.

They cover no low offset. For a factorization of total at most \(7p-2\), color a generator odd
exactly when it lies in \([3p,4p-2]\). Every equality of two pair sums preserves the parity of
the number of odd generators: the sole cross-block possibility replaces two middle-block
generators by one low and one high generator. If \(a\le p\), the two factorizations of
\(X_af_p\) have parities one and zero; if \(3p\le a\le4p-2\), they have parities zero and one.
Thus
\begin{equation}
(\mathfrak q_p:f_p)_1
=\operatorname{span}_{\kfield}\{X_a:a\in\mathcal E_{p,1},\ a\ge6p\},
\label{eq:quadratic-colon}
\end{equation}
which has dimension \(8p\).

Apply the mapping cone to
\[
0\longrightarrow P_p/(\mathfrak q_p:f_p)(-3)
\xrightarrow{\ f_p\ }P_p/\mathfrak q_p
\longrightarrow\mathcal F_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
Every linear generator in~\eqref{eq:quadratic-colon} supplies a homological-degree-two summand
shifted by four. No cancellation is possible: its maps to the shift-three free summand by a
linear entry and to the quadratic-generator module by degree-two entries. Thus there is one
nonzero class at each offset \(3p+a\) in~\eqref{eq:interior-support}. Combined with
\eqref{eq:relative-upper}, this proves \(\beta_{2,4}=8p\), the exact support, and freeness over
\(\mathbb Z\): the matching is integral and each mapping-cone class is primitive.

Finally, no minimal degree-four equation exists by Theorem~\ref{thm:presentation}, and minimal
shifts exclude homological degree at least four from internal degree four. The coefficient of
\(z^4\) in~\eqref{eq:betti-polynomial} is therefore
\[
\beta_{2,4}-\beta_{3,4}
=-\frac{p(2500p^3-2700p^2+675p-63)}{2}.
\]
Substitution of \(\beta_{2,4}=8p\) gives the second formula in~\eqref{eq:first-interior}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The complete second Betti row}

The preceding theorem leaves only internal degrees five and six unresolved in homological degree
two. We retain the same offset grading and relative complex~\eqref{eq:relative-betti}.

\begin{theorem}[Complete second Betti row, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:second-betti-row}
For every integer \(p\ge4\) and every field \(\kfield\),
\begin{equation}
\beta_{2,5}=p(2p-3),
\qquad
\beta_{2,6}=0.
\label{eq:second-betti-tail}
\end{equation}
The degree-five offset support is
\begin{equation}
[3p+2,5p-2]\cup[6p+1,8p-3]\cup[9p,11p-4].
\label{eq:degree-five-support}
\end{equation}
For \(0\le r\le2p-4\), put
\begin{align*}
m_{\rm out}(r)&=\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac r2\right\rfloor+1,
 \left\lfloor\frac{2p-4-r}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right\},\\
m_{\rm mid}(r)&=\min\{r+1,2p-3-r,p-2\}.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{align}
\beta_{2,(5,3p+2+r)}&=m_{\rm out}(r),\notag\\
\beta_{2,(5,6p+1+r)}&=m_{\rm mid}(r),\label{eq:degree-five-profile}\\
\beta_{2,(5,9p+r)}&=m_{\rm out}(2p-4-r).\notag
\end{align}
The integral first homology in total degree five is free abelian, and it vanishes in total degree
six. Consequently the complete second row is
\[
\beta_{2,3}=\frac{2p(500p^2-330p+31)}{3},\quad
\beta_{2,4}=8p,\quad
\beta_{2,5}=p(2p-3),\quad
\beta_{2,6}=0,
\]
with no other nonzero internal degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cumulative offset sets needed in total degrees five and six are
\begin{align*}
\mathcal E_{p,2}&=[0,2p]\cup[3p,5p-2]\cup[6p,24p-1],\\
\mathcal E_{p,3}&=[0,24p-1]\setminus\{6p-1\},\\
\mathcal E_{p,n}&=[0,24p-1]\qquad(n\ge4).
\end{align*}
Thus a degree-five vertex, edge, or triangle at offset \(b\) occurs precisely when its residual
belongs respectively to \(\mathcal E_{p,4},\mathcal E_{p,3},\mathcal E_{p,2}\). In degree six,
the first two residual sets are full intervals and only the triangle condition retains the hole
\(6p-1\).

Apply the integral lexicographic matching used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:interior-strand}. Substitution of the eleven blocks of
\(\mathcal E_{p,1}\) gives the following complete degree-five normal form. In each row
\(0\le r\le2p-4\).
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Offset & critical edge labels & surviving free rank\\
\midrule
\(3p+2+r\) &
\(\{(i,j):1\le i<j\le p,\ i+j=r+3\}\) & \(m_{\rm out}(r)\)\\
\(6p+1+r\) &
\(\begin{array}{l}\{(i,j):1\le i<j\le p,\ i+j=r+4\}\\
{}\cup\{(i,j):2\le i<j\le p,\ i+j=r+3\}\end{array}\) &
\(m_{\rm mid}(r)\)\\
\(9p+r\) &
\(\begin{array}{l}\{(i,3p+j):2\le i\le p-1,\ 0\le j\le p-2,\ i+j=r+2\}\\
{}\cup\{(p,4p-2):r=2p-4\}\end{array}\) &
\(m_{\rm out}(2p-4-r)\)\\
all other \(b\) & transient labels only & \(0\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the first row, the absent star triangles are exactly the low-block pairs of sum \(r+3\);
every other chord toggles at its least available vertex. For the second row, the hole in
\(\mathcal E_{p,3}\) and the two gaps in \(\mathcal E_{p,2}\) leave the two adjacent pair-sum
diagonals displayed in the table. Their reduced Morse boundary is zero. For the third row,
ordering the low--middle critical chords by the low endpoint makes the remaining triangle
boundary a unit staircase. Its Smith form has
\[
c_C(r)-m_{\rm out}(2p-4-r)\quad\hbox{unit factors}
\]
and \(m_{\rm out}(2p-4-r)\) zero factors, where
\[
c_C(r)=
\begin{cases}
1,&r=2p-4,\\
\min\{r+1,2p-4-r,p-2\},&r<2p-4.
\end{cases}
\]
Outside the three rows, each transient edge has a distinct least admissible triangle and hence a
unit pivot. All cancellations are over \(\mathbb Z\).

In total degree six, the edge residual set is full. A chord whose star triangle is deleted at
residual \(6p-1\) pairs with the next admissible vertex in its interval block; at a block endpoint
the first vertex of the next block supplies the pair. Endpoint substitution leaves no critical
edge. Hence the integral first homology is zero in every degree-six offset.

It remains to count the zero factors. The first diagonal consists of unordered pairs in
\([1,p]\), and therefore
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{2p-4}m_{\rm out}(r)=\binom p2.
\]
Across all \(r\), the two middle diagonals contain every pair in \([1,p]\) except \((1,2)\), and
every pair in \([2,p]\). Thus
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{2p-4}m_{\rm mid}(r)
=\left(\binom p2-1\right)+\binom{p-1}{2}=p(p-2).
\]
The reflected third block contributes another \(\binom p2\), proving
\(\beta_{2,5}=p(2p-3)\). Unit Smith factors prove integral freeness and therefore
characteristic independence. Finally, no linear equations force the minimum second-syzygy shift
to be three, while \(\operatorname{reg}\mathcal F_p=4\) forces it to be at most six. The known
degree-three and degree-four entries and~\eqref{eq:second-betti-tail} therefore exhaust the row.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The colon--Koszul degree-five diagonal}

Put
\begin{equation}
H_p=\{a\in\mathcal E_{p,1}:a\ge6p\}.
\label{eq:high-colon-set}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:quadratic-colon} gives \(|H_p|=8p\). The next theorem reads the second Koszul
wedges of this exact linear colon inside the total-degree-five relative complex.

\begin{theorem}[Colon--Koszul degree-five diagonal, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:colon-koszul-diagonal}
For every integer \(p\ge4\), every field \(\kfield\), and every offset \(b\),
\begin{equation}
\beta_{3,(5,b)}=
\#\bigl\{\{a,c\}\subset H_p:a<c,\ a+c=b-3p\bigr\}.
\label{eq:colon-koszul-profile}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{3,5}=\binom{8p}{2}=4p(8p-1),
\label{eq:beta-three-five}
\end{equation}
and its offset support is
\begin{equation}
[15p+1,39p-3]\setminus\{33p-1\}.
\label{eq:colon-koszul-support}
\end{equation}
The corresponding integral relative homology is free abelian. The complete internal-degree-five
diagonal is therefore
\begin{align}
\beta_{2,5}&=p(2p-3),\notag\\
\beta_{3,5}&=4p(8p-1),\label{eq:degree-five-diagonal}\\
\beta_{4,5}&=\frac{2p(5p-1)(10p-3)(100p^2-110p+13)}{3},\notag
\end{align}
with \(\beta_{i,5}=0\) in every other homological degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The eleven-block formula for \(\mathcal E_{p,1}\) gives the disjoint decomposition
\begin{align}
H_p={}&[6p,8p-2]\cup[8p,10p-2]\cup\{10p\}\cup[11p-1,12p-1]\notag\\
&\cup[13p+1,14p-2]\cup[14p,15p-1]\cup\{16p\}\cup[17p-1,18p-1].
\label{eq:high-colon-blocks}
\end{align}
Its block lengths sum to \(8p\). The linear strand of
\(P_p/(\mathfrak q_p:f_p)\) is the Koszul strand on these coordinate variables. Its second
wedges are indexed by unordered distinct pairs \(\{a,c\}\subset H_p\), and the cubic shift gives
bidegree \((5,3p+a+c)\). We prove that these candidate classes are complete and primitive rather
than assuming that the full mapping cone is minimal.

For fixed \(b\), the total-degree-five relative complex from~\eqref{eq:relative-betti} contains a
cell \(F\) of size \(s\) exactly when
\[
b-\sum_{a\in F}a\in\mathcal E_{p,5-s}.
\]
Apply the same increasing-vertex integral matching as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:second-betti-row}, now through cells of size four. Every matched incidence is a
unit. The only failed toggles come from
\[
[2p+1,3p-1],\qquad[5p-1,6p-1],\qquad\{6p-1\},
\]
the gaps of \(\mathcal E_{p,2}\) and \(\mathcal E_{p,3}\). Substitution of the eleven blocks of
\(\mathcal E_{p,1}\) gives the following integral normal form. If
\[
K_b=\{(p,a,c):a<c,\ a,c\in H_p,\ a+c=b-3p\},
\]
then, after contractible unit cancellations, the part bearing on second homology is
\begin{equation}
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{T_b}
\xrightarrow{(I,0)}\mathbb Z^{T_b}\oplus\mathbb Z^{K_b}
\longrightarrow0.
\label{eq:degree-five-normal-form}
\end{equation}
Here \(T_b\) indexes the transient critical triangles. Indeed, a critical triangle not of the
form \((p,a,c)\) with \(a,c\in H_p\) has a least admissible tetrahedral filler in the same block
of~\eqref{eq:high-colon-blocks}, or at the first vertex of the next block. Ordering first by the
failed toggle and then lexicographically makes this boundary submatrix upper triangular with unit
diagonal. For \((p,a,c)\), the residual is \(2p\), the right endpoint of the first component of
\(\mathcal E_{p,2}\); the next toggle crosses one of the three displayed gaps, so no filler
remains. The least-toggle order prevents a transient boundary from entering a pair row. These two
cases exhaust the block endpoints and give~\eqref{eq:degree-five-normal-form}. Thus
\[
H_2(K_{(5,b)};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{K_b},
\]
which proves~\eqref{eq:colon-koszul-profile}, primitivity, and characteristic independence.

It remains to identify the support. The following pair families already cover every possible sum
except one; the letters denote the corresponding nonsingleton blocks of
\eqref{eq:high-colon-blocks}.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Pair & Sum interval & Pair & Sum interval\\
\midrule
\(AA\) & \([12p+1,16p-5]\) & \(AB\) & \([14p,18p-4]\)\\
\(AD\) & \([17p-1,20p-3]\) & \(BD\) & \([19p-1,22p-3]\)\\
\(BE\) & \([21p+1,24p-4]\) & \(AK\) & \([23p-1,26p-3]\)\\
\(BK\) & \([25p-1,28p-3]\) & \(DK\) & \([28p-2,30p-2]\)\\
\(EK\) & \([30p,32p-3]\) & \(FK\) & \([31p-1,33p-2]\)\\
\(GK\) & \([33p-1,34p-1]\) & \(KK\) & \([34p-1,36p-3]\)\\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

unshifted support is \([12p+1,36p-3]\setminus\{30p-1\}\), proving
\eqref{eq:colon-koszul-support}. Summing~\eqref{eq:colon-koszul-profile} over \(b\) counts all
unordered pairs and proves~\eqref{eq:beta-three-five}.

Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:second-betti-row} gives \(\beta_{2,5}=p(2p-3)\). No degree-five minimal
equation exists, and the minimum-shift bound excludes homological degree at least five from
internal degree five. Taking the coefficient of \(z^5\) in~\eqref{eq:betti-polynomial} therefore
gives
\[
[z^5]\eqref{eq:betti-polynomial}=\beta_{2,5}-\beta_{3,5}+\beta_{4,5}.
\]
Substitution and expansion yield the third formula in~\eqref{eq:degree-five-diagonal}; one of its
displayed factors is divisible by three in every residue class of \(p\) modulo three.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The cubic-colon idealization and degree-six third strand}

The preceding theorem uses only the linear part of the cubic colon. We now identify the complete
colon and read its first nonlinear strand inside total degree six. Retain $H_p$ from
\eqref{eq:high-colon-set}, and put
\[
L_p=[0,p]\cup[3p,4p-2],\qquad
A_i=X_i\ (0\le i\le p),\qquad B_j=X_{3p+j}\ (0\le j\le p-2).
\]
Let $V_p=\kfield[s,t]^{(p)}$ be the $p$-th Veronese ring and let
$\omega_{V_p}$ carry its natural grading, so
$(\omega_{V_p})_d=\kfield[s,t]_{dp-2}$.

For the multigraded formula, set
\begin{align*}
\ell_p(T)&=\sum_{a\in L_p}T^a,
&\eta_p(T)&=\sum_{a\in H_p}T^a,\\
e_{p,r}(T)&=\sum_{\substack{F\subset L_p\\|F|=r}}T^{\sum F},
&u_{p,0}(T)&=1,\\
u_{p,d}(T)&=\sum_{v=0}^{dp}T^v+\sum_{v=3p}^{(d+3)p-2}T^v &&(d\ge1),
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align}
r_p(T)&=\ell_p(T)u_{p,1}(T)-u_{p,2}(T)-e_{p,2}(T),\label{eq:colon-quadratic-profile}\\
s_p(T)&=u_{p,3}(T)-\ell_p(T)u_{p,2}(T)
 +e_{p,2}(T)u_{p,1}(T)-e_{p,3}(T).\label{eq:colon-linear-syzygy-profile}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Cubic-colon idealization and degree-six third strand, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:colon-idealization-six}
For every integer $p\ge4$ and every field $\kfield$,
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak q_p:f_p=\mathfrak q_p+(X_h:h\in H_p),\qquad |H_p|=8p.\label{eq:complete-cubic-colon}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
P_p/(\mathfrak q_p:f_p)\cong V_p\ltimes\omega_{V_p},\label{eq:colon-idealization}
\end{equation}
where the right side is the square-zero canonical idealization under
\[
A_i\longmapsto s^{p-i}t^i,\qquad
B_j\longmapsto \epsilon s^{p-2-j}t^j,\qquad \epsilon^2=0.
\]
It is a two-dimensional graded Gorenstein algebra with Hilbert series
\[
\frac{1+(2p-2)z+z^2}{(1-z)^2}.
\]
The complete total-degree-six third-syzygy profile is
\begin{equation}
\beta_{3,(6,b)}=[T^{b-3p}]\bigl(s_p(T)+\eta_p(T)r_p(T)\bigr).\label{eq:degree-six-profile}
\end{equation}
The corresponding integral relative homology is free abelian, its support is
\begin{equation}
[3p+4,29p-5]\setminus\bigl([6p-3,6p+1]\cup[9p-3,9p]\bigr),\label{eq:degree-six-support}
\end{equation}
and consequently
\begin{equation}
\beta_{3,6}=\frac{8p(7p^2-12p+2)}{3}.\label{eq:beta-three-six}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Kill the variables indexed by $H_p$, and write
$S_p=\kfield[A_0,\ldots,A_p,B_0,\ldots,B_{p-2}]$. The image of $\mathfrak q_p$ is generated by
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
A_iA_j-A_kA_l&\quad(i+j=k+l),\\
A_iB_j-A_kB_l&\quad(i+j=k+l),\\
B_iB_j&\quad\hbox{for all }i,j.
\end{split}\label{eq:low-colon-relations}
\end{equation}
The first two rows are the equal-offset quadratic fibers. For the third, the offset of
$B_iB_j$ lies in $[6p,8p-4]\subset H_p$, and
$B_iB_j-X_0X_{6p+i+j}\in\mathfrak q_p$. Conversely, no $AA$ or $AB$ total has a
high-variable factorization or vanishes in degree two, so this list is exact.

Relations~\eqref{eq:low-colon-relations} give a surjection to $V_p\ltimes\omega_{V_p}$.
Every nonzero degree-$d$ monomial has a unique normal form of one of two types: an $A$-only
class indexed by $[0,dp]$, or a class with one $B$, indexed by
$[3p,(d+3)p-2]$. Their images are the monomial bases of
$\kfield[s,t]_{dp}$ and $\epsilon\kfield[s,t]_{dp-2}$, respectively, and remain independent
where the intervals overlap. This proves the idealization presentation after the high variables
are killed and gives dimension $2pd$ in every positive degree, hence the displayed Hilbert
series.

Equation~\eqref{eq:quadratic-colon} already places all $X_h$, $h\in H_p$, in
$\mathfrak q_p:f_p$. In the idealization, the two monomials of $f_p$ have nonzero components
in the ring and canonical-module summands. An annihilator first kills a nonzero element of the
Veronese domain and is therefore zero in the ring summand; it then kills a nonzero element on the
torsion-free canonical module and is zero there as well. Thus $f_p$ is a nonzerodivisor modulo
$\mathfrak q_p+(X_h:h\in H_p)$, proving \eqref{eq:complete-cubic-colon} and
\eqref{eq:colon-idealization}.

The two normal-form intervals give the offset Hilbert profiles $u_{p,d}(T)$. Multiplication by
$\prod_{a\in L_p}(1-zT^a)$ shows that the degree-two numerator coefficient is $-r_p(T)$ and
the degree-three coefficient is $s_p(T)$. The ideal in~\eqref{eq:low-colon-relations} is
generated by quadrics and there are no linear equations. Minimum shifts therefore identify these
two coefficients with the complete low-variable $\beta_{1,2}$ and $\beta_{2,3}$ profiles.
Tensoring with the Koszul complex on the $8p$ killed high variables gives
$s_p(T)+\eta_p(T)r_p(T)$ in homological degree two and total degree three.

It remains to prove that the cubic shift survives without cancellation. In the total-degree-six
relative complex, a cell $F$ occurs exactly when
\[
b-\sum_{a\in F}a\in\mathcal E_{p,6-|F|}.
\]
The edge residual set is full, the triangle residual set has the single hole $6p-1$, and the
tetrahedron residual gaps are $[2p+1,3p-1]$ and $[5p-1,6p-1]$. Apply increasing-vertex
integral matching through tetrahedra, rooting each equal-sum low quadratic fiber and choosing a
lexicographic spanning forest in every low degree-three reduction graph. The only primitive
unmatched triangles are the low cycle labels counted by $s_p(T)$, and one high vertex times a
rooted low quadratic label counted by $\eta_p(T)r_p(T)$. Every triangle with at least two high
vertices receives a least tetrahedral filler in the same high block or at the first vertex of the
next block. The apparent endpoint $8p-1$ is not a generator.

Ordering all other triangles first by failed residual gap, then by number of high vertices, then
lexicographically makes their tetrahedron boundary block upper triangular with unit diagonal and
no entry in a primitive label row. Thus the reduced integral block is
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{T_b}
\xrightarrow{(I,0)}\mathbb Z^{T_b}\oplus\mathbb Z^{U_b}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
where $U_b$ has generating polynomial
$[T^{b-3p}](s_p+\eta_pr_p)$. This proves \eqref{eq:degree-six-profile}, primitivity, and
characteristic independence.

Direct expansion of the interval polynomials gives the support in
\eqref{eq:degree-six-support}. At $T=1$, with $c=2p-2$,
\[
r_p(1)=\frac{c(c+1)}2-1=p(2p-3),\qquad
s_p(1)=\frac{c(c-2)(c+2)}3=\frac{8p(p-1)(p-2)}3.
\]
Adding $8p\,r_p(1)$ gives \eqref{eq:beta-three-six}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Integral degree-seven contraction and the complete third row}

The regularity bound permits one further shift in homological degree three. Its relative complex
has a simpler defect than the degree-six strand and admits a uniform integral contraction.

\begin{theorem}[Complete third homological row, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:complete-third-row}
For every integer $p\ge4$, every field $\kfield$, and every multigraded offset $b$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{3,(7,b)}(C_p)=0.\label{eq:degree-seven-vanishing}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\beta_{3,7}=0$, and the complete third homological row is
\begin{align}
\beta_{3,4}&=\frac{p(5p-1)(500p^2-440p+47)}2,\notag\\
\beta_{3,5}&=4p(8p-1),\notag\\
\beta_{3,6}&=\frac{8p(7p^2-12p+2)}3,\notag\\
\beta_{3,7}&=0,\qquad \beta_{3,j}=0\quad\hbox{otherwise}.\label{eq:complete-third-row}
\end{align}
Its total rank is
\begin{equation}
\beta_3=\frac{p(7500p^3-7988p^2+2025p-133)}6.\label{eq:beta-three-total}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a total offset $b$ and put $h=6p-1$. The relative-complex dictionary used above says that a
squarefree cell $F$ occurs in total degree seven exactly when
\[
b-\sum_{a\in F}a\in\mathcal E_{p,7-|F|}.
\]
The three cumulative offset sets governing edges, triangles, and tetrahedra are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_{p,5}=\mathcal E_{p,4}=[0,24p-1],\qquad
\mathcal E_{p,3}=[0,24p-1]\setminus\{h\}.\label{eq:degree-seven-residuals}
\end{equation}
All cellular incidence coefficients are units over $\mathbb Z$.

Use vertex $0$ for a partial Boolean matching. Match every edge not containing $0$ with the
triangle obtained by adjoining $0$. Match every triangle $F$ not containing $0$ with
$F\cup\{0\}$ whenever $b-\sum F\ne h$. Equation~\eqref{eq:degree-seven-residuals} makes every
pair valid. The matching is acyclic: a reversed matched arrow adds $0$, while deletion of any
other vertex leaves $0$ present and cannot lead to another lower cell of the same matching.
The unmatched triangles are therefore precisely
\begin{equation}
0\notin F,\qquad |F|=3,\qquad b-\sum F=h.\label{eq:critical-triangles}
\end{equation}

For such a triangle choose the least $x\in\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus F$ and put
$T=F\cup\{x\}$. These four vertices belong to the degree-one offset set for every $p\ge4$, and
\[
b-\sum T=h-x\in[0,24p-1]\setminus\{h\},
\]
so $T$ is an unmatched tetrahedron in the same offset complex. If $y\in T$, then the residual of
its face $T\setminus\{y\}$ is $h-x+y$, which equals $h$ exactly when $y=x$. Thus $F$ is the
unique critical triangle face of $T$, and its direct boundary coefficient is $+1$ or $-1$.

There is no additional gradient contribution to another critical triangle. Any other face $G$ of
$T$ not containing $0$ is paired upward with $G\cup\{0\}$; all its other triangle faces contain
$0$ and are paired downward with edges. At fixed $b$, two critical triangles cannot receive the
same tetrahedron, since that tetrahedron would then have two faces satisfying
\eqref{eq:critical-triangles}. Hence the reduced integral boundary from unmatched tetrahedra onto
unmatched triangles contains a signed identity block. It is surjective, so the integral second
homology vanishes at every offset. Base change to any field proves
\eqref{eq:degree-seven-vanishing}.

The preceding interior-strand, colon--Koszul, and cubic-colon theorems give the entries in degrees
four, five, and six. The minimum-shift bound excludes smaller degrees, and regularity four excludes
degrees greater than seven. This proves~\eqref{eq:complete-third-row}. Adding its three nonzero
entries and simplifying gives~\eqref{eq:beta-three-total}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The complete graded Betti polynomial of the cubic-colon quotient}

The canonical idealization in Theorem~\ref{thm:colon-idealization-six} also determines every
ordinary graded free-module rank and shift of the colon quotient. In this subsection put
\[
c=2p-2,\qquad m=8p,\qquad D_p=P_p/(\mathfrak q_p:f_p),
\]
and let \(S_p=\kfield[A_0,\ldots,A_p,B_0,\ldots,B_{p-2}]\) be the polynomial ring on the \(2p\)
low variables. Write \(E_p=V_p\ltimes\omega_{V_p}\), so \(D_p\cong E_p\) as graded algebras, but
the two displayed ambient polynomial rings record different Betti tables. For a graded module
\(M\), set \(B_M(x,z)=\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}(M)x^iz^j\).

\begin{theorem}[Complete cubic-colon Betti polynomial, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:colon-betti-polynomial}
For every integer \(p\ge4\) and every field \(\kfield\), put
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{c,a}=c\binom ca-\binom c{a+1}-\binom c{a-1}
\qquad(1\le a\le c-1).
\label{eq:colon-linear-ranks}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
B_{E_p}^{S_p}(x,z)
 &=1+\sum_{a=1}^{c-1}\lambda_{c,a}x^az^{a+1}+x^cz^{c+2},
 \label{eq:low-colon-betti}\\
B_{D_p}^{P_p}(x,z)
 &=(1+xz)^m B_{E_p}^{S_p}(x,z).
 \label{eq:full-colon-betti}
\end{align}
Equivalently, with out-of-range binomial coefficients read as zero,
\begin{align}
\beta_{i,i}^{P_p}(D_p)&=\binom mi,\notag\\
\beta_{i,i+1}^{P_p}(D_p)&=
 \sum_{a=1}^{c-1}\lambda_{c,a}\binom m{i-a},\label{eq:full-colon-rows}\\
\beta_{i,i+2}^{P_p}(D_p)&=\binom m{i-c},
\end{align}
and every other entry vanishes. In particular,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{pd}_{P_p}D_p=10p-2,\qquad
\operatorname{reg}_{P_p}D_p=2.
\label{eq:colon-pd-reg}
\end{equation}
The total Betti rank is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}^{P_p}(D_p)
=2^{8p}\left((2p-4)2^{2p-2}+4\right).
\label{eq:colon-total-rank}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:colon-idealization-six} identifies \(E_p\) as a two-dimensional standard graded
Gorenstein algebra with Hilbert series
\[
H_{E_p}(z)=\frac{1+cz+z^2}{(1-z)^2}.
\]
It has codimension \(c\), no linear equations, regularity two, and a-invariant zero. Minimality
therefore excludes \(\beta_{i,j}\) for \(i>0\) and \(j\le i\), while regularity excludes
\(j-i>2\). Gorenstein self-duality has final shift \((c+2)+0=c+2\) and gives
\begin{equation}
\beta_{i,j}^{S_p}(E_p)=\beta_{c-i,c+2-j}^{S_p}(E_p).
\label{eq:colon-self-duality}
\end{equation}
The row \(j-i=2\) is dual to the diagonal row, which contains only \(\beta_{0,0}=1\).
Consequently its only entry is \(\beta_{c,c+2}=1\); every remaining entry lies on the linear
strand.

The alternating Betti numerator is
\begin{equation}
(1-z)^{c+2}H_{E_p}(z)=(1+cz+z^2)(1-z)^c.
\label{eq:colon-hilbert-numerator}
\end{equation}
For \(1\le a\le c-1\), its coefficient in degree \(a+1\) is
\[
(-1)^a\left(c\binom ca-\binom c{a+1}-\binom c{a-1}\right).
\]
The proved resolution shape leaves only \((-1)^a\beta_{a,a+1}\) in that degree, which proves
\eqref{eq:colon-linear-ranks} and \eqref{eq:low-colon-betti}. The factorization
\[
\lambda_{c,a}=\binom ca
 \frac{a(c-a)(c+2)}{(a+1)(c-a+1)}
\]
proves positivity and makes self-dual symmetry visible. The endpoint-separated binomial sum gives
\[
\sum_{a=1}^{c-1}\lambda_{c,a}=(c-2)2^c+2.
\]

Finally, write \(P_p=S_p\otimes_\kfield T_p\), where \(T_p\) is the polynomial ring in the \(m\)
high variables. Equation~\eqref{eq:complete-cubic-colon} gives
\[
D_p=E_p\otimes_\kfield T_p/(\text{all high variables}).
\]
Tensor the minimal \(S_p\)-resolution of \(E_p\) with the Koszul resolution over \(T_p\). The
variables are disjoint, so the tensor complex is exact, and every differential entry lies in the
homogeneous maximal ideal, so it is minimal. Its Betti polynomial is multiplied by \((1+xz)^m\),
which proves \eqref{eq:full-colon-betti} and \eqref{eq:full-colon-rows}. The endpoint, regularity,
and evaluation at \(x=z=1\) give \eqref{eq:colon-pd-reg} and \eqref{eq:colon-total-rank}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The quadratic quotient and the minimal cubic mapping cone}

The preceding colon theorem controls the source of the cubic mapping cone. A pullback through the
same colon now bounds the target tightly enough to decide all possible cancellations. Retain
\(c=2p-2\) and \(m=8p\), put \(N=10p\), and write
\[
A_p=P_p/\mathfrak q_p,\qquad
L_p=\mathfrak q_p:f_p,\qquad
U_p=D_p/f_pD_p.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Minimal cubic mapping cone, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:minimal-cubic-cone}
For every integer \(p\ge4\) and every field \(\kfield\),
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{depth}_{P_p}A_p=1,\qquad
\operatorname{pd}_{P_p}A_p=N-1,\qquad
\operatorname{reg}_{P_p}A_p=2.
\label{eq:quadratic-quotient-invariants}
\end{equation}
The mapping cone of
\[
0\longrightarrow D_p(-3)\xrightarrow{\,f_p\,}A_p
 \longrightarrow C_p\longrightarrow0
\]
is minimal. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
B_{C_p}^{P_p}(x,z)=B_{A_p}^{P_p}(x,z)+xz^3B_{D_p}^{P_p}(x,z).
\label{eq:minimal-cubic-cone}
\end{equation}
In particular, with out-of-range binomial coefficients read as zero, the two highest regularity
strands of \(C_p\) are
\begin{align}
\beta_{i,i+3}^{P_p}(C_p)
 &=\sum_{a=1}^{c-1}\lambda_{c,a}\binom m{i-1-a},
 \label{eq:special-fiber-row-three}\\
\beta_{i,i+4}^{P_p}(C_p)
 &=\binom m{i-1-c}.
 \label{eq:special-fiber-row-four}
\end{align}
The first of these strands is positive exactly for \(2\le i\le N-2\), while the second is
positive exactly for \(2p-1\le i\le N-1\). Their total ranks are
\begin{equation}
\sum_i\beta_{i,i+3}^{P_p}(C_p)=2^m\bigl((c-2)2^c+2\bigr),
\qquad
\sum_i\beta_{i,i+4}^{P_p}(C_p)=2^m.
\label{eq:special-fiber-upper-totals}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:colon-idealization-six} proves that \(f_p\) is a nonzerodivisor on
\(P_p/L_p=D_p\). We first claim
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak q_p=(\mathfrak q_p,f_p)\cap L_p.
\label{eq:cubic-colon-intersection}
\end{equation}
Only the reverse inclusion needs proof. If \(g=q+af_p\) belongs to the intersection, with
\(q\in\mathfrak q_p\), then \(af_p\in L_p\). Regularity of \(f_p\) modulo \(L_p\) gives
\(a\in L_p=\mathfrak q_p:f_p\), and hence \(af_p\in\mathfrak q_p\). This proves
\eqref{eq:cubic-colon-intersection}.

The standard intersection sequence is therefore
\[
0\longrightarrow A_p\longrightarrow C_p\oplus D_p
 \longrightarrow U_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
Projecting this pullback onto \(D_p\) gives
\begin{equation}
0\longrightarrow K_p\longrightarrow A_p\longrightarrow D_p\longrightarrow0,
\qquad K_p=\ker(C_p\longrightarrow U_p).
\label{eq:quadratic-pullback}
\end{equation}
Since \(f_p\) is \(D_p\)-regular of degree three, Theorem~\ref{thm:colon-betti-polynomial}
and the known special-fiber Hilbert series give
\begin{align*}
H_{U_p}(z)
 &=\frac{1+(2p-1)z+2pz^2+(2p-1)z^3+z^4}{1-z},\\
H_{K_p}(z)
 &=H_{C_p}(z)-H_{U_p}(z)
   =\frac{8pz+10pz^2}{1-z}.
\end{align*}
The linear form \(X_0\) is regular on \(C_p\) by the flat degeneration used in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:homological-edge}; it is therefore regular on the submodule \(K_p\). Thus \(K_p\)
is one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay, and
\[
H_{K_p/X_0K_p}(z)=8pz+10pz^2
\]
shows that \(\operatorname{reg}K_p=2\).

The module \(D_p\) is two-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay of regularity two. The depth lemma applied
to~\eqref{eq:quadratic-pullback} gives \(\operatorname{depth}A_p=1\), while the regularity
inequality gives \(\operatorname{reg}A_p\le2\). Auslander--Buchsbaum yields
\(\operatorname{pd}A_p=N-1\). Moreover,
\begin{equation}
H_{A_p}(z)=
\frac{1+(N-2)z+(2p+1)z^2-Nz^3}{(1-z)^2}.
\label{eq:quadratic-quotient-hilbert}
\end{equation}
The coefficient of \(z^{N+1}\) in \((1-z)^NH_{A_p}(z)\) is \(-N\), because \(N\) is even.
Under the bounds \(\operatorname{pd}A_p=N-1\) and \(\operatorname{reg}A_p\le2\), only
\((-1)^{N-1}\beta_{N-1,N+1}(A_p)\) can contribute there. Hence
\(\beta_{N-1,N+1}(A_p)=N\), proving equality in the regularity bound and all of
\eqref{eq:quadratic-quotient-invariants}.

In homological degree \(i\), every source summand in a lifted comparison map from the minimal
resolution of \(D_p(-3)\) has shift at least \(i+3\), by
Theorem~\ref{thm:colon-betti-polynomial}. Every target summand in the minimal resolution of \(A_p\)
has shift at most \(i+2\), by~\eqref{eq:quadratic-quotient-invariants}. Every comparison entry
therefore has positive degree. The cone admits no scalar cancellation and is minimal, proving
\eqref{eq:minimal-cubic-cone}.

The \(A_p\)-summand contributes only regularity at most two. The regularity-three and
regularity-four strands consequently come from, respectively, the linear and terminal rows of
\(D_p\), shifted by one homological and three internal degrees. Substitution in
\eqref{eq:full-colon-rows} proves \eqref{eq:special-fiber-row-three} and
\eqref{eq:special-fiber-row-four}. Positivity of the \(\lambda_{c,a}\), their support, and the
binomial support give the stated endpoints. Summing the convolution and using
\(\sum_{a=1}^{c-1}\lambda_{c,a}=(c-2)2^c+2\) proves
\eqref{eq:special-fiber-upper-totals}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The first surviving lower-strand class}

The same pullback can determine an individual class in one of the two still-unknown strands.
Retain the offset grading \(\deg X_g=(1,g)\), and put
\begin{equation}
b_p=8p-1,\qquad F_p=\{1,\ldots,p\},\qquad
\tau_p=b_p+\sum_{g=1}^p g=8p-1+\frac{p(p+1)}2.
\label{eq:first-surviving-data}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[First surviving lower-strand class, {\bf[D]}]
\label{thm:first-surviving-lower}
For every integer \(p\ge4\) and every field \(\kfield\),
\begin{equation}
\beta_{p,(p+2,\tau_p)}^{P_p}(K_p)
=\beta_{p,(p+2,\tau_p)}^{P_p}(A_p)
=\beta_{p,(p+2,\tau_p)}^{P_p}(C_p)=1.
\label{eq:first-surviving-class}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{p,p+2}^{P_p}(A_p)\ge1,
\qquad
\beta_{p,p+2}^{P_p}(C_p)\ge\binom{8p}{p-1}+1.
\label{eq:first-surviving-ordinary}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The element \(X_0\) is regular on \(K_p\) by the preceding proof. It is regular on \(D_p\) as
well: in the canonical idealization of Theorem~\ref{thm:colon-idealization-six}, it acts as
\(s^p\) on both the Veronese domain and its torsion-free canonical module. Sequence
\eqref{eq:quadratic-pullback} then makes it regular on \(A_p\), and reduction modulo \(X_0\)
preserves that exact sequence and all minimal Betti numbers. Write
\[
S_p=P_p/(X_0),\qquad M_p=K_p/X_0K_p.
\]
The offset bases used above give
\begin{align*}
(M_p)_1&=\langle u_h:h\in H_p\rangle,
  &H_p&=\{g\in\mathcal E_{p,1}:g\ge6p\},\\
(M_p)_2&=\langle v_b:b\in B_p\rangle,
  &B_p&=[6p,24p-1]\setminus H_p,
\end{align*}
with dimensions \(8p\) and \(10p\), respectively, and \((M_p)_d=0\) for \(d\ge3\). For
\(g\in\mathcal E_{p,1}\setminus\{0\}\), multiplication is
\begin{equation}
X_gu_h=\begin{cases}v_{g+h},&g+h\in B_p,\\0,&g+h\notin B_p.\end{cases}
\label{eq:two-layer-multiplication}
\end{equation}
Indeed, an offset in \(H_p\) is the image of \(X_0u_{g+h}\), while an offset outside the stable
kernel interval vanishes.

Let \(V_p\) have basis \(e_g\), indexed by the positive degree-one offsets. In standard internal
degree \(i+1\), the Koszul complex of \(M_p\) has only the map
\begin{equation}
\delta_i:\bigwedge^iV_p\otimes(M_p)_1
 \longrightarrow\bigwedge^{i-1}V_p\otimes(M_p)_2,
\label{eq:two-layer-incidence}
\end{equation}
where
\[
\delta_i(e_F\otimes u_h)=
\sum_{\substack{g\in F\\h+g\in B_p}}
(-1)^{\operatorname{pos}(g,F)}e_{F\setminus\{g\}}\otimes v_{h+g}.
\]
Thus the two Betti strands of \(K_p\) are the kernels and cokernels of these integral incidence
maps.

The smallest element of \(B_p\) is \(b_p=8p-1\), and the \(p\) smallest positive variable
offsets are exactly \(F_p\). Hence the codomain of \(\delta_{p+1}\) in total offset \(\tau_p\) is
one-dimensional, on \(e_{F_p}\otimes v_{b_p}\). Its possible incoming variables form
\[
R_{b_p}=\{g\in\mathcal E_{p,1}\setminus\{0\}:b_p-g\in H_p\}.
\]
For \(1\le g\le p\), the complement is in \([7p-1,8p-2]\subset H_p\). A variable in the
second low block gives a complement at most \(5p-1\), while a high variable gives one at most
\(2p-1\). Therefore \(R_{b_p}=F_p\). An incoming face would require an element of
\(R_{b_p}\setminus F_p\), so this integral cokernel is free of rank one.

It remains to exclude the connecting map from \(D_p\). The degree-one basis of
\(D_p/X_0D_p\) has low offsets
\[
L'_p=[1,p]\cup[3p,4p-2].
\]
A differential contributing to the distinguished coordinate must remove a high exterior variable
\(h_l=b_p-l\), where \(1\le l\le p\), and leaves the low chain
\(e_{F_p}\otimes X_l\) at low total offset \(\sum F_p+l\). This is the only low chain in that
multidegree. Indeed, using one exterior variable in the second block raises the exterior sum to
at least \(3p+1+\cdots+(p-1)=\sum F_p+2p\), and using a coefficient in that block raises it by at
least \(3p>l\). Without the second block, a size-\(p\) exterior set must be \(F_p\), and its
coefficient is forced to be \(l\).

The unique chain is not a cycle: removing \(p\) from its Koszul boundary gives the unit coordinate
\[
(-1)^{p-1}e_{\{1,\ldots,p-1\}}\otimes X_pX_l,
\]
and \(X_pX_l\ne0\) in the ring summand of \(D_p/X_0D_p\), at offset
\(p+l\in[p+1,2p]\). The high variables annihilate \(D_p\), so its Koszul complex splits by high
exterior set. Consequently every source cycle has zero coefficient on all of the selected chains,
and the connecting map cannot hit the rank-one class.

The row-two strand of \(D_p\) starts at homological degree \(c=2p-2>p\), by
Theorem~\ref{thm:colon-betti-polynomial}. The long exact Tor sequence therefore gives the exact
middle equality in~\eqref{eq:first-surviving-class}. Finally, the multigraded minimal cone could
add at this offset only a shifted diagonal wedge of \(p-1\) high variables. Its smallest offset is
\(3p+\sum_{h=6p}^{7p-2}h\), which exceeds \(\tau_p\) by
\((p-2)(6p-1)>0\). This proves the last equality. Summing offsets and using the shifted diagonal
rank \(\binom{8p}{p-1}\) proves~\eqref{eq:first-surviving-ordinary}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Primitive zero rows and characteristic dependence}

For $b\in B_p$, define the representation set
\begin{equation}
R_b=\{g\in\mathcal E_{p,1}\setminus\{0\}:b-g\in H_p\}.
\label{eq:representation-set}
\end{equation}
The EXP-034 cell is the case $R_{8p-1}=\{1,\ldots,p\}$. The same invariant classifies a
canonical part of every incidence cokernel.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-row summands and a characteristic-dependent cell, {\bf[D/MV]}]
\label{thm:zero-row-characteristic}
Let $p\ge4$, and put $n=10p-1$. The coordinate
$e_F\otimes v_b$ in the codomain of the two-layer incidence map is an integral zero row if and
only if $R_b\subseteq F$. Consequently, in homological degree $i$, these coordinates split off
a primitive free summand of rank
\begin{equation}
z_{p,i}=\sum_{\substack{b\in B_p\\|R_b|\le i}}
\binom{10p-1-|R_b|}{i-|R_b|}
\label{eq:zero-row-rank}
\end{equation}
from the regularity-two cokernel of $K_p$.

For every $2\le t\le p-2$, set
\begin{equation}
b_{p,t}=10p+t,\qquad
F_{p,t}=[3p,4p-2]\cup\{t\}\cup[t+2,p].
\label{eq:zero-row-family}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
R_{b_{p,t}}=F_{p,t},\qquad
|F_{p,t}|=2p-t-1,\qquad
b_{p,t}+\sum F_{p,t}=4p^2+6p-\frac{t(t-1)}2.
\label{eq:zero-row-family-data}
\end{equation}
Thus $K_p$ has a primitive zero-coordinate class in every homological degree
$p+1,\ldots,2p-3$.

At $(p,t)=(4,2)$, the complete multigraded target satisfies
\begin{align}
\beta_{5,(7,87)}^{P_4}(K_4)&=
\begin{cases}5,&\kfield=\mathbf F_2,\\4,&\kfield=\mathbf F_3,\end{cases}
\label{eq:characteristic-kernel}\\
\beta_{5,(7,87)}^{P_4}(A_4)
=\beta_{5,(7,87)}^{P_4}(C_4)&=
\begin{cases}4,&\kfield=\mathbf F_2,\\3,&\kfield=\mathbf F_3.\end{cases}
\label{eq:characteristic-quotients}
\end{align}
The integral cokernel in~\eqref{eq:characteristic-kernel} is
$\mathbb Z^4\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. In particular, the lower multigraded Betti tables of
$K_4$, $A_4$, and $C_4$ depend on the characteristic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:two-layer-incidence}, a coefficient can enter the row
$e_F\otimes v_b$ only from
\[
e_{F\cup\{g\}}\otimes u_{b-g},\qquad g\in R_b\setminus F.
\]
If this set is empty, the row is zero. Conversely, any element of it supplies an incidence entry
with coefficient $1$ or $-1$. Hence the zero-row criterion is integral. The image of the
incidence map has zero projection onto the coordinate module spanned by all such rows, so that
module splits off from the cokernel. For fixed $b$, the number of size-$i$ supersets of $R_b$
is the binomial coefficient in~\eqref{eq:zero-row-rank}, proving the first claim.

Fix $2\le t\le p-2$ and $b=10p+t$. For $1\le g<t$, the complement $b-g$ lies strictly
between the high singleton $10p$ and the next high block beginning at $11p-1$. The value
$g=t$ gives $b-g=10p$, while $g=t+1$ gives the missing offset $10p-1$. Every
$t+2\le g\le p$ gives $8p\le b-g\le10p-2$. For
$3p\le g\le4p-2$,
\[
6p+t+2\le b-g\le7p+t\le8p-2,
\]
so the entire second low block represents $b$. A high variable $g\ge6p$ gives
$b-g<6p$ and contributes nothing. This proves the set equality in
\eqref{eq:zero-row-family-data}. Counting and summing its intervals gives the other two formulas.

It remains to compute the first complete target. Here
\[
b=42,\qquad F=\{2,4,12,13,14\},\qquad i=5,\qquad\tau=87.
\]
The selected coordinate is not protected by the EXP-034 unit-pivot argument. With a fixed high
exterior factor $e_{40}$, an exact ten-term low Koszul cycle has coefficient one on
$e_F\otimes X_2$, and its connecting image therefore reaches $e_F\otimes v_{42}$.
This refutes coordinatewise survival, but not survival of the full target quotient.

The full target has $79$ kernel codomain rows and $119$ kernel boundary columns. Let $d_D$
be the boundary on all $710$ degree-one source chains, $J$ the connecting chain map, and
$\delta_K$ the kernel incidence boundary. For
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}d_D&0\\J&\delta_K\end{pmatrix},
\]
projection of $\operatorname{im}M$ onto $\operatorname{im}d_D$ has kernel
$\operatorname{im}\delta_K+J(\ker d_D)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\dim\operatorname{coker}\bigl(\operatorname{Tor}(D_4)\to
\operatorname{coker}\delta_K\bigr)=79+\operatorname{rank}d_D-\operatorname{rank}M.
\label{eq:complete-target-rank}
\end{equation}
Exact elimination gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\kfield&\operatorname{rank}\delta_K&\dim\operatorname{coker}\delta_K&
\operatorname{rank}d_D&\operatorname{rank}M&\operatorname{rank}J&\dim A_4\\ \hline
\mathbf F_2&74&5&513&588&1&4\\
\mathbf F_3&75&4&513&589&1&3
\end{array}
\]
and the same odd-field row over $\mathbf F_5$ and $\mathbf F_{1000003}$. Smith reduction of
the integral $79$-by-$119$ matrix $\delta_K$ has rank $75$, free cokernel rank four, and
one nonunit invariant factor, equal to $2$. This proves
\eqref{eq:characteristic-kernel} and~\eqref{eq:characteristic-quotients} for $A_4$.

The minimal cubic cone can add to the displayed $C_4$ component only from a four-high-variable
diagonal at shifted offset $87-3p=75$. Its smallest possible offset is
$24+25+26+27=102$. That source is absent, so the $C_4$ values equal the $A_4$ values.
\end{proof}
