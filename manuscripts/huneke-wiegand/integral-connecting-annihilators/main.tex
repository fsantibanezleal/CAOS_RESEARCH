\begin{abstract}
Let $M_p$ be the explicit integral matrix of the connecting map in a lower
Koszul strand of the quadratic part of the presentation ideal of the fiber cone
of the conductor, in a family of Huneke--Wiegand counterexamples. For every
integer $p\geq8$ we prove that its cokernel contains a direct summand
$(\Z/2)^{\lfloor((p-2)^2+3)/12\rfloor}$, indexed by the strictly increasing
nonnegative triples of sum $p-2$. Reflection-symmetric interval potentials give
explicit integral sources for twice each class; relative parity functionals
supported on ten to twelve rows detect each class independently over
$\mathbb F_2$, and their extension gives a nonconstructive splitting. A
previously tracked four-row vector is integrally congruent to one triangle
class. For a semantic projection retaining four of six recurring row types, a
face classification of one source column proves that two endpoint triangle rows
vanish for every $p\geq8$. Exact computations test each identity: complete
$\mathbb F_2$ row-span certificates for all 27 triangles at $p=8,\ldots,12$,
586 endpoint identities through $p=300$, and a finite carrier comparison at
$p=8,\ldots,11$. That $M_p$ presents the stated strand is a premise, machine
verified for $p=8,9,10$ and conjectural for larger $p$. The complete cokernel,
an upper bound, and a uniform comparison with isolated subpresentations remain
open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}

Exact linear algebra can certify a finite presentation without revealing why
the same phenomenon should persist with a parameter. This distinction matters
for the connecting maps studied here: a displayed target vector, an integral
multiple in the image, and a nonzero quotient class are different assertions.
The source identity and relative parity argument close the second and third
of these obligations for a quadratically growing independent family of target
classes. Their subgroup is a direct summand, although its complement remains
undetermined.

The underlying family and its conductor-fiber-cone and graded-syzygy context
were developed in \cite{CAOSMain}; Section~\ref{sec:context} states the problem,
the complex, the strand and the module that the matrix below is meant to
present. We make the present matrix problem self-contained by defining its
complete integral presentation in Section~\ref{sec:matrix}. Every theorem about
that matrix is therefore checkable without reproducing the semigroup
construction. That the matrix presents the stated Koszul strand for every
$p\geq8$ is a premise (Section~\ref{sec:premise}), verified by exact
computation for $p=8,9,10$ and conjectural beyond; the paper neither repeats nor
claims priority for the discovery of the original public Huneke--Wiegand
counterexample.

Signed Koszul strands admit useful combinatorial descriptions. For semigroup
quotients, Bruns and Herzog provide a relative simplicial viewpoint
\cite{BrunsHerzog}. Here the arguments use explicit oriented bases and
low-exterior complements. We do not infer a simplicial or projective-plane
model from a matrix with entries $0,1,-1$, and no unidentified topological
model is a premise of the proof. Potentials and signed complements are
classical tools; the contribution is the explicit triangle family in this
presentation, including its full sources, all boundary corrections, and
complete relative functionals. The general fact that vector-space functionals
extend is used only to deduce splitting; it is not claimed as a new method.

Statements proved deductively are marked [D]; finite exact checks are marked
[MV]. Remaining research questions and every statement that needs the premise
for all $p$ are marked [C]. The annihilator theorem
alone says \emph{order dividing two}. The additional nonvanishing proof in
Section~\ref{sec:parity} upgrades the tracked class to order exactly two.
Section~\ref{sec:family} constructs all triangle classes, proves their exact
relation lattice and count, and derives the direct-summand corollary.
Section~\ref{sec:projection} gives a separate constructive comparison with a
four-type semantic row projection and identifies two uniformly vanishing
endpoint rows. Section~\ref{sec:related} places the construction among the
adjacent methods, Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} gives the verification
algorithms with their cost and reproduction data, and
Section~\ref{sec:validation} reports the finite results, including the carrier
comparison at $p=8,\ldots,11$.
These are lower-bound and partial-decomposition results, not the whole quotient.

\paragraph{Deductive proofs and finite certificates.}
The generic parity, independence and splitting arguments below are deductive.
An independent full-sector recomputation also certifies all 27 triangles at
the five parameters $p=8,\ldots,12$.
Section~\ref{sec:validation} distinguishes these finite certificates from
the exhaustive all-parameter proof; no finite rank pattern is extrapolated.

\section{Problem and context}\label{sec:context}

\subsection{The conjecture and its disproofs}

Let $R$ be a one-dimensional Gorenstein local domain and let $M$ be a nonzero
finitely generated nonfree $R$-module. The Huneke--Wiegand conjecture predicted
that $M\otimes_R\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$ has nontrivial torsion
\cite{HunekeWiegand}. For a numerical semigroup $\Gamma\subseteq\N$ the
semigroup ring is Gorenstein exactly when $\Gamma$ is symmetric, and this
specialization turns part of the module problem into finite additive
combinatorics. Garc\'ia-S\'anchez and Leamer proved broad positive classes for
complete intersection numerical semigroup rings and reported a computer check
of every symmetric semigroup with Frobenius number below $69$
\cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer}. Landeros, O'Neill, Pelayo, Pe\~na, Ren and Wissman
proved the positive case of two-generated monomial ideals for generalized
arithmetic-sequence semigroups \cite{Landeros}. Goto, Takahashi, Taniguchi and
Le Truong study the conjecture through a change of rings to a ring between $R$
and $I{:}I$ \cite{Goto}.

The conjecture is false. Son Pham published a symmetric numerical semigroup of
Frobenius number $181$ and the ideal $(t^{56},t^{70})$ for which the predicted
torsion is absent, with the verification materials in a public repository
\cite{PhamRepo}; an independent verification by C.~Huneke is archived with that
candidate record. A later preprint of the same author \cite{Pham} gives a
numerical-semigroup counterexample that is independent of the characteristic,
reduces its finite core to a sumset identity, and records the trace ideal, the
endomorphism ring, the completion and a direct graded tensor verification.
Christensen, Gerko and Iyengar \cite{CGI} give an independent construction
through a field extension: their criterion is a statement about subspace
multiplication in a field extension of degree $24$, and it differs structurally
from the semigroup family. Priority for the first public counterexample belongs
to Pham, and the present paper claims none.

The preprint \cite{CAOSMain} continues the analysis of Pham's example in two
directions. The Frobenius number $181$ is the least possible in the class of
two-generated monomial ideals over symmetric numerical semigroup rings, and the
same class contains an explicit family $\Gamma_p$, $p\geq4$, of multiplicity
$24p$, embedding dimension $11p$ and conductor $78p$, with the nonprincipal
rigid ideal $(t^{24p},t^{30p})$. After the disproofs the question is no longer
whether the conjecture holds. It is how the counterexample family behaves
homologically, and in particular which integral phenomena produce the
characteristic dependence of its graded Betti numbers.

\subsection{The complex, the strand and the module}

For each $p\geq4$ the conductor $T_p$ of the semigroup ring of $\Gamma_p$ has
reduction number four \cite{NorthcottRees}, and its tangent cone has depth zero
with a Valabrega--Valla defect \cite{ValabregaValla} of length $p$
\cite{CAOSMain}. The tangent cone is Buchsbaum without being Cohen--Macaulay, by the
dimension-one local-cohomology criterion of \cite{DAnnaMezzasalma,DAnnaMicale}, and its
complete graded module over the Noether normalization follows the graded-module framework of
\cite{CortadellasZarzuelaA,CortadellasZarzuelaB}. The endomorphism ring of the ideal
$(t^{24p},t^{30p})$ is strictly larger than the semigroup ring, with $10p$ pseudo-Frobenius elements
and reduced type $10p$ \cite{MaitraMukundan}; its failure of reflexivity and of the Ext and Tor
conditions follows from \cite{DeyLyle}; and the equality of the two colengths, both $p+1$, of the
trace and conductor is the duality identity of \cite{HerzogKumashiro}. Its special fiber, the fiber cone $\mathcal C_p$, is a quotient
$P_p/J_p$ of a polynomial ring $P_p$ in $10p$ degree-one variables $X_g$, where
the index $g$ is the semigroup offset of the variable. The defining ideal is
$J_p=(Q_p,f_p)$: $Q_p$ is generated by $50p^2-17p$ quadrics and
$f_p=X_0^2X_{3p}-X_p^3$ is the single further minimal equation, so that the fiber
cone is not Koszul; the computation of the equations uses the defining-ideal
construction of \cite{AbdolmalekiKumashiro}. The quadratic quotient
$\mathcal A_p=P_p/Q_p$ has depth one and regularity two. It sits in an exact
sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ses}
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal K_p\longrightarrow\mathcal A_p\longrightarrow
 \mathcal D_p\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal D_p=P_p/(Q_p{:}f_p)$ is the canonical idealization of the
Veronese rational normal curve ring of degree $p$, and $\mathcal K_p$ is the
high-variable kernel, with Hilbert numerator $8pz+10pz^2$. The variable offsets
are the $10p-1$ nonzero elements of $L\cup H$, with $L=[1,p]\cup[3p,4p-2]$ the
low offsets and $H=\{g\geq6p\}$ the $8p$ high offsets, in the notation of
Section~\ref{sec:matrix}. The $10p$ degree-two offsets of $\mathcal K_p$ form
the set $\mathcal B$ of that section. The ring $\mathcal C_p$ is a quotient of
$\mathcal A_p$, and the minimal cubic mapping cone relates their graded Betti
numbers by
$B_{\mathcal C_p}(x,z)=B_{\mathcal A_p}(x,z)+xz^3B_{\mathcal D_p}(x,z)$
\cite{CAOSMain}.

Write $\mathcal V$ for the free module on the positive degree-one offsets, with
basis $e_g$, and $u_h$, $v_b$ for the bases of the degree-one and degree-two
parts of $\mathcal K_p/X_0\mathcal K_p$, where $h\in H$ and $b\in\mathcal B$.
Multiplication is $X_gu_h=v_{g+h}$ when $g+h\in\mathcal B$ and zero otherwise,
and the Koszul complex of this module has, in each standard internal degree, the
single integral incidence map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:koszul-incidence}
 \delta(e_F\otimes u_h)=\sum_{\substack{g\in F\\h+g\in\mathcal B}}
 (-1)^{\mathrm{pos}(g,F)}\,e_{F\setminus\{g\}}\otimes v_{h+g},
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{pos}(g,F)$ the zero-based position of $g$ in $F$
\cite{CAOSMain}. The two Betti strands of $\mathcal K_p$ are the kernels and
cokernels of these maps; the lower strand, whose cokernel is the object of this
paper, is the one of regularity two. In the long exact $\operatorname{Tor}$
sequence of \eqref{eq:ses} the connecting map sends cycles of the Koszul
complex of $\mathcal D_p$ into that cokernel. Let $d_D$ be the Koszul boundary on
the degree-one chains of $\mathcal D_p/X_0\mathcal D_p$, $J$ the connecting chain
map, $\delta_K$ the incidence map \eqref{eq:koszul-incidence}, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockmatrix}
 \mathbf M=\begin{pmatrix}d_D&0\\J&\delta_K\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Then the dimension over a field $k$ of the quotient of
$\operatorname{coker}\delta_K$ by the image of the connecting map is
$\operatorname{rows}(\delta_K)+\operatorname{rank}d_D-\operatorname{rank}\mathbf M$:
the image of $\mathbf M$ projects onto $\operatorname{im}d_D$, and the kernel of
that projection is $\operatorname{im}\delta_K+J(\ker d_D)$ \cite{CAOSMain}. This
identity is linear algebra and holds for every $p$ once $\mathbf M$ is given.

The multidegrees of interest are the cells $(p,t)$, $2\leq t\leq p-2$, with
target offset $b_{p,t}=10p+t$, exterior set
$F_{p,t}=[3p,4p-2]\cup\{t\}\cup[t+2,p]$, homological degree $i_{p,t}=2p-t-1$
and total offset $\tau_{p,t}=4p^2+6p-t(t-1)/2$. At $t=2$ these are
$|F_{p,2}|=2p-3$ and $\tau_{p,2}=4p^2+6p-1$, the offset $\tau$ of
Section~\ref{sec:matrix}. The matrix $M_p$ below is $\mathbf M$ in this cell.
Table~\ref{tab:dictionary} gives the dictionary between its labels and the
chains of the Koszul complexes.

\subsection{What integral two-torsion in this cokernel says}

Betti numbers over a field $k$ are dimensions of cokernels of $\mathbf M\otimes k$.
For a finitely generated abelian group $C\cong\Z^r\oplus\bigoplus_i\Z/n_i$
the dimension of $C\otimes k$ is $r$ plus the number of indices with
$\operatorname{char}k\mid n_i$. A change of the Betti number with the
characteristic therefore detects integral torsion: the number of even cyclic
factors minus the number of cyclic factors divisible by three is the excess
$\dim_{\mathbb F_2}-\dim_{\mathbb F_3}$ of the quotient. At the first cell with a
surviving connecting map, $(p,t)=(4,2)$, the multigraded Betti number of
$\mathcal A_4$ and of $\mathcal C_4$ is $4$ over $\mathbb F_2$ and $3$ over
$\mathbb F_3$, and the integral cokernel of $\delta_K$ is $\Z^4\oplus\Z/2$
\cite{CAOSMain}. The characteristic dependence persists on the diagonal $t=2$:
the excess takes the values $1,4,9,18,31,49,72$ for $p=4,\ldots,10$
(\cite{CAOSMain} through $p=9$, and the computation \Exp{EXP-037-connecting-quasipolynomial}{EXP-037}
at $p=10$). For $5\leq p\leq10$ it is created entirely by the connecting-image
quotient, since the kernel cokernel has the same dimension over the two fields.
The values do not fit $(p-3)^2$ or $2p^2-17p+39$, and a period-six cubic
generating function matched to the first six values predicts $73$ at $p=10$
against the exact $72$. No formula for the excess is known.

The groups behind these numbers are the cokernels of the integral matrices
$M_p$, and their structure is the question of this paper.
Table~\ref{tab:excess} compares the lower bound proved in
Section~\ref{sec:family} with the exact excess at the three parameters
$p\geq8$ where both are available. Under the premise of
Section~\ref{sec:premise} the family gives at least $q(p)$ even cyclic factors,
a small part of the excess. The complement $\mathcal C'_p$ of
Corollary~\ref{cor:splitting} therefore carries most of the characteristic-two
behavior, and the number of its cyclic factors divisible by three is unknown.

\section{Related work and adjacent methods}\label{sec:related}

Five families of methods are adjacent to the matching problem posed by the
matrices $M_p$, namely cancelling unit entries until a torsion core remains and
reading its structure uniformly in $p$. Each gives something, and none settles
the matching.

\paragraph{Algebraic Morse theory.} Sk\"oldberg \cite{Skoldberg} and Chen, Liu and
Zhou \cite{ChenLiuZhou} justify unit cancellation and perturbative reconstruction
of chain maps. The finite carrier comparison of Section~\ref{sec:carrier} relies on
exactly this elementary step: a unit pivot gives an integral equivalence
$M\sim[\pm1]\oplus C$ with the Schur complement $C$, and a reduced source lifts
through the ordered pivots. The cited theory does not provide the
problem-specific matching. The actionable target is an explicit parametric
matching or rewrite system for the labels of Section~\ref{sec:matrix}, not a
further appeal to Morse theory.

\paragraph{Matroids over rings.} Fink and Moci \cite{FinkMoci} package deletion,
contraction and torsion data over Dedekind domains. The six recurring row types
of Section~\ref{sec:projection} suggest this language, but the row projections
used here have not been proved to form the required matroid minors, so the
framework applies only after explicit maps are built.

\paragraph{Representation stability.} Church and Ellenberg \cite{ChurchEllenberg}
supply integral stable-range machinery for finitely generated FI-modules. The
labels of $M_p$ depend on affine intervals and on distinguished endpoints, so
ordinary FI-functoriality is not established; an ordered-injection or
finite-state rewrite model would have to be proved first.

\paragraph{Smith forms of structured matrices.} Alfaro and coauthors
\cite{Alfaro} obtain exact Smith forms for Laplacian matrices of simplicial
annuli and high-dimensional trees through graph and sandpile models. The matrices
$M_p$ are not known to be Laplacians, so this is an analogy and a recognition
target rather than evidence. Lu, Ruan, Wang and Xiao \cite{LuRuan} study the
equivalence of multivariate polynomial matrices to a Smith form through
determinantal ideals; the size of $M_p$ changes with $p$, so a polynomial-matrix
model would first require a finite-state block encoding.

\paragraph{Relative simplicial and graded methods.} Bruns and Herzog
\cite{BrunsHerzog} describe relative squarefree-divisor complexes of semigroup
quotients, the framework in which the incidence map
\eqref{eq:koszul-incidence} is read, and Stamate \cite{Stamate} treats Betti
numbers of numerical semigroup rings together with their dependence on the
characteristic. The positive results for generalized arithmetic-sequence
semigroups \cite{Landeros} classify semigroup families; they do not address the
integer presentation once the counterexample is fixed.

What the present construction adds is complementary: explicit integral sources
and detectors, with an exact count of independent order-two classes, but no
matching that exhausts the matrix. The complement $\mathcal C'_p$ is the part
that a matching theorem would have to describe.

\section{The complete integral presentation}\label{sec:matrix}

All intervals in this paper contain integers, and an empty interval contributes
nothing. Fix $p\geq8$. Put
\[
 L_0=[1,p],\qquad L_1=[3p,4p-2],\qquad L=L_0\cup L_1,
 \qquad N=|L|=2p-1.
\]
The high set is the following disjoint union:
\begin{align*}
 H_0&=[6p,8p-2],& H_1&=[8p,10p-2],& H_2&=\{10p\},\\
 H_3&=[11p-1,12p-1],& H_4&=[13p+1,14p-2],\\
 H_5&=[14p,15p-1],& H_6&=\{16p\},& H_7&=[17p-1,18p-1].
\end{align*}
Write $H=\bigcup_{i=0}^7H_i$ and $G=L\cup H$. The degree-two high
coefficient set is $\mathcal B=[6p,24p-1]\setminus H$, explicitly
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal B={}&\{8p-1,10p-1,14p-1\}\cup[10p+1,11p-2]\cup[12p,13p]\\
 &\cup[15p,16p-1]\cup[16p+1,17p-2]\cup[18p,24p-1].
\end{align*}
For later labels, $\mathrm C_0=\{8p-1\}$ and
$\mathrm C_2=[10p+1,11p-2]$. These are names for coefficient subsets,
not subquotients asserted to split off.

Define a low product with values in formal degree-two symbols by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowproduct}
 \mu(a,c)=
 \begin{cases}
  \mathsf A_{a+c},&a,c\in L_0,\ a+c>p,\\
  \mathsf B_{a+c},&a,c\text{ lie in different }L_i,\ a+c\geq4p-1,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The total offset of the strand is
\[
 \tau=4p^2+6p-1=\sum_{a\in L}a+10p-2,
 \qquad \sum_{a\in L}a=4p^2-4p+1.
\]
Let $\mathcal S_p$ be free over $\Z$ on all labels $S[E;c]$ with
$E\subseteq G$, $|E|=2p-2$, $c\in L$, and $\sum E+c=\tau$.
Let $\mathcal K_p$ have the analogous basis $K[E;c]$ with $c\in H$.
All exterior sets are ordered increasingly. The target is free on labels
\[
 D[E;\mathsf A_d],\quad D[E;\mathsf B_d],\quad K[E;b],
 \qquad |E|=2p-3,\quad \sum E+d=\sum E+b=\tau,
\]
where $d\in[p+1,2p]$ for type $\mathsf A$,
$d\in[4p-1,5p-2]$ for type $\mathsf B$, and $b\in\mathcal B$.
Only the appropriate offset is used in each label. Denote the D and K
target summands by $\mathcal D'_p$ and $\mathcal K'_p$.

For $E=\{e_0<\cdots<e_{2p-3}\}$, define
\begin{align}
 M_pS[E;c]={}&
 \sum_{\substack{i:e_i\in L\\\mu(e_i,c)\ne0}}
 (-1)^iD[E\setminus\{e_i\};\mu(e_i,c)]\notag\\
 &+\sum_{\substack{i:e_i\in H\\e_i+c\in\mathcal B}}
 (-1)^iK[E\setminus\{e_i\};e_i+c],\label{eq:MS}\\
 M_pK[E;c]={}&\sum_{\substack{i:e_i+c\in\mathcal B}}
 (-1)^iK[E\setminus\{e_i\};e_i+c].\label{eq:MK}
\end{align}
Thus $M_p:\mathcal S_p\oplus\mathcal K_p\longrightarrow
\mathcal D'_p\oplus\mathcal K'_p$ is completely specified over the integers.
There is no implicit projection in these definitions. Our quotient is
$\coker_{\Z}M_p$, and a source identity always refers to this entire map.


The map $M_p$ is bipartite in the sense that every column has a source type
(an $S$ column or a $K$ column) and every row has a target type (a $D$ row or a
$K$ row), and every nonzero entry is $+1$ or $-1$ by construction: each face of a
column deletes one exterior element, and the sign is the position parity of that
element. An $S$ column has a $D$ part, produced when the deleted low element
multiplies the low coefficient into a degree-two symbol, and a $K$ part,
produced when the deleted element is a high variable whose sum with the low
coefficient is a degree-two offset. A $K$ column has only the $K$ part. The $K$
rows with coefficient in $\mathrm C_0$ and $\mathrm C_2$ carry the combinatorics
of the sections below, because the high set $H$ leaves exactly these two
families of degree-two offsets reachable from the low block. The size of the
matrix grows rapidly with $p$ (the visible high sectors alone hold $1{,}690$
sources at $p=8$, Table~\ref{tab:sectors}), and the proofs therefore never work
with a global basis. They use the coordinates of the labels above.

\section{The four-row target and its earlier representative}\label{sec:eta}

The class that first motivated the construction is a vector of four original
$K$ rows, recorded in the earlier finite computations as a sparse
representative of a connecting image. This section fixes its coordinates in the
labels of Section~\ref{sec:matrix}, and then gives an explicit original source
$s_p+q_p$ whose boundary differs from it by a second representative $b_p$. The
transfer is an identity of integral vectors, not a rank statement, so it
survives to the cokernel with no division and no field reduction. The two
representatives are later shown to have the same class up to sign
(Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}), and the order of that class is the subject of
Sections~\ref{sec:annihilator} and~\ref{sec:parity}.

Set
\[
 G_p(a,j;c)=K[(L\setminus\{a,3p,3p+j\})\cup\{6p\};10p+c].
\]
The tracked endpoint vector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
 \begin{split}
 \eta_p=(-1)^p\bigl(&2G_p(p-3,2;p-3)-G_p(p-2,2;p-2)\\
                  &+2G_p(p-2,1;p-3)-2G_p(p-3,1;p-4)\bigr).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
These are four distinct original rows. Their coefficient offsets are
$11p-3,11p-2,11p-3,11p-4$, all in $\mathrm C_2$.
Exactly one row coefficient is odd. This is a statement about a vector,
not a nonvanishing result in the cokernel. The two representatives of the class
differ in size: $\eta_p$ has four rows with coefficients of height two, whereas
the second representative $b_p$ below is spread over a number of $D$ and $K$ rows that
grows with $p$, because its weights run over $a=1,\ldots,p-4$. The transfer
therefore allows the compact vector to stand in for $b_p$. The single odd
coefficient is what the parity functional of Section~\ref{sec:parity} pairs with, so
the vector $\eta_p$ is also the natural carrier of the nonvanishing proof.

For completeness, define the larger D representative without a choice of
quotient coordinates. Put
\[
 T(a,j)=S[(L\setminus\{a,3p,3p+j\})\cup\{6p,10p\};a+j-2]
\]
and set $s_p=\sum w_j(a)T(a,j)$, with the only nonzero weights
of Table~\ref{tab:weights}.

Let $b_p^A$ and $b_p^B$ be respectively the type-A and type-B parts of

\[
 q_p=K[(L\setminus\{3p,3p+2\})\cup\{6p\};10p].
\]

\begin{proposition}[{[D]} Original representative transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
For every $p\geq8$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
 M_p(s_p+q_p)=b_p+\eta_p.
\end{equation}
Consequently $[b_p]=-[\eta_p]$ in $\coker_{\Z}M_p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every coefficient of $s_p$ lies in $[1,p-3]$. Its $6p$ face vanishes,
while its $10p$ face has negative sign and survives. Hence
$M_ps_p=b_p+\gamma_p$ with
$\gamma_p=-\sum w_j(a)G_p(a,j;a+j-2)$.
In $q_p$, the first-low faces $a=1,\ldots,p-2$ survive with sign
$(-1)^{a-1}$, and the other first-low faces vanish in $H_3$.
Every present second-low face vanishes in $H_4$, and the $6p$ face
vanishes at coefficient offset $16p$. Thus
\[
 M_pq_p=\sum_{a=1}^{p-2}(-1)^{a-1}G_p(a,2;a).
\]
Adding this expression to $\gamma_p$ cancels the interval
$1\leq a\leq p-4$ and leaves exactly \eqref{eq:eta}.
\end{proof}

\section{A complete integral potential basis in one sector}\label{sec:basis}

First fix the exterior high set $\{6p,8p-4\}$, not the high set used
later for the final annihilator. Index first-low variables as $p-r$,
$0\leq r\leq p-1$, and second-low variables as $3p+u$,
$0\leq u\leq p-2$. There are exactly two possible source families:
\begin{align*}
 A_u(r,s)&=S[(L\setminus\{p-r,p-s,3p+u\})\cup\{6p,8p-4\};p+2+u-r-s],\\
 B_r(u,v)&=S[(L\setminus\{p-r,3p+u,3p+v\})\cup\{6p,8p-4\};3p+2+u+v-r].
\end{align*}
Here $r<s$, $u+2\leq r+s\leq p+u+1$ in the first family. In the
second, $u<v$ and
\[
 \max(0,u+v-p+4)\leq r\leq\min(p-1,u+v+2).
\]
Indeed, the coefficient offset is the sum of the three omitted low
variables minus $4p-2$. Three omitted first-low variables make it negative;
three omitted second-low variables make it exceed $4p-2$. The remaining
two types give exactly these bounds. Thus no original source in this
fixed-high sector has been omitted.

\begin{theorem}[{[D]} Integral potential basis]\label{thm:basis}
Let $d_D$ be the D component of $M_p$ restricted to this sector. Choose
arbitrary integral potentials $f_u(r)$ vanishing for $r\leq u+1$.
For $r<s$ put $\alpha_u(r,s)=f_u(s)-f_u(r)$. For $u<v$ put
$R=u+v$, $F=f_u-f_v$, and
\[
 \beta_r(u,v)=
 \begin{cases}F(r)-F(R+3),&R\leq p-4,\\F(r),&R\geq p-3.
 \end{cases}
\]
The source with actual coefficients
\[
 (-1)^{p+r+s+u}\alpha_u(r,s)\quad\hbox{on }A_u(r,s),\qquad
 (-1)^{p+r+u+v}\beta_r(u,v)\quad\hbox{on }B_r(u,v)
\]
parametrizes $\ker_{\Z}d_D$ uniquely. Its unit potentials, indexed by
$0\leq u\leq p-3$, $u+2\leq r\leq p-1$, form an integral basis of
rank $\binom{p-1}{2}$. Each basis source has coefficient height one and
at most $3p-5$ terms; its full $M_p$ boundary has at most seven K rows
and no D rows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We give the signed reconstruction, including truncation boundaries.
Complement an increasing low exterior $E$ inside $L$, and orient its
omitted variables in increasing first-endpoint order followed by increasing
second-endpoint order. For missing positions $d_1,\ldots,d_k$ in $L$,
the unreordered complement sign is
\[
 (-1)^{k(N-1)-\sum d_i-k(k-1)/2}.
\]
For $k=3$, reversing the omitted first-low variables gives exactly the
two source signs in the theorem. Complementing contraction gives wedge
insertion, with common factor $(-1)^{|E|-1}=-1$ here. Therefore all
nonzero D equations, up to a common unit sign on each row, are
\begin{align}
 \alpha_u(r,s)-\alpha_u(r,t)+\alpha_u(s,t)&=0,\label{eq:Aclosure}\\
 \alpha_u(r,s)-\alpha_v(r,s)+\beta_r(u,v)-\beta_s(u,v)&=0.\label{eq:Bclosure}
\end{align}
Equation \eqref{eq:Aclosure} has $r<s<t$ and
$u+2\leq r+s+t\leq p+u+1$. Equation \eqref{eq:Bclosure} has
$r<s$, $u<v$, and
$\max(0,R-p+4)\leq r+s\leq R+3$. Inadmissible source coefficients
are zero. These equations exhaust the A and B products in
\eqref{eq:lowproduct}.

For fixed $u$, read $f_u(r)=\alpha_u(0,r)$ when $r\geq u+2$ and
set the remaining values to zero. Every admissible pair $0<r<s$ occurs
in the A equation with endpoint zero. It forces
$\alpha_u(r,s)=f_u(s)-f_u(r)$. Pairs below the lower sum bound have
both potentials zero. Pairs above the upper bound cannot occur in an A
equation. Conversely these differences solve every A equation.

For a B equation, $r+s\leq R+3\leq p+u+1$ since $v\leq p-2$,
so upper truncation cannot spoil the potential formula. Lower truncation
has both relevant potentials zero. Equation \eqref{eq:Bclosure} becomes
$\beta_r-F(r)=\beta_s-F(s)$. If $R\leq p-4$, the beta interval is
$0,\ldots,R+2$. The equation on $\{0,R+3\}$ forces
$\beta_0=-F(R+3)$, and the equations on $\{0,r\}$ give the displayed
formula. If $R\geq p-3$, write $a=R-p+4\geq1$. Its beta interval
is $a,\ldots,p-1$, and $a\leq u+2$. Both potentials vanish below
$a$, so the equations with endpoint zero give $\beta_r=F(r)$.
The remaining equations hold by subtraction. Reconstruction is unique
and uses no division. The distinguished alpha coordinates therefore give
an integral inverse and an identity coordinate minor.

For one unit potential, alpha has at most $p-1$ terms. Beta is supported
only on second pairs containing its active second index. Usually such a
pair contributes at most one term. At most one pair satisfies
$R=r_0-3$ and contributes a constant interval of at most $p-1$ terms.
Thus beta has at most $2p-4$ terms, and all coefficients have absolute
value one.

Finally, every $6p$ face vanishes because
$6p+L_0\subset H_0$ and $6p+L_1\subset H_1$.
The negative $8p-4$ face survives only for first-low coefficient 3
or second-low coefficients $3p,3p+1,3p+2$. A unit potential gives at
most one first case, on $r+s=p+u-1$. In the second case there are at
most three ordinary partners, determined by $r_0-u_0$, $r_0-u_0-1$,
$r_0-u_0-2$, and at most three rows from the exceptional partner
$r_0-u_0-3$. This proves the full seven-row bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is a complete basis theorem for one D kernel. It is not a normal form
of $\coker M_p$, nor a claim that the whole connecting presentation has
seven rows. In particular, subsequent K relations may kill its images.
\end{remark}

\section{The shifted potential operator}\label{sec:shift}

For the annihilator, move the last high variable to $h=8p-2$.
Let $f_u(r)$ be integral and vanish for $r\leq u$. Define $P(f)$ by
the following two families, omitting zero terms and combining equal labels:
\begin{align}
 &S[(L\setminus\{p-r,p-s,3p+u\})\cup\{6p,h\};p+u-r-s],\notag\\[-3pt]
 &\hspace{18mm}\text{weight }(-1)^{p+r+s+u}(f_u(s)-f_u(r)),
 \quad r<s,\ u\leq r+s\leq p+u-1;\label{eq:Palpha}\\
 &S[(L\setminus\{p-r,3p+u,3p+v\})\cup\{6p,h\};3p+R-r],\notag\\[-3pt]
 &\hspace{18mm}\text{weight }(-1)^{p+r+u+v}\beta_r(u,v),
 \quad u<v,\ \max(0,R-p+2)\leq r\leq\min(p-1,R),\label{eq:Pbeta}
\end{align}
where $R=u+v$, $F=f_u-f_v$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shiftbeta}
 \beta_r(u,v)=\begin{cases}F(r)-F(R+1),&R\leq p-2,\\F(r),&R\geq p-1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Compared with the fixed-high sector of Section~\ref{sec:basis}, the last high
variable moves from $8p-4$ to $8p-2$. This shifts every admissibility window by
two units and changes the surviving faces: in the fixed-high sector the
negative $8p-4$ face survives for first-low coefficient $3$, whereas here
it survives for first-low coefficient $1$, and the C2 family of rows appears
with a diagonal structure that Lemma~\ref{lem:shift} makes explicit. In the shifted
sector the surviving faces lie at the coefficients $8p-1$ (the C0 row) and $11p-2$
(the C2 offset of the rows $x_{uv}$), which is the pair of rows on which the
annihilator of Section~\ref{sec:annihilator} is built. The coefficient offset is now the omitted-low sum minus $4p$, so all
labels have total offset $\tau$. The bounds put their coefficients in
$L_0$ and $L_1$, respectively. Moving the last high variable does not
change the exterior ordering sign. We assert a construction for these
potentials, not a complete basis theorem for the shifted sector.

\begin{lemma}[{[D]} Full shifted boundary]\label{lem:shift}
The D component of $M_pP(f)$ is zero. Every $6p$ face vanishes. The
only other surviving faces are the negative $h$ faces with first-low
coefficient 1 or second-low coefficient $3p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The A and B equations retain the signs \eqref{eq:Aclosure} and
\eqref{eq:Bclosure}, with bounds now
\[
 u\leq r+s+t\leq p+u-1,
 \qquad \max(0,R-p+2)\leq r+s\leq R+1.
\]
In an A equation, a pair below its admissibility bound has both potentials
zero, and no pair exceeds the upper bound. Its differences telescope.
In a B equation, $r+s\leq R+1\leq p+u-1$, so alpha again has no
upper truncation. Its equation is $\beta_r-F(r)=\beta_s-F(s)$.
For $R\leq p-2$, formula \eqref{eq:shiftbeta} extends as zero at
the absent endpoint $R+1$. No endpoint in the equation can exceed it.
For $R\geq p-1$, the lower beta endpoint is $a=R-p+2\leq u$;
both potentials vanish below $a$, so the second formula extends there
by zero. This proves every D equation.

As before, the $6p$ products lie in $H_0$ or $H_1$.
For $c\in L_0$, $h+c$ is in $\mathcal B$ only at $c=1$,
where it equals $8p-1$. The remaining values lie in $H_1$.
For $c=3p+t\in L_1$, $h+c=11p-2+t$ survives only at $t=0$;
the remaining values lie in $H_3$. The low exterior has even size
$2p-4$, so the last high face has negative sign. No other original
boundary row occurs.
\end{proof}

Use the signed K row
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erow}
 e(r;u,v)=(-1)^{p+r+u+v}
 K[(L\setminus\{p-r,3p+u,3p+v\})\cup\{6p\};11p-2+u+v-r],
\end{equation}
only for admissible indices and degree-two offsets. Put
$x_{uv}=e(u+v;u,v)$ when $u<v$ and $u+v\leq p-1$.
For orientation, let $y_{uv}=x_{uv}$ if $u<v$,
$y_{uv}=-x_{vu}$ if $u>v$, and $y_{uu}=0$.
In particular, $x_{01}=e(1;0,1)$ is different from $e(2;0,1)$.

The C0 boundary in Lemma~\ref{lem:shift} lies on
$r+s=p+u-1$. Its normalized coefficient is $-\alpha_u(r,s)$.
The C2 boundary is diagonal: for $R\leq p-2$ its coefficient on
$x_{uv}$ is $F(R+1)-F(R)$; at $R=p-1$ it is $-F(p-1)$.
There is no such diagonal row for $R>p-1$. This last endpoint cannot
be replaced by an unrestricted difference formula.

\section{Reflection intervals and the uniform annihilator}\label{sec:annihilator}

\begin{lemma}[{[D]} Signed interval triangle]\label{lem:triangle}
Let $i,a,b$ be distinct nonnegative second indices, with $a<b$ and
$i+a+b=p-2$. Let $T_i(a,b)$ have the single nonzero potential
\[
 f_i(r)=1\quad\text{for }i+a+1\leq r\leq i+b.
\]
Then the exact original boundary is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
 M_pP(T_i(a,b))=y_{ia}-y_{ib}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The interval lies strictly inside $i<r<p-1$ and its endpoints sum to
$p+i-1$. It is therefore invariant under $r\mapsto p+i-1-r$.
Every possible C0 pair has this sum, so its two potential values agree,
including pairs outside the interval. All C0 rows vanish.
For C2 write $g(t)=f_i(i+t)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:shift} gives the
oriented edge coefficient $g(v+1)-g(v)$. The interval indicator jumps
by $+1$ at $v=a$ and by $-1$ at $v=b$, and nowhere else.
There is no top-endpoint contribution because the interval ends below
$p-1$. This proves \eqref{eq:triangle} in the full target, not just
after quotienting by K relations.
\end{proof}

For $j=1,2$, put $k=p-2-j$. Define the three-interval potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fj}
 \begin{aligned}
 (F_j)_0(r)&=1&&\text{for }j+1\leq r\leq k,\\
 (F_j)_j(r)&=-1&&\text{for }j+1\leq r\leq p-2,\\
 (F_j)_k(r)&=-1&&\text{for }k+1\leq r\leq p-2,
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and set all other values to zero. The indices $0,j,k$ are distinct
for every $p\geq8$.

\begin{proposition}[{[D]} Twice-edge source]\label{prop:twiceedge}
For $j=1,2$, $M_pP(F_j)=2x_{0j}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The definition is $F_j=T_0(j,k)-T_j(0,k)-T_k(0,j)$. Since $j<k$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:triangle} gives
\[
 M_pP(F_j)=(x_{0j}-x_{0k})-(-x_{0j}-x_{jk})-(-x_{0k}+x_{jk})
          =2x_{0j}.
\]
All three interval sources have zero C0 boundary individually.
The auxiliary edge satisfies $j+k=p-2$, within the diagonal domain.
\end{proof}

Two original K-source corrections are required to recover the particular
four-row target, rather than just the diagonal edges. Let $\delta_{0a}$
be the unit potential at $(u,r)=(0,a)$, and, for $a=2,3$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qa}
 Q_a=K[(L\setminus\{p-a,3p\})\cup\{6p\};8p-2-a]
\end{equation}
with \emph{positive} unit coefficient.

\begin{lemma}[{[D]} Exact short corrections]\label{lem:corrections}
Put $B=P(\delta_{03})-Q_3$ and $D=P(\delta_{02})+Q_2$. Then
\begin{align}
 M_pB&=e(3;0,1)+x_{02}+e(3;0,2),\label{eq:Bboundary}\\
 M_pD&=x_{01}+e(2;0,1).\label{eq:Dboundary}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:shift} gives diagonal boundary
$x_{0,a-1}-x_{0a}$ for $P(\delta_{0a})$.
Its only C0 pair is $(a,p-1-a)$, with distinct increasing endpoints
for $a=2,3$ and $p\geq8$. The normalized alpha value is $-1$;
its source sign is $(-1)^{2p-1}=-1$ and the high-face sign is negative.
Thus the actual C0 coefficient is $-1$.

The only first-low face of $Q_a$ surviving in $\mathcal B$ removes
$a+1$, has coefficient offset $8p-1$, and sign $(-1)^a$.
Indeed the omitted first-low variable $p-a$ is larger than $a+1$,
so the latter has exterior position $a$. This is the same C0 row.
The surviving second-low faces remove $3p+v$, $1\leq v\leq a$.
Their coefficient offset is $11p-2-a+v$, and their exterior sign is
$(-1)^{p+v-2}$. In the signed convention \eqref{eq:erow}, their
coefficient on $e(a;0,v)$ is $(-1)^a$. Every other low product lies
in $H$. The high face has coefficient offset $14p-2-a$; the two
values $14p-4$ and $14p-5$ both lie in $H_4$ when $p\geq8$.
There is therefore no discarded high-free row or hidden filler.

Consequently the source $J_a=P(\delta_{0a})+(-1)^aQ_a$ cancels C0 exactly.
Its boundary is
\[
 M_pJ_a=x_{0,a-1}+\sum_{v=1}^{a-1}e(a;0,v).
\]
Specializing to $a=3$ and $a=2$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[{[D]} Uniform original-map annihilation]\label{thm:main}
For every integer $p\geq8$, the explicitly defined integral source
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V}
 V_p=P(F_2+2F_1-4\delta_{03}-4\delta_{02})+4Q_3-4Q_2
\end{equation}
satisfies $M_pV_p=2\eta_p$. Hence both $[\eta_p]$ and $[b_p]$ have
order dividing two in the full integral cokernel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Changing \eqref{eq:eta} from raw to signed row labels gives
\[
 \eta_p=-2e(3;0,2)-x_{02}-2e(2;0,1)-2e(3;0,1).
\]
Equations \eqref{eq:Bboundary}--\eqref{eq:Dboundary} therefore give
the exact target-vector equality
\[
 \eta_p=x_{02}+2x_{01}-2M_pB-2M_pD.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:twiceedge}, twice the right-hand side is
\[
 M_p\bigl(P(F_2)+2P(F_1)-4B-4D\bigr)=M_pV_p.
\]
Every term is an admissible original source and every boundary row has
been included. Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} also gives the explicit
identity $M_p(2s_p+2q_p-V_p)=2b_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[{[D]} Base change with two invertible]\label{cor:basechange}
Let $R$ be any commutative coefficient ring in which $2$ is a unit.
In the cokernel of $M_p\otimes_{\Z}R$, the images of $\eta_p$ and
$b_p$ vanish, with explicit sources $V_p/2$ and
$s_p+q_p-V_p/2$, respectively.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Tensor the two original source identities with $R$ and multiply by the
unit $2^{-1}$. This argument concerns only the two tracked classes.
\end{proof}

\section{A relative parity functional and exact order two}\label{sec:parity}

All coefficients in this section lie in $\mathbb F_2$. Write the full map as
\[
 M_p(s,t)=(d_Ds,\ d_K^Ss+d_K^Kt).
\]
A functional on the K target need not annihilate every S column individually.
It suffices to annihilate $d_K^K$ and $d_K^S(\ker d_D)$ while taking
value one on $\eta_p$. This relative formulation is essential: no functional
on a truncated matrix is substituted for a statement about the full map.

\subsection{The reflection template and its twelve-row specialization}

Let $T$ be any unordered triple of distinct indices in $[0,p-2]$ whose
sum is $p-2$. Define
\[
 z^T_{uv}=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&\{u,v,p-2-u-v\}\text{ is the multiset }T,\\
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Invalid indices and diagonals have value zero. Write $z=z^T$ temporarily.
It is symmetric and has the reflection identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zreflection}
 z_{uv}=z_{vu}=z_{u,p-2-u-v}.
\end{equation}
Assign the row $e(r;u,v)$ the value $z_{uv}$ when $r=u+v$ or $u+v+1$,
and zero otherwise. On a C0 row
\[
 K[(L\setminus\{p-r,p-s,3p+u\})\cup\{6p\};8p-1],
 \qquad r<s,\quad r+s=p+u-1,
\]
assign value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dreflection}
 d_u(r)=z_{u,r-u}+z_{u,r-u-1}.
\end{equation}
Set every other original K row to zero. This defines $\lambda_T$.

The C0 definition does not depend on which endpoint is used. If
$n=p-1-u$ and $t=r-u$, then $s-u=n-t$, and
\eqref{eq:zreflection} exchanges the two summands of $d_u(r)$ and
$d_u(s)$. At a reflected fixed point $r=s$, the summands coincide and
$d_u(r)=0$. Thus no repeated exterior variable is introduced. Possible
cancellations in \eqref{eq:dreflection} are retained. More precisely, write
$T=\{i,j,k\}$ with $i<j<k$. For each $u\in T$, let $v<w$ be its other
two entries. The C0 pairs are
\[
 (r,s;u)=(u+v,u+w+1;u),\qquad (u+v+1,u+w;u).
\]
The second pair is omitted if $w=v+1$: it is a fixed point and its two
values cancel modulo two. All endpoints lie in $[0,p-1]$, and distinct
$u$ give distinct omitted second-low variables. There are always six
distinct C2 rows, so the exact support size is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:template-support}
 |\supp\lambda_T|=12-\mathbf1_{j=i+1}-\mathbf1_{k=j+1}.
\end{equation}
It ranges from ten to twelve; for example $T=\{1,2,3\}$ at $p=8$ has
ten rows. Figure~\ref{fig:support}(a) draws the twelve-row case. No numerical
support count is assumed in this argument.

For the tracked class choose $T=\{0,2,k\}$, where $k=p-4$, and write
$\lambda_K=\lambda_T$. Its six C2 rows are
\[
 e(S;u,v),\ e(S+1;u,v),\qquad
 (u,v)=(0,2),(0,k),(2,k),\quad S=u+v.
\]
Its six C0 rows have omitted endpoints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twelveC0}
 \begin{gathered}
 (r,s;u)=(2,p-3;0),(3,p-4;0),(2,p-1;2),\\
 (3,p-2;2),(p-4,p-1;k),(p-3,p-2;k).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All twelve rows are distinct and admissible for $p\geq8$. The C0
omitted-low sum is $4p+1$, which verifies its target grading; C2 grading
follows from \eqref{eq:erow}. The two coefficient types are distinct.
Modulo two, \eqref{eq:eta} is just $e(2;0,2)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-pairing}
 \lambda_K(\eta_p)=z_{02}=1.
\end{equation}

\subsection{All original K sources}

\begin{lemma}[{[D]} Complete K-source annihilation]\label{lem:Kparity}
Every reflection template $\lambda_T$ annihilates $d_K^K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A source with no exterior high variable cannot reach a supporting row.
With at least two exterior highs, a low face retains too many highs,
while a high face has coefficient at least $12p$, larger than every
supported coefficient. With one high different from $6p$, a low face
retains the wrong high and a high face has none. It remains only to
consider exterior high set $\{6p\}$.

Its low exterior omits two elements of $L$. If their sum is $m$, grading
forces coefficient $4p-2+m$. Two omitted first-low endpoints $r<s$
would give $6p-2-r-s<6p$, not an original K coefficient.
One omitted first-low and one second-low variable gives
\[
 X(r,u)=K[(L\setminus\{p-r,3p+u\})\cup\{6p\};8p-2+u-r].
\]
The only excluded high value in its possible coefficient range is $8p-1$,
so this column exists precisely when $r\geq u$ or $r\leq u-2$.
If $r\leq u-2$, neither supported C2 endpoint is possible and the
other C0 endpoint would exceed $p-1$. If $r\geq u$, its possible
C0 face has reflected endpoint $s=p+u-1-r$ and value $d_u(r)$.
At $s=r$ the face is absent but its assigned value is zero. Its C2
faces have second indices $r-u$ and $r-u-1$, with total value again
$d_u(r)$; invalid or already omitted indices have value zero.
The two contributions cancel, and the high face is invisible.

Finally, two omitted second-low indices $u<v$, of sum $S$, force
coefficient $10p-2+S$. This is in $H$ precisely for $S=2$ or
$S\geq p+1$. In the first case both supported first endpoints 2 and
3 are present and have the same value $z_{uv}$. In the second neither
$S$ nor $S+1$ is an available endpoint. Other faces are invisible.
This exhausts every original K source.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reachability and complete D kernels}

An S source can reach the functional only by deleting a high variable
from an exterior high set $\{6p,h\}$. If $b$ is a supported target
coefficient and $c\in L$ the source coefficient, then $h=b-c\in H$.
The complete intersection calculation is given in Table~\ref{tab:visible}.

$b$ & $c\in L_0$ & $c\in L_1$\\
\midrule
$8p-1$ & $[7p-1,8p-2]$ & none\\
$11p-2$ & $\{10p-2,10p\}$ & $[7p,8p-2]$\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\end{table}
Thus only
\begin{equation}\label{eq:visible-highs}
 h\in[7p-1,8p-2]\cup\{10p-3,10p-2,10p\}
\end{equation}
can matter. In particular $10p-3$ is present: first-low coefficient
$p$ reaches $11p-3$. The D differential preserves the complete
exterior-high set. Its kernel therefore splits into kernels of the
individual high-set sectors, and all sectors outside
\eqref{eq:visible-highs} have zero pairing term by term.

Consider first $h=8p-d$, with $2\leq d\leq p+1$. A source omits
three low variables of sum $m$, with coefficient $m-4p+d-2$.
Three omitted first-low variables make this at most $d-p-5<1$;
three omitted second-low variables make it at least $5p+d+1>4p-2$.
The two exhaustive source families and their coefficients are
\begin{align*}
 \alpha_u(r,s):&\quad p+d-2+u-r-s,
 &u+d-2&\leq r+s\leq p+u+d-3,\quad r<s;\\
 \beta_r(u,v):&\quad 3p+d-2+S-r,
 &\max(0,S+d-p)&\leq r\leq\min(p-1,S+d-2),\quad u<v.
\end{align*}
They have the same omitted-variable patterns as the alpha and beta
sources of Section~\ref{sec:basis}. Alpha coefficients can only lie
in $L_0$, beta coefficients only in $L_1$.

\begin{proposition}[{[D]} Complete generalized potential reconstruction]\label{prop:complete-d}
Every D cycle in this sector is uniquely parametrized over $\mathbb F_2$
by potentials satisfying
\[
 f_u(0)=0,\qquad f_u(r)=0\text{ for }r<u+d-2.
\]
Its coefficients are
\begin{align}
 \alpha_u(r,s)&=f_u(r)+f_u(s),\label{eq:fullalpha}\\
 \beta_r(u,v)&=
 \begin{cases}
 F(r)+F(S+d-1),&S\leq p-d,\\
 F(r),&S\geq p-d+1,
 \end{cases}\qquad F=f_u+f_v.\label{eq:fullbeta}
\end{align}
In particular, for $d=2$ the coordinates $f_u(u)$ are free for every
$u\geq1$. Only $f_0(0)$ is excluded by the endpoint-zero convention.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The complete A equations are
\[
 \alpha_u(r,s)+\alpha_u(r,t)+\alpha_u(s,t)=0,
 \quad u+d-2\leq r+s+t\leq p+u+d-3.
\]
Set $f_u(r)=\alpha_u(0,r)$ for $r>0$ with $r\geq u+d-2$,
and zero otherwise. The pair's upper bound is at least $p-1$.
Every other admissible pair occurs in its A equation with endpoint zero,
which forces \eqref{eq:fullalpha}. Conversely these potentials solve
all A equations: a pair below the lower bound has both potentials zero,
and a pair above the upper bound cannot occur in an admissible triple.

The complete B equations are
\[
 \alpha_u(r,s)+\alpha_v(r,s)+\beta_r(u,v)+\beta_s(u,v)=0,
 \quad\max(0,S+d-p)\leq r+s\leq S+d-1.
\]
Since $S+d-1\leq p+u+d-3$, both alpha terms can be replaced by
their potential sums, including lower truncations. The equation becomes
$\beta_r+F(r)=\beta_s+F(s)$.

If $S\leq p-d$, the beta interval is $0,\ldots,b$ with
$b=S+d-2\leq p-2$. The equation on $\{0,b+1\}$ forces
$\beta_0=F(b+1)$; the other endpoint-zero equations force the first
formula in \eqref{eq:fullbeta}. It extends as zero at $b+1$ and solves
every B equation. If $S\geq p-d+1$, put $a=S+d-p\geq1$.
The beta interval is $a,\ldots,p-1$, possibly empty, and
$a\leq u+d-2$. Both potentials vanish below $a$, so the endpoint-zero
equations force $\beta_r=F(r)$; this extends by zero below $a$.
If $a>p-1$, both potentials vanish everywhere and there is no beta
source. Thus all truncations, empty intervals, and converses are checked.
The reconstruction reads original coordinates, not a rank pattern.
\end{proof}

The additional free coordinates at $d=2$ cannot be suppressed here.
Section~\ref{sec:shift} used a restricted integral construction with
$f_u(r)=0$ for $r\leq u$; nonvanishing requires the complete kernel,
including its previously unused diagonal coordinates. The first-low endpoint
zero used in the reconstruction is available even when the smallest
second-low index in $T$ is positive. It places no restriction on the triangle.

\subsection{Pairing the complete kernels and the remaining sectors}

For a supported C0 face in a generalized potential cycle, the source
coefficient is $d-1$ and $r+s=p+u-1$. Reflection makes its value
$(f_u(r)+f_u(s))d_u(r)$. Fixed points contribute zero. Every nonzero
potential has $r\geq u$, so its reflected endpoint is also available.
Summing all distinct pairs gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity-equal}
 \sum_{u,r}f_u(r)d_u(r)
   =\sum_{u<v}z_{uv}\bigl(F(u+v)+F(u+v+1)\bigr).
\end{equation}
On the support of a triple template, $1\leq S=u+v\leq p-2$,
so both potential indices in this sum are available.

For C2, only $r=S$ and $r=S+1$ can pair. Their source coefficients
are $3p+d-2$ and $3p+d-3$. If $d=2$, only $r=S$ is admissible;
\eqref{eq:fullbeta} gives $F(S)+F(S+1)$. If $3\leq d\leq p$,
both endpoints are admissible, and their common beta constant, when
present, cancels. If $d=p+1$, only $r=S+1$ is admissible. The sole
possible nonzero potential is $f_0(p-1)$, so $F(S)=0$ and the same
expression remains. Thus C2 pairing equals \eqref{eq:parity-equal}.
It cancels C0 over $\mathbb F_2$. Every other K face has functional
value zero; it need not itself vanish.

It remains to treat $h=10p-3,10p-2,10p$. Sources omitting two first-low
and one second-low variable cannot occur: their omitted sum is at most
$6p-3$, giving coefficient at most $-2$ even for $h=10p-3$.
Three omitted first-low variables also cannot occur. Sources omitting
three second-low variables, if admissible, have second-low coefficients
and do not contribute to A rows. Therefore the only source family with
first-low coefficient omits one first-low and two second-low variables.
For fixed $u<v$ and $S=u+v$, write its coefficients as $a_r$.
Its coefficient offsets are respectively
\[
 p+1+S-r,\qquad p+S-r,\qquad p-2+S-r.
\]
The lower endpoint bounds are $r\geq S+1$, $r\geq S$, and
$r\geq\max(0,S-2)$.

For each existing $r>0$, removal of the first-low variable $p$ gives
an A equation on endpoints $\{0,r\}$. Its only possible other
contributor is $a_0$. In the first two high sectors $a_0$ is absent,
so these equations force every $a_r$ to zero. For $h=10p$ the same
argument applies when $S\geq3$. For the remaining possible sums
$S=1,2$, use the A row on endpoints $\{S,S+1\}$. Its coefficient
offset is $2p-3-S>p$ and its only contributors are
$a_S+a_{S+1}$, which therefore vanishes. These are exactly the two
supported C2 contributions. This also covers triple templates containing
$\{0,1\}$; the selected twelve-row template itself has sums
$2,p-4,p-2$. There is no C0 contribution in these three high sectors.
Other families may contribute to B equations but cannot change the A
constraints used here.

We have proved the following relative statement on the complete map.
\begin{proposition}[{[D]} Relative reflection functional]\label{prop:relative-functional}
For every distinct triple template $T$ above,
\[
 \lambda_Td_K^K=0,\qquad
 \lambda_Td_K^S(s)=0\quad\text{whenever }d_Ds=0.
\]
This statement applies to every triangle, including those with positive
smallest entry; Section~\ref{sec:family} uses it to establish independence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[{[D]} Exact order of the tracked class]\label{thm:exact-order}
For every $p\geq8$, $\eta_p$ is nonzero in the cokernel of
$M_p\otimes\mathbb F_2$. Its class in $\coker_{\Z}M_p$ has exact
order two. The same is true of the class of $b_p$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $\eta_p=M_p(s,t)$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Its zero D component
implies $d_Ds=0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:Kparity} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-functional}, for $T=\{0,2,p-4\}$,
would give
\[
 1=\lambda_K(\eta_p)=\lambda_K(d_K^Ss+d_K^Kt)=0,
\]
contradicting \eqref{eq:eta-pairing}. An integral source for $\eta_p$
would reduce to a source modulo two, so its integral class is also nonzero.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} proves twice the class is zero. Its order is
therefore exactly two. Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} transfers the
conclusion to $[b_p]=-[\eta_p]$.
\end{proof}

\section{Theory of the quadratic triangle family and its splitting}\label{sec:family}

Return to integral coefficients for source identities, and put $n=p-2$.
Let
\[
 \mathcal T_p=\{(i,j,k):0\leq i<j<k,\ i+j+k=n\},\qquad
 \mathcal C_p=\coker_{\Z}M_p.
\]
For $T=(i,j,k)$ select the original signed row $x_T=x_{ij}$ from
\eqref{eq:erow}. Its first endpoint $i+j$ is at most $p-2$, and its
coefficient offset is $11p-2$, so it is an admissible original target.
All subsequent relation statements take place in $\mathcal C_p$, not in
a projected or isolated subpresentation.

\begin{proposition}[{[D]} Full original triangle sources]\label{prop:all-triangle-sources}
For every $T=(i,j,k)\in\mathcal T_p$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WT}
 F_T=T_i(j,k)-T_j(i,k)-T_k(i,j),\qquad W_T=P(F_T).
\end{equation}
Here each $T_u(a,b)$ is the integral interval potential of
Lemma~\ref{lem:triangle}, and $P$ retains exactly the source labels and
signs in \eqref{eq:Palpha}--\eqref{eq:shiftbeta}. Then
$M_pW_T=2x_T$ in the full original target.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For distinct $u,a,b$ with $a<b$ and $u+a+b=n$, the interval
$[u+a+1,u+b]$ is nonempty, lies strictly above $u$, and ends at
$n-a\leq p-2$. Its endpoints sum to $p+u-1$. Thus every interval
in \eqref{eq:WT} satisfies all hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:triangle},
regardless of whether $i=0$. The complete D boundary cancels by the
signed potential equations; every $6p$ face vanishes; reflection kills
C0; and the two interval jumps give precisely its oriented C2 edges.
Consequently
\[
 \begin{split}
 M_pW_T
  &=(x_{ij}-x_{ik})-(-x_{ij}-x_{jk})-(-x_{ik}+x_{jk})\\
  &=2x_{ij}.
 \end{split}
\]
All coefficients here are integers. No unsupported boundary row has been
dropped, and no subsequent K relation is needed for the equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[{[D]} Independent triangle classes and exact count]\label{thm:quadratic}
For every $p\geq8$, the relation lattice of the classes
$([x_T])_{T\in\mathcal T_p}$ in $\mathcal C_p$ is exactly
$2\Z^{\mathcal T_p}$. In particular there is an injection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-injection}
 \iota:(\Z/2)^{\mathcal T_p}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathcal C_p,
 \qquad \mathbf e_T\longmapsto[x_T],
 \qquad |\mathcal T_p|=\left\lfloor\frac{(p-2)^2+3}{12}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An unordered edge $\{a,b\}$ belongs to at most one triple of sum $n$:
its third entry must be $n-a-b$. Thus different triangles have disjoint
unordered edge sets. By the C2 definition of the relative functionals,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonal-pairing}
 \lambda_T(x_U)=\delta_{TU}\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_2.
\end{equation}
Suppose that $\sum_U m_Ux_U=M_p(s,t)$ integrally. Reducing the full
source equation modulo two, its zero D component gives $d_Ds=0$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-functional} therefore forces every
$\lambda_T$ to evaluate to zero on the right-hand K component.
Equation~\eqref{eq:diagonal-pairing} gives $m_T\equiv0\pmod2$ for
every $T$. Conversely, every even coefficient vector is a relation by
Proposition~\ref{prop:all-triangle-sources}. This proves the exact
relation lattice and the injection, not merely nonzero target vectors.

To count the triples, let $q(n)$ count strictly increasing nonnegative
triples of sum $n$. For $n\geq6$, triples with smallest entry at least
two correspond bijectively to those of sum $n-6$, by subtracting two
from every entry. Those with smallest entry zero number
$\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$, and those with smallest entry one number
$\lfloor(n-2)/2\rfloor-1$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-recurrence}
 q(n)-q(n-6)=n-3\qquad(n\geq6).
\end{equation}
The initial counts for $n=0,\ldots,5$ are $0,0,0,1,1,2$.
The function $\lfloor(n^2+3)/12\rfloor$ has those same values and
the same recurrence, since the two unrounded arguments differ by the
integer $n-3$. Induction in each residue class modulo six proves the
formula for all $n\geq0$, hence for $n=p-2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[{[D]} A direct summand of integral two-torsion]\label{cor:splitting}
The injection \eqref{eq:family-injection} splits as a homomorphism of
abelian groups. Thus, for some unspecified abelian group $\mathcal C'_p$,
\[
 \mathcal C_p\cong
 (\Z/2)^{\lfloor((p-2)^2+3)/12\rfloor}\oplus\mathcal C'_p.
\]
This is an existence statement for a retraction, not an explicit uniform
formula for its values on all D rows or a complete cokernel computation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Work first over $\mathbb F_2$. For each $T$, define a functional on
the subspace $\operatorname{im}d_D$ of the full D target by
\[
 \mu_T(d_Ds)=\lambda_T(d_K^Ss).
\]
It is well-defined: two choices of $s$ differ by a complete D cycle,
whose connecting image is annihilated by
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-functional}. Extend $\mu_T$ linearly
to the entire D target, by completing a basis of $\operatorname{im}d_D$.
The target is finite-dimensional for each fixed $p$, so such an extension
exists. The functional $(\mu_T,\lambda_T)$ annihilates the full map:
on an S source its two values are equal and cancel over $\mathbb F_2$,
and on a K source the value is zero by Lemma~\ref{lem:Kparity}.

Reduction modulo two followed by these extended functionals therefore
defines a group homomorphism
$\rho:\mathcal C_p\longrightarrow(\Z/2)^{\mathcal T_p}$.
Every selected target $x_U$ has zero D component, so
\eqref{eq:diagonal-pairing} gives $\rho\iota=\mathrm{id}$.
Thus $\mathcal C_p=\operatorname{im}\iota\oplus\ker\rho$.
The extension is noncanonical; no global D-row certificate is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[{[D]} Exact transfer of the tracked class]\label{prop:eta-edge}
The original source
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta-edge-source}
 C=P(F_1)-2B-2D
   =P(F_1-2\delta_{03}-2\delta_{02})+2Q_3-2Q_2
\end{equation}
satisfies $M_pC=\eta_p-x_{02}$. Hence
$[\eta_p]=[x_{02}]$ and $[b_p]=-[x_{02}]$ in $\mathcal C_p$,
where $x_{02}$ is chosen for the triangle $(0,2,p-4)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The target identity in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
$\eta_p=x_{02}+2x_{01}-2M_pB-2M_pD$. Apply
$M_pP(F_1)=2x_{01}$ and then Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}.
This proves a quotient equality with an explicit source for its difference,
not equality of the original target vectors $\eta_p$ and $x_{02}$.
\end{proof}

Let $\Pi_{58}$ be the coordinate projection that retains all rows of types
$R_1,R_3,R_4,R_5$ and discards every other target row. The numeral is only a
stable name for this four-type mask; it is not a modulus.

\begin{theorem}[{[D]} Support-one endpoint sources]\label{thm:mask58-endpoints}
For $r\in\{1,2\}$, put
\begin{align*}
 E_{p,r}&=(L\setminus\{p-r,3p,3p+r\})\cup\{6p,10p\},\\
 s_{p,r}&=S[E_{p,r};p-2].
\end{align*}
Then, for every $p\geq8$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mask58-endpoints}
 \Pi_{58}M_ps_{p,r}=(-1)^{p+1}x_{(0,r,p-2-r)},
\end{equation}
that is, minus the unsigned $K$ row that underlies $x_{(0,r,p-2-r)}$ in~\eqref{eq:erow}.
Thus the two endpoint triangle rows vanish integrally in
$\coker(\Pi_{58}M_p)$, each with a one-column source of coefficient height one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exterior $E_{p,r}$ contains $p-1$ first-low generators, $p-3$
second-low generators, and the two displayed high generators, hence has
size $2p-2$ as required.

Write $a=p-2$. If $v\in L_0$, then $\mu(v,a)$ is nonzero exactly when
$v+a>p$, or $v\geq3$. Deleting such a present generator gives normalized
counts $(-2,-3,1,0,1,0,0,0,0,0)$, hence an $R_0$ row. There are $p-3$
of them because $p-r$ is omitted. If $v\in L_1$, then $\mu(v,a)$ is
nonzero exactly when $v+a\geq4p-1$, or $v\geq3p+1$. The source already
omits $3p$ and $3p+r$, so all its $p-3$ remaining second-low deletions
give $R_2$ rows, with normalized counts
$(-1,-4,1,0,1,0,0,0,0,0)$.

Deleting $6p$ would give coefficient $7p-2$, which belongs to the
degree-one interval $H_0$ and not to the degree-two target basis, so that
face contributes nothing. Deleting $10p$ gives coefficient $11p-2\in
\mathrm C_2$ and normalized counts
$(-1,-3,1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)$, hence type $R_5$. The generator $10p$ is
last in a $2p-2$ element exterior set, at zero-based position $2p-3$,
so its sign is $-1$. Its face is the unsigned row
$K[(L\setminus\{p-r,3p,3p+r\})\cup\{6p\};11p-2]$, which by~\eqref{eq:erow} with $u=0$, $v=r$ equals
$(-1)^{p+2r}x_{(0,r,p-2-r)}=(-1)^p x_{(0,r,p-2-r)}$.

The complete nonzero boundary therefore consists of $p-3$ rows of type
$R_0$, $p-3$ rows of type $R_2$, and the single $R_5$ row with coefficient $-1$ on the
unsigned row, which is~\eqref{eq:mask58-endpoints}. The projection discards the first two types
and retains the last one.
\end{proof}

The identity is stronger than a finite rank observation because it is proved
in the full original presentation before contraction. It is also narrower
than an identification of presentations: no uniform isomorphism between this
semantic projection and a persistent isolated component is asserted. At the
locked parameter $p=11$, the frozen component contains unique matching source
columns 210 and 308, whose retained entries are respectively $-1$ in the
unsigned rows 12559 and 12560 [MV], as~\eqref{eq:mask58-endpoints} predicts. This exact finite agreement does not extend the scope of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mask58-endpoints} to a uniform component reconstruction.

In particular there are at least $3,4,5,7$ independent integral
two-torsion classes at $p=8,9,10,11$, respectively, and their number
is quadratically unbounded. If two is invertible in a coefficient ring,
each selected class vanishes with source $W_T/2$; this gives no vanishing
claim for the complementary quotient. Agreement of these lower bounds
with earlier finite isolated ranks does not identify that isolated object
with $\mathcal C_p$, nor does it establish an upper bound.

\section{Algorithms and reproducibility}\label{sec:algorithms}

The deductive results of Sections~\ref{sec:basis}--\ref{sec:projection} are
independent of any program. The finite results of Section~\ref{sec:validation}
come from four procedures, each written twice in the computation records, once
for the primary computation and once for the independent recomputation that does
not import the primary formulas. This section gives each procedure as
pseudocode, with the quantities it reads and writes, its cost in the parameter
$p$, and the commands, software and measured times that reproduce it. Every
number in the cost tables is read from a stored artifact of the cited
computation, and every operation count is read from the loop structure of its
program.

\subsection{Potential-chain construction}

The operator $P$ of Section~\ref{sec:shift} maps a potential, that is, an
integral weight $f(u,r)$ on pairs with $r>u$, to an original source. The
primary program is a direct transcription of \eqref{eq:Palpha} and
\eqref{eq:Pbeta}. Algorithm~\ref{alg:potential} lists it for the shifted
sector with exterior high set $\{6p,8p-2\}$. Each emitted label has exterior set
of size $2p-2$ and total offset $\tau$ by the grading argument of
Section~\ref{sec:shift}, which the program asserts for every term. All indices
are explicit, so a term can be regenerated from $(p,u,r)$ without reading any
other term, which is what the independent recomputation relies on.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Potential chain $P(f)$ in the sector with exterior high set $\{6p,8p-2\}$.}
\label{alg:potential}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $p\geq8$; a potential $f$, an integer-valued map on pairs $(u,r)$, $0\leq u\leq p-2$, $0\leq r\leq p-1$, zero for $r\leq u$
\Ensure a multiset of weighted source labels $S[E;c]$
\State $\mathrm{active}\gets\{u:\ f(u,r)\ne0\text{ for some }r\}$; $\ \mathrm{low}\gets[1,p]\cup[3p,4p-2]$; $\ \mathrm{high}\gets\{6p,8p-2\}$
\For{$u\in\mathrm{active}$, $0\leq r<s\leq p-1$ with $u\leq r+s\leq p+u-1$}
  \State $w\gets(-1)^{p+r+s+u}\,(f(u,s)-f(u,r))$
  \State emit $w\cdot S[(\mathrm{low}\setminus\{p-r,p-s,3p+u\})\cup\mathrm{high};\ p+u-r-s]$
\EndFor
\For{$u\in\mathrm{active}$, $v\in[0,p-2]$, $v\ne u$; $\ (u,v)\gets$ sorted pair, $R\gets u+v$}
  \State $\sigma\gets f(u,R+1)-f(v,R+1)$ if $R\leq p-2$, else $0$
  \For{$r=\max(0,R-p+2),\ldots,\min(p-1,R)$}
    \State $w\gets(-1)^{p+r+u+v}\,(f(u,r)-f(v,r)-\sigma)$
    \State emit $w\cdot S[(\mathrm{low}\setminus\{p-r,3p+u,3p+v\})\cup\mathrm{high};\ 3p+R-r]$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State combine equal labels and discard zero weights
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The first loop tests at most $\binom p2$ pairs for each active index and the
second loop at most $p-1$ partner indices with at most $p$ values of $r$ each, so
one call costs $O(|\mathrm{active}|\,p^2)$ elementary steps, with $|\mathrm{active}|\leq3$
for the potentials of Section~\ref{sec:annihilator}. The source
$V_p$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has $32p-146$ terms at every one of the 18
checked parameters, from $110$ at $p=8$ to $3054$ at $p=100$ [MV]. This linear
law is an observation of the stored sources, not a proved formula, and the
theorem does not use it.

\subsection{Bitset differential}

The independent recomputation evaluates the full original differential with
exterior sets encoded as integers, so that deleting an element is one exclusive-or.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:bitset} accumulates all faces of all terms before it decodes
any nonzero label. It does not use the potential formulas, the face
classification or the boundary formulas of the proofs, and it is the second of the
two encodings in every identity check of Section~\ref{sec:validation}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Bitset differential of a source support, in exact integer arithmetic.}
\label{alg:bitset}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $p$; a support: terms $(w,E,c)$ with $E$ increasing, $|E|=2p-2$, $\sum E+c=\tau$
\Ensure the complete boundary as a map from target rows to nonzero integers
\State $\mathrm{acc}\gets\emptyset$
\For{each term $(w,E,c)$}
  \State $\mathrm{bits}\gets\sum_{e\in E}2^{e}$; $\ \sigma\gets-1$
  \For{$x\in E$ in decreasing order}
    \State $t\gets x+c$
    \If{$x$ is a low offset}
      \State $\kappa\gets\mathsf A$ if $x,c\in L_0$ and $t>p$; $\ \mathsf B$ if $x,c$ lie in different low blocks and $t\geq4p-1$; else none
      \State row $\gets(D,\ \mathrm{bits}\oplus2^{x},\ \kappa,\ t)$ if $\kappa$ is defined, else none
    \Else
      \State row $\gets(K,\ \mathrm{bits}\oplus2^{x},\ t)$ if $t\in\mathcal B$, else none
    \EndIf
    \State if row is defined, $\mathrm{acc}[\text{row}]\gets\mathrm{acc}[\text{row}]+\sigma w$, deleting the entry when it becomes zero
    \State $\sigma\gets-\sigma$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State decode each key's bit pattern into its increasing exterior set; \Return acc
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The sign $\sigma$ starts at $-1$ because the last of the $2p-2$ positions has odd
zero-based index $2p-3$; the same rule gives the negative sign of the $10p$ face in
Theorem~\ref{thm:mask58-endpoints}. Equal labels are merged before any label is decoded,
so the accumulated boundary is the boundary of the sum of the terms and cancellations
between terms are exact. A term has $2p-2$ faces, each costing a constant number of operations on integers of at
most $24p$ bits, so a support of $T$ terms costs $O(Tp)$ face evaluations. For $V_p$
this is $110\cdot14=1540$ faces at $p=8$ and $3054\cdot198=604{,}692$ at $p=100$.
The $525$ small cases of the potential basis are additionally cross-checked
against a literal left-to-right differential.

\subsection{Row-span certificate over \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb F_2$}{F2}}

A relative functional is certified without any potential formula by enumerating
every source of a high sector and testing membership in a row space.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:rowspan} takes the sector with last high variable $h$, builds the
complete original $D$ matrix of all its sources, and decides by exact elimination
over $\mathbb F_2$ whether the pairing vector of the functional lies in the span of
the $D$ rows. If it does, the provenance of the elimination lists the original $D$
rows that sum to the pairing vector, and the pairing therefore vanishes on the whole
kernel, which is computed explicitly and checked row by row. If it does not, a
kernel vector with pairing one refutes the functional.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Row-span certificate for one sector $(p,h)$ and one functional $\lambda$.}
\label{alg:rowspan}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $p$; a last high variable $h$; the support of $\lambda$ as a set of $K$ rows
\Ensure a certificate that $\lambda\,d^S_K$ vanishes on $\ker d_D$, or a kernel vector with $\lambda\,d^S_K\neq0$
\State sources $\gets$ all $S[(L\setminus\mathrm{missing})\cup\{6p,h\};\ c]$ over triples $\mathrm{missing}\subset L$ with $c=\sum\mathrm{missing}+4p-2-h\in L$
\For{each source $j$}
  \State $\partial_j\gets$ original boundary of source $j$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:bitset}), reduced mod $2$
  \State record the $D$ rows of $\partial_j$; set pairing bit $j\gets|\partial_j\cap\lambda|\bmod2$
\EndFor
\State for every $D$ row $\rho$ form the row vector $R_\rho\in\mathbb F_2^{|\mathrm{sources}|}$ whose bit $j$ is set when $\rho\in\partial_j$
\State eliminate: for each $R_\rho$, reduce by the pivot table, keeping a provenance mask of the original rows used; install a new pivot when the reduction is nonzero
\State reduce the pairing vector $t$ the same way, with provenance $\pi$
\State compute a basis of the kernel from the free columns; assert that every $R_\rho$ and $t$ vanish on each kernel vector, or locate a kernel vector $z$ with $t\cdot z=1$
\If{the residual of $t$ is zero}
  \State assert $t=\sum_{\rho\in\pi}R_\rho$; \Return (rank, kernel basis, dual rows $\pi$)
\Else
  \State \Return a refutation: the kernel vector $z$
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The enumeration tests $\binom{2p-1}{3}$ triples per sector. Elimination costs
$O(n_D\cdot r)$ exclusive-ors of $n_S$-bit integers, where $n_D$ is the number of
$D$ rows, $n_S$ the number of sources and $r$ the rank. Table~\ref{tab:sectors}
gives the measured sizes of the reachable high sectors for the twelve-row
functional of the tracked class at $p=8,\ldots,12$.

\subsection{Neighbourhood expansion}

The local obstruction of Section~\ref{sec:validation} uses the inverse
incidence: a coefficient-first enumeration of every source column incident to a
row, with no assumption about which columns matter.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:expand} expands a target vector by alternating row and
column rounds, and then solves for the target over $\mathbb Q$ with source
provenance, retaining every additional boundary row with right-hand side zero.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Radius-$R$ neighbourhood of a target and its rational solve.}
\label{alg:expand}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $p$; a target vector $y$ with support $\mathrm{supp}(y)$; $R\geq1$; caps on columns and nonzeros
\Ensure a classification: integral witness, rational inconsistency with an integer dual, or rational section
\State columns $\gets\emptyset$; frontier $\gets\mathrm{supp}(y)$; expanded $\gets\emptyset$
\For{$r=1,\ldots,R$}
  \For{each row $\rho$ in frontier, with kind $\kappa$, exterior $E$ and offset $t$}
    \For{each variable $x\in L\cup H$ with $x\notin E$}
      \State $c\gets t-x$
      \State source kind $\gets K$ if $\kappa=K$ and $c\in H$; $\ S$ if $\kappa=K$, $x\in H$, $c\in L$; $\ S$ if $\kappa=D$, $x,c\in L$ and the low product of $x,c$ equals the type and offset of $\rho$
      \If{a source kind is defined and the source $(E\cup\{x\};c)$ is new}
        \State sign $\gets(-1)^{|\{e\in E:\ e<x\}|}$; boundary $\gets$ Algorithm~\ref{alg:bitset} of the source
        \State assert that the boundary contains $\rho$ with that sign; stop with an inconclusive cap if a cap is exceeded
        \State store the column
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
  \State solve $A z=y$ over $\mathbb Q$ by elimination with unit-first pivots and source provenance
  \If{an integral solution exists} \Return it, after multiplying it by the original map
  \ElsIf{no rational solution exists} record an integer dual $\lambda$ with $\lambda A=0$, $\lambda y\ne0$
  \EndIf
  \State expanded $\gets$ expanded $\cup$ frontier; frontier $\gets$ (rows of stored columns) $\setminus$ expanded
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Each frontier row tests the $10p-1$ variables of $L\cup H$ and pays one
Algorithm~\ref{alg:bitset} boundary per accepted column, that is, $O(p)$. At $p=8$
the radius-one neighbourhood of the four endpoint rows has $47$ columns and $240$ rows and the
radius-two neighbourhood has $330$ columns and $1803$ rows (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
The programs cap a neighbourhood at $1200$ columns and $20{,}000$ nonzero entries, and
a cap is reported as inconclusive, never as a refutation. The constant-time
face classification of Section~\ref{sec:projection} needs no search. It keeps one
count vector $\nu$ of the source and updates it in constant time for each of
the $O(p)$ deletions, so one pair $(p,r)$ costs $O(p)$; the sweep over $p=8,\ldots,300$ is
$293\cdot2=586$ checks, each with five altered cases rejected: three altered
source columns and two altered projection masks.

\subsection{Ablation of the corrections in \texorpdfstring{$V_p$}{V\_p}}

The source $V_p$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has three ingredients beyond the
twice-edge sources of Proposition~\ref{prop:twiceedge}: the shifted potentials
$P(\delta_{03})$ and $P(\delta_{02})$ together with their short $K$-source
corrections $Q_3$ and $Q_2$, and the sign of $P(F_1)$. Two of them are ablated in the stored computation, and in addition a sign and a single
coefficient of the stored source are mutated, with the full boundary compared with $2\eta_p$ at
every one of the 18 checked parameters. The mutated sources are rejected at each of them [MV]. Two variants of
$V_p$ fail uniformly. Omitting the $\delta_{02}/Q_2$
correction gives $V_{\mathrm{omit}}=V_p+4D$ and
\[
 M_pV_{\mathrm{omit}}-2\eta_p=4(x_{01}+e(2;0,1))\ne0.
\]
Reversing, in addition, the sign of $F_1$ gives
$V_{\mathrm{early}}=V_{\mathrm{omit}}-4P(F_1)$ and
\[
 M_pV_{\mathrm{early}}-2\eta_p=4(e(2;0,1)-x_{01})\ne0.
\]
The second residual is the first minus $8x_{01}$, because
$M_pP(F_1)=2x_{01}$. The rows $x_{01}$ and $e(2;0,1)$ are distinct, so both failures hold uniformly.
The variant $V_{\mathrm{early}}$ is the literal combination
$P(F_2-2F_1-4\delta_{03})+4Q_3$ stored in the computation, and the full boundary rejects it. The term
$4D$ contributes exactly the boundary $4(x_{01}+e(2;0,1))$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:corrections}, so omitting it
leaves that residual, and the sign of $F_1$ changes the coefficient of $x_{01}$ by $8$ because
$M_pP(F_1)=2x_{01}$. The $\delta_{02}/Q_2$ correction and the sign of $F_1$ are therefore each needed.

\subsection{Finite carrier comparison at \texorpdfstring{$p=8,\ldots,11$}{p = 8 to 11}}\label{sec:carrier}

The presentation $M_p$ was compared with the earlier persistent finite carrier
matrices, which are the isolated components obtained by exact unit contraction
of the original map and then projected by one of the masks of
Section~\ref{sec:projection}. A mask is the set of retained row types read as
a binary number with the bit $n$ standing for $R_n$, so that mask $58$ retains
$R_1,R_3,R_4,R_5$ and masks $56$, $59$, $62$ retain $R_3,R_4,R_5$, then
$R_0,R_1,R_3,R_4,R_5$ and $R_1,\ldots,R_5$. The comparison has two parts, and
Table~\ref{tab:carrier} reports both for every parameter and mask.

The first part is a labelled comparison [MV]. All $19$ triangle rows of
Section~\ref{sec:family}, three, four, five and seven at $p=8,\ldots,11$,
survive the unit contraction, occur exactly once in the persistent component, are
rows of type $R_5$, and are carried to the literal same target coordinate. In the
full carrier masks $59$ and $62$ the quotient rank of the triangle span modulo two is
the whole count $3,4,5,7$ and no triangle relation occurs. In mask $58$ the span has
rank $1,2,3,5$, and its complete relation space is spanned, throughout the tested
range, by the two endpoint triangles $(0,1,p-3)$ and $(0,2,p-4)$. In mask $56$ the ranks
are $0,0,0,1$, and $(2,3,4)$ is the only surviving direction, at $p=11$. These ranks
reproduce the independent Bockstein ranks of the carriers, and a separate recomputation
rebuilds the four labelled components, checks all $19$ stored boundaries $\pm2x_T$,
exhausts every triangle combination in all $16$ mask cases and rejects $12$ mutated cases.

The second part determines the exact $2$-primary structure [MV]. Exact certificates
modulo products of word-size primes, with the squared column norms bounded by the Hadamard
inequality, fix the rational rank of each of the $16$ carrier matrices. In every case the gap between
the rational rank and the rank modulo two equals the first integral Bockstein rank, so every
even Smith factor has $2$-adic valuation exactly one and the complete $2$-primary
type is elementary. The types are $(\Z/2)^{3,4,5,7}$ for masks $59$ and $62$,
$(\Z/2)^{1,2,3,5}$ for mask $58$, and $0,0,0,(\Z/2)^1$ for mask $56$. An independent
recomputation repeats $1{,}100$ prime-rank pairs, tests $16$ minimally sufficient prime
prefixes, $16$ entry mutations and $2$ synthetic exponent cases, and checks the triangle consequences.

Combined with the twice-sources of Proposition~\ref{prop:all-triangle-sources}, which are
exact integral identities, the elementary exponent gives an exact integral finite result: the
endpoint triangles $(0,1,p-3)$ and $(0,2,p-4)$ vanish integrally in mask $58$, all remaining
triangle images form the complete $2$-primary subgroup, and all triangles generate it in
masks $59$ and $62$. The limits are as follows. The comparison is finite, for $p=8,\ldots,11$.
The integral vanishings follow from finite exponent certificates and not from explicit
all-parameter sources, which Theorem~\ref{thm:mask58-endpoints} supplies afterwards for
the two endpoint rows only. A mod-two-zero class of order two can still be twice a class of
order four, and only the exponent certificates exclude that. No complementary upper bound and no
uniform isomorphism between a carrier and $\mathcal C_p$ follows.

\section{Discussion, limitations and remaining obligations}\label{sec:discussion}

The results fit together in one chain. The explicit matrix $M_p$ has a quadratically
growing direct summand of order-two classes, with explicit sources for twice each class and
explicit detectors over $\mathbb F_2$ for each class independently
(Theorems~\ref{thm:main}, \ref{thm:exact-order} and~\ref{thm:quadratic}). The tracked
four-row vector of the earlier finite computations is one of these classes up to the
congruence of Proposition~\ref{prop:eta-edge}. A semantic projection of four of the six row
types kills two endpoint triangle rows for every $p$ by a face count that needs no search
(Theorem~\ref{thm:mask58-endpoints}), and the finite carrier comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:carrier} agrees with this and with the count of independent classes at
$p=8,\ldots,11$. Each exact identity is also tested by two independent encodings of the
differential (Section~\ref{sec:algorithms}).

The uniform twice-source, nonvanishing and independent-family obligations
are closed: Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic} and Corollary~\ref{cor:splitting}
give the quadratically growing direct summand for every $p\geq8$.
The tracked endpoint class is explicitly identified with one selected
triangle class. What remains [C] is the complementary group: the complete
cokernel, a matching upper bound, its free rank and any additional torsion
are not determined. The functionals are not asserted to exhaust the full
dual space. At $p=8,9,10$ the proved summand has $3,4,5$ factors against an
exact characteristic-two excess of $31,49,72$ (Table~\ref{tab:excess}), so most of the
characteristic-two behavior sits in the complement $\mathcal C'_p$, whose
structure, including any three-torsion that would change the excess, is open.
In particular, the theorem does not identify the full
presentation with an earlier isolated component or relative-completion
quotient, and it does not prove a lower-strand recurrence.
Theorem~\ref{thm:mask58-endpoints} gives explicit sources for two endpoint
rows after one semantic projection. It does not prove independence or an upper
bound for the other projected triangle rows, nor that this projection agrees
uniformly with the earlier persistent isolated component.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Three limits bound the scope. First, the application to the fiber-cone family
depends on the premise of Section~\ref{sec:premise}: the matrix $M_p$ is
the Koszul-strand block matrix for $p=8,9,10$ by exact computation, and for larger $p$
that equality is not derived. The proofs concerning $M_p$ do not use it, and they
are self-contained within the explicit presentation of Section~\ref{sec:matrix}.
Independent encodings test for implementation,
sign and boundary errors but do not rederive that identification. Second, the finite
certificates and the carrier comparison are exact but finite, and no all-parameter
statement is extrapolated from them. The new potential basis, signed
interval triangle and relative parity proof provide explicit sources and
detectors for further reductions. The parity argument exhausts the sectors
visible to each functional; it does not assert that those sectors exhaust
the whole map. Third, the splitting is a proved existence result, but computing
a uniform global D-row retraction remains a separate constructive task. The
computations are exact integer and finite-field computations, so they carry no
statistical uncertainty; the only variability is the elapsed time of the carrier
comparison, which is a single stored run.

\paragraph{Next obligations.} The route that would settle the matching is an explicit
parametric matching or a finite-state rewrite system for the labels of
Section~\ref{sec:matrix}, as described in Section~\ref{sec:related}; it would construct
both the sources and a free complement. Until then, an integral map from the triangle
family to the isolated carriers for every $p$, a bound on $\mathcal C'_p$ and an
explanation of the excess of Table~\ref{tab:excess} are open.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

For every $p\geq8$ the cokernel of the explicit integral matrix $M_p$ contains a direct summand
$(\Z/2)^{\lfloor((p-2)^2+3)/12\rfloor}$ generated by the triangle classes $x_T$, with explicit integral
sources for twice each class and $\mathbb F_2$ functionals that detect each class independently. The
tracked four-row vector is one of these classes, and a four-type semantic projection kills two endpoint triangle rows
uniformly. Four verification procedures with their cost, commands, run times and full digests are given in
Section~\ref{sec:algorithms}, and the finite carrier comparison at $p=8,\ldots,11$ is in
Section~\ref{sec:carrier}. The identification of $M_p$ with the Koszul-strand matrix is a premise,
verified for $p=8,9,10$ and open beyond, and the complete cokernel is not determined.
The results are lower-bound and partial-decomposition statements. The complement of the direct summand,
an upper bound for the whole cokernel, the odd torsion and the free rank, an explicit retraction, and an
all-parameter map to the isolated carriers are the open obligations, and a matching theorem for the
labels of $M_p$ is the route that would settle them.
