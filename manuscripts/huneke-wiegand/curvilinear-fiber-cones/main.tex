\begin{abstract}
For every integer $p\geq4$, a previously constructed symmetric numerical semigroup ring $R_p$
and rigid two-generated monomial ideal determine a conductor ideal $T_p$ whose special fiber
$\Cfiber_p=\Fcone(T_p)$ is one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay, generated in degree one by $10p$
monomials, with multiplicity $24p$. Taking the imported offset bases as input, we prove that
$\Cfiber_p\cong\kfield[xy^a:a\in G_p]\subseteq\kfield[x,y]/(y^{24p})$, that its defining ideal
$J_p$ is primary to the ideal of positive offsets, with nilpotency index $24p$, and that
$\operatorname{Proj}(\Cfiber_p)$ is a saturated length-$24p$ curvilinear fat point, locally
Gorenstein although the coordinate ring has Cohen--Macaulay type $10p+1$. The
K\"ahler differentials split by characteristic. We then determine the reduced Gr\"obner basis for
graded reverse lexicographic order with $X_0$ last. Its degree profile is $(50p^2-17p,5p-1,p-2)$
and no minimal leading monomial contains $X_0$, which gives a flat Cohen--Macaulay monomial
degeneration. The quadratic part, the stabilization of the staircase, the absence of elements in
degree five and higher, the absence of $X_0$ from the leading monomials and the flatness are
derived from derived imported input; the imported relation type three is machine-verified. The cubic and quartic classification, for all $p$, is
machine-verified and not derived: 16 Presburger obligations are decided unsatisfiable by Z3 and
by cvc5 on identical queries, with no checked proof object. Exact sweeps at every
$p=4,\ldots,300$, an independent clique recomputation and negative controls validate the
formulas.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Huneke--Wiegand conjecture asks whether, over a one-dimensional Gorenstein local domain $R$,
a nonzero finitely generated nonfree module $M$ always has $M\otimes_R\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$
with nontrivial torsion \cite{HunekeWiegand1994}. For numerical semigroup rings the question
becomes a problem of finite additive combinatorics, and in 2026 it received negative answers
\cite{Pham2026,PhamArxiv2026,CGI2026}. One of them is an infinite family of symmetric numerical
semigroups with a rigid two-generated monomial ideal, constructed after Pham's first public
example \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}. The family is indexed by an integer $p\geq4$, and its
multiplicity is $24p$.

Once a counterexample family exists, the useful questions concern its structure. What is the
conductor of the endomorphism ring of the rigid ideal, and what algebra does its powers generate?
The special fiber $\Cfiber_p$ of that conductor is a standard graded algebra with $10p$
generators in degree one, one-dimensional and Cohen--Macaulay, and the defining ideal of its
presentation needs $50p^2-17p+1$ generators. Such an algebra invites three kinds of questions.
Algebraic: what is its primary decomposition and its nilradical? Geometric: what is the
projective scheme it defines, and how does that scheme sit between local and arithmetic
Gorenstein properties? Computational: is there a term order whose initial ideal is as simple as
possible, and does it give a flat degeneration to a monomial Cohen--Macaulay algebra?

This paper answers these questions for every $p\geq4$. The presence of offsets $0$ and $1$ in
the degree-one generators, combined with a hard truncation at $q=24p$, makes the whole
projective scheme one-variable after dehomogenization. The consequences are a truncated monomial
model, a one-component primary decomposition with sharp nilpotency index $24p$, a curvilinear
fat point that is locally Gorenstein while its homogeneous coordinate ring is neither level nor
Gorenstein, and a differential module whose dimension depends on the characteristic. A second
group of results concerns the reduced Gr\"obner basis for the graded reverse lexicographic order
with $X_0$ last: its degree profile is $(50p^2-17p,5p-1,p-2)$ in degrees two, three and four,
and no later element exists.

The evidence behind these statements has two classes, and the paper keeps them apart. Each
result carries a mark: \mD{} for a statement derived by a written argument and \mMV{} for a
statement established by exact machine computation. The imported input of
Section~\ref{sec:problem} is derived, except the relation type three of its item (I5), which is
machine-verified in the paper that constructs the family. Everything in Sections~\ref{sec:theory-a}
and~\ref{sec:theory-b} is derived from the derived items (I1) to (I4), with exactly two exceptions,
both machine-verified: the classification of the cubic and quartic boundary of the Gr\"obner basis
and its tails (Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}(b), (c)), which rests on 16 Presburger obligations decided
by Z3 and by cvc5 on identical queries but with no checked proof object, and the two clauses of
Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneration} that need it or (I5), namely that the basis reaches degree four
and that the relation type is three. Table~\ref{tab:marks} states the class of every result with
its premises, and the abstract, the conclusions and that table describe the evidence in the same
terms.

The contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A truncated monomial model of the fiber cone, its one-component primary decomposition and
sharp nilpotency index, and its curvilinear geometry with the separation between local and
arithmetic Gorenstein properties (Section~\ref{sec:theory-a}), all derived.
\item The reduced Gr\"obner basis for the grevlex order with $X_0$ last, with the derived
quadratic part, the derived stabilization and the machine-verified cubic and quartic classification
(Section~\ref{sec:theory-b}).
\item The decision of the 16 obligations behind that classification by a second solver on
queries identical to the first, with four perturbed controls (Sections~\ref{sec:algorithms}
and~\ref{sec:results}).
\item The algorithms, costs, hashes and controls of every computation, and the placement of the
results beside the known Betti entries of the fiber cone (Sections~\ref{sec:algorithms}
to~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{enumerate}

The remaining sections are organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} places the work among
the literature on the conjecture, fiber cones and curvilinear schemes. Section~\ref{sec:problem}
fixes the objects and states the imported input with exact pointers. Sections~\ref{sec:theory-a}
and~\ref{sec:theory-b} contain the theory. Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} describes every
computation as an algorithm with its cost, and Section~\ref{sec:design} the experimental design.
Sections~\ref{sec:results} and~\ref{sec:ablation} give the results and the controls, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} reads them against the known Betti entries of $\Cfiber_p$. The
limits, the means of reproduction and the conclusions follow, and three appendices print the
solver obligations as formulas, the perturbed obligations and the zero-relation count.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The conjecture and its counterexamples.}
Huneke and Wiegand predicted nontrivial torsion in $M\otimes_R\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$ for
nonfree modules over one-dimensional Gorenstein local domains \cite{HunekeWiegand1994}.
Garc\'ia-S\'anchez and Leamer proved positive results for complete intersection numerical
semigroup rings and reported a computer check of every symmetric numerical semigroup with
Frobenius number below $69$ \cite{GarciaSanchezLeamer2013}. Landeros, O'Neill, Pelayo, Pe\~na,
Ren and Wissman proved the case of two-generated monomial ideals over rings of generalized
arithmetic sequence semigroups \cite{Landeros2024}. Pham then published a symmetric numerical
semigroup and a two-generated monomial ideal for which the predicted torsion is absent
\cite{Pham2026}, and a preprint that reduces the verification to a finite sumset identity,
checked with the Huneke--Iyengar--Wiegand colon criterion and Leamer's tensor-torsion formula,
and that computes the trace ideal, the endomorphism ring and its conductor and shows that the
example persists under completion \cite{PhamArxiv2026}. Christensen, Gerko and Iyengar gave an
independent construction from a field extension, a rigid nonprincipal ideal in a
one-dimensional Gorenstein domain, together with a proof of the conjecture for equal
characteristic and embedding dimension at most three \cite{CGI2026}. The family of
\cite{SantibanezLeal2026} extends the numerical-semigroup example to every $p\geq4$ and shows,
by a result that is both machine-verified and derived \cite[Theorem~6.1]{SantibanezLeal2026},
that the minimal Frobenius number is attained by Pham's semigroup; Pham's discovery priority is
preserved. The present paper does not bear on the truth of the conjecture. It studies the
algebra attached to the family after the counterexample is fixed.

\paragraph{Algebraic structure of the family.}
The same family has a body of structural results. Rigidity descends to the endomorphism ring
by results of Dey and Lyle \cite{DeyLyle2025}. The colengths of the trace and the conductor are
computed in the pattern of Herzog and Kumashiro \cite{HerzogKumashiro2022}. The conductor is not
stable, in agreement with Dey's criterion \cite{Dey2022}, and the equality of two traces is
consistent with the reflexive-trace criterion of Lindo, Maitra and Zhang \cite{LindoMaitraZhang2026}.
The reduced type is $10p$ in the sense of Maitra and Mukundan \cite{MaitraMukundan2023}. Blanco and
Rosales supply the tree of irreducible numerical semigroups used to show minimality of the
Frobenius number \cite{BlancoRosales2013}. Tangent-cone depth and Buchsbaumness of the conductor
follow the Valabrega--Valla criterion \cite{ValabregaValla1978} and the local-cohomology
criterion of D'Anna, Mezzasalma, Micale and Sammartano
\cite{DAnnaMezzasalmaMicale2009,DAnnaMicaleSammartano2011}. Every one of these statements is
about the family and is proved in \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}; this paper uses only the fiber
cone results listed in Proposition~\ref{prop:input}.

\paragraph{Fiber cones of monomial ideals.}
Herzog, Qureshi and Mohammadi Saem determine the depth of the fiber cone and its relation
ideal for classes of monomial ideals in two variables, among them concave, convex and symmetric
ideals \cite{HerzogQureshiSaem2017}. Herzog and Zhu show that the fiber cone of a three-generated
height-two monomial ideal in $\kfield[x,y]$ is a hypersurface ring, that certain four-generated
classes have positive depth, and that for those classes the fiber cone is Cohen--Macaulay
exactly when its defining ideal has at most three generators \cite{HerzogZhu2019}. The fiber
cone here differs in three respects. It belongs to an ideal of a numerical semigroup ring, not
to a monomial ideal of $\kfield[x,y]$; it has $10p$ generators, far beyond the three or four of
those classes; and its defining ideal has $50p^2-17p+1$ minimal generators, so it lies far from
the hypersurface and few-generator regimes. The general framework for fiber cones of ideals of
analytic spread one is due to Cortadellas and Zarzuela \cite{CortadellasZarzuela2007}, and for
tangent cones of numerical semigroup rings to the same authors \cite{CortadellasZarzuela2009}.
Abdolmaleki and Kumashiro give the construction of defining ideals of Cohen--Macaulay fiber
cones, whose Theorem~2.8 bounds the degrees of the equations through the reduction number
\cite{AbdolmalekiKumashiro2024}; the conductor has a principal reduction, in the sense of
Northcott and Rees \cite{NorthcottRees1954}, of reduction number four \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}.

\paragraph{Curvilinear schemes and differentials.}
Kreuzer, Linh and Long characterize weakly curvilinear zero-dimensional schemes in projective
space over a perfect field by the Hilbert polynomials of their K\"ahler differential modules,
without a primary decomposition, and compute those modules for fat point schemes
\cite{KreuzerLinhLong2023}. Our scheme is a length-$24p$ curvilinear fat point and the
differential module of its affine chart is computed here directly in every characteristic,
including the characteristic split that depends on whether the characteristic divides $24p$.

\paragraph{Gr\"obner bases and semigroup algebras.}
Gr\"obner methods for projective monomial curves and for associated graded rings of semigroup
algebras are developed by Bhardwaj, Chau and Javadekar and by Saha, Sengupta and Srivastava
\cite{BhardwajChauJavadekar2025,SahaSenguptaSrivastava2022}. The last-variable order and the use
of standard-monomial counts here are in the same spirit; the staircase and the formulas are
specific to the truncated conductor family. The graded Betti numbers of $\Cfiber_p$ discussed
in Section~\ref{sec:discussion} were computed with relative squarefree-divisor complexes, a
standard device for affine semigroup quotients by monomial ideals
\cite{BrunsHerzog1997}, and the semigroup-ring Betti framework of Stamate
\cite{Stamate2018}, whose warning about characteristic dependence is borne out there.

\section{Problem formulation and imported input}\label{sec:problem}

Fix $p\geq4$ and put $s=6p$ and $q=4s=24p$. Define residue sets in $[0,s-1]$ by
\begin{align*}
A_p&=[0,p]\cup[3p,4p-2],\\
B_p&=\bigl([p+1,3p-1]\setminus\{2p-1\}\bigr)
      \cup\{4p\}\cup[5p-1,6p-1],\\
D_p&=[0,2p]\cup[3p,5p-2].
\end{align*}
The numerical semigroup $\Gamma_p$ contains
\[
\{0\},\quad 4s+A_p,\quad [5s,6s-1],\quad 6s+B_p,\quad 8s+D_p,
\quad [9s,13s-2],\quad [13s,\infty),
\]
and no other nonnegative integers. The family theorem \cite[Theorem~9.2]{SantibanezLeal2026}
proves that $\Gamma_p$ is symmetric, with multiplicity $24p$ and Frobenius number $78p-1$, and that
$I_p=(t^{24p},t^{30p})$ is a nonprincipal rigid ideal in the localization $R_p$ of
$\kfield[t^{\Gamma_p}]$ at its positive-degree maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_p$.

Let $E_p=\operatorname{End}_{R_p}(I_p)$ and let $T_p=R_p:E_p$ be the conductor. The object
studied is its special fiber
\[
\Cfiber_p=\Fcone(T_p)=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}T_p^n/\mathfrak m_pT_p^n.
\]
\textbf{Problem.} For every $p\geq4$, describe $\Cfiber_p$ as a graded algebra over a field
$\kfield$ of arbitrary characteristic; determine the primary decomposition of its defining
ideal, the projective scheme it defines and the K\"ahler differentials of that scheme; find the
reduced Gr\"obner basis for the graded reverse lexicographic order with $X_0$ last and decide
whether it gives a flat Cohen--Macaulay monomial degeneration; and state for every result
whether it is derived or machine-verified.

The imported input is the following. Each item names the theorem of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}
(the consolidated paper, section numbering of its current text) and the archived computation
that supports it, and each carries the mark under which it is imported.

\begin{proposition}[Imported offset-basis input]\label{prop:input}
Put $q=24p$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(I1)}] \mD{} \emph{Presentation and offset bases.} There is a presentation
\[
 P_p=\kfield[X_a:a\in G_p]\longrightarrow\Cfiber_p,
 \qquad X_a\longmapsto[t^{q+a}],
\]
where $|G_p|=10p$ and
\begin{align}
G_p={}&\{0\}\cup[1,p]\cup[3p,4p-2]\cup[6p,8p-2]\cup[8p,10p-2]
\cup\{10p\}\notag\\
&\cup[11p-1,12p-1]\cup[13p+1,14p-2]\cup[14p,15p-1]
\cup\{16p\}\cup[17p-1,18p-1].\label{eq:generators}
\end{align}
The offset basis of $(\Cfiber_p)_n$ is the set $E_n$ with
\begin{align}
E_1&=G_p,\\
E_2&=[0,2p]\cup[3p,5p-2]\cup[6p,q-1],\\
E_3&=[0,q-1]\setminus\{6p-1\},\\
E_n&=[0,q-1]\qquad(n\geq4).\label{eq:bases}
\end{align}
Source: Theorem~15.4 and its proof, the displayed offset equations of the subsection ``The
defining ideal of the special fiber'' of Section~15, and the value blocks of Theorems~12.1 and~13.1
of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}; computation EXP-021.
\item[\textup{(I2)}] \mD{} \emph{Value congruence.} A degree-$n$ monomial of $P_p$ is zero in
$\Cfiber_p$ exactly when its total offset is absent from $E_n$, and two nonzero monomials of
the same degree have the same class exactly when their total offsets agree. Source: the first
paragraph of the proof of Theorem~15.5 of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}, which uses only the linear
independence of the semigroup monomials and the bases of (I1); computation EXP-022
(proof, section~1).
\item[\textup{(I3)}] \mD{} \emph{Sumset identity.} For $n\geq1$, $E_n$ equals the $n$-fold
sumset of $G_p$ truncated below $q$. Source: derived from (I1) in
Remark~\ref{rem:sumset}; checked degree by degree up to six at every $p=4,\ldots,300$ by
computations EXP-025 and EXP-026.
\item[\textup{(I4)}] \mD{} \emph{Structure.} $\Cfiber_p$ is a one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay
standard graded algebra, free of rank $q$ over $\kfield[X_0]$, of Cohen--Macaulay type $10p+1$;
its Artinian reduction by $X_0$ has socle in two degrees, so $\Cfiber_p$ is neither level nor
Gorenstein. Source: Theorem~15.4 and, for the socle count $10p+1$ in the last Betti row,
Theorem~15.6 of \cite{SantibanezLeal2026}; computations EXP-021 and EXP-024.
\item[\textup{(I5)}] \mMV{} \emph{Relation type.} The defining ideal has relation type three:
it is generated by its quadrics and the single cubic $X_0^2X_{3p}-X_p^3$. Source: Theorem~15.5 of
\cite{SantibanezLeal2026}; computation EXP-023.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The marks need explanation. Items (I1) and (I4) are derived in the cited paper from exact value
blocks and the structure of the Noether normalization. Item (I2) is derived in the first
paragraph of the proof of Theorem~15.5 there, from the linear independence of the semigroup
monomials and the bases of (I1), and in the proof of computation EXP-022. The solver boundary of Theorem~15.5 covers only the
connectivity computation in degrees three to five, which is the content of (I5).
Item (I5) is the conclusion of that computation: the exact solver closed $133$ terminal formulas
as unsatisfiable after four affine-cell bisections, and no checked proof object was produced, so
(I5) is the only imported item marked \mMV{}. Item (I3) is derived in the next remark.

\begin{remark}[The sumset identity]\label{rem:sumset}
Write $\Sigma_n$ for the $n$-fold sumset of $G_p$ truncated below $q$. Every class of $(\Cfiber_p)_n$
is a product of $n$ classes of degree one, so its offset is a sum of $n$ elements of $G_p$ and
$E_n\subseteq\Sigma_n$. The reverse inclusions follow from the shape of the bases in
Equation~\eqref{eq:bases}.

\emph{Case $n=2$.} Split $G_p$ into its low part $[0,p]\cup[3p,4p-2]$ and its high part, contained
in $[6p,18p-1]$. Two low elements of $[0,p]$ sum to at most $2p$. An element of $[0,p]$ and one of
$[3p,4p-2]$ sum into $[3p,5p-2]$. An element of $[0,p]$ and a high element sum to at least $6p$,
and so do two elements of $[3p,4p-2]$ and every pair with a high element. Hence
$\Sigma_2\subseteq[0,2p]\cup[3p,5p-2]\cup[6p,q-1]=E_2$, and $\Sigma_2=E_2$.

\emph{Case $n=3$.} The set $E_2+G_p$ contains $E_2$ because $0\in G_p$, and every sum with an
element of $[6p,q-1]$ is at least $6p$. The values of $[0,6p-1]\setminus E_2$ are
$[2p+1,3p-1]\cup[5p-1,6p-1]$. The first interval lies in $[0,2p]+[1,p]$, and
$[5p-1,6p-2]$ lies in $[3p,5p-2]+[1,p]$. The value $6p-1$ is not attained: writing it as $x+g$
with $x\in[0,2p]$ needs $g\in[4p-1,6p-1]$, and with $x\in[3p,5p-2]$ needs $g\in[p+1,3p-1]$, and
$G_p$ meets neither interval. Hence $\Sigma_3=[0,q-1]\setminus\{6p-1\}=E_3$.

\emph{Cases $n\geq4$.} Every value except $6p-1$ lies in $\Sigma_3$, and $6p-1=(6p-2)+1$ with
$6p-2\in \Sigma_3$ and $1\in G_p$, so $\Sigma_4=[0,q-1]=E_4$. Because $0\in G_p$, the later sumsets contain
$\Sigma_4$ and are contained in $[0,q-1]$, so $\Sigma_n=E_n$ for all $n$.
\end{remark}

The computations EXP-025 and EXP-026 check $\Sigma_n=E_n$ for $n\leq5$ and $n\leq6$ at every
$p=4,\ldots,300$; this agrees with the derivation and is not used in place of it.

\begin{remark}[What is not claimed]
The result concerns the conductor fiber cones of the explicit family recalled in this section.
It does not assert that arbitrary monomial fiber cones are primary, curvilinear,
Cohen--Macaulay or locally Gorenstein, and it does not strengthen the underlying
Huneke--Wiegand counterexample theorem for arbitrary modules or arbitrary one-dimensional
Gorenstein domains.
\end{remark}

\section{Theory I: truncated model, primary structure and geometry}\label{sec:theory-a}

All results of this section are \mD{}: their proofs are written arguments over the derived imported
items (I1) to (I4) of Proposition~\ref{prop:input}, and none uses a machine-verified statement.

\subsection{The truncated monomial model}

Assign standard degree one to each monomial $xy^a$ and define
\[
 \mathcal A_p=\kfield[xy^a:a\in G_p]\subseteq\kfield[x,y]/(y^q).
\]
Figure~\ref{fig:strips} shows the bases $E_1,\ldots,E_4$ at $p=4$, where $q=96$. The set $E_1=G_4$
has $40$ elements in eleven blocks, $E_2$ has $88$, $E_3$ has $95$ and $E_4$ is the full window of $96$
offsets. The single hole of $E_3$ at $6p-1=23$ is the only place where the strips of degrees
three and four differ, and it is the reason that degree four, not three, is the first degree in
which the truncated algebra is full.

\begin{theorem}[Truncated parametrization, \mD]\label{thm:parametrization}
For every $p\geq4$, the homomorphism
\[
 P_p\longrightarrow\mathcal A_p,
 \qquad X_a\longmapsto xy^a,
\]
induces a standard-graded isomorphism $\Cfiber_p\cong\mathcal A_p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A degree-$n$ monomial with total offset $b$ maps to $x^ny^b$. This is zero exactly when
$b\geq q$, and distinct surviving offsets give linearly independent monomials of the truncated
polynomial ring. By (I2), the corresponding class in $(\Cfiber_p)_n$ is zero precisely when the
offset is absent from $E_n$, and otherwise depends only on the offset. The offsets that survive
in $\mathcal A_p$ are exactly the $n$-fold sums of elements of $G_p$ below $q$, which by (I3) is
$E_n$; the offsets that survive in $\Cfiber_p$ are $E_n$ by definition. Hence the two kernels
agree in every graded degree.
\end{proof}

The proof uses two facts of different kind. Linear independence of the monomials $x^ny^b$ in the
truncated ring is elementary. The identification of the surviving offsets of $\Cfiber_p$ with
the truncated sumsets uses (I3), which is the premise added to the input because the proof
cannot do without it: the ring $\mathcal A_p$ knows nothing about $T_p$, and only (I3) says that
the powers of the conductor realize every truncated sum.

\begin{corollary}[Exact dehomogenization, \mD]\label{cor:dehom}
There is an isomorphism
\[
 \Cfiber_p/(X_0-1)\cong\kfield[y]/(y^q),
 \qquad X_a\longmapsto y^a.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The offsets $0$ and $1$ belong to $G_p$. For every positive $a\in G_p$, the two degree-$a$
monomials $X_0^{a-1}X_a$ and $X_1^a$ have the same surviving offset $a<q$, so
$X_0^{a-1}X_a=X_1^a$. After setting $X_0=1$, every generator is a power of $X_1$, and
$X_1^q=0$. This gives a surjection from $\kfield[y]/(y^q)$. Freeness of rank $q$ over
$\kfield[X_0]$ in (I4) shows that the target also has vector-space dimension $q$, hence the
surjection is an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

At $p=4$ the corollary says, for instance, that $X_0^{11}X_{12}=X_1^{12}$ in $\Cfiber_4$ and that
the $40$ generators become the monomials $y^a$ with $a$ in the eleven blocks of $G_4$, all
inside the one-variable algebra $\kfield[y]/(y^{96})$. The surviving offset $12=3p$ lies in
$E_{12}=[0,95]$, so the relation is not vacuous.

\subsection{Primary decomposition and sharp nilpotence}

Let
\[
 L_p=(X_a:a>0)\subseteq P_p,
 \qquad N_p=L_p/J_p\subseteq\Cfiber_p.
\]

\begin{theorem}[One primary component, \mD]\label{thm:primary}
For every $p\geq4$,
\[
 \sqrt{J_p}=L_p,\qquad J_p\text{ is }L_p\text{-primary}.
\]
Thus $J_p$ itself is the complete primary decomposition. The nilradical $N_p$ has exact
nilpotency index $q=24p$:
\[
 N_p^{q-1}\neq0,\qquad N_p^q=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The quotient $\Cfiber_p/N_p$ is $\kfield[X_0]$, so it is reduced. Every product of $q$ generators
with positive offset has total offset at least $q$ and vanishes by
Theorem~\ref{thm:parametrization}. Hence $N_p$ is nilpotent and equals the nilradical. It is the
unique minimal prime. The element $X_1^{q-1}$ maps to $x^{q-1}y^{q-1}$ and is nonzero, proving
the sharp exponent.

The unique-minimal-prime statement alone would not prove primaryness. Here the
Cohen--Macaulay hypothesis in (I4) is essential: a one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay ring has no
embedded associated primes. Therefore $\operatorname{Ass}(\Cfiber_p)=\{N_p\}$, so the zero ideal
is $N_p$-primary. Taking its inverse image in $P_p$ proves the assertion about $J_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why Cohen--Macaulayness is load-bearing]
The ring $\kfield[u,v]/(u^2,uv)$ has a unique minimal prime but also an embedded associated
prime; its zero ideal is not primary. This example shows that the associated-prime argument
cannot be replaced by a radical-only inference, and the computations of
Section~\ref{sec:ablation} include it as a negative control.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Curvilinear geometry and the Gorenstein separation}

\begin{theorem}[The projective fat point, \mD]\label{thm:geometry}
The ideal $J_p$ is saturated for the irrelevant ideal. The projective scheme
$\operatorname{Proj}(\Cfiber_p)$ is supported at $[1:0:\cdots:0]$ and is a length-$q$
curvilinear fat point. Its Zariski tangent space has dimension one. The scheme is locally
Gorenstein, while the homogeneous coordinate ring $\Cfiber_p$ is not level and not Gorenstein.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\Cfiber_p$ is one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay, its zeroth local cohomology for the
irrelevant ideal vanishes. The identity
$J_p^{\mathrm{sat}}/J_p=H^0_{P_{p,+}}(\Cfiber_p)$ gives saturation. Theorem~\ref{thm:primary}
shows that the support is the single coordinate point and hence lies entirely in $D_+(X_0)$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:dehom} identifies its affine coordinate ring with $\kfield[y]/(y^q)$. This
has length $q$ and principal maximal ideal $(y)$, so the scheme is curvilinear and its tangent
space $(y)/(y^2)$ is one-dimensional. Its socle is generated by $y^{q-1}$, proving local
Gorensteinness. Item (I4) gives type $10p+1$ and nonlevelness for the homogeneous coordinate
ring, proving the arithmetic contrast.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This example makes the embedding distinction concrete:
\[
 \text{locally Gorenstein}\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad\text{arithmetically Gorenstein}.
\]
The local scheme is always the one-variable truncation, while the standard grading retains the
nonlevel offset-layer structure: the socle of the Artinian reduction lies in degree two with
dimension $10p$ and in degree four with dimension one.
\end{remark}

\subsection{K\"ahler differentials}

The curvilinear chart has a short differential fingerprint which also records the
characteristic of the ground field.

\begin{proposition}[Differential signature, \mD]\label{prop:differentials}
Let $B_p=\Cfiber_p/(X_0-1)$. Then
\[
 \Omega^1_{B_p/\kfield}
 \cong \bigl(B_p/(q y^{q-1})\bigr)\,dy,
 \qquad \bigwedge
 ^m\Omega^1_{B_p/\kfield}=0\quad(m\geq2).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \dim_{\kfield}\Omega^1_{B_p/\kfield}=
 \begin{cases}
 q-1,&\operatorname{char}(\kfield)\nmid q,\\
 q,&\operatorname{char}(\kfield)\mid q.
 \end{cases}
\]
The cotangent space at the closed point has dimension one in every characteristic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the conormal sequence to $B_p=\kfield[y]/(y^q)$ from Corollary~\ref{cor:dehom}.
Differentiating the defining equation gives $q y^{q-1}dy=0$, which proves the presentation
and the characteristic split. The differential module is cyclic, so every exterior power of
degree at least two vanishes. Tensoring with $B_p/(y)$ leaves the single generator $dy$.
\end{proof}

The characteristic split is concrete. Since $q=24p=2^3\cdot3\cdot p$, the dimension is $q$ in
characteristic two and three and in every prime characteristic dividing $p$, and $q-1$ in all
other characteristics. At $p=4$, where $q=96=2^5\cdot3$, the dimension is $96$ over fields of characteristic two or three and $95$ over every other field, and the cotangent space of the closed point is the one-dimensional space spanned by $dy$ in all of them. This agrees with the general differential characterization of weakly
curvilinear schemes in \cite{KreuzerLinhLong2023}, while the exact truncation and the
characteristic formula come from the conductor-family parametrization.

\section{Theory II: the canonical grevlex degeneration}\label{sec:theory-b}

Order the variables by decreasing offset and use graded reverse lexicographic order $\prec$;
thus $X_a\succ X_b$ exactly when $a>b$, and $X_0$ is last. If a monomial is written as a
nondecreasing tuple of offsets, then the $\prec$-smallest monomial in a fixed degree and offset
fiber is the lexicographically smallest tuple. Write it as $N_{n,b}$. Both facts hold because
a larger offset in an earlier coordinate makes the monomial larger in the last-variable order.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical staircase, \mD]\label{lem:staircase}
In each surviving bidegree $(n,b)$ with $b\in E_n$, the unique standard monomial of
$P_p/\operatorname{in}_{\prec}(J_p)$ is $N_{n,b}$, and $\operatorname{in}_\prec(J_p)$ is spanned by
all other monomials. A monomial is a minimal generator of the initial ideal exactly when it is
nonstandard and every one-variable divisor is standard.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The ideal $J_p$ is bihomogeneous for degree and offset. By (I2), the quotient has dimension one
in every bidegree $(n,b)$ with $b\in E_n$ and zero in every other, and in every surviving
fiber all monomials have the same class. Hence $J_p$ is spanned by the differences of two
monomials in the same fiber and by the monomials of absent fibers. Its initial ideal contains
every monomial of an absent fiber and every non-smallest monomial of a surviving fiber, and the
smallest monomial of each surviving fiber is not in the initial ideal because the quotient
is one-dimensional there. The last assertion is the minimal-boundary criterion for a monomial
ideal.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Adding $X_0$, \mD]\label{lem:x0}
For $n\geq1$ and $b\in E_n$, one has $N_{n+1,b}=X_0N_{n,b}$. Consequently, a standard
monomial of degree $n$ and offset $b$ contains $X_0$ if and only if $b\in E_{n-1}$, and for
$n\geq4$ and $0\leq b<q$,
\[
 N_{n,b}=X_0^{n-4}N_{4,b}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $0\in G_p$, $E_n\subseteq E_{n+1}$, so $N_{n+1,b}$ exists. Let $(a_1\leq\cdots\leq a_{n+1})$ be
a degree-$(n+1)$ tuple of offset $b$. If $a_1=0$, removing it leaves a degree-$n$ tuple of
offset $b$, which is lexicographically at least $N_{n,b}$; hence the tuple is at least
$(0,N_{n,b})$. If $a_1>0$, the tuple exceeds $(0,N_{n,b})$ in its first coordinate. So
$X_0N_{n,b}$ is the smallest. The second assertion follows because a standard monomial
containing $X_0$ yields, after removal of $X_0$, a degree-$(n-1)$ monomial of the same offset,
and conversely. The last follows by induction from $E_n=[0,q-1]$ for $n\geq4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit reduced Gr\"obner basis]\label{thm:groebner}
For every $p\geq4$, the reduced Gr\"obner basis of $J_p$ for $\prec$ has the following
structure.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] \mD{} Degree profile in degrees two: $50p^2-17p$ elements, one for every
noncanonical quadratic monomial $M$ of total offset $b$:
\[
 M-N_{2,b}\quad(b<q),\qquad M\quad(b\geq q).
\]
Among these, $(77p^2-49p+2)/2$ are binomials and $(23p^2+15p-2)/2$ are monomial
zero-relations.
\item[\textup{(b)}] \mMV{} The minimal cubic leading monomials are exactly
\begin{align}
A_i&=X_iX_pX_{12p-1} &&(1\leq i\leq p),\notag\\
B_i&=X_iX_pX_{15p-1} &&(1\leq i\leq p),\notag\\
C_i&=X_iX_pX_{18p-1} &&(1\leq i\leq p),\notag\\
D_i&=X_iX_{4p-2}X_{16p} &&(1\leq i\leq p-2),\notag\\
E_i&=X_iX_{4p-2}X_{18p-1} &&(1\leq i\leq p),\label{eq:cubics}
\end{align}
together with $X_p^3$; there are $5p-1$ of them. The minimal quartic leading monomials are exactly
$X_iX_p^2X_{4p-2}$ for $2\leq i\leq p-1$, so there are $p-2$.
\item[\textup{(c)}] \mMV{} The reduced tails are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tails}
\begin{aligned}
A_1&\longmapsto X_0X_{3p}X_{10p},&
A_i&\longmapsto X_0^2X_{13p+i-1} &&(2\leq i\leq p-1),\\
A_p&\longmapsto X_0X_1X_{14p-2},&
B_i&\longmapsto X_0X_{i-1}X_{16p} &&(1\leq i\leq p-1),\\
B_p&\longmapsto X_0^2X_{17p-1},&
C_i&\longmapsto X_0X_{3p+i-1}X_{16p} &&(1\leq i\leq p-1),\\
C_p&\longmapsto X_0X_{3p}X_{17p-1},&
D_i&\longmapsto X_0X_{3p}X_{17p+i-2},\\
E_i&\longmapsto X_0X_{7p+i-2}X_{15p-1} &&&&(i=1,2),\\
E_i&\longmapsto X_0X_{6p+i-3}X_{16p} &&&&(3\leq i\leq p),\\
X_p^3&\longmapsto X_0^2X_{3p},&&
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and the quartic tails are $X_0^3X_{6p+i-2}$.
\item[\textup{(d)}] \mD{} There is no element of degree at least five.
\item[\textup{(e)}] \mD{} No minimal leading monomial, in any degree, is divisible by $X_0$.
\end{enumerate}
The degree profile is therefore $(50p^2-17p,\;5p-1,\;p-2)$ in degrees two, three and four.
Only parts (b) and (c) are machine-verified, decided for all $p$ by two solvers
(Section~\ref{sec:algorithms}, Appendix~\ref{app:obligations}); parts (a), (d) and (e) are
derived from the bases of Proposition~\ref{prop:input} and Lemmas~\ref{lem:staircase}
and~\ref{lem:x0}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).} There are $\binom{10p+1}{2}=50p^2+5p$ quadratic monomials and $|E_2|=22p$
canonical ones, since each surviving offset of $E_2$ contributes exactly one standard
quadratic by Lemma~\ref{lem:staircase}. This gives $50p^2-17p$ quadratic boundary elements; each
is a minimal generator because no variable is a nonzero-fiber failure ($0\in G_p$ and the
maximal generator $18p-1<q$ make every variable standard). The monomial zero-relations are the
unordered pairs $\{a\leq b\}\subseteq G_p$ with $a+b\geq q$. Counting them block pair by block
pair, with the eleven blocks of $G_p$ numbered in the order of their definition, gives the
polynomials of Table~\ref{tab:zeropairs} in Appendix~\ref{app:zero}; their sum is
$(23p^2+15p-2)/2$. Subtracting the zero-relations and the $22p$ canonical pairs from all quadratic
monomials gives the binomial count $50p^2+5p-(23p^2+15p-2)/2-22p=(77p^2-49p+2)/2$.

\emph{Part (e).} Let $M$ be a minimal generator of the initial ideal of degree $n\geq2$ and offset
$b$, and suppose $X_0\mid M$. Then $M/X_0$ is standard by minimality, so $b<q$, $b\in E_{n-1}$ and
$M/X_0=N_{n-1,b}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:x0}, $M=X_0N_{n-1,b}=N_{n,b}$ is standard, which contradicts
the nonstandardness of $M$. If $b\geq q$, the divisor $M/X_0$ already has offset $b\geq q$ and is
not standard, again a contradiction. So no minimal generator is divisible by $X_0$, in degree two
and in every other degree. (The tails of Part (c) contain $X_0$; they are standard monomials, not
leading monomials.)

\emph{Parts (b), (c).} By Lemma~\ref{lem:staircase}, completeness and soundness of the
families are statements about the Presburger-definable predicates of Appendix~\ref{app:obligations}
over the eleven interval blocks of $G_p$. The obligations are: no minimal cubic boundary lies
outside the declared families; every declared cubic is a minimal boundary; the same two
statements for the quartic family; and, for each of the twelve endpoint and interior cases of
Table~\ref{tab:tails}, the declared tail is standard, has the offset of its leading monomial and is
lexicographically smaller. The negation of each is unsatisfiable for all integers $p\geq4$
(Table~\ref{tab:obligations}). The family sizes give $p+p+p+(p-2)+p+1=5p-1$ cubics and $p-2$
quartics, because the leading monomials of different families are distinct (they differ in
their second or third coordinate). The sizes and the absence of $X_0$ in the leading monomials of
these families agree with Part (e), which was derived without the solvers.

\emph{Part (d).} Let $M$ be a minimal generator of the initial ideal of degree $n\geq5$ and offset
$b$. By Part (e), $X_0\nmid M$. For every variable $X_a$ dividing $M$, the divisor $M/X_a$ is
a standard monomial of degree $n-1\geq4$ without $X_0$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:x0} its offset
$b-a$ is not in $E_{n-2}$, and $b-a<q$. For $n\geq6$, $E_{n-2}\supseteq E_4=[0,q-1]$, which is
impossible. For $n=5$, $b-a\notin E_3$ forces $b-a=6p-1$ for every variable $X_a$ dividing $M$,
so all the variables are equal, $M=X_a^5$ and $4a=6p-1$. The left side is even and the right
side odd, a contradiction. This covers both the case of offset below $q$ and that of offset at
least $q$ with no hypothesis on the divisors beyond minimality: the divisors must be standard,
so their offsets are below $q$ whatever the offset of $M$.

\emph{Conclusion.} The listed polynomials are monic, have pairwise distinct minimal leading
monomials, and have standard tails (the cubic and quartic tails are standard by (c)); each lies in $J_p$ because both
of its monomials lie in the same fiber (or its monomial lies in an absent fiber). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:staircase} the leading monomials are the minimal generators of the initial ideal.
Hence they form the reduced Gr\"obner basis.
\end{proof}

The proof of part (d) closes a case that a direct appeal to the formula $N_{n,b}=X_0^{n-4}N_{4,b}$
leaves open: a monomial of degree at least five whose offset is at least $q$ and whose proper
divisors are all standard. The argument above treats it through Part (e), which removes every
monomial divisible by $X_0$, and the parity of $6p-1$ is what excludes the last candidate, the
fifth power $X_a^5$.

\begin{corollary}[Flat Cohen--Macaulay degeneration, \mD; the last two clauses \mMV]\label{cor:degeneration}
The initial algebra $S_p=P_p/\operatorname{in}_\prec(J_p)$ is Cohen--Macaulay, $X_0$ is a nonzerodivisor
on it, and its Artinian reduction by $X_0$ has Hilbert function
\[
 (1,\;10p-1,\;12p,\;2p-1,\;1).
\]
These statements are derived. The defining ideal has relation type three by (I5), which is
machine-verified, while the reduced Gr\"obner basis reaches degree four, which needs the quartic
family of Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}(b) and is machine-verified.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}(e), derived from Lemma~\ref{lem:x0}, no minimal generator of the initial ideal contains $X_0$,
so $S_p$ is $\kfield[X_0]\otimes_\kfield P_p'/I'$ with $P_p'=\kfield[X_a:a>0]$ and $I'$ the monomial
ideal generated by the same monomials. Multiplication by $X_0$ is injective, and $S_p$ is flat
over $\kfield[X_0]$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:x0}, a standard monomial of degree $n$ contains no $X_0$ exactly when its
offset lies in $E_n\setminus E_{n-1}$, and the layers of the bases have sizes
$1,10p-1,12p,2p-1,1$, which sum to $24p$. So $P_p'/I'$ is Artinian of length $24p$ and $S_p$ is
one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay. The Gr\"obner degeneration is flat. Relation type three is
(I5), whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner} has $p-2>0$ quartics.
\end{proof}

\section{Algorithms and implementation}\label{sec:algorithms}

Every computation of this paper is an exact computation with integers and finite sets, with no
randomness and no floating point in any decision. The archived programs are Python scripts
whose paths are listed in Section~\ref{sec:repro}. This section describes each algorithm, its
cost in $p$, and the artifacts it produces. Costs are derived from the structure of the
algorithms; the measured run times are in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

Throughout, $G=G_p$ is built from its eleven blocks, $q=24p$, and sets of offsets below $q$ are held as
$q$-bit integers whose bit $i$ is set when offset $i$ belongs to the set.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Truncated sumset sweep for one parameter $p$ (computations EXP-025 and EXP-026).}
\label{alg:sumset}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require integer $p\geq4$
\State $q\gets24p$; $G\gets$ blocks of $G_p$; $B_1\gets$ bits of $G$
\For{$n=2,\ldots,6$ (EXP-026) or $n=2,\ldots,5$ (EXP-025)}
  \State $B_n\gets\bigl(\bigvee_{g\in G}(B_{n-1}\ll g)\bigr)\wedge(2^q-1)$
  \State compare the set of $B_n$ with the closed formula $E_n$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:input}
\EndFor
\State check $\dim=(1,10p,22p,q-1,q,q,q)$ and the layer sizes $(1,10p-1,12p,2p-1,1)$
\State \textbf{for each} positive $a\in G$: check $a\in E_{\min(a,4)}$ (the relation $X_0^{a-1}X_a=X_1^a$ exists)
\State count the zero pairs $\{a\leq b\}\subseteq G$ with $a+b\geq q$ by bisection; the binomials are the other non-canonical pairs
\State evaluate the mutation controls and write the row with its SHA-256 row hash
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:sumset} costs $|G|=10p$ shifts and ORs of $q$-bit integers per degree, that is
$O(5\cdot10p\cdot q/w)=O(p^2/w)$ word operations for machine word size $w$, plus $O(p\log p)$
for the zero-pair count; a sweep over $p\leq P$ costs $O(P^3/w)$. Its output is, per parameter,
the dimensions, the layer sizes, the quadratic split and a canonical-JSON SHA-256 row hash; the
aggregate over all rows is the SHA-256 of the list of row hashes.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Independent recomputation from the disjoint layers (computation EXP-025 audit).}
\label{alg:layers}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require integer $p\geq4$; no use of Algorithm~\ref{alg:sumset} state
\State write the five layers $\ell_0=\{0\}$, $\ell_1=G\setminus\{0\}$, $\ell_2$, $\ell_3$, $\ell_4$ as explicit unions of intervals
\State $E_n\gets\ell_0\cup\cdots\cup\ell_n$ and $E_5\gets E_4$
\State assert that the layers are pairwise disjoint and partition $[0,q-1]$
\State assert $a\in E_{\min(a,4)}$ for every positive $a\in G$
\State assert $q-1\in E_4$ and $q\notin E_4$ (sharp nilpotence)
\State compare dimensions and hashes with the stored row; rehash every stored row and the aggregate
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The layers are $\ell_3=[2p+1,3p-1]\cup[5p-1,6p-2]$, $\ell_4=\{6p-1\}$ and
\begin{align*}
\ell_2={}&[p+1,2p]\cup[4p-1,5p-2]\cup\{8p-1,10p-1\}\cup[10p+1,11p-2]\cup[12p,13p]\\
&\cup\{14p-1\}\cup[15p,16p-1]\cup[16p+1,17p-2]\cup[18p,24p-1],
\end{align*}
which has $12p$ elements. The cost of Algorithm~\ref{alg:layers} is $O(p)$ interval operations per parameter plus the
rehash of all rows.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Cached quadratic oracle and boundary-divisibility test (computation EXP-026, sweep).}
\label{alg:oracle}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require integer $p\geq4$; the declared cubic leads $\mathcal L_3$ and quartic leads $\mathcal L_4$ with their tails
\Function{Canon}{$t$} \Comment{lexicographically smallest pair of $G$ with sum $t$, cached}
  \State \Return $(\ell,t-\ell)$ with the smallest $\ell\in G$, $\ell\leq t/2$, $t-\ell\in G$; none if $t\geq q$
\EndFunction
\Function{Standard}{$M$}
  \State \Return $\sum M<q$ and every pair $\{u,v\}\subseteq M$ equals \Call{Canon}{$u+v$} and no triple of $M$ is in $\mathcal L_3$ and no quadruple in $\mathcal L_4$
\EndFunction
\For{each relation (lead, tail)}
  \State check lead and tail have equal degree and offset, lead $\succ$ tail
  \State check \Call{Standard}{tail}, not \Call{Standard}{lead}, and every one-variable divisor of lead is standard
\EndFor
\State check $|\mathcal L_3|=5p-1$, $|\mathcal L_4|=p-2$, no lead contains $0$, and the total size $50p^2-11p-3$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:oracle} replaced a first implementation that repeated a recursive search for
canonical factorizations. The cache makes each call to \textsc{Canon} cost $O(1)$ once the total
has been evaluated, and $O(p)$ at its first evaluation (a scan of the generators up to $t/2$), each standardness test cost $O(1)$ lookups for a monomial of degree at most five,
and the validation of one parameter cost $O(p)$ relations times a constant. The sweep therefore
establishes, at every $p=4,\ldots,300$, that every declared lead is a minimal boundary element
with a standard tail of the right offset: the soundness of the families. It does not by itself
enumerate all boundary monomials; completeness at the sampled parameters comes from
Algorithm~\ref{alg:clique}, and completeness for all $p$ from the solver obligations.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Clique reconstruction of the staircase (computation EXP-026, independent recomputation).}
\label{alg:clique}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require integer $p\in\{4,5,6,17,73,151,300\}$; the generators rebuilt from the eleven blocks, not from the sweep
\State enumerate the unordered pairs of sum $<q$ in increasing order and keep for each total the first pair (canonical pairs); count the zero pairs, those of sum $\geq q$, by bisection
\State $\mathrm{Adj}\gets$ adjacency of the graph whose edges are the canonical pairs
\State triples: for each canonical pair $(u,m)$ and $r\in\mathrm{Adj}(u)\cap\mathrm{Adj}(m)$, $r\geq m$, form $(u,m,r)$
\State standard triples: the lexicographically smallest triple per total $<q$; cubic boundary $=$ triples minus standard triples
\For{degree $d=4,5,6$}
  \State candidates: extend each standard $(d-1)$-tuple by a common neighbour such that every distinct divisor is standard
  \State standard $d$-tuples: smallest per total; boundary $=$ candidates minus standard
\EndFor
\State assert the counts $22p$, $q-1$, $q$, $q$, $q$ of standard monomials in degrees two to six and the boundary counts $(5p-1,p-2,0,0)$
\State assert no boundary monomial contains $0$; compare hashes with the sweep row
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:clique} treats the term order only through ``lexicographically smallest'' and
shares no code with Algorithm~\ref{alg:oracle}. The pair enumeration costs $O(p^2)$, the
adjacency lists have size $O(p)$ per vertex, and each degree adds one pass over the standard
tuples of the previous degree; it enumerates every candidate boundary monomial, so it decides
completeness at the sampled parameters. It stops at degree six, one above the proved last degree.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Decision of the Presburger obligations by two solvers (computations EXP-026 and EXP-069).}
\label{alg:solvers}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the 16 obligations of Appendix~\ref{app:obligations}, each built by the program of EXP-026 in a fresh process
\For{each obligation $\Phi$}
  \State build the formula $p\geq4\wedge\Phi$; run the Z3 check (limit $120$~s) and require \texttt{unsat}
  \State take the SHA-256 of the printed query \emph{after} that check, as EXP-026 did, and require equality with the stored digest
  \State export the query as SMT-LIB text; decide it with cvc5 (logic LIA, limit $600$~s) and require \texttt{unsat}
  \State decide the exported text with Z3 (limit $600$~s) and require \texttt{unsat}
\EndFor
\For{each perturbed obligation $C_1,\ldots,C_4$}
  \State require \texttt{sat} from cvc5 and from Z3; evaluate the cvc5 model with an exact integer evaluator on the perturbed formula (true) and on the original (false)
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The solver time is not bounded by a polynomial in $p$: the obligations are formulas over all
integers $p\geq4$ and the cost is that of the quantifier procedures of the solvers, which the
experiment measures in Table~\ref{tab:obligations}. Quantified linear integer arithmetic is
decided by Z3 \cite{DeMouraBjorner2008} through model-based quantifier instantiation and by
cvc5 \cite{Barbosa2022} through counterexample-guided instantiation, two procedures with no shared code, which is why
agreement between them is evidence of a different kind than a repeated run of one solver.

\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design}

The experiments test one family of hypotheses: that the formulas of Theorems~\ref{thm:parametrization}
to~\ref{thm:groebner} and Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneration} hold with no exception at every
parameter at which they can be exactly decided, and that the classification of the cubic and
quartic boundary holds for all integers $p\geq4$. Each computation is a test that can fail. A
mismatch of a boundary, tail, count, order or Hilbert value at any parameter refutes the
affected statement and stops the run; an exhausted time budget is recorded as inconclusive and is
never read as a negative mathematical result.

\paragraph{Data and parameters.} The input is the family itself, generated from its eleven
interval blocks. The sweeps cover all $297$ parameters $p=4,\ldots,300$. The independent
recomputations cover the spaced sample $p=4,5,6,17,73,151,300$ (seven parameters in EXP-026,
six in EXP-025), chosen to include the smallest parameter, consecutive small parameters, a
prime ($17$, $73$, $151$) and the largest swept value. The solver obligations are the 16
formulas of Appendix~\ref{app:obligations}.

\paragraph{Baselines.} The sweep is the principal computation. Its baselines are three
recomputations that are encoded separately: Algorithm~\ref{alg:layers}, which starts from the
five disjoint layers and not from sumsets; Algorithm~\ref{alg:clique}, which enumerates canonical
pairs and rebuilds higher boundaries from cliques without any state of the sweep; and the
second solver, which decides the same obligations that the first solver decided. A finite
computation validates the implementation and the encoding at the tested parameters; it cannot
replace the argument for all $p$, and no finite sweep is used as an extrapolation principle.

\paragraph{Premises.} The sweeps are run only if the SHA-256 hashes of four premise files
(Table~\ref{tab:premises}) equal the hashes recorded when the experiments were designed;
a mismatch stops the run. The solver experiment is run only if the rebuilt queries equal the
stored ones: the SHA-256 of each rebuilt query must equal the digest stored by the original
certificate, which is the identity test of Algorithm~\ref{alg:solvers}.

\paragraph{Controls.} Two kinds of control are used. In the sweeps, ten (EXP-025) and eight
(EXP-026) mutation predicates are evaluated in every row; they are described in
Section~\ref{sec:ablation} together with their limits. In the solver experiment, four obligations
are perturbed so that a counterexample exists, and both solvers must find it.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics.} The metrics are exact: integer dimensions, counts, equality of
SHA-256 hashes, and solver answers. Every computation is deterministic, so there is no
sampling error, and no confidence interval or standard deviation applies to a count or a
hash; the only variable quantity is the solver wall-clock time, which is reported as a single
measurement per query and carries the machine's scheduling noise. The two executions of Z3 in
Table~\ref{tab:obligations}, the original and the one on the exported text, give an indication of
that noise.

\paragraph{Budget.} The sweep of computation EXP-026 was limited to $120$~s and the
recomputation to $120$~s; the solver experiment limited each query to $600$~s with a three-hour
total. Atomic checkpoints are written after every parameter.

\subsection{Capped first attempt}

The first implementation of the sweep, which repeated recursive searches for canonical
factorizations, reached $p=\FirstAttemptLastP$ (\FirstAttemptRows{} rows) when the declared cap of
$120$~s stopped it, after \FirstAttemptSeconds{}~s. The rows computed before the stop were
identical to those of the later implementation, whose caching of the quadratic oracle and
divisibility test (Algorithm~\ref{alg:oracle}) reproduced the earlier row hashes and completed
the sweep. The stop is a statement about the cost of the first implementation and is not
evidence for or against any formula. It is reported because a sweep that stops at a budget has
checked fewer parameters than it was asked to check, and a reader comparing the two
implementations should know that repeated recursive searches for canonical factorizations were
the dominant cost of the first one. The checks themselves were the same in both implementations:
the bases, the layer sizes, the quadratic split, the cubic and quartic families with their tails,
and the controls. Only the way of finding canonical factorizations changed.

\section{Ablation of premises and controls}\label{sec:ablation}

An ablation here asks what each premise buys: which statement fails when a premise is removed,
and whether the computations detect the failure.

\paragraph{Without the Cohen--Macaulay hypothesis.} Theorem~\ref{thm:primary} needs
$\operatorname{Ass}(\Cfiber_p)=\{N_p\}$. Without Cohen--Macaulayness, a unique minimal prime does not
make the zero ideal primary: the quotient of $\kfield[u,v]$ by $(u^2,uv)$ is the standard example. The proof does not use
the inference. The computation of EXP-025 records this control as a constant flag in every row, not as a
test of the pipeline, so it documents the argument and does not exercise code.

\paragraph{Without offset one.} Corollary~\ref{cor:dehom} uses $1\in G_p$ to write every generator as
a power of $X_1$. Deleting offset $1$ from the generators breaks the surjection from
$\kfield[y]/(y^q)$, and the control for it is evaluated in every row.

\paragraph{Without the exact truncation.} Replacing $q$ by $q-1$ or $q+1$ changes the length of the scheme and the
nilpotency index; the controls for the truncation and for the exponents $q\pm1$ compare the predicted
exponent with the wrong one in every row.

\paragraph{Without the characteristic split.} A characteristic-free differential length would claim
$q-1$ everywhere; the control compares $q-1$ with $q$.

\paragraph{Mutations of the Gr\"obner data (EXP-026).} Table~\ref{tab:controls026} lists the
eight mutations of the staircase computation and what each exercises.

had decided the obligations, a defect of its quantifier instantiation would be undetectable. The
agreement of cvc5 on queries with equal digests, with no use of a Z3 answer in its input, removes
a single-implementation failure from the premises, and leaves the encoding and the shared reading of
the text as the remaining ones (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The degeneration and the Betti table}

The degeneration of Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneration} gives $\Cfiber_p$ the Hilbert function of an
Artinian monomial algebra on the $10p-1$ variables of positive offset, with layers
$(1,10p-1,12p,2p-1,1)$, and it explains two numbers of the presentation. The relation type is three
but the natural Gr\"obner degree is four, because the single cubic obstruction at total $3p$
is a minimal relation, while the quartic family $X_iX_p^2X_{4p-2}$ is a boundary of the
staircase that is a consequence of lower relations. The two measures of complexity need not
agree, and here they do not.

\subsection{The open question}

The question that remains for this family is therefore sharper than the one it replaces. The two lowest
regularity strands of $\Cfiber_p$ and of the quadratic quotient $P_p/(J_p)_2$ are not determined, and
at least some multigraded entries depend on the characteristic. Two precise questions follow.
(Q1) Do the ordinary graded Betti numbers $\beta_{i,i+1}$ and $\beta_{i,i+2}$ of $\Cfiber_p$ have
closed formulas in $p$, valid over every field, or do they exhibit parameter-dependent
combinatorial phases? (Q2) Can the parity-sensitive connecting quotient that creates the
characteristic dependence be identified by an integral or topological complex, so as to prove or
refute infinitely many characteristic-dependent cells without extrapolating finite
multiplicities? Both questions concern integral chain complexes of the relative divisor type used
for the computed entries, and (Q1) is the one whose answer would complete the minimal resolution.
A possible route, not pursued here and not claimed, replaces value-semigroup
products by the combinatorics of the single finite staircase of Section~\ref{sec:theory-b}: the
degeneration has no $X_0$ in its generators, and its Artinian part is an explicit monomial
algebra.

\subsection{Reading the results as a whole}

Two features stand out. First, the whole projective geometry of $\Cfiber_p$ is one-variable, while
its algebra has $10p$ generators and a Gr\"obner basis of $50p^2$ elements; the singularity of the
scheme is a fat point of length $24p$, so everything that is complicated about $\Cfiber_p$ lies in its embedding.
Second, the proof architecture isolates a very small unproved core. Parts (a), (d) and (e) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}, Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneration} apart from its last two clauses, Lemmas~\ref{lem:staircase}
and~\ref{lem:x0} and all of Section~\ref{sec:theory-a} are written arguments over the derived input of
Proposition~\ref{prop:input}; the machine-verified part is a set of sixteen quantified
inequalities about interval blocks. A reader who trusts neither the imported input nor the solvers
still has exact agreement of independently encoded computations at every parameter up to $300$
and, at seven parameters, a complete enumeration of the boundary monomials. A reader who trusts the
solvers has in addition the classification for every $p$, and a reader who trusts the cited paper
has the geometry of Section~\ref{sec:theory-a} as derived consequences. The marks of
Table~\ref{tab:marks} say which of these readers each statement serves.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The evidence class.} The cubic and quartic classification (Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}(b),
(c)) is machine-verified by two solvers, Z3 \cite{DeMouraBjorner2008} and cvc5 \cite{Barbosa2022}, not
derived. No proof object was checked by either
solver, so the soundness of Z3 and of cvc5 in quantified linear integer arithmetic is a premise.
The two solvers use different procedures, and a defect would have to coincide in two unrelated
implementations, but this is an argument about independence, not a proof. A derivation by case analysis over
the eleven blocks would remove the solvers from the trusted base and would make Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}(b), (c) a derived statement; it is feasible, since the twelve tails are
short interval computations, and it is not done here.

\paragraph{The encoding.} The obligations encode Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner} through the formulas of
Appendix~\ref{app:obligations}. Both solvers read the same text, and an error in the encoding
(for example a wrong candidate family) would be shared by them. The exact evaluator and the controls test the
reading of the text on four satisfiable instances, not on the sixteen unsatisfiable ones, since an
unsatisfiable formula has no model to evaluate. The finite sweep up to $p=300$ and the clique
enumeration at seven parameters are the independent checks of the encoding.

\paragraph{The imported input.} Only the relation type three, item (I5), was established in
\cite{SantibanezLeal2026} by an all-parameter computation without a checked proof object; it enters
only the relation-type clause of Corollary~\ref{cor:degeneration} and the Discussion. Items (I1) to (I4)
are derived, and the sumset identity (I3) is derived in Remark~\ref{rem:sumset} for every $p$. A defect
in a derived imported item would nevertheless propagate to everything that uses it even if all
implementations agreed; the sweeps over $4\leq p\leq300$ are checks of those items at the tested parameters
and do not replace the derivations.

\paragraph{The controls.} Most mutation controls of the sweeps compare a predicted number with a wrong
one and do not test detection by the pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Scope.} The results concern one explicit family, indexed by one integer parameter, and say
nothing about other conductor ideals or about fiber cones of other counterexamples. The finite sweeps cover $p\leq300$ and
validate the implementations; they do not prove any statement for larger $p$. Only
the solver obligations and the written arguments reach every $p\geq4$. The Betti entries of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} are imported with the classes of the cited paper and are not reproved here; they are reported for
context, and no result of Sections~\ref{sec:theory-a} and~\ref{sec:theory-b} depends on them. The
original conjecture is false in general by \cite{Pham2026,PhamArxiv2026,CGI2026}, and nothing in this
paper addresses the classification of rigid ideals.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

For every $p\geq4$, the conductor fiber cone of the family is the truncated monomial algebra
$\kfield[xy^a:a\in G_p]\subseteq\kfield[x,y]/(y^{24p})$. Its defining ideal is primary with the
ideal of positive offsets as radical, and the nilradical has sharp nilpotency index $24p$. The
projective scheme is a saturated curvilinear fat point of length $24p$, locally Gorenstein
although the coordinate ring is neither level nor Gorenstein, and its differential module has
dimension $q-1$ or $q$ according to the characteristic. The reduced Gr\"obner basis for the
grevlex order with $X_0$ last has degree profile $(50p^2-17p,5p-1,p-2)$ and gives a flat
Cohen--Macaulay degeneration with Artinian Hilbert function $(1,10p-1,12p,2p-1,1)$. The evidence
classes are the same as in the abstract, the introduction and Table~\ref{tab:marks}. Everything
above, including the quadratic part of the basis, the absence of elements in degree five and
higher, the absence of $X_0$ from the leading monomials, the flatness and the Hilbert function, is
derived from the derived imported items (I1) to (I4). Two statements are machine-verified: the
classification of the cubic and quartic part of the basis and its tails, decided for all $p$ by
Z3 and by cvc5 on identical queries without a checked proof object, and the relation type three
imported as (I5), together with the clause that the basis reaches degree four, which needs the
quartic family. What the remaining Betti question requires is the integral structure of the two
lowest strands, where the characteristic enters.

\section{The sixteen Presburger obligations}\label{app:obligations}

All formulas are over the integers, with the parameter $p\geq4$ free. The obligations are
transcribed from the program \texttt{symbolic\_certificate.py} of computation EXP-026; each is the
negation of a property, so that its unsatisfiability proves the property for every $p\geq4$.

The generator predicate is the eleven-block formula of Proposition~\ref{prop:input}:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{gen}_p(v)\;:=\;& v=0\;\vee\;1\leq v\leq p\;\vee\;3p\leq v\leq4p-2\;\vee\;6p\leq v\leq8p-2\;\vee\;8p\leq v\leq10p-2\\
&\vee\;v=10p\;\vee\;11p-1\leq v\leq12p-1\;\vee\;13p+1\leq v\leq14p-2\\
&\vee\;14p\leq v\leq15p-1\;\vee\;v=16p\;\vee\;17p-1\leq v\leq18p-1.
\end{align*}
For a tuple $v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ put
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{valid}(v)\;:=\;&\bigwedge_{k}\mathrm{gen}_p(v_k)\;\wedge\;v_1\leq\cdots\leq v_n\;\wedge\;\textstyle\sum_kv_k<24p,\\
v<_{\mathrm{lex}}w\;:=\;&\bigvee_{k}\bigl(v_1=w_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k-1}=w_{k-1}\wedge v_k<w_k\bigr),\\
\mathrm{standard}(v)\;:=\;&\mathrm{valid}(v)\;\wedge\;\neg\exists w\,\bigl(\mathrm{valid}(w)\wedge\textstyle\sum w=\sum v\wedge w<_{\mathrm{lex}}v\bigr),\\
\mathrm{minbd}(v)\;:=\;&\bigwedge_k\mathrm{gen}_p(v_k)\;\wedge\;v_1\leq\cdots\leq v_n\;\wedge\;\neg\mathrm{standard}(v)\\
&\wedge\;\bigwedge_{k}\mathrm{standard}(v\setminus v_k),
\end{align*}
where $v\setminus v_k$ deletes the $k$th entry and $w$ ranges over tuples of the same length as $v$.
The two candidate predicates are
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{cub}(a,b,c)\;:=\;&(a,b,c)=(p,p,p)\;\vee\;\exists i\,\bigl(1\leq i\leq p\wedge\bigl[(a,b,c)=(i,p,12p-1)\\
&\vee(a,b,c)=(i,p,15p-1)\vee(a,b,c)=(i,p,18p-1)\\
&\vee(i\leq p-2\wedge(a,b,c)=(i,4p-2,16p))\vee(a,b,c)=(i,4p-2,18p-1)\bigr]\bigr),\\
\mathrm{qua}(a,b,c,d)\;:=\;&\exists i\,\bigl(2\leq i\leq p-1\wedge(a,b,c,d)=(i,p,p,4p-2)\bigr).
\end{align*}
The first four obligations are the unsatisfiability, together with $p\geq4$, of
\begin{align*}
&\mathrm{minbd}(a,b,c)\wedge\neg\mathrm{cub}(a,b,c)&&\texttt{cubic-completeness},\\
&\mathrm{cub}(a,b,c)\wedge\neg\mathrm{minbd}(a,b,c)&&\texttt{cubic-soundness},\\
&\mathrm{minbd}(a,b,c,d)\wedge\neg\mathrm{qua}(a,b,c,d)&&\texttt{quartic-completeness},\\
&\mathrm{qua}(a,b,c,d)\wedge\neg\mathrm{minbd}(a,b,c,d)&&\texttt{quartic-soundness}.
\end{align*}
Each of the remaining twelve is the unsatisfiability, together with $p\geq4$, of
\[
 \mathrm{region}(i,p)\;\wedge\;\neg\bigl(\mathrm{standard}(\mathrm{tail})\wedge\textstyle\sum\mathrm{lead}=\sum\mathrm{tail}\wedge\mathrm{tail}<_{\mathrm{lex}}\mathrm{lead}\bigr),
\]
with the region, the leading monomial and the tail of Table~\ref{tab:tails}. Table~\ref{tab:tails}
reads as the reduced tails of Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}(c) written as offset tuples.

\section{The perturbed obligations}\label{app:controls}

The four perturbations of computation EXP-069 are built from the same program as the
obligations. C1 replaces $\mathrm{cub}$ in \texttt{cubic-completeness} by the candidate predicate without
its first disjunct $(a,b,c)=(p,p,p)$; since $X_p^3$ is a minimal boundary element, the perturbed
formula has a model, and cvc5 returns $p=4$ and $(4,4,4)$. C2 adds the triple $(0,0,0)$ to
the candidates in \texttt{cubic-soundness}; $X_0^3$ is standard and is not a boundary element, so a model
exists, and cvc5 returns $p=8$ and $(0,0,0)$. C3 replaces the tail $(0,3p,10p)$ of
\texttt{tail-c1-first} by $(0,3p,10p+1)$, whose total differs from that of the lead, and C4 widens the
index range of \texttt{tail-quartic} to include $i=1$, for which the tail offset $6p-1$ is not in $G_p$.
Both solvers return a model at $p=4$, $i=1$ in both cases. The exact evaluator parses the exported
text with its own parser and enumerates bounded quantifiers; it raises an error on an unbounded one,
so it cannot accept a formula that it did not fully evaluate. The model that satisfies each
perturbed formula fails the original obligation, which shows that the evaluator tells the perturbed
formula from the original one.

\section{The monomial zero-relations by block pairs}\label{app:zero}

The monomial zero-relations of Theorem~\ref{thm:groebner}(a) are the unordered pairs
$\{a\leq b\}\subseteq G_p$ with $a+b\geq24p$. Table~\ref{tab:zeropairs} counts them for every pair of the
eleven blocks of $G_p$ that contains at least one such pair, as polynomials in $p$. A pair of
blocks whose largest possible sum is below $24p$ contributes nothing, and every pair not listed is of
this kind: for example $[6p,8p-2]$ with $\{16p\}$ has largest sum $24p-2$, and $[13p+1,14p-2]$ with
itself is listed because its largest sum is $28p-4$, whereas $[11p-1,12p-1]$ with itself has largest
sum $24p-2$ and is not. The counts follow for every $p\geq4$ from three observations. Write
$B_4=[6p,8p-2]$, $B_5=[8p,10p-2]$, $B_6=\{10p\}$, $B_7=[11p-1,12p-1]$, $B_8=[13p+1,14p-2]$,

the table. The sizes of $B_5$ to $B_{11}$ are $2p-1$, $1$, $p+1$, $p-2$, $p$, $1$ and $p+1$.

\emph{The list is complete.} A pair of blocks contributes only if the sum of its two largest
elements is at least $24p$. The twenty-one listed pairs are those: $B_4$ with $B_{11}$ ($26p-3$);
$B_5$ with $B_9$, $B_{10}$, $B_{11}$ ($25p-3$ and more); $B_6$ with $B_9$, $B_{10}$, $B_{11}$
($25p-1$ and more); $B_7$ with $B_8$ ($26p-3$), $B_9$, $B_{10}$, $B_{11}$; $B_8$ with $B_8$
