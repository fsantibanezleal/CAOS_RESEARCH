\begin{abstract}
Let $f(n,\alpha,k)$ be the minimum size of a $k$-connected graph of order $n$ and independence number $\alpha$.
Bougard and Joret conjectured a two-line formula for $f$, and Das and Gupta determined the boundary $n=\alpha+k$ and disproved the first line there.
We determine $f$ on the entire first interior shell $n=\alpha+k+1$:
$f(\alpha+k+1,\alpha,k)=\lceil k(\alpha+k+1)/2\rceil$ for every $k\ge3$ and $2\le\alpha\le k+1$, the degree-sum bound.
The extremal graphs combine a Harary-type residual graph of order $k+1$ with an independent set joined through distinct missing-neighbor pairs; an elementary cyclic-gap argument gives connectivity in every parity case.
For $\alpha=2$ the extremizers are exactly the complements of disjoint unions of cycles of length at least five.
For $\alpha=k-1$ they are exactly the graphs built on a nonstar tree with prescribed fibers, so $f(2k,k-1,k)=k^2$, one more than the boundary value $k^2-1$.
Exact computations test the proofs: the 75 constructions with $3\le k\le12$ are rebuilt by a second implementation, 75 damaged-edge and 8 complement-cycle controls behave as predicted, and labeled enumerations for $k\le5$ and a census of all 32,768 graphs on six vertices agree with the theorems.
The universal statements rest on the proofs, not on the finite computations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected.
Write $\e(G)$ for the number of edges, $\alpha(G)$ for the independence number and $\kappa(G)$ for the vertex connectivity of a graph $G$.
A graph is $k$-connected if it has more than $k$ vertices and deletion of fewer than $k$ vertices leaves it connected.
For integers $n,\alpha,k$, let $f(n,\alpha,k)$ be the least number of edges of a $k$-connected graph of order $n$ and independence number exactly $\alpha$.
Without the connectivity requirement, the least size is the edge count $t(n,\alpha)$ of a union of $\alpha$ balanced cliques (Tur\'an's theorem in its complementary form \cite{Turan}); that graph is disconnected, and requiring $k$-connectivity is what makes the problem hard.

Bougard and Joret \cite{BJ} settled $k\le2$ and conjectured, for $n\ge2\alpha$, $n\ge\alpha+k$, $\alpha\ge2$ and $k\ge3$, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conj}
f(n,\alpha,k)=
\begin{cases}
\lceil nk/2\rceil,&n\le k\alpha,\\
t(n,\alpha)+\lceil k\alpha/2\rceil,&n>k\alpha.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The first line is the degree-sum bound: every vertex of a $k$-connected graph has degree at least $k$.
The conjecture predicts that, up to order $k\alpha$, no connectivity cost is paid beyond the degree sum.
Das and Gupta \cite{DG} determined $f$ on the whole boundary $n=\alpha+k$ and showed that the first line fails there for $\alpha=k-1$, $k\ge4$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:old}
f(2k-1,k-1,k)=k^2-1,
\end{equation}
with extremal graphs exactly the joins of an independent set of size $k-1$ and a tree on $k$ vertices.
The smallest failure is $f(7,3,4)=15$, against a predicted $14$.
The disproof and this boundary characterization are their results; they are used here only as the point of comparison.

The question taken up here is the next order: does the degree-sum bound hold on the shell $n=\alpha+k+1$, and for which graphs?
The answer is that it holds for every admissible parameter, and the proofs show how the connectivity cost disappears.
The results are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:shell} (Section~\ref{sec:shell}): $f(\alpha+k+1,\alpha,k)=\lceil k(\alpha+k+1)/2\rceil$ for every $k\ge3$ and $2\le\alpha\le k+1$.
The construction joins an independent set to a residual graph of order $k+1$ through distinct missing-neighbor pairs (Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}); the residual graph is a Harary-type cycle power with a complement matching (Lemma~\ref{lem:harary}), or a short explicit graph at $\alpha\in\{k-1,k,k+1\}$ and a complement of a cycle at $\alpha=2$.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:cycles}: for $\alpha=2$ the extremizers are exactly the complements of disjoint unions of cycles of length at least five.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:main} (Section~\ref{sec:strip}): for $\alpha=k-1$, so that $n=2k$, the extremizers are exactly the graphs $G(T,(S_t))$ on a nonstar tree $T$ with $k+1$ vertices, and $f(2k,k-1,k)=k^2$.
This is one more than the boundary value $k^2-1$ of \eqref{eq:old}, and the excess over the degree-sum prediction drops from $\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1$ to zero.
\item A set of exact computations (Sections~\ref{sec:computations} and~\ref{sec:results}): the 75 shell constructions with $3\le k\le12$, an independent recomputation with NetworkX, damaged-edge and complement-cycle controls, labeled enumerations of all residual graphs for $k\le5$, and a census of all 32,768 labeled graphs on six vertices.
\end{enumerate}
Universal deductions are labeled [D], exact finite computations [MV], statements read on a primary source [V], and statements not confirmed on a primary source [U].
The proofs and computations are archived as EXP-001 and EXP-002 \cite{Record,ShellRecord}.

Section~\ref{sec:formulation} fixes the notation and the residual-graph viewpoint, and Section~\ref{sec:related} places the results among the proved cases of Bougard and Joret, the boundary theorem, the computations on the second line of \eqref{eq:conj}, and the next-matching value at order $2k-1$.
Sections~\ref{sec:shell} and~\ref{sec:strip} contain the proofs, Section~\ref{sec:computations} the algorithms behind the computations, and Section~\ref{sec:results} their results.
Sections~\ref{sec:discussion} to~\ref{sec:conclusions} discuss the findings and their limits, give the commands that reproduce every number, and conclude.

\section{Problem formulation and notation}\label{sec:formulation}

Fix integers $k\ge3$ and $\alpha\ge2$.
The parameters $(n,\alpha,k)$ are admissible when $n\ge2\alpha$ and $n\ge\alpha+k$, the range of the conjecture \eqref{eq:conj} \cite{BJ}.
On the boundary $n=\alpha+k$ deleting a maximum independent set leaves exactly $k$ vertices; the shell is the next order $n=\alpha+k+1$, where it leaves $k+1$.
The upper limit $\alpha\le k+1$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:shell} is exactly the condition $n\ge2\alpha$ on the shell.
On the shell $n\le k\alpha$ as well, because $\alpha(k-1)\ge k+1$ for $\alpha\ge2$ and $k\ge3$; hence the first line of \eqref{eq:conj} applies throughout the shell.

Two elementary facts frame everything below.
First, $f(n,\alpha,k)\ge\lceil nk/2\rceil$ for every admissible triple [D]: a $k$-connected graph has minimum degree at least $k$, so its degree sum is at least $nk$ and its size at least $\lceil nk/2\rceil$.
A $k$-connected graph attains this bound exactly when all degrees equal $k$, with one vertex of degree $k+1$ when $nk$ is odd.
Put $\varepsilon=nk\bmod2$; the attaining graphs are the $k$-regular graphs ($\varepsilon=0$) and the graphs with exactly one vertex of degree $k+1$ ($\varepsilon=1$).
On the shell $\varepsilon=1$ exactly when $n$ and $k$ are both odd, which for $3\le k\le12$ gives the 15 cases listed in Table~\ref{tab:shell75}.
Second, let $G$ be $k$-connected of order $n=\alpha+k+1$ with a maximum independent set $S$ of size $\alpha$, and let $H=G-S$ be the residual graph on $k+1$ vertices [D].
Since $S$ is independent, every neighbor of $s\in S$ lies in $H$, and $s$ has at least $k$ neighbors there, so $s$ is adjacent to all of $H$ except at most one vertex.
On the boundary the corresponding statement is that every vertex of $S$ is adjacent to all of $H$, which is why $H$ must itself carry the connectivity of the graph at the price of extra edges \cite{DG}.
On the shell each $s$ has a \emph{missing neighbor} in $H$, and the choice of the missing neighbors is the design freedom the constructions use.
Writing $S_t\subseteq S$ for the vertices of $S$ that miss $t\in V(H)$, the degree of $t$ in a $k$-regular graph of this form is $\dd_G(t)=\dd_H(t)+\alpha-|S_t|=k$, so the fiber sizes are determined by the residual degrees; for $\alpha=k-1$ this is the identity $|S_t|=\dd_H(t)-1$, and the classification of Section~\ref{sec:strip} follows from it.

For the Bougard--Joret function, $t(n,\alpha)=r\binom{q+1}{2}+(\alpha-r)\binom q2$ with $n=q\alpha+r$ and $0\le r<\alpha$.
The symbols used in the proofs are collected in Table~\ref{tab:notation}.
In Section~\ref{sec:shell} the letter $a$ denotes the prescribed value of $\alpha(G)$ and $\alpha(\cdot)$ always denotes the function, so that expressions such as $\alpha(H)\le a$ are unambiguous; the shell parameter $g=k-a$ is named to avoid the letter $d$ used for degrees and, in the cited next-matching work, for a maximum degree.
