\begin{abstract}
Let $T(d,m)$ be the maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph with maximum degree at most $d$ and matching number at most $m$, with no restriction on its order. We prove $T(d,d+1)=d^2+d+2$ for every integer $d\ge7$. A shortest-odd-cycle deficit bound, followed by a five-type equality obstruction, first gives the same maximum on $2d+3$ vertices; the Tutte--Berge formula extends it to arbitrary order. The attaining construction is due to Banak, Ekim and Ta\c{s}k\i n; the contribution is the uniform upper bound. It resolves the published bounds $184\le T(13,14)\le185$ in favor of 184, and it places the level $m=d+1$ inside the range $d<m<Z(d)$ of the Ahanjideh--Ekim--Y\i ld\i z conjecture for every $d\ge7$, including the odd degrees 7, 9 and 11 where their proved bounds on $Z(d)$ do not. As a consequence the Bougard--Joret function satisfies $f(2d+3,2,d+2)=d^2+4d+2$ for every $d\ge7$, an unbounded strict-interior discrepancy from the degree-sum prediction; the lower value is excluded because every extremal triangle-free graph on $2d+3$ vertices has two vertices with the same $d$ neighbours. Exact computations support the proofs without replacing them: \nGraphs\ saved graphs and \nCandidates\ five-type tuples pass two implementations, and a configuration-split satisfiability search finds no counterexample at $d=7$ (all 8 configurations) and $d=8$ (7 of 8; the eighth stopped at its time limit and is excluded by an identity). The full conjecture remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem and the results}

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Write $\e(F)$, $\Delta(F)$, $\delta(F)$, $\nu(F)$ and $\kappa(F)$ for the number of edges, the maximum degree, the minimum degree, the maximum matching size and the vertex connectivity of $F$. For integers $d,m\ge1$ define
\[
T(d,m)=\max\{\e(F): F\text{ triangle-free},\ \Delta(F)\le d,\ \nu(F)\le m\}.
\]
There is no restriction on the order of $F$; isolated vertices are allowed and do not change the maximum. Ahanjideh, Ekim and Y\i ld\i z (AEY) write $f_\triangle(d,m)$ for this quantity~\cite{AEY}; we write $T$ to keep it apart from the Bougard--Joret minimum-edge function $f(n,\alpha,k)$ of Section~\ref{sec:bj}. Two statements are proved here.

\begin{theorem}[Next matching level, D]\label{thm:main}
For every integer $d\ge7$, $T(d,d+1)=d^2+d+2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Bougard--Joret value, D]\label{thm:bjexact}
For every integer $d\ge7$, $f(2d+3,2,d+2)=d^2+4d+2$.
\end{theorem}

The contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A uniform upper bound for $T(d,d+1)$ valid for every $d\ge7$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). It rests on a shortest-odd-cycle deficit bound, a five-type equality obstruction on $2d+3$ vertices (Theorem~\ref{thm:order}) and the Tutte--Berge formula (Section~\ref{sec:tb}). The attaining graph is the construction of Banak, Ekim and Ta\c{s}k\i n~\cite{BET}, which Section~\ref{sec:attain} re-verifies for every $d\ge7$.
\item The value $T(13,14)=184$, which closes the interval $184\le T(13,14)\le185$ left by the integer-programming computations of~\cite{BET}.
\item A proof that $d+1<Z(d)$ for every $d\ge7$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:Z}), where $Z(d)$ is the threshold of AEY defined exactly in Section~\ref{sec:Z}. Their proved bounds on $Z(d)$ give this only for even $d$ and for odd $d\ge13$; the corollary adds $d=7,9,11$. Hence the slice $m=d+1$ lies inside the range $d<m<Z(d)$ of their Conjecture~6.1 for every $d\ge7$.
\item The exact value of the Bougard--Joret function at the strict-interior parameters $(2d+3,2,d+2)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact}), through the structural Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d}: every extremal triangle-free graph on $2d+3$ vertices has two vertices with the same $d$ neighbours.
\item A documented set of exact computations (Section~\ref{sec:comp}): a certificate generator, an independent NetworkX audit, a five-type enumeration and a configuration-split satisfiability search, each given as an algorithm with its commands, versions where recorded, run times and hashes, and their results in Tables~\ref{tab:graphs}--\ref{tab:models}.
\end{enumerate}
The attaining construction and the finite values through $d=12$ are prior work. The proofs of Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:bjexact} are for all $d\ge7$ and do not depend on the computations; the computations are checks, and Section~\ref{sec:valid} states what each one covers. Statements carry a mark: [D] derived here, [MV] machine-verified by a finite computation, [V] read on a primary source, [U] not verified. Table~\ref{tab:notation} collects the notation.

\paragraph{Scope of the novelty claim.}

Searches located no explicit prior uniform solution of the slice $m=d+1$, without establishing exhaustive bibliographic priority; the sources compared are listed in Section~\ref{sec:related}. Regular triangle-free extremal results, including Caro--Tuza~\cite[Theorem 2(iii)]{CT} and the later regular Tur\'an literature~\cite{CDK}, give strong regular-degree obstructions and are not claimed as new. Our fixed-order theorem permits nonregular graphs of maximum degree at most $d$; its attaining graph has one vertex of degree four and all others of degree $d$.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{The degree and matching extremal problem}

The problem of maximizing the number of edges of a graph under a maximum-degree bound and a matching-number bound was first posed by Erd\H{o}s and Rado in a more general setting~\cite{ER}; Chv\'atal and Hanson first answered it for general graphs, using Berge's matching formula~\cite{CH}, and Balachandran and Khare later gave a constructive proof~\cite{BK}. [V, as reported by~\cite{BET}; the three originals were not read here.] In the form stated by AEY, Theorem~2.1 of~\cite{AEY} gives, for general graphs, $f_{\mathrm{GEN}}(d,m)=dm+\lfloor d/2\rfloor\lfloor m/\lceil d/2\rceil\rfloor$ [V]. At $m=d+1$ and $d\ge3$ the last factor equals two, so the general maximum is $d(d+1)+2\lfloor d/2\rfloor$ [D]. Forbidding triangles lowers it to $d^2+d+2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}), a loss of $2\lfloor d/2\rfloor-2$ edges, which is $4$ at $d=7$ and $6$ at $d=8$ [D]. Vizing's theorem gives the weaker general bound $(\Delta+1)\nu$ edges~\cite{Vizing}, here $(d+1)^2=d^2+2d+1$ [V, as used in~\cite{AEY}].

The extremal graphs of the general problem contain stars, cliques and almost complete graphs, so the same question for graph classes that exclude one of these components is natural. Claw-free graphs~\cite{Dibek}, chordal graphs~\cite{Blair} and several further classes~\cite{Maland} were treated before AEY [V, as reported by~\cite{AEY}; not read here]. AEY treat triangle-free graphs. They prove structural lemmas that hold for every class closed under vertex deletion and disjoint union with stars, and they solve the triangle-free problem for $d\ge m$, for $d\le6$ and for $Z(d)\le m<2d$ (Theorems~3.3, 3.4, 4.7, 4.9 and~4.10 of~\cite{AEY}) [V]. Two of these theorems matter below.
\begin{itemize}
\item Theorem~3.4: $f_\triangle(d,d)=d^2+1$ for $d\ge2$. The extremal graph is a blow-up of $C_5$ on $2d+1$ vertices.
\item Corollary~3.5: if $G$ is an edge-extremal graph with the largest possible number of $d$-star components, every other component $H$ is factor-critical with $|V(H)|=2\nu(H)+1$ and $\nu(H)\ge d$.
\end{itemize}
Gallai's lemma~\cite{Gallai} supplies the factor-criticality [V, as used in~\cite{AEY}]. The cases left open by AEY are $7\le d<m$ with $m<Z(d)$ or $m\ge2d$.

\subsection{Lineage of the extremal problem and its bearing on \texorpdfstring{$Z(d)$}{Z(d)} and the bracket}\label{sec:lineage}

Each work below is described as it was read: [V] on the final text of~\cite{AEY} or the full text of~\cite{BET}, which report it, with the original not read here.

\emph{Erd\H{o}s and Rado~\cite{ER}} posed a more general problem of which the degree and matching problem is a special case; no hypothesis or conclusion of theirs is used here. \emph{Chv\'atal and Hanson~\cite{CH}} solved it for general graphs: for all $d$ and $m$ they determined the maximum number of edges of a graph with $\Delta\le d$ and $\nu\le m$, by optimisation arguments and Berge's matching formula~\cite{BET,AEY}. The same formula, in the form~\eqref{eq:TB}, is the engine of Section~\ref{sec:tb}, so both arguments rest on Berge's formula. \emph{Balachandran and Khare~\cite{BK}} gave a constructive proof. Their formula, printed as Theorem~2.1 of~\cite{AEY}, is $f_{\mathrm{GEN}}(d,m)=dm+\lfloor d/2\rfloor\lfloor m/\lceil d/2\rceil\rfloor$ for all $d,m$, attained by disjoint unions of $d$-stars, complete graphs and, for some parameters, an almost complete graph that contains $C_4$ [V]. The clique components are what triangle-freeness forbids: $Z(d)$ of Definition~\ref{def:Z} is the matching number of the triangle-free replacement of such a component, an (almost) $d$-regular factor-critical graph on $2Z(d)+1$ vertices, so the threshold at which AEY's conjecture~\eqref{eq:AEYconj} stops applying is the point at which one such component fits in the matching budget. \emph{Belmonte, Heggernes, van 't Hof and Saei~\cite{Belmonte}} showed, as reported in~\cite{AEY}, that the general problem is equivalent to computing Ramsey numbers of line graphs; no such equivalence is claimed for the triangle-free problem here.

The restrictions of the problem to graph classes follow the structure of the extremal graphs of~\cite{BK}. Dibek, Ekim and Heggernes~\cite{Dibek} settled the claw-free case, with some parameters at which the general maximum is attained and others at which it is strictly smaller. Blair et al.~\cite{Blair} showed that chordal graphs attain the general bound, by replacing the almost complete components with chordal graphs of the same size, and M\aa land~\cite{Maland} treated bipartite, split, unions of split and unit interval graphs [V, as reported in~\cite{AEY}]. For triangle-free graphs AEY show that the general maximum is strictly lowered in most cases; at $m=d+1$ the loss is $2\lfloor d/2\rfloor-2$ edges (Section~\ref{sec:attain} and the comparison above), so the slice of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a case in which the class restriction is costly and the exact value is not the general one.

AEY also restate the problem as a degree-bounded Tur\'an problem: for $d\le i\le Z(d)$, $f_\triangle(d,i)=\mathrm{ex}(2i+1,\{K_3,K_{1,d}\})$, the largest size of a graph on $2i+1$ vertices without a triangle and without the star printed as $K_{1,d}$ (a bound $\Delta\le d$ excludes $K_{1,d+1}$, so the printed index is read here as a notational slip [U]), and they contrast the asymptotic theorem of Erd\H{o}s and Stone~\cite{ErdosStone}, described as fundamental in~\cite{Bollobas}, with the exact values sought here [V, as stated in~\cite{AEY}]. In this language Theorem~\ref{thm:order} computes the extremal number of triangle-free graphs on $2d+3$ vertices with maximum degree at most $d$, and equals $d^2+d+2$ for $d\ge7$ [D]. The bracket of Section~\ref{sec:bj} is the same extremal number seen through complementation: a graph with independence number two on $2d+3$ vertices and minimum degree at least $d+2$ has a triangle-free complement of maximum degree at most $d$, so $f(2d+3,2,d+2)\ge\binom{2d+3}{2}-(d^2+d+2)=d^2+4d+1$ and equality would require the complement of a maximizer to be $(d+2)$-connected, which Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} excludes [D].

Finally, H\"aggkvist~\cite{Haggkvist} is cited in~\cite{AEY} for the statement that a triangle-free graph with $\delta>3|V|/8$ is a subgraph of a blow-up of $C_5$ [V, as stated in~\cite{AEY}]; AEY use it to support their Conjecture~6.5, since the graphs realising $Z(d)$ for large odd $d$ have minimum degree at least $d-2$ and order $2Z(d)+1$, above that threshold. The five types of Section~\ref{sec:fixed} are the classes of the same quotient to $C_5$, obtained here from the shortest odd cycle and the deficit bound and not from a minimum-degree hypothesis; the graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:order} have a vertex of degree four, so the minimum-degree threshold does not apply to them [D].

\subsection{The threshold \texorpdfstring{$Z(d)$}{Z(d)} and the proved range}\label{sec:Z}

\begin{definition}[$Z(d)$, \cite{AEY} Definition~4.2]\label{def:Z}
A graph is \emph{almost $d$-regular} if one vertex has degree $d-1$ and every other vertex has degree $d$. For $d\ge2$, $Z(d)$ is the smallest natural number $n$ such that there is a $d$-regular (for $d$ even) or almost $d$-regular (for $d$ odd) triangle-free factor-critical graph $\Gamma$ with $\nu(\Gamma)=n$. [V]
\end{definition}

Such a graph has $2Z(d)+1$ vertices, and a degree count gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Zedges}
\e(\Gamma)=\Big\lfloor\frac{(2Z(d)+1)d}{2}\Big\rfloor=dZ(d)+\Big\lfloor\frac d2\Big\rfloor,
\end{equation}
as in the proof of Lemma~4.5 of~\cite{AEY} [V]. Their Lemma~4.5 gives $Z(d)\ge d+1$ for every $d\ge4$, and their Lemma~4.6 gives $Z(d)=\lfloor5d/4\rfloor$ for even $d$ and $\lfloor5(d-1)/4\rfloor\le Z(d)\le\lfloor5(d+1)/4\rfloor$ for odd $d$ [V]. The lower bound comes from the Andr\'asfai--Erd\H{o}s--S\'os theorem~\cite{AES}: a triangle-free graph of order $n$ with minimum degree above $2n/5$ is bipartite, and factor-critical graphs are not. AEY's Conjecture~6.1 states
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AEYconj}
T(d,i)=di+i-d+1\qquad\text{for }7\le d<i<Z(d),
\end{equation}
and their Conjecture~6.5 states $Z(d)=\lfloor5(d+1)/4\rfloor$ for odd $d\ge21$ [V]. At $i=d+1$ the right side of~\eqref{eq:AEYconj} is $d^2+d+2$, the value of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Whether $i=d+1$ is inside the range depends on whether $Z(d)\ge d+2$. Table~\ref{tab:z} evaluates the intervals of Lemmas~4.5 and~4.6 for $d=7,\ldots,14$: for even $d\ge8$ the exact value $\lfloor5d/4\rfloor=d+\lfloor d/4\rfloor\ge d+2$ settles it, for odd $d\ge13$ the bound $\lfloor5(d-1)/4\rfloor=d-1+\lfloor(d-1)/4\rfloor\ge d+2$ does, and for $d=7,9,11$ the proved interval still contains $d+1$ [D, from the cited lemmas]. Corollary~\ref{cor:Z} closes these three cases.

\subsection{Integer-programming computations and the attaining family}

Banak, Ekim and Ta\c{s}k\i n (BET) built an integer-programming formulation for the extremal components between matching numbers $d$ and $Z(d)$, with orbital branching and an iterative restriction of the feasible region, and solved the knapsack model over components with CPLEX~\cite{BET}. They obtained all extremal triangle-free graphs for $d=7,8,9,10$ and every $m$, and some for $d=11,12,13$ [V, AEY's closing note and the abstract of~\cite{BET}]. Their Proposition~4.1 gives a graph $B_{d,d+t}$ with $di+i-d+1$ edges, maximum degree $d$ and matching number $i=d+t$, for $d<i<Z(d)$; the case $t=1$ is the graph $Q_d$ of Section~\ref{sec:attain}, written there in crown notation [V]. \subsection{The Bougard--Joret function}

Bougard and Joret~\cite{BJ} study $f(n,\alpha,k)$ and conjecture, for $n>k\alpha$, the formula $t(n,\alpha)+\lceil k\alpha/2\rceil$, where $t(n,\alpha)$ is the number of edges of $\alpha$ disjoint balanced cliques [V, as restated in~\cite{SR}]. Every $k$-connected graph has minimum degree at least $k$, so $f(n,\alpha,k)\ge\lceil nk/2\rceil$ for all parameters [D]; the excess over this degree-sum prediction measures how far the extremal graphs are from being regular. Das and Gupta disprove the conjectured formula on the boundary $n=\alpha+k$: their Theorem~3.1 determines that boundary and their Corollary~3.3 gives $f(2k-1,k-1,k)=k^2-1$ for $k\ge4$~\cite{DG} [V]. Their final section leaves the second regime open. The companion first-shell paper~\cite{Shell} determines the first strict-interior shell $f(\alpha+k+1,\alpha,k)=\lceil k(\alpha+k+1)/2\rceil$ for $k\ge3$ and $2\le\alpha\le k+1$ [as stated in~\cite{Shell}]. The parameters $(2d+3,2,d+2)$ are not in that shell, because $\alpha+k+1=d+5<2d+3$ for $d\ge3$ [D]. The second-regime paper~\cite{SR} checks the conjectured value exhaustively for the open triples with $n\le24$ in the regime $n>k\alpha$; a proof of the lower bound in that regime has been announced by Didin~\cite{Didin} [U as to correctness; the statement is taken from~\cite{SR}]. The parameters $(2d+3,2,d+2)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact} satisfy $n<k\alpha$ and $n>\alpha+k$, so they lie in the first regime, away from both of these results.

\section{Proof method for the fixed order}\label{sec:fixed}

\begin{lemma}[Shortest odd cycle, D]\label{lem:cycle}
Let $F$ be triangle-free on $n$ vertices and let $C$ be a shortest odd cycle, of length $\ell$. Then $\ell\ge5$, $C$ is chordless, every vertex outside $C$ has at most two neighbors on $C$, and
\[
\sum_{x\in V(C)}\deg_F(x)\le2n.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Triangle-freeness gives $\ell\ge5$. A chord would split $C$ into two cycles of opposite parity, producing a shorter odd cycle.
If an outside vertex has at least three neighbors on $C$, list all of them in cyclic order. The intervening arc lengths sum to the odd number $\ell$, so at least one arc is odd. Every arc has length at least two, by triangle-freeness. The other arcs have total length at least four, so the odd arc has length at most $\ell-4$. Adding the two edges to the outside vertex produces an odd cycle of length at most $\ell-2$, a contradiction.
The cycle contributes $2\ell$ to its degree sum and the $n-\ell$ outside vertices contribute at most $2(n-\ell)$, proving the bound.
\end{proof}

If $\Delta(F)\le d$, its total degree deficit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficitdef}
D(F)=\sum_{x\in V(F)}(d-\deg_F(x))=nd-2\e(F).
\end{equation}
Every summand is nonnegative. For nonbipartite $F$, Lemma~\ref{lem:cycle} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficit}
D(F)\ge\sum_{x\in V(C)}(d-\deg_F(x))
\ge\ell d-2n\ge5d-2n.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed order, D]\label{thm:order}
For every integer $d\ge7$, the maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph on $2d+3$ vertices with maximum degree at most $d$ is $d^2+d+2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof of the upper bound]
Let $n=2d+3$. If $F$ is bipartite, one part has at most $d+1$ vertices, so summing degrees on that part gives $\e(F)\le d(d+1)$.
Otherwise~\eqref{eq:deficit} gives $D(F)\ge d-6$ and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oneedge}
\e(F)\le d^2+d+3.
\end{equation}
Suppose equality holds. Then every inequality in~\eqref{eq:deficit} is an equality. Thus a shortest odd cycle has length five, every outside vertex has degree exactly $d$, and every outside vertex has exactly two neighbors on the cycle.

Number the cycle $c_0,\ldots,c_4$, with indices modulo five. Its two neighbors at an outside vertex must form a nonadjacent pair, uniquely $\{c_{i-1},c_{i+1}\}$ for some $i$. Let $V_i$ contain $c_i$ and all outside vertices with this pair, and put $n_i=|V_i|$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:types}
n_i\ge1,\qquad \sum_i n_i=2d+3.
\end{equation}
Each $V_i$ is independent, and there are no edges between $V_i$ and $V_{i+2}$: such vertices share a cycle neighbor. Every possible edge therefore joins consecutive types. We do not assume completeness between types.

By definition, $c_i$ is adjacent to every member of $V_{i-1}\cup V_{i+1}$ and no others. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b}
b_i:=n_{i-1}+n_{i+1}=\deg_F(c_i)\le d.
\end{equation}
If $n_i\ge2$, an outside vertex in $V_i$ has degree $d$ and all its neighbors lie in the two adjacent types. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:large}
n_i\ge2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_i=d.
\end{equation}

These constraints are impossible for $d>6$. If every $n_i\ge2$, summing~\eqref{eq:large} gives $5d=2\sum_i n_i=4d+6$, so $d=6$. Otherwise rotate indices so that $n_0=1$.

If $n_1,n_4\ge2$, equation~\eqref{eq:large} gives $n_2=n_3=d-1$. The total in~\eqref{eq:types} forces $n_1+n_4=4$, so $n_1=n_4=2$. But then $b_2=n_1+n_3=d+1$, contradicting~\eqref{eq:b}.

We may therefore reflect indices and assume $n_0=n_1=1$. If $n_2,n_4\ge2$, equation~\eqref{eq:large} gives $n_3=d-1$. Now~\eqref{eq:types} forces $n_2+n_4=d+2$, contrary to $b_3\le d$.

Otherwise there are three consecutive singleton types; after relabeling, $n_0=n_1=n_2=1$. Then $n_3+n_4=2d$, while~\eqref{eq:b} at types 0 and 2 gives $n_4,n_3\le d-1$, another contradiction.

Equality in~\eqref{eq:oneedge} is impossible. Integrality improves the upper bound to $d^2+d+2$. No parity assumption was used. Attainment is proved in Section~\ref{sec:attain}.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:types} summarises the five types and the three constraints. The enumeration of Section~\ref{sec:types} checks by machine, for $d=7,\ldots,30$, that no tuple $(n_0,\ldots,n_4)$ with $n_0=1$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:types}--\eqref{eq:large}, which is the finite content of the case split above [MV]. The all-large case is excluded symbolically.

\subsection{The attaining construction}\label{sec:attain}

Put $p=d+1$. Start with the crown graph on classes $A=\{A_0,\ldots,A_{p-1}\}$ and $B=\{B_0,\ldots,B_{p-1}\}$: it contains $A_iB_j$ exactly when $i\ne j$. Delete $A_0B_1$ and $A_1B_0$, then add a vertex $v$ adjacent to $A_0,A_1,B_0,B_1$. Denote this graph by $Q_d$.

This is the $t=1$ case of BET's construction~\cite[Proposition 4.1]{BET}, written in crown notation: their graph deletes one perfect matching inside $d-1$ vertices of each side and all edges between the remaining two vertices of each side, which are the same edges [D]. It is prior work. The four neighbors of $v$ are independent, and $Q_d-v$ is bipartite, so $Q_d$ is triangle-free. Every old vertex has degree $d$ and $v$ has degree four. Consequently, for $d\ge7$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:attain}
|V(Q_d)|=2d+3,\qquad \Delta(Q_d)=d,\qquad
\e(Q_d)=d(d+1)-2+4=d^2+d+2.
\end{equation}
The edges $A_iB_{i+2\bmod p}$ form a perfect matching on the old vertices. Since $p\ge8$, they avoid the deleted diagonal and both further deleted edges. Thus $\nu(Q_d)\ge d+1$, and its order gives $\nu(Q_d)\le d+1$.
This proves attainment in Theorem~\ref{thm:order} and the lower bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The degrees, edge counts and matching witnesses are also checked by machine for $d=7,\ldots,30$ [MV] (Table~\ref{tab:graphs}).

\begin{remark}[The degree-six boundary]
The balanced blowup of $C_5$ with five parts of size three has order 15, degree six and $\boundaryEdges$ edges, exceeding $6^2+6+2=44$. Its matching number is at most seven by order. Thus neither theorem extends unchanged to $d=6$ [MV for the edge count].
\end{remark}

\section{Theory: arbitrary order through Tutte--Berge}\label{sec:tb}

We first need the adjacent odd-order bound, which agrees with Theorem~3.4 of~\cite{AEY}.
\begin{lemma}[D]\label{lem:smaller}
If $J$ is triangle-free on $2d+1$ vertices with $\Delta(J)\le d$, then $\e(J)\le d^2+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $J$ is bipartite, a smaller part has at most $d$ vertices, so $\e(J)\le d^2$. Otherwise~\eqref{eq:deficit} gives $D(J)\ge d-2$ and hence
$2\e(J)\le(2d+1)d-(d-2)=2d^2+2$.
\end{proof}

The Tutte--Berge formula~\cite{Berge,Goemans} states
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TB}
\nu(F)=\min_{S\subseteq V(F)}\frac{|V(F)|+|S|-o(F-S)}2,
\end{equation}
where $o(F-S)$ is the number of odd-order components of $F-S$. Choose a set $S$ attaining this minimum. Writing $t_C=\lfloor |V(C)|/2\rfloor$ for each component $C$ of $F-S$ gives the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
\nu(F)=|S|+\sum_C t_C.
\end{equation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $F$ be triangle-free, with $\Delta(F)\le d$ and $\nu(F)\le d+1$, and take $S$ as in~\eqref{eq:budget}. The number of edges incident to $S$ is at most $\sum_{x\in S}\deg_F(x)\le d|S|$. Edges inside $S$ are counted twice in the degree sum, which is harmless for this upper bound.

Every remaining edge belongs to one of the components $C$. If $C$ has even order $2t_C$, its degree bound gives $\e(C)\le dt_C$. For odd order $2t_C+1$, the bounds are
\[
\e(C)\le
\begin{cases}
t_C(t_C+1)\le dt_C,&t_C\le d-1,\\
dt_C+1,&t_C=d,\\
dt_C+2,&t_C=d+1.
\end{cases}
\]
The first line is the triangle case of Tur\'an's theorem (a triangle-free graph of order $2t_C+1$ has at most $t_C(t_C+1)$ edges)~\cite{Turan}; the second is Lemma~\ref{lem:smaller}; the third is Theorem~\ref{thm:order}. Formula~\eqref{eq:budget} ensures that $t_C\le d+1$ for every component and that at most one component has $t_C\ge d$, since $2d>d+1$. The total surplus over $d\sum_Ct_C$ is therefore at most two. Hence
\[
\e(F)\le d|S|+d\sum_Ct_C+2=d\nu(F)+2\le d(d+1)+2.
\]
The graph $Q_d$ attains this value by~\eqref{eq:attain}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The slice lies inside the conjectured range, D]\label{cor:Z}
For every $d\ge7$, $Z(d)\ge d+2$. In particular $d<d+1<Z(d)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~4.5 of~\cite{AEY}, $Z(d)\ge d+1$. Suppose $Z(d)=d+1$. By Definition~\ref{def:Z} there is a triangle-free (almost) $d$-regular factor-critical graph $\Gamma$ with $\nu(\Gamma)=d+1$, hence $2d+3$ vertices and maximum degree at most $d$. By~\eqref{eq:Zedges} it has $d(d+1)+\lfloor d/2\rfloor\ge d^2+d+3$ edges for $d\ge7$. This contradicts Theorem~\ref{thm:order}, which bounds such a graph by $d^2+d+2$.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:Z} uses only the existence statement in Definition~\ref{def:Z}, not any construction of~\cite{AEY}. It decides the three degrees $d=7,9,11$ of Table~\ref{tab:z}, so Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a statement about the range $d<m<Z(d)$ of~\eqref{eq:AEYconj} for every $d\ge7$: it proves the single level $i=d+1$ of that conjecture and no other. At $d=7$ the corollary gives $Z(7)\ge9$; AEY's Proposition~6.2 shows that Conjecture~6.1 would force $Z(7)\in\{8,9\}$ [V], so the conjecture would give $Z(7)=9$ [D, conditional on the conjecture].

\begin{remark}[A second reduction]
One may instead discard isolated vertices and select an edge-extremizer with the largest number of $d$-star components. AEY Corollary~3.5 makes every other component factor-critical, with matching size $t\ge d$ and order $2t+1$. At budget $d+1$ there is at most one such component. If its matching size is $d$, Lemma~\ref{lem:smaller} plus at most one additional star gives at most $d^2+d+1$ edges; if it is $d+1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:order} gives $d^2+d+2$. With only stars the bound is $d(d+1)$. This corroborates the upper bound. The direct Tutte--Berge proof does not rely on this extremal structural reduction.
\end{remark}

\section{A Bougard--Joret consequence}\label{sec:bj}

Let $f(n,\alpha,k)$ be the minimum number of edges in a $k$-connected graph of order $n$ and independence number exactly $\alpha$, in the notation of Bougard and Joret~\cite{BJ}. The following consequence concerns strict-interior parameters. Corollary~\ref{cor:bj} first brackets the value within one edge; Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact} then excludes the lower endpoint.

\begin{corollary}[One-edge bracket, D]\label{cor:bj}
For every integer $d\ge7$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
d^2+4d+1\le f(2d+3,2,d+2)\le d^2+4d+2.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $G$ has these parameters, then $F=\overline G$ is triangle-free. Since $\delta(G)\ge d+2$, it has $\Delta(F)\le d$. Theorem~\ref{thm:order} gives
\[
\e(G)=\binom{2d+3}{2}-\e(F)\ge d^2+4d+1.
\]

For the upper bound, start with the crown $K_{d+1,d+1}$ minus its diagonal matching. Subdivide one existing edge $ab$ by a new vertex $w$, and call the resulting graph $P_d$. Its old vertices have degree $d$ and $w$ has degree two. It is triangle-free: $P_d-w$ is bipartite and $a,b$ are nonadjacent. It has an edge, so $G_d=\overline{P_d}$ has independence number exactly two. Moreover,
\[
\e(P_d)=d(d+1)+1,\qquad \e(G_d)=d^2+4d+2.
\]

It remains to prove $(d+2)$-connectivity of $G_d$. A smaller vertex cut would leave at least $d+2$ vertices partitioned into two nonempty sets with no $G_d$ edges between them. Thus $P_d$ would contain a complete bipartite subgraph on at least $d+2$ vertices.

If that subgraph excludes $w$, its sides lie in opposite original crown classes. The sets of matched indices used by the two sides are disjoint because diagonal edges are missing. Its total order is therefore at most $d+1$.
If it includes $w$, its opposite side is contained in $\{a,b\}$. An opposite side of size one gives a star of order at most $\Delta(P_d)+1=d+1$. If the opposite side is $\{a,b\}$, no old vertex can join $w$ in its own side: an old vertex cannot be adjacent to both original classes in the bipartite graph $P_d-w$. This subgraph has order only three. All cases contradict $d+2$ surviving vertices. Therefore $G_d$ is $(d+2)$-connected; its minimum degree also shows that its connectivity is exactly $d+2$.
\end{proof}

These parameters satisfy $2d+3>2+(d+2)$ and $2d+3<2(d+2)$, placing them strictly inside the originally proposed first regime. The excess of the lower endpoint over its degree-sum prediction is
\[
(d^2+4d+1)-\left\lceil\frac{(2d+3)(d+2)}2\right\rceil
=\left\lfloor\frac d2\right\rfloor-2.
\]
This is positive for $d\ge7$ and unbounded. Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact} shows that the upper endpoint in~\eqref{eq:bracket} is the value, so the excess is $\lfloor d/2\rfloor-1$; for example $f(19,2,10)=98$, whereas the degree-sum prediction is $95$, a difference of $\excessAtNineteen$. Figure~\ref{fig:excess} plots both excesses for $d=7,\ldots,30$ from the certificate data: the lower endpoint would lie $\lfloor d/2\rfloor-2$ above the prediction, the exact value lies one edge higher, and both grow without bound.

\paragraph{A worked example at $d=7$.} Take $n=17$, $\alpha=2$ and $k=9$. The complement of $Q_7$ has $\binom{17}{2}-58=78$ edges and minimum degree $16-7=9$, which is the lower endpoint $d^2+4d+1=78$ of~\eqref{eq:bracket}; but its connectivity is $8=d+1$ (Table~\ref{tab:graphs}), because $A_0$ and $A_1$ have the same seven neighbours in $Q_7$ so deleting the other eight vertices leaves them separated from the common neighbourhood in the complement. The complement of $P_7$ has $136-57=79$ edges, independence number two and connectivity $9=d+2$, so it is a valid graph and $f(17,2,9)\le79$. Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact} states that no graph with $78$ edges is valid, and the degree-sum prediction $\lceil17\cdot9/2\rceil=77$ lies two edges below the value; the excluded endpoint $78$ would have been one edge above it. The same computation for $d=8$ gives $f(19,2,10)=98$, with the prediction $95$.

\subsection{The exact value}\label{sec:exact}

By Corollary~\ref{cor:bj} and complementation, Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact} reduces to a structural statement. A complement $\overline F$ on $2d+3$ vertices fails to be $(d+2)$-connected exactly when $F$ contains a complete bipartite subgraph $K_{U,W}$, with $U,W$ nonempty and every $U$--$W$ pair an edge, on at least $d+2$ vertices: deleting the other at most $d+1$ vertices leaves $U$ and $W$ with no edge of $\overline F$ between them.

\begin{theorem}[Shared neighbourhood, D]\label{thm:k2d}
Let $d\ge7$ and let $F$ be triangle-free on $n=2d+3$ vertices with $\Delta(F)\le d$ and $\e(F)=d^2+d+2$. Then two vertices of $F$ have the same $d$ neighbours; in particular $F\supseteq K_{2,d}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact} from Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d}]
A graph $G$ of order $2d+3$ with independence number two, connectivity at least $d+2$ and $d^2+4d+1$ edges would have a triangle-free complement $F$ with $\Delta(F)\le d$ and $\e(F)=d^2+d+2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} gives a $K_{2,d}$ in $F$, on $d+2$ vertices, hence a vertex cut of $G$ of size $d+1$. Fewer edges are excluded by Corollary~\ref{cor:bj}, whose construction $G_d$ attains $d^2+4d+2$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Structure.} Let $F$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d}. Its deficit is $D(F)=nd-2\e(F)=d-4$. It is not bipartite, since a bipartite $F$ has at most $d(d+1)$ edges. A shortest odd cycle has length five: length at least seven would give $D(F)\ge7d-2n=3d-6>d-4$ by~\eqref{eq:deficit}. Write it $c_0\cdots c_4$, indices modulo five. For an outside vertex $x$ let $\sigma(x)=2-|N(x)\cap C|\ge0$, and put $s=\sum_{x\notin C}\sigma(x)$ and $u=\sum_{x\notin C}(d-\deg x)$. Since the cycle is chordless, $\sum_{v\in C}\deg v=10+2(n-5)-s$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ab}
\sum_{v\in C}(d-\deg v)=d-6+s,\qquad D(F)=d-6+s+u,\qquad s+u=2 .
\end{equation}
As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:order}, outside vertices with two cycle neighbours fall into types: $V_i$ consists of $c_i$ and the outside vertices adjacent to $c_{i-1},c_{i+1}$; put $n_i=|V_i|$. Each $V_i$ is independent and there is no edge between $V_i$ and $V_{i\pm2}$. The remaining outside vertices form the set $X$ of \emph{defective} vertices, those with at most one cycle neighbour; each contributes at least one to $s$, so $r:=|X|\le2$ and $\sum_in_i=2d+3-r$. Put $W_i=V_{i-1}\cup V_{i+1}$, $b_i=|W_i|$ and $\varepsilon_i=|N(c_i)\cap X|$. The vertex $c_i$ is adjacent to all of $W_i$ and to no other typed vertex, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ci}
\deg c_i=b_i+\varepsilon_i\le d .
\end{equation}
A defective vertex adjacent to $c_j$ has no neighbour in $V_{j\pm1}$, by triangle-freeness.

\begin{lemma}[Helpers, D]\label{lem:helper}
Let $v\in V_i$ be an outside vertex of degree $d$. If every neighbour of $v$ in $X$ is adjacent to $c_i$, then $N(v)\subseteq N(c_i)$, and $\{c_i,v\}$ together with $N(v)$ span a $K_{2,d}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The typed neighbours of $v$ lie in consecutive types, so $N(v)\subseteq W_i\cup X$; both parts lie in $N(c_i)$, the second by hypothesis, and $c_i\notin N(v)$ because $V_i$ is independent.
\end{proof}

Call $x\in X$ a \emph{helper} of an outside $v\in V_i$ if $x\sim v$ and $x\not\sim c_i$. Assume, for contradiction, that no two vertices of $F$ share $d$ neighbours. Then every outside typed vertex of degree $d$ has a helper. There are $2d-2-r$ outside typed vertices.

\paragraph{At most one defective vertex.} If $r=0$, then $s=0$ and $u=2$, so at most two of the $2d-2\ge12$ outside typed vertices lack degree $d$, and none has a helper. If $X=\{x\}$ and $x$ has no cycle neighbour, then $u=0$ and $x$ must be adjacent to all $2d-3>d$ outside typed vertices. If $x$ has one cycle neighbour, then $s=u=1$ and $x$ is adjacent to at least $2d-4$ outside typed vertices besides its cycle neighbour, so $\deg x\ge2d-3>d$.

\paragraph{Two defective vertices.} Now $s=2$, $u=0$: every outside vertex has degree exactly $d$, $X=\{x_1,x_2\}$ with $x_1\sim c_p$ and $x_2\sim c_q$ as their only cycle neighbours, $\sum_in_i=2d+1$ and $\sum_i\varepsilon_i=2$. The vertex $x_1$ helps only vertices of $V_{p+2}\cup V_{p+3}$, and $x_2$ only vertices of $V_{q+2}\cup V_{q+3}$; a type with no possible helper is a singleton. For an outside $v\in V_i$ let $\xi(v)=|N(v)\cap X|$; then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi}
|N(v)\cap W_i|=d-\xi(v).
\end{equation}
\emph{Rule (T):} if some $x\in X$ is adjacent to $u'\in V_i$ and $w\in V_{i+1}$, then $u'\not\sim w$. Up to rotation and reflection, $q-p\in\{0,1,2\}$: the $25$ ordered pairs $(p,q)\in\mathbb Z_5^2$ fall into five classes under rotation, indexed by $q-p\in\mathbb Z_5$, and a reflection of the cycle sends $q-p$ to $p-q$, which identifies the classes $1,4$ and $2,3$ and leaves the three classes $0,1,2$ [D].

\emph{Case $q=p$.} Only $V_{p+2},V_{p+3}$ can be helped, so $n_{p-1}=n_p=n_{p+1}=1$ and $n_{p+2}+n_{p+3}=2d+1-3=2d-2$. Since $x_1,x_2$ are adjacent only to $c_p$, $\varepsilon_{p\pm1}=0$, and~\eqref{eq:ci} at $c_{p+1}$ and $c_{p-1}$ gives $1+n_{p+2}\le d$ and $1+n_{p+3}\le d$. Two upper bounds of $d-1$ on two numbers that sum to $2d-2$ force $n_{p+2}=n_{p+3}=d-1$. Let $A^\circ,B^\circ$ be the outside vertices of $V_{p+2},V_{p+3}$ ($d-2$ each). By~\eqref{eq:xi} an outside vertex of $V_{p+2}$ misses exactly $\xi\le2$ vertices of $V_{p+3}$, and symmetrically. If $x_k$ had neighbours on both sides, Rule (T) would bound its typed outside neighbours by four, while $\deg x_k=d$ forces at least $d-2\ge5$. So $x_1$ covers $A^\circ$ and $x_2$ covers $B^\circ$, say, and $d=\deg x_1\le1+(d-2)+[x_1\sim x_2]$ forces $x_1\sim x_2$, a triangle with $c_p$.

\emph{Case $q=p+1$.} Here $n_p=n_{p+1}=1$ and $\varepsilon_p=\varepsilon_{p+1}=1$, so $n_{p+2}+n_{p+3}+n_{p+4}=2d-1$. Inequality~\eqref{eq:ci} at $c_p,c_{p+1},c_{p+2},c_{p+3}$ gives $n_{p+4}\le d-2$, $n_{p+2}\le d-2$, $n_{p+3}\le d-1$ and $n_{p+2}+n_{p+4}\le d$; with the sum this forces $n_{p+3}=d-1$, $n_{p+2}+n_{p+4}=d$, and both $n_{p+2},n_{p+4}\ge2$. An outside vertex of $V_{p+2}$ has $x_1$ as its only possible neighbour in $X$, so $\xi=1$ and by~\eqref{eq:xi} it misses exactly one vertex of $V_{p+3}$. As all outside vertices of $V_{p+2}$ are adjacent to $x_1$, Rule (T) lets $x_1$ have at most one neighbour among the $d-2\ge5$ outside vertices of $V_{p+3}$; likewise $x_2$. Those vertices cannot all be helped.

\emph{Case $q=p+2$.} Now $n_{p+1}=1$ and $\varepsilon_p=\varepsilon_{p+2}=1$. Write $\lambda_0,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,\lambda_4$ for $n_p,n_{p+2},n_{p+3},n_{p+4}$, so $\lambda_0+\lambda_2+\lambda_3+\lambda_4=2d$, and~\eqref{eq:ci} gives $\lambda_4\le d-2$, $\lambda_0+\lambda_2\le d$, $\lambda_3\le d-2$, $\lambda_2+\lambda_4\le d$, $\lambda_3+\lambda_0\le d$; hence $\lambda_3,\lambda_4\ge2$. Outside vertices of $V_{p+3}$ (respectively $V_{p+4}$) have $x_1$ (respectively $x_2$) as their only neighbour in $X$, so each has $d-1$ neighbours in $V_{p+2}\cup V_{p+4}$ (respectively $V_{p+3}\cup V_p$). If $\lambda_2=1$ this forces $\lambda_4=d-2$ and then $\lambda_0+\lambda_3=d+1$, a contradiction. If $\lambda_2\ge2$, Rule (T) at $x_1$ separates the outside vertices of $V_{p+2}$ and $V_{p+3}$, so $\lambda_4=d-2$. If moreover $\lambda_0=1$, then $\lambda_3=d-2$ and $\lambda_2=3$, contradicting $\lambda_2+\lambda_4\le d$. If $\lambda_0\ge2$, Rule (T) at $x_2$ gives $\lambda_3=d-2$, hence $\lambda_0=\lambda_2=2$, and the single outside vertex of $V_{p+2}$ has degree at most $|\{c_{p+1},c_{p+3}\}|+|X|=4<d$.

Every case is contradictory, which proves Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d}. \qed

\paragraph{Where the hypothesis $d\ge7$ is used.} The identity $s+u=2$ of~\eqref{eq:ab} holds for every $d$, so the hypothesis is not needed there; it enters later [D]. In the case $r=0$, the proof needs more than two outside typed vertices, and there are $2d-2\ge12$. In the case $q=p$, the number $d-2\ge5$ of typed neighbours that $x_k$ must have exceeds the bound four given by Rule (T) exactly when $d\ge7$; this is the sharpest use of the hypothesis, because at $d=6$ the two quantities are equal. In the cases $q=p+1$ and $q=p+2$, the inequalities $d-2\ge5>1$ and $4<d$ are used, and the latter needs only $d\ge5$. The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} therefore does not extend to $d=6$ as written, and whether the statement itself holds at $d=6$ is not decided here. The fixed-order Theorem~\ref{thm:order} is false at $d=6$, by the balanced $C_5$ blow-up of Section~\ref{sec:attain}.

Section~\ref{sec:valid} states how this proof was reviewed and which finite computations corroborate it (Table~\ref{tab:sat}); the universal claim rests on the proof.

\subsection{The rejected complement shortcut}
Although $Q_d$ attains the triangle-free maximum, its complement fails the connectivity required above. Indeed,
\[
N_{Q_d}(A_0)=N_{Q_d}(A_1)=\{v,B_2,\ldots,B_d\}.
\]
This gives a $K_{2,d}$ on $d+2$ vertices. Deleting the other $d+1$ vertices disconnects the complement, so $\kappa(\overline{Q_d})\le d+1$.

In fact equality holds. A cut of size at most $d$ would leave a complete bipartite subgraph of $Q_d$ on at least $d+3$ vertices. Without $v$, its order is at most $d+1$ by the crown argument. With $v$ in one side, the other side is contained in $\{A_0,A_1,B_0,B_1\}$. If it uses both original classes, no old vertex can join $v$ in its side, and total order is at most five. If it uses only one original class, it has size at most two, and the degree bound at an opposite vertex bounds the side containing $v$ by $d$. Its total order is at most $d+2$. Thus
\[
\kappa(\overline{Q_d})=d+1.
\]
The shortcut fails because extremality under a degree bound does not supply the needed complement connectivity. Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} shows that the failure is forced: every fixed-order maximizer has two vertices with a common $d$-neighbourhood, as $A_0,A_1$ do in $Q_d$. The values $\kappa(\overline{Q_d})=d+1$ and $\kappa(\overline{P_d})=d+2$ are confirmed by machine for $d=7,\ldots,30$ [MV] (Table~\ref{tab:graphs}).

\subsection{The five-type equality enumeration}\label{sec:types}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:types} is the finite content of the case split in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:order}. An equality graph determines a composition $(n_0,\ldots,n_4)$ of $2d+3$ into five positive parts satisfying~\eqref{eq:b} and~\eqref{eq:large}. Since the all-large case is excluded symbolically, one part equals one and, rotating indices, $n_0=1$. The remaining four parts are a composition of $2d+2$ into four positive parts, which a choice of three cut points among $2d+1$ positions encodes, so there are $\binom{2d+1}{3}$ tuples per degree.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Five-type equality enumeration with a designated singleton.}\label{alg:types}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require degree bound $d$
\Ensure the set of surviving tuples (expected empty)
\State $\mathcal S\gets\emptyset$
\For{each triple of cut points $1\le a<b<c\le 2d+1$}
  \State $(n_0,\ldots,n_4)\gets(1,\;a,\;b-a,\;c-b,\;2d+2-c)$
  \For{$i=0,\ldots,4$} $b_i\gets n_{i-1}+n_{i+1}$ (indices modulo five) \EndFor
  \If{$b_i\le d$ for all $i$ \textbf{and} ($n_i=1$ \textbf{or} $b_i=d$) for all $i$}
     \State add $(n_0,\ldots,n_4)$ to $\mathcal S$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathcal S$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The loop of Algorithm~\ref{alg:types} runs $\binom{2d+1}{3}$ times with constant work per iteration, so it takes $\Theta(d^3)$ time per degree. Over $d=7,\ldots,30$ it examines $\sum_d\binom{2d+1}{3}=\nCandidates$ tuples and the artifact records no survivor at any degree [MV]. The independent audit repeats the test without designating a singleton: for $d=7,\ldots,10$ it enumerates all $\binom{2d+2}{4}$ positive compositions, builds the quotient $C_5$ with NetworkX, computes the quotient degrees $b_i$, and applies the same two conditions to the compositions containing a part equal to one; in total $\nAllComp$ compositions, again with no survivor [MV].

\subsection{The certificate checks}\label{sec:cert}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:cert} checks, for one degree $d$, the two explicit graphs of the paper: $Q_d$ (a maximizer with a shared neighbourhood) and $P_d$ (the subdivided crown whose complement attains the Bougard--Joret value). Graphs are stored as bit-mask adjacency rows. The triangle test uses that an edge $uv$ lies in a triangle exactly when the rows of $u$ and $v$ intersect.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Certificate checks for one degree (standard library only).}\label{alg:cert}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $d$; $n\gets2d+3$, $p\gets d+1$, $k\gets d+2$
\For{$\mathrm{kind}\in\{Q_d,P_d\}$}
  \State build the crown on $A,B$ and apply the edits of the kind; add the extra vertex $v$
  \State assert the edge count $d^2+d+2$ (for $Q_d$) or $d^2+d+1$ (for $P_d$)
  \State assert no edge $uv$ has a common neighbour (triangle-free)
  \State assert the degree sequence $[d,\ldots,d,4]$ for $Q_d$ or $[d,\ldots,d,2]$ for $P_d$
  \State assert that $\{A_iB_{(i+2)\bmod p}\}$ is a matching of size $p$ among the edges
  \State $H\gets\overline{F}$; \textbf{if} $d\le18$: assert $\alpha(H)=2$ by memoised branching on bit-masks
  \State \textbf{if} $d\le18$: assert (all non-adjacent pairs of $H$ have $k$ internally disjoint paths) $=$ ($\mathrm{kind}=P_d$), by a vertex-split unit-capacity maximum flow stopped at $k$
  \If{$\mathrm{kind}=Q_d$}
     \State $N\gets N(A_0)\cap N(A_1)$; assert $|N|=d$; let $R\gets N\cup\{A_0,A_1\}$
     \State assert $H[R]$ is disconnected; record the cut $V\setminus R$ of size $d+1$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State enumerate the five-type tuples with Algorithm~\ref{alg:types}; assert none survives
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

By Menger's theorem the flow test certifies $\kappa(H)\ge k$ exactly when every non-adjacent pair has $k$ internally disjoint paths. There are at most $\e(F)=O(d^2)$ such pairs, each needing at most $k=d+2$ breadth-first augmentations over a $2n\times2n$ capacity matrix of $O(d^2)$ entries, which bounds the work by $O(d^5)$ elementary steps per graph up to constants [D]. The cost of the memoised independence-number routine is not analysed here; it finished within the five-minute stage budget of the script. The flow and independence checks are limited to $d\le18$ because of this cost, which gives $\nFlow$ graphs with direct flow checks; the degree, edge, triangle, matching and cut checks cover all $\nGraphs$ graphs. The script also checks two controls: the balanced $C_5$ blow-up at $d=6$ (it satisfies the equality constraints and has $\boundaryEdges>44$ edges) and a copy of $Q_7$ with the edge $A_0A_1$ added, which must contain a triangle.

\subsection{The independent audit}\label{sec:audit}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:audit} reconstructs every saved graph from its edge list in NetworkX without importing any routine of the certificate generator, and recomputes each property with the library's own algorithms. The two implementations share no code: the generator tests triangles with bit-mask intersections, bounds the matching number by a witness plus the order, and certifies connectivity with its own vertex-split flow; the audit counts triangles with the library, computes a maximum-cardinality matching with a blossom algorithm, obtains the independence number of the complement from maximal cliques, and takes the node connectivity from the library. Agreement therefore tests the saved edge lists and the generator's checks against a second reading of the same definitions, which is stronger than a rerun of the generator. The cost is dominated by the library's node-connectivity routine on the $48$ complements, which have up to $2\cdot30+3=63$ vertices; the record gives about six seconds for the whole audit. Before any graph is read, the audit asserts that each source hash recorded in the certificate equals the hash of the file on disk, so a changed generator or base module would be detected, and it finishes by writing the LF-normalised hashes of the files it examined (Table~\ref{tab:records}).

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Independent NetworkX audit of the saved certificate.}\label{alg:audit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the saved certificate; assert NetworkX $=\nxVersion$ and that each recorded source hash equals the hash of the file
\For{each degree $d=7,\ldots,30$ and each saved graph $F$}
  \State rebuild $F$; assert zero triangles, the degree sequence, the edge count and the stored degrees
  \State assert $\nu(F)=d+1$ by a maximum-cardinality blossom matching
  \State $H\gets\overline F$; assert $\alpha(H)=2$ as the largest maximal clique of $\overline H=F$
  \State $\kappa\gets$ node connectivity of $H$; assert $\kappa=d+2$ for $P_d$ and $\kappa<d+2$ for $Q_d$
  \State for $Q_d$, assert the stored cut has $d+1$ distinct vertices and disconnects $H$
\EndFor
\State rebuild the $d=6$ control and the corrupted $Q_7$; assert both behave as in Algorithm~\ref{alg:cert}
\State enumerate all positive compositions for $d=7,\ldots,10$ as in Section~\ref{sec:types}
\State write the report with the LF-normalised hashes of the audited files
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{The configuration-split satisfiability search}\label{sec:satenc}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} fixes an induced five-cycle $c_0\cdots c_4$, types the outside vertices and allows at most two defective vertices. The search of EXP-004 uses exactly this structure and asks the solver for an extremal graph in which no two vertices share $d$ neighbours. A \emph{configuration} fixes the cycle neighbours of the defective vertices up to rotation and reflection of the cycle: \texttt{none} (no defective vertex), \texttt{-} (one defective vertex without cycle neighbour), \texttt{0} (one at $c_0$), \texttt{0,0}, \texttt{0,1}, \texttt{0,2} (two, adjacent to $c_0$ and $c_q$ with $q\in\{0,1,2\}$, since $q$ and $-q$ are exchanged by reflection) and \texttt{-,0}, \texttt{-,-} (two, one or both without cycle neighbour). Figure~\ref{fig:config} sketches the three placements of two defective vertices on the cycle.

\label{fig:config}

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Configuration-split satisfiability search at fixed $d$.}\label{alg:sat}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $d$, a configuration with $r\le2$ defective vertices, a mode (\textsc{k2d} or \textsc{free}), a time limit
\State $n\gets2d+3$; Boolean variable $e_{ij}$ for each of the $\binom n2$ pairs
\State fix $e_{ij}$ on vertices $0,\ldots,4$ to the induced five-cycle; vertices $5,\ldots,4+r$ are defective with the cycle neighbourhood of the configuration
\For{each remaining (typed) vertex $v$}
  \State one-hot Boolean $t_{v,0..4}$; if $t_{v,t}$ then $e_{vj}$ holds exactly for $j\in\{c_{t-1},c_{t+1}\}$
\EndFor
\State types are non-decreasing along the typed vertices (symmetry breaking)
\State for each triple $i<j<k$: $\neg(e_{ij}\wedge e_{jk}\wedge e_{ik})$
\State for each vertex $v$: $\sum_ue_{vu}\le d$;\quad $\sum_{i<j}e_{ij}=d^2+d+2$
\If{mode $=$ \textsc{k2d}}
  \State for each pair $u<v$: $\sum_{w}(e_{uw}\wedge e_{vw})\le d-1$
\EndIf
\State run the solver under the limit; \Return \textsc{sat} (with the model), \textsc{unsat} or \textsc{unknown}, and the elapsed time
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

At $d=7$ ($n=17$) the encoding has $\binom{17}2=136$ edge variables, $\binom{17}3=680$ triangle clauses and, in \textsc{k2d} mode, $136$ pair constraints over $15$ terms each; at $d=8$ ($n=19$) it has $171$, $969$ and $171$ constraints over $17$ terms [D, from the formulas]. In \textsc{k2d} mode, \textsc{unsat} for a configuration means that no extremal graph of that configuration avoids two vertices with $d$ common neighbours, which is the statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} restricted to the configuration. In \textsc{free} mode, \textsc{sat} shows that the encoding admits extremal graphs, and every model is re-checked by \texttt{check\_models.py} with NetworkX, independently of the solver: order $2d+3$, no triangle, maximum degree at most $d$, exactly $d^2+d+2$ edges, a pair with $d$ common neighbours, and a complement connectivity at most $d+1$.

The identity~\eqref{eq:ab} excludes two of the eight configurations without any search. A defective vertex with no cycle neighbour has $\sigma=2$, a vertex adjacent to one cycle vertex has $\sigma=1$, and $s+u=2$. Hence \texttt{-,-} would give $s\ge4$ and \texttt{-,0} would give $s\ge3$, both impossible [D]. The search nevertheless runs them, because their \textsc{unsat} answers test the encoding.

\section{Ablation: what each ingredient contributes}\label{sec:ablation}

The proofs combine four ingredients, and removing any one weakens the conclusion by a computable amount. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} states the bound that remains in each case. The deficit bound alone is the shortest-odd-cycle inequality~\eqref{eq:deficit}; it gives $\e(F)\le d^2+d+3$ on $2d+3$ vertices, one more than the truth, and the same Tutte--Berge reduction would then give $T(d,d+1)\le d^2+d+3$ and, by complementation, $f(2d+3,2,d+2)\ge d^2+4d$ [D]. The five-type obstruction removes the last edge and is therefore the whole difference between the bound $d^2+d+3$ and the value $d^2+d+2$. The Tutte--Berge reduction is what lifts the fixed-order statement to every order: without it the theorem concerns only graphs of order $2d+3$. The shared-neighbourhood Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} is what moves the Bougard--Joret lower bound from $d^2+4d+1$ to $d^2+4d+2$; without it the value is known only to within one edge.

Without the clauses forbidding two vertices with $d$ common neighbours, the search is satisfiable in three configurations, and each model has the shared neighbourhood and the complement connectivity at most $d+1$ that the theorem predicts (Table~\ref{tab:models}); with the clauses, every finished run is \textsc{unsat}. The clauses are therefore what separates extremal graphs from the absence of extremal graphs in the encoding, which is the behaviour the theorem requires. No ablation of the symmetry-breaking clause was recorded [U], and no ablation of the type-ordering constraint either [U]. The deficit bound is the only ingredient used by every proof, so it is the one whose failure would propagate everywhere. The ablation is exact rather than statistical: each row of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} is a deterministic bound or a single archived run, so no confidence interval applies, and repetitions of the search with different solver seeds were not recorded [U]. The free model with the largest number of shared-neighbourhood pairs ($18$ pairs at $d=7$, configuration \texttt{none}) and the models with two pairs show that the encoding admits both a highly symmetric extremal graph and graphs with a minimal shared neighbourhood, so the unsatisfiability under the clauses is not an artefact of a degenerate model space.

\section{Validation, scope and deviations}\label{sec:valid}

\subsection{What each check covers}

The universal statements are Theorems~\ref{thm:main}, \ref{thm:bjexact} and~\ref{thm:k2d}, with Corollary~\ref{cor:Z}; they rest on the proofs of Sections~\ref{sec:fixed} to~\ref{sec:bj}. The computations cover finite parts.
\begin{itemize}
\item Algorithms~\ref{alg:types} and~\ref{alg:cert} and the audit of Algorithm~\ref{alg:audit} cover degrees $7$ to $30$ (and $7$ to $10$ for the full composition enumeration). They verify properties of explicit graphs and a finite constraint system, not an optimum over all graphs.
\item Algorithm~\ref{alg:sat} covers $d=7$ and $d=8$ and assumes the structure facts of Section~\ref{sec:exact} (an induced five-cycle, the types, at most two defective vertices). Those facts are parts of the proof, so the search corroborates the final case analysis and the contradiction, not the structure itself.
\item The proofs were read in full by an internal reader other than the author, who re-derived every case of Theorem~\ref{thm:order}, of the Tutte--Berge reduction and of Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d}, including the reduction of the $25$ ordered pairs $(p,q)$ to three cases, and found no gap; three wording points were corrected. This is an internal review, not peer review and not a formal proof. The reader also ran its own type-size enumeration for $d=7,\ldots,12$, a separate z3 encoding at $d=7$ and checks of the attaining graphs for $d=7,\ldots,10$. Those scripts were not archived, so their outputs are not used as evidence here and are mentioned only to state the scope of that review.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deviation: the global lazy-cut search}

The first search planned for Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} was a global one: for $d=7$ and $d=8$, ask the solver for a triangle-free graph of maximum degree at most $d$ with at least $d^2+d+2$ edges and a fixed five-cycle whose complement is $(d+2)$-connected, and add a cut constraint lazily for each model whose complement had a small vertex cut. That search did not finish. At $d=7$ the known-answer gate (target $d^2+d+1$, where such graphs exist) and the search itself, with and without the clauses forbidding $K_{2,d}$, produced no output within their caps (50 minutes without the clauses, two hours with them; the process was stopped by its time limit, exit status 124). The configuration split of Section~\ref{sec:satenc} replaced it, and its results (Table~\ref{tab:sat}) are the only satisfiability evidence reported here. It is a different encoding with the same target, which exploits the structure facts that the proof establishes, and it affects only the finite corroboration: no result of this paper depends on the global search.

\paragraph{Residual risks.}

A missed case in the symbolic case split, or a misapplied matching reduction, would invalidate Theorem~\ref{thm:main} even though all finite checks pass; the finite checks cannot rule out a gap in an all-parameter argument. The proofs are not formalised in a proof assistant. The bibliographic search cannot guarantee priority. The solver, Python and NetworkX versions for EXP-004 are not recorded [U], and the search timings depend on hardware that is not recorded [U]; the timings are therefore comparative within the archived runs only.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:disc}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} settles one level of the matching-number range of Conjecture~6.1 of~\cite{AEY}: $m=d+1$ for every $d\ge7$. Corollary~\ref{cor:Z} places that level inside the range $d<m<Z(d)$ for every $d\ge7$, including the three odd degrees that the proved bounds on $Z(d)$ leave open. It does not settle the intermediate levels $d+2\le m<Z(d)$ (the integer-programming values of~\cite{BET} for $d\le10$ remain the only evidence there), the levels $m\ge2d$, or the exact value of $Z(d)$ for odd $d$. It does not classify the extremal graphs: Theorem~\ref{thm:k2d} gives only the shared-neighbourhood property.

For the Bougard--Joret function, Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact} determines $f(2d+3,2,d+2)$ for every $d\ge7$, a family of parameters with $\alpha=2$ strictly inside the first regime. The excess over the degree-sum prediction is $\lfloor d/2\rfloor-1$ and unbounded (Figure~\ref{fig:excess}). The general revised first regime is not determined by it. The second regime $n>k\alpha$ is a different range of parameters: the paper~\cite{SR} checks the conjectured value exhaustively there for the open triples with $n\le24$, and a proof of the lower bound has been announced~\cite{Didin} [U]; neither bears on the parameters of Theorem~\ref{thm:bjexact}.

The numbers show two further features. First, the level $m=d+1$ is where the triangle-free value separates from the general one by $2\lfloor d/2\rfloor-2$ edges, so forbidding triangles costs more as $d$ grows; the extremal graph $Q_d$ is a crown with one extra vertex of degree four and has total degree deficit $d-4$ [D]. Second, the Bougard--Joret excess $\lfloor d/2\rfloor-1$ over the degree-sum prediction grows linearly in $d$, so at the parameters $(2d+3,2,d+2)$ the discrepancy from that prediction is unbounded and no fixed additive correction removes it [D]. For the threshold of AEY, Corollary~\ref{cor:Z} gives a lower bound only; the upper bounds $\lfloor5(d+1)/4\rfloor$ for odd $d$ are untouched, so the exact value of $Z(d)$ for odd $d\le19$ remains undetermined by the published results and by this paper, and Conjecture~6.5 of~\cite{AEY} is neither supported nor contradicted here.

Three limitations bound the claims, and a reader who wants to rely on a single numerical value should use $T(13,14)=184$ only through the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, with Table~\ref{tab:bet} as a consistency check.

The limitations are as follows. First, the computations are finite and the proofs are not formalised. Second, the attaining graph and the values through $d=12$ are prior work; the new content is the uniform upper bound, the deduction about $Z(d)$ and the exact Bougard--Joret value. Third, the searches for prior solutions of the slice are bounded and may have missed a source.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:concl}

For every $d\ge7$, a triangle-free graph with maximum degree at most $d$ and matching number at most $d+1$ has at most $d^2+d+2$ edges, and $Q_d$ attains it; hence $T(13,14)=184$. The proof combines a deficit bound along a shortest odd cycle, a five-type obstruction on $2d+3$ vertices and the Tutte--Berge formula. The bound forces $Z(d)\ge d+2$, so the level lies inside the conjectured range for all $d\ge7$. Complementation gives $f(2d+3,2,d+2)=d^2+4d+2$, and the lower value is excluded because every extremal graph has two vertices with the same $d$ neighbours. The ablation of Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows that each ingredient carries one statement. The exact computations back these statements for $d$ up to $30$ (explicit graphs and a finite constraint system) and for $d=7,8$ (the configuration-split search). Taken together, the proofs, the exact verification and the documented limits give a complete account of the level $m=d+1$ and of one family of Bougard--Joret parameters. The next steps are the levels $d+2\le m<Z(d)$, the classification of the extremal graphs, an archived solver version and hardware record for the search, a satisfiability check at $d=9$ with a stronger encoding, and a formal proof of Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:k2d}.
