# JCB-102: three recent papers read into context (2026-11-07)

Files archived in the data root under `caos-research/jacobian-conjecture/sources/jcb102/` (arXiv PDFs and their text
extraction; the PDFs are the arXiv v1 files).
Marks: [V] read in the primary text.

## Long, arXiv:2608.23777v1 (2026-07-22), "An Explicit Counterexample to the Rank-Two Poisson Conjecture" [V]

Explicit polynomials R, T, D, S in Q[x, q, p, z] with {D, R} = {S, T} = 1 and the other brackets zero, and
R = x(2 - 3xq) reducible, so (x, q, p, z) -> (R, T, D, S) is a Poisson endomorphism of P_2 that is not an
automorphism: the Poisson conjecture fails for two canonical pairs and every larger number. The map preserves the
symplectic form on A^4, has Jacobian one and a fiber of exactly three points. It is built from Alpoge's
counterexample to JC(3) (announced 2026-07-19, found with an AI model). An appendix gives a non-automorphic
endomorphism of the fourth Weyl algebra.
Relevance to our planar programme: none direct. It concerns dimension >= 4 (two canonical pairs); JC(2) and the
planar (72,108) frontier are untouched.

## van den Essen, arXiv:2609.17795v1 (2026-09-15), "An elementary way to find a counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture" [V]

An elementary search for a three-variable Keller map F = (a + bz, c + dz, x + x^2 y + x^3 z) with a, b, c, d in
C[x, y], guided by the reducible third component (a reducible coordinate cannot belong to an automorphism); it
recovers Alpoge's map up to linear changes. It recalls Magnus's theorem: a planar Keller map (f, g) with deg f or
deg g prime is invertible, which is why x + x^2 y cannot start a planar counterexample.
Relevance: none to the planar frontier beyond the classical Magnus criterion (already known to the programme).

## Kanel-Belov and Fedorov, arXiv:2610.33919v1 (2026-10-24), "The generalized Dixmier conjecture and the two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture" [V]

Theorem 1.6: (i) JC(2) implies the generalized Dixmier conjecture DC_1' for the first Weyl algebra (every
endomorphism of Mat_2(A_1) is invertible); (ii) assuming their Conjecture 1.5 (existence of a holonomic A_2-module
lift with constant arithmetic support of multiplicity one for every Jacobian-one endomorphism of A^2), DC_1
implies JC(2). They prove such a lift is unique when it exists (Proposition 3.4), give counterexamples to a
Kontsevich conjecture on holonomic D-modules with prescribed arithmetic support for every n >= 3 (from Long's
map), and state that Theorem 1.7 and Corollary 1.9 of Dodd's preprint need additional assumptions. No counterexample
to DC_2 or JC(2) is announced.
Relevance: the planar conjecture stays open; the paper offers an algebraic (Weyl-algebra) reformulation route, not
a degree-frontier result; nothing in it bears on the (72,108) exclusion or our Case 1 certificate (JCB-103).
