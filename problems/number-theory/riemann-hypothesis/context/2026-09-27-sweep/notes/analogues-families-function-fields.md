## Q1. Dirichlet L-functions, families, degree-2 / Selberg class: what family averages achieve that single-function methods cannot; the "short interval as a family" analogy

### Takeaway
Averaging over families lengthens the admissible mollifier past the single-function barrier, because the asymptotic large sieve supplies orthogonality. That extra length is exactly what pushes the proportion up: 56% (CIS), then 61.07% critical and 60.44% simple (Sono), then about 67.25% simple (2026, prime-modulus family). Degree-2 and 3 results are much weaker: positive proportion for single GL(2) forms (Hafner, Rezvyakova), and 1/9 for GL(3)×χ families (2026). The short-interval window around t behaves like a family of twists n^{-it} whose "orthogonality length" is H = T^θ. That analogy explains the ν < θ − 1/2 constraint.

### Cited Findings
**Degree 1 families (Dirichlet characters)**
- Conrey–Iwaniec–Soundararajan, "Critical zeros of Dirichlet L-functions" (J. reine angew. Math. 681, 2013). They prove that at least 56% of zeros of the family of Dirichlet L-functions lie on the critical line. The average is over primitive characters and conductors. The inputs are the Asymptotic Large Sieve and Levinson's method, and the method gives lower bounds for the proportion of **simple** critical zeros of twists by primitive characters of a fixed L-function of degree 1, 2 or 3. The authors remark: "the Riemann Hypothesis for this family is more likely to be true than not!" - [aimath PDF](https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/78.pdf); [arXiv:1105.1177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.1177); [Crelle](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/crelle-2012-0032/html). Companion paper on the asymptotic large sieve: [arXiv:1105.1176](https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/u14.pdf).
- Keiju Sono, "Zeros of Dirichlet L-functions on the critical line" (arXiv:2105.07422; v1 May 2021, latest June 2024). Using **Feng's mollifier** plus an asymptotic mean-square formula for the family, averaged over primitive characters and conductors, he gets ≥ **61.07%** of zeros on the critical line and ≥ **60.44%** simple and on the line. This improves CIS - [arXiv:2105.07422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07422).
- Xiaosheng Wu, "Distinct zeros and simple zeros of Dirichlet L-functions": in the family of Dirichlet L-functions, more than **80.13%** of zeros are distinct and more than **60.261%** simple. Under GRH these become **83.216%** and **66.433%** - [arXiv:1206.1679](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.1679) (figures as quoted in a 2026 citing abstract, via search snippet).
- Hua & Yang, "Simple and distinct zeros in a prime-modulus Dirichlet family from near-microscopic to polylogarithmic heights" (arXiv:2608.16034), with a sequel "Gevrey localization for simple and distinct zeros in a prime-modulus Dirichlet family" (arXiv:2609.27808). For odd primes q, with height T(q) under growth conditions, they prove **unconditional** lower bounds for simple and distinct critical zeros. When log T = o(log q) the proportions are ≈ **0.6725007036** (simple and critical) and ≈ **0.8362503518** (distinct). This covers every fixed T = (log q)^A with A > 1 - [arXiv:2609.27808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27808); [arXiv:2608.16034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16034). These constants match the 2026 zeta constants 0.67250 and 0.83625 (see Q6), so this is the family transplant of the pair-correlation-plus-inequality method. The "Gevrey localization" title suggests a smooth-cutoff (Gevrey-class weight) localization in height, which may be a directly transferable tool for localizing to short t-windows.
- Dickinson, "Zeros of Dirichlet L-functions near the critical line" (Mathematika, 2024) - [Wiley](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/mtk.12239). Details not retrieved.

**Degree 2 and 3**
- Conrey + 3 coauthors, including Chung-Hang Kwan (per his publication page), "Critical Zeros and Unconditional Mean Value Theorems for twisted PGL(2) and PGL(3) L-functions" (arXiv:2607.00282, July 2026). They use Levinson's method. As Q → ∞, at least **1/9** of the zeros of L(s, Π₀ × χ) lie on the critical line, with χ running over primitive characters of conductor ≤ Q. This is unconditional when Π₀ is self-dual, and holds under a mild condition otherwise. The PGL(2) analogues are fully unconditional, quantitatively stronger, and apparently new. The key input is a new asymptotic formula, with power saving, for the **mean square of L(s, Π₀×χ) times a Dirichlet polynomial with arbitrary coefficients** in both the T- and Q-aspects, for **Q^ε ≤ T ≤ Q^{1/3−ε}** - [arXiv:2607.00282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00282); [Kwan publications](https://sites.google.com/view/kevinchkwan/home/publications); [GL3 asymptotic large sieve code](https://github.com/Chung-Hang-Kwan/GL3-Asymptotic-Large-Sieve).
- Rezvyakova: a positive proportion of nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line for L-functions of Hecke-eigen automorphic cusp forms of integer weight k ≥ 1 for congruence subgroups. This extends the Hafner/Selberg method - [Math. Notes 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0001434610090154). She also gave improved lower bounds for zeros of **linear combinations** of Hecke L-functions on short intervals of the critical line, improving Gritsenko (1997) - [Izv. Math. 2010 (ADS)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IzMat..74.1277R); [Doklady 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S1064562410020390); [Doklady 2015, Selberg's method for linear combinations](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S1064562415040158).
- Rezvyakova, "The Epstein zeta-function contains a positive proportion of non-trivial zeros on the critical line", Izv. Math. 90:2 (2026), 311–386 (arXiv:2411.18492). The Epstein zeta function has no Euler product, so this is a Selberg-method result for a non-Selberg-class function - [Math-Net.ru](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?wshow=paper&jrnid=im&paperid=9664&option_lang=eng); [arXiv:2411.18492](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18492).
- "On the zeros of linear combinations of L-functions of degree two on the critical line. Selberg's approach" (arXiv:2501.00551) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00551). Also "Zeros of linear combinations of Dirichlet L-functions on the critical line" (arXiv:2311.10285) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.10285). Constants not retrieved.
- Other degree-2 Levinson work: "Simple zeros of modular L-functions" (arXiv:1306.0854) and "Modular case of Levinson's theorem" (arXiv:1404.6432) - [1306.0854](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.0854), [1404.6432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.6432). Constants not retrieved.

### Inferences
- **What families buy.** In CIS/Sono the gain comes entirely from the mean square of L × (Dirichlet polynomial) being computable for a longer polynomial. The asymptotic large sieve uses orthogonality of characters in the q-aspect on top of the t-aspect. For a single zeta function, Conrey's barrier is ν < 4/7. The family effectively lengthens the mollifier relative to the (log of the) analytic conductor, and that is the whole source of 56% → 61% versus 41.7% for zeta.
- **"Short interval as a family".** Integrating over t ∈ [T, T+H] with H = T^θ acts like an orthogonality relation for the twists n^{-it}. The mean-value theorem ∫_T^{T+H} |Σ_{n≤N} a_n n^{-it}|² dt ≈ (H + O(N)) Σ|a_n|² gives diagonal dominance only when N ≲ H. In the mollified second moment, ζ(1/2+it) itself behaves like a Dirichlet polynomial of length ~ T^{1/2} (approximate functional equation). So ζ·M is effectively a polynomial of length T^{1/2+ν}, and diagonal dominance needs 1/2 + ν < θ, i.e. ν < θ − 1/2. That is exactly the program's constraint. In family language, the short window is a "family" whose size (H) is smaller than the conductor square root. To get below θ = 1/2 you need off-diagonal (Kloosterman / shifted-convolution) information, as in Conrey 4/7, Pratt–Robles, Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł. Those are the analogue of the asymptotic large sieve.
- **The GL(3) result as a template.** Its mean-value theorem holds in the hybrid range Q^ε ≤ T ≤ Q^{1/3−ε}, a "small t-window" regime inside a family. It is the closest existing technology for a mean square of L × polynomial on restricted t-ranges with power saving. Techniques there (hybrid large sieve and Voronoi in the t-aspect) are a candidate for off-diagonal gains in short windows.
- **The Hua–Yang constants** (0.6725007036, 0.8362503518) coincide with the 2026 zeta constants. This shows the pair-correlation-plus-inequality method is robust to replacing the t-average by a family average, down to heights T = (log q)^A. So the method does not need long t-ranges once another source of averaging is present. This supports the program's view of short intervals as a family.

## Q2. Zeros of derivatives ζ^(k) and Ξ^(k); Conrey 1983; can derivative detectors help in short intervals?

### Takeaway
Conrey (1983) gave explicit lower bounds for the proportion of zeros of Ξ^(m) on the critical line and showed they tend to 1 as m → ∞. The most transferable modern descendant is "Short mollifiers of the Riemann zeta-function" (arXiv:2508.11108, 2025, from Conrey's publication list). It uses the calculus of variations on linear combinations of derivatives, i.e. Levinson's Q(d/ds), to get a **positive proportion however short the mollifier is**. This is the exact regime the program is in at θ → 1/2⁺, where ν = θ − 1/2 → 0.

### Cited Findings
- Conrey, "Zeros of derivatives of Riemann's ξ-function on the critical line", J. Number Theory 16 (1983), 49–74. Explicit lower bounds for the proportion of zeros of derivatives of ξ on the critical line; **the proportion tends to one as the order of the derivative tends to infinity** - [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022314X83900318); [aimath PDF](https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/4.pdf). Part II: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022314X83900070).
- "Short mollifiers of the Riemann zeta-function" (arXiv:2508.11108, 2025; listed as publication #110 on Conrey's aimath page). It applies the **calculus of variations** to build linear combinations of derivatives of ζ adapted to Levinson's method. These give **a positive proportion of zeros on the critical line regardless of how short the mollifier is** - [arXiv:2508.11108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11108); [aimath PDF](https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/110.pdf).
- "Zeros of higher derivatives of Riemann zeta function" (arXiv:2104.10243; J. Math. Anal. Appl. 2024) extends Conrey's results to shorter intervals for higher derivatives. It obtains a better mollifier length in some short intervals than Conrey's result implies - [arXiv:2104.10243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.10243); [JMAA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022247X24004530).
- Background: Levinson's theorem admits θ < 1/2, and Conrey extended the range to θ < 4/7 - [search summary of 2508.11108 / 2104.10243 context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11108).
- Zeros of ζ' near the critical line (Conrey–Ghosh survey chapter) - [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-3464-7_8).

### Inferences
- **Direct transfer.** With ν = θ − 1/2 small, the classical Levinson functional with a low-degree Q gives a proportion κ(ν) that can be poor or even nonpositive. 2508.11108 claims a positive proportion for every mollifier length, so it gives κ(ν) > 0 for all ν > 0 after optimizing Q variationally. If its small-ν asymptotic is κ ~ c·ν, compare c with the program's slope 0.717. If the optimal c is larger, the constants improve at once. If 2508.11108 gets κ ≥ c₀ > 0 at a fixed small ν rather than κ → 0 as ν → 0, the short-interval barrier could move toward θ = 1/2 + ε for every ε.
- **Why derivatives help (Conrey 1983 mechanism).** Ξ^(m) behaves like cos-type functions with zeros increasingly regular. High-order differentiation damps the influence of the "off-line" part, so the Levinson loss term shrinks as the operator gets "longer" in the derivative direction. Levinson's V = Q(−(1/L) d/ds)ζ exploits the same effect. In short windows, a higher-degree or variationally optimal Q is a zero-cost change: it does not lengthen the Dirichlet polynomial, so it does not tighten ν < θ − 1/2. That makes it the most promising knob for improving constants near θ = 1/2.
- Zeros of Ξ^(m) themselves do not detect zeros of Ξ. Their value lies only in the operator Q(d/ds), which the program already uses via Conrey's general Q. The gain would come from optimizing Q in the small-ν regime, as 2508.11108 does.

## Q3. Function field analogues: short intervals over F_q[t], Katz–Sarnak, KRRR; what they suggest about the true barrier

### Takeaway
Over F_q[t], RH is a theorem (Weil), so "critical" is automatic and only simplicity and statistics remain. The short-interval work (Keating–Rudnick; Keating–Rodgers–Roditty-Gershon–Rudnick) turns short-interval arithmetic variances into unitary matrix integrals as q → ∞. Those integrals change behaviour piecewise-polynomially with the interval length. This supports reading the θ = 1/2 barrier for zeta as a feature of the method (mean-value and approximate-functional-equation length), not of the zeros themselves. Bui–Florea's hyperelliptic results show how one-level density plus pair correlation give simple-zero proportions in function-field families.

### Cited Findings
- Keating–Rodgers–Roditty-Gershon–Rudnick, "Sums of divisor functions in F_q[t] and matrix integrals" (Math. Z. 2018; arXiv:1504.07804). They study the mean square of sums of d_k over **short intervals** and arithmetic progressions in F_q[t]. As q → ∞ this equals a matrix integral over the unitary group, which evaluates to a lattice-point count given by a **piecewise polynomial** function. They suggest conjectures over the integers that agree with the known cases - [Rudnick PDF](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/papers/KRRRdivisors.pdf); [arXiv:1504.07804](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.07804).
- Related: "Arithmetic functions in short intervals and the symmetric group" (arXiv:1609.02967) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.02967).
- Bui–Florea, "Zeros of quadratic Dirichlet L-functions in the hyperelliptic ensemble", Trans. AMS 370(11) (2018), 8013–8045. They study one-level density and pair correlation of zeros over the hyperelliptic ensemble, which yields **non-vanishing results and lower bounds for the proportion of simple zeros**. For support of the Fourier transform in (1/3, 1) they find a secondary term of size q^{-4g/3}/g that the Ratios Conjecture does not predict - [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Zeros-of-quadratic-Dirichlet-$L$-functions-in-the-Bui-Florea/152aac6ec4e5d24a597a1d822afdb1fdc2bacd84).
- Related: mollified moments of quadratic Dirichlet L-functions over function fields (arXiv:2201.11005) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.11005v3); one-level density of Dirichlet L-functions over function fields (arXiv:2211.00076) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00076).

### Inferences
- **No intrinsic θ = 1/2 wall in the model.** In the q → ∞ function-field model, short-interval statistics are governed by unitary matrix integrals for all relative lengths h/n, with transitions at rational breakpoints (piecewise polynomial). Nothing singles out "half the degree" as a barrier for zero statistics. The zeta barrier ν < θ − 1/2 comes from the length T^{1/2} of ζ's approximate functional equation, a method artifact. The function-field analogue suggests the truth is "positive proportion of simple critical zeros in (T, T+T^θ] for every θ > 0" and in fact down to H ≫ 1/log T scales, as RMT predicts.
- **Pair correlation versus Levinson.** Bui–Florea get simple-zero proportions from pair correlation or one-level density with limited Fourier support, which is the same mechanism as Montgomery's 2/3 and the 2026 zeta results. The function-field literature therefore favours pair-correlation-type arguments over Levinson-type ones for simplicity in short ranges. That is consistent with the 2026 short-interval work of Biao Wang (Q6).
- Katz–Sarnak monodromy gives equidistribution with GUE/USp statistics as q → ∞ for geometric families. In that limit 100% of L-functions in such families have simple zeros [background, not re-verified: Katz–Sarnak 1999; Chavdarov; Kowalski's large sieve and monodromy]. This is a "big family" statement and does not address a single fixed L-function in a short window.

### Gaps
- I found no function-field paper on zeros of a **single** L-function in short "height" intervals, or on a function-field Levinson method with a short mollifier.
- Bui–Florea's exact numerical proportions (non-vanishing, simple zeros) were not in the retrieved snippets. The often-quoted ≥ 94.27% non-vanishing figure is [background, not re-verified].
- Keating–Rudnick (IMRN 2014, primes in short intervals in F_q[t]) was not re-retrieved.

## Q4. Random matrix models for mollifier/Levinson efficiency; κ(ν); the θ = ∞ conjecture; is 0.717ν near-optimal for small ν?

### Takeaway
For eigenvalues, the RMT "proportion simple" is trivially 1. The relevant RMT-style statements are about Levinson's efficiency as a function of mollifier length. Farmer's θ = ∞ conjecture says κ → 1 as the mollifier length → ∞, and it implies RH (for automorphic L-functions, a quasi-RH for the family; 2026 paper). Record constants for zeta come from lengthening the mollifier: 5/12 in 2020 via Pratt–Robles–Zaharescu–Zeindler. No retrieved source gives an RMT prediction for the small-ν slope of κ(ν). Whether 0.717ν is near-optimal is open. The best available benchmark is the variational construction in "Short mollifiers" (2508.11108).

### Cited Findings
- Farmer proposed the "θ = ∞" conjecture: the admissible mollifier length θ can be arbitrarily large. It implies RH. The 2026 paper "The θ=∞ Conjecture and the Riemann Hypothesis for Automorphic L-functions" (arXiv:2605.24363) shows the θ = ∞ conjecture for a family implies a quasi-RH for that family. It uses the ratios technology of CFKRS (2005), Conrey–Farmer–Zirnbauer (2008) and Conrey–Snaith (2007) - [arXiv:2605.24363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24363); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/405264312_The_th_infty_Conjecture_and_the_Riemann_Hypothesis_for_Automorphic_L-functions).
- Pratt, Robles, Zaharescu, Zeindler, "More than five-twelfths of the zeros of ζ are on the critical line", Res. Math. Sci. (2020); arXiv:1802.10521. It builds on long mollifiers and sums of Kloosterman sums due to Pratt–Robles (2017) - [arXiv:1802.10521](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10521); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40687-019-0199-8); Pratt–Robles, "Perturbed moments and a longer mollifier for critical zeros of ζ" - [arXiv:1706.04593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.04593).
- "On mean values of mollifiers and L-functions associated to primitive cusp forms" (arXiv:1609.03738) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.03738).
- Pearce-Crump, "Optimising Selberg's method for critical zeros" (arXiv:2609.15329, Sept 2026). The paper revisits Zhuravlev's 1974 quantitative Selberg method. It optimises the arithmetic mean value (exact closed form for reciprocal-square-root coefficients) and improves it further with a **positive-semidefinite family of mollifiers**, proving **≥ 7%** of zeros are critical. This is the strongest result from Selberg's method - [arXiv:2609.15329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15329). Selberg's method is the classical source of short-interval results (see Q5), so a quantitatively optimised Selberg method matters for short intervals.

### Inferences
- **Heuristic κ(ν) for small ν.** In Levinson's method with a mollifier of relative length ν and optimised Q, the loss term is roughly (1/R)·log of a mollified mean square, which tends to that of an unmollified zeta as ν → 0. An unmollified Levinson argument (ν = 0) gives nothing, so κ(ν) → 0 as ν → 0. A linear rate κ ≈ c·ν is the natural first-order behaviour. Whether c = 0.717 is optimal depends on whether Q is optimised jointly with the mollifier shape at leading order in ν. 2508.11108 does exactly this variationally, so it is the right comparison point. If its optimal c is bigger than 0.717, the program's constants are not optimal. If 2508.11108 obtains κ bounded below as ν → 0, the program's slope framing is itself sub-optimal. **This is an inference; no source retrieved states the optimal small-ν slope.**
- **RMT and ratios limits.** Under the ratios conjecture (CFZ, Conrey–Snaith) mollified moments can be predicted for any length. The θ = ∞ picture says κ(ν) is increasing with limit 1. For short intervals, the ratios-conjecture analogue on a short window would predict that κ(ν) depends only on ν and not on θ. The θ-dependence enters only through the admissibility constraint ν < θ − 1/2.
- **Alternative route.** Pearce-Crump's PSD-mollifier optimisation of Selberg's method (7%) is a more natural engine for short intervals than Levinson, because Selberg's method works in intervals of length T^{27/82+ε} (Karatsuba). A short-interval optimised Selberg method could give an explicit proportion of critical zeros below θ = 1/2. Simplicity would then need a separate input, e.g. short-interval pair correlation plus Lamzouri's inequality.

### Gaps
- No source found giving an explicit RMT prediction of Levinson's κ(ν). I could not verify Bettin's work on mollifier optimality or a paper literally titled "mollification and random matrix theory".
- Bui–Conrey–Young (41.05%, 2011) and Feng (41.28%) are [background, not re-verified]. Exact PRZZ constants beyond "slightly over five-twelfths" (41.7293%?) were not re-verified.

## Q5. Short-interval zero counting on the line: smallest θ for a positive proportion of critical zeros; combining with simplicity

### Takeaway
The smallest known θ for a positive proportion of critical zeros (counted by odd order, not necessarily simple) in (T, T+T^θ] is **θ = 27/82 + ε**, due to Karatsuba. Selberg's original method gives θ = 1/2 + ε. Levinson/Conrey methods with simplicity need θ > 1/2 in the localized form (ν < θ − 1/2). A combination route exists: Karatsuba–Selberg critical zeros at θ < 1/2, plus a simplicity mechanism that works in short intervals, e.g. short-interval pair correlation with Lamzouri's inequality (Q6). No retrieved paper has done this for θ < 1/2.

### Cited Findings
- Karatsuba: for any ε > 0 there is c = c(ε) > 0 such that for H = T^{27/82+ε} and T large, **N₀(T+H) − N₀(T) ≥ c·H·log T**, where N₀ counts **odd-order** zeros on the critical line. This is a positive proportion of all zeros in every such interval - [Wikipedia, Selberg's zeta function conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selberg%27s_zeta_function_conjecture); survey in Russian Math. Surveys 40:5 (1985), 23–82 - [Math-Net PDF](https://www.mathnet.ru/links/effb84beb8228976cac13d5467675901/rm2762_eng.pdf); [Karatsuba "Vienna talk", Chebyshevskii Sbornik](https://www.chebsbornik.ru/jour/article/view/31?locale=en_US).
- Karatsuba's method also reaches "supershort" intervals, of length growing slower than any power of T, for related Z-functions - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selberg%27s_zeta_function_conjecture). Earlier short-interval simple-zero work: "On simple zeros of the Riemann zeta-function in short intervals on the critical line", Acta Math. Hungar. - [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1019767816190); "On the multiplicities of zeros of ζ(s) and its values over short intervals" (arXiv:1706.08268) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.08268).
- Goldston–Suriajaya, "Zeta Zeros on the Critical Line" (arXiv:2511.20059, Nov 2025, rev. Feb 2026). Removing RH from Montgomery's pair-correlation proof that ≥ 2/3 of zeros are simple would also show that 2/3 are on the critical line. They prove this when all zeros lie in a narrow enough region around the line. Assuming the Pair Correlation Conjecture **without RH**, 100% of zeros are simple and critical - [arXiv:2511.20059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20059). Related: "Zeta Zeros in a Narrow Vertical Box" (arXiv:2603.28104) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28104).

### Inferences
- **Hybrid for θ < 1/2.** The odd-order counts N₀ from Karatsuba give critical zeros at θ > 27/82. A simple-zero proportion at the same θ needs (a) an upper bound on the multiplicity-weighted count of multiple zeros in the window, or (b) pair-correlation-type control. Route (b) requires an unconditional Montgomery-type F(α) theorem valid in windows H = T^θ. Biao Wang (2609.07918) proves such a theorem for "0 < θ < 1", but the range where it has content is unverified. If short-interval pair correlation holds with nontrivial support at θ < 1/2, the Alpöge–Furman/Lamzouri mechanism would give simple critical zeros below 1/2. This is the most promising transfer for pushing θ to 1/2 and below.
- **Odd-order versus simple.** Selberg/Karatsuba sign-change counts give odd-order critical zeros. Any positive-proportion bound on odd-order critical zeros combined with an upper bound on Σ(m_ρ − 1) over the window gives simple critical zeros. Short-interval versions of the Montgomery/Cheer–Goldston multiplicity bounds would supply that. The program's "Hilbert-parity product" may be an instance of this kind of parity argument; the Zenodo titles suggest so.

## Q6. Multiplicity bounds and simple-zero proportions (Bui–Milinovich, Bui–Heath-Brown, 2020–2026 updates)

### Takeaway
Under RH the record was **19/27 ≈ 70.37%** simple (Bui–Heath-Brown 2013). Unconditionally, before 2026, it was about 40.7% (Levinson/Conrey type). In **August 2026, Alpöge–Furman proved unconditionally that ≥ 2/3 of zeros are simple and on the critical line and ≥ 5/6 are distinct**, rising to **0.67250 / 0.83625** with a Montgomery–Taylor window. They state the argument was discovered by Claude (Anthropic) and verified by them. Lamzouri gave a new proof, Biao Wang refined the constants, and Wang extended the result to short intervals (T, T+T^θ]. This is directly relevant to, and possibly overlaps, the program's result.

### Cited Findings
- Bui–Heath-Brown, "On simple zeros of the Riemann zeta-function" (Bull. LMS 2013; arXiv:1302.5018): **under RH**, at least **19/27** of zeros are simple. This was previously proved by Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek under RH + Generalised Lindelöf; GLH was removed with the generalised Vaughan identity - [arXiv:1302.5018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5018).
- Alpöge & Furman, "More than two thirds of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line" (arXiv:2608.13637, Aug 2026). **Unconditionally**, at least **2/3** of nontrivial zeros, counted with multiplicity, are simple and on the critical line, and at least **5/6** are distinct. With the Montgomery–Taylor window the constants become **0.67250** and **0.83625**. The paper states the argument was discovered and written by Claude and verified by the authors - [arXiv:2608.13637](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13637v1); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13637).
- Lamzouri, "A new proof that more than 2/3 of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line" (arXiv:2609.02882, Sept 2026) is described as a "different and more direct proof". It relies on an inequality on finite multisets of complex numbers invariant under complex conjugation, per Wang's description - [arXiv:2609.02882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02882); [Wang 2609.07918 abstract](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07918).
- Biao Wang, "Proportions of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function" (arXiv:2609.24167) refines Lamzouri's method to get more than **67.25%** simple and critical and more than **83.62%** distinct - [arXiv:2609.24167](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24167).

### Inferences
- **Competitive situation.** Biao Wang's 2609.07918 already claims short-interval simple-critical proportions for all 0 < θ < 1, using pair correlation and Lamzouri's inequality with no Levinson mollifier. If its bounds are nontrivial for θ close to 1/2, or below it, it may supersede θ ≥ 0.534. The program should obtain the full text and compare (i) the θ-range where Wang's bound is positive and (ii) the constants. Possibly the pair-correlation route alone is nontrivial only for θ above some threshold, perhaps because Montgomery's F(α) in a window of length T^θ needs the Fourier support restricted in terms of θ. The Levinson input (κ > 0) may be what the program adds. **This is inference; Wang's thresholds are unverified.**
- **What pushes θ down.** In the pair-correlation-plus-inequality method, the short-interval loss enters through the admissible Fourier support of Montgomery's F(α) in windows of length H. With H = T^θ the natural unconditional support shrinks as θ decreases, heuristically like |α| < θ-dependent. Any short-interval extension of the support is therefore the key lever. Tools include Goldston–Montgomery-type short-interval explicit formulas, the Montgomery–Taylor window, and Gevrey-localized weights as in Hua–Yang 2609.27808.
- **"Hilbert-parity product" and Lamzouri's inequality.** Lamzouri's inequality on conjugation-invariant multisets is a parity/symmetry device. The program's "Hilbert-parity product" appears (from the Zenodo titles) to be in the same spirit. A sharper inequality of that kind directly improves constants and does not interact with θ.
