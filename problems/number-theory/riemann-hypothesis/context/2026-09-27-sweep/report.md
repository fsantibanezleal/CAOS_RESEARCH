# Rescope RH-027, then sharpen the kernel

No external result posted through 27 September 2026 beats the program's short-interval onset (θ ≥ 0.534) or its attributed global constant (0.67250079959...). The only external global gain is Biao Wang's δ₀ = 6.66624×10⁻⁸, which gives C₀ + δ₀ ≈ 0.6725007703. The program's EXP-009 value sits about 2.9×10⁻⁸ above that. Every short-interval onset near 0.5459 that search surfaced is the program's own Zenodo output. The findings that should change the experiment plan therefore concern inputs and methods, not competitors. First, the claim behind RH-027 overstates its source. Steuding proved his T^{1/3+ε}M^{4/3} short-window mean square only for Levinson's degree-one Q, only with fixed shifts, only under ν < 3/8, and with the final error analysis specialized to derivative orders ≤ 2. The range ν < (3θ−1)/4 is an inference (the condition that his error term be o(H)); it is never stated as a theorem. RH-027 is therefore a new theorem to prove, not a citation to import. Second, the Fourier-optimization literature explains the program's thresholds. Wang's 0.55019 is exactly the pure first-moment Montgomery–Taylor threshold. The Cohn–Elkies/SDP class moves that threshold only to about 0.5487. So future onset gains must come from second-order inequalities (the program's parity product, Wang's spectral term Δ_K, n-level constraints) or from new short-window information about F, not from better kernels. Third, of all the cross-field representations surveyed, only finite compressions of Weil's Hermitian form have a working link to zero proportions today. The report ends with nine prioritized routes. They are led by a primary-source verification gate, a cheap value-of-information computation for the rescoped RH-027, and a Cohn–Elkies admissibility test whose global payoff (about +0.0067) dwarfs every other gain in view.

## Steuding's theorem covers degree-one Q only, so RH-027 is a new theorem

The backlog claims that Steuding supplies a uniform two-shift short-window moment for general Q in the range ν < (3θ−1)/4. **The sources do not support that claim.** The Acta Math. Hungar. 96 (2002) abstract says that for a mollifier of length M = T^ν on short intervals, "if [ν] < 3/8" the mean square equals the main term plus O(T^{1/3+ε}M^{4/3}). This beats the O(T^{1/2+ε}M) of other approaches, and Levinson's method then gives positivity for H ≥ T^{0.552} ([Springer abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1019767816190)) **[S]**. The program read the 1999 dissertation directly earlier. Theorem 2.1 there treats F = ζ + ζ′/log T, i.e. **Levinson's degree-one Q with fixed shifts**. Arbitrary derivative orders are introduced, but the final error analysis "explicitly specializes to orders at most two" on p. 41 ([dissertation PDF](https://webdoc.sub.gwdg.de/ebook/e/1999/steuding/253563305.pdf); program note `context/2026-09-12-critical-mass-and-multiplicity-route.md` §8.2). **The formula (3θ−1)/4 appears nowhere as a statement** **[I]**. It is what one gets by requiring T^{1/3+4ν/3} = o(T^θ). It matches Steuding's thresholds: θ = 0.552 gives ν = 0.164 and θ = 0.591 gives ν = 0.193, which are exactly the onsets of Levinson's Q(x) = 1−x under his two mollifier choices. With the cap included, **the admissible Steuding range is ν < min{(3θ−1)/4, 3/8}, proved only for degree-one Q and fixed shifts**. The 2002 journal text itself could not be read, so whether it keeps the 3/8 cap and which Q it covers remains open.

This rescoping changes what RH-027 buys and what it costs. **A faithful degree-one Steuding moment adds nothing below 0.534**, because Levinson's degree-one Q is positive only from θ = 0.552. All the value lies in the extension to general Q with uniform shifts |α|, |β| ≪ 1/L, which is exactly the part nobody has proved. A degree-d operator Q plausibly needs derivative pairs up to total order 2d, well beyond Steuding's order-two analysis **[I]**. The table shows the payoff *if* the extension exists and *if* the program's slope κ > 0.7170ν persisted at these larger ν. The second assumption is an untested extrapolation, since EXP-010 certifies the slope only inside ν < θ − ½.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Three structural facts follow **[I]**. First, the Steuding-type range exceeds the localized Young range by (1−θ)/4. It tends to 1/8 as θ → ½⁺, which is why a general-Q version would give positivity for every θ > ½. Second, the cap binds only for θ > 5/6, and localized Young is strictly better for θ > 7/8. Third, the T^{1/3}M^{4/3} error is the twisted analogue of Atkinson's E(T) ≪ T^{1/3}. It comes from Atkinson/Jutila/Motohashi transformations that *evaluate* the off-diagonal rather than bound it by parts ([Steuding, LNM 1877 snippet](https://nzdr.ru/data/media/biblio/kolxoz/M/Mln/Steuding%20J.%20Value%20Distribution%20of%20L-Functions%20(LNM1877,%20Springer,%202007)(ISBN%203540265260)(319s)_Mln_.pdf)). A general-Q version must therefore track an extra off-diagonal main term inside the window, or show that it is negligible below (3θ−1)/4.

**Two existing tools are the natural engines.** Ishikawa–Matsumoto give a complete explicit Atkinson-type formula for the error term E(T; A) in the mean square of ζ times a Dirichlet polynomial with complex coefficients ([Open Math. 9 (2011)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.2478/s11533-010-0085-5); [EuDML](https://eudml.org/doc/269380)). It is the obvious starting point for a shifted, general-coefficient Steuding estimate. **Tang's arXiv:2608.14852 (tracked) is the single most important unread source.** It gives a reciprocity formula for a twisted second moment over windows of length T^δ, δ ∈ (½, 1), and "makes explicit the relation between the twisting parameters and the interval length" ([arXiv:2608.14852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14852)) **[S]**. The program's preflight classed it as unusable for mollifiers. But summing a Khan-type identity over h, k ≤ T^ν could yield a short-window twisted moment directly, if Tang's admissible (h, k, δ) range reaches hk ≈ T^{2(3δ−1)/4}. That range is unverified. Three other directions do not help. Guth–Maynard's large-value estimates ([arXiv:2405.20552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20552)) control how often a Dirichlet polynomial is large, not the main term of a mean square with oscillating ζ. The global Kloosterman routes (Bettin–Chandee–Radziwiłł 17/33, Pratt–Robles 6/11, Conrey 4/7) have no window versions ([arXiv:1706.04593](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04593)). Conrey's own Gaussian-window proposition needs θ > 6/7 + ν/4 (program preflight note).

RH-028 shrinks in scope for the same reason. The Feng/BCY second piece relevant here has the same small length ν, not PRZZ's lengths ([PRZZ arXiv:1802.10521](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10521); [BCY](https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/69.pdf)). Its window off-diagonal condition (hk ≤ Δ²/T) is then identical to the Conrey piece's. So a localized two-piece moment at ν < θ − ½ should follow from the same localized Young argument, and **the entire gain is main-term optimization** **[I]**. That makes RH-028 a numerical question first. The right benchmark is Conrey–Farmer–Kwan–Lin–Turnage-Butterbaugh's variational construction, which gives "a positive proportion of zeros on the critical line, regardless of how short the mollifier is" ([arXiv:2508.11108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11108)) **[S]**. Its small-ν slope has not yet been compared with 0.7170.

## Kernel choice explains 0.55019; second-order information moves onsets

Every pair-correlation multiplicity bound reduces to one linear-fractional extremal problem. Minimize Q(r) = (r̂(0) + ∫|α| r̂(α)dα)/r(0) over r ≥ 0 with r̂ supported where F is known. Then simple ≥ 2 − Q and distinct ≥ (3 − Q)/2. With Q = 4/3 this gives 2/3 and 5/6, and with Montgomery–Taylor's Q = 1.3275 it gives 0.6725 and 0.83625 ([Alpöge–Furman arXiv:2608.13637](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13637v1); [Lamzouri arXiv:2609.02882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02882)). The notes solved this problem as a Gram quotient, 1/⟨1, (I+K)⁻¹1⟩ with K(x, y) = |x − y|, and as a Cohn–Elkies LP. The computation reproduced the published constants 0.6725 (Montgomery–Taylor), 0.6727 (Cheer–Goldston) and 0.6792 (Chirre–Gonçalves–de Laat) ([CGdL arXiv:1810.08843](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08843)) **[I, floating point]**. Applied at short-interval support λ = θ, it gives:

| Support λ | Montgomery–Taylor Q | Simple ≥ | Cohn–Elkies Q | Simple ≥ (CE) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 1.32750 | 0.67250 | 1.32071 | 0.6793 |
| 0.6 | 1.86545 | 0.13455 | 1.86061 | 0.1394 |
| 0.55 | 2.00058 | −0.0006 | 1.99609 | 0.0039 |
| 0.534 | 2.04980 | −0.0498 | 2.04544 | −0.0454 |
| **Positivity threshold** | **λ\* ≈ 0.55019** | | **λ ≈ 0.5487** | |

**The pure first-moment threshold equals the value 0.55019 that the program's manuscript attributes to Wang.** This is strong indirect evidence that Wang's short-interval theorem is the Montgomery–Taylor quotient at support λ < θ, fed through Lamzouri's inequality. It is still [I] until R1 reads the paper. It also shows that **the program's 0.534 already lies below the entire classical bandlimited family**. Its gain comes from the second-order Hilbert-parity product and the Levinson κ, not from the kernel. The Cohn–Elkies relaxation gains only about 0.0015 in threshold and about 0.004 in proportion at small support, against 0.0067 at λ = 1. Historically, thresholds moved when new information about F arrived, not when kernels improved. GGOS's GRH lower bound F(α) ≥ 3/2 − α on [1, 3/2] lifted 0.6727 to 0.6738 ([GGOS, Proc. LMS 80](https://academic.oup.com/plms/article-abstract/80/1/31/1529728)). No unconditional short-window lower bound on F_θ(α) for α slightly above θ is known. It would need GRH-type prime equidistribution in short intervals.

**The Cohn–Elkies transplant hinges on one admissibility question, and its answer decides a large global prize.** The 2026 unconditional constants equal the RH constants because Lamzouri's inequality consumes F linearly, through the same test-function functional as Montgomery's. That suggests every RH-conditional kernel improvement could be made unconditional. The Cohn–Elkies class, however, uses r̂ ≤ 0 outside the support together with **F(α) ≥ 0 for all α**. Under RH that positivity is automatic, because the ordinates are real. In the unconditional setting, off-line zeros have complex "ordinates", and F ≥ 0 beyond the support is not free **[I]**. If the Weil-form/Lamzouri framework can absorb a sign-constrained tail, the global constant moves from about 0.6725 to about 0.679. That is roughly 10⁵ times Wang's δ₀. A Zenodo record already claims 67.92% under a vanishing-vertical-box localization hypothesis ([Zenodo 21879591](https://zenodo.org/records/21879591)) **[S]**, consistent with this reading. A small side observation affects the same test **[I]**. By the Krein/Akhiezer factorization, every integrable r ≥ 0 with compactly supported r̂ has the form |ǧ|², so the bandlimited class coincides with Montgomery–Taylor's g∗g̃ class. The small Cheer–Goldston excess (0.6727 against 0.67250) must therefore come from a different functional or a discretization effect, and it should be pinned down before either constant is fed into the unconditional framework.

**Second-order levers are where the short-interval gains live.** Wang's spectral term Δ_K is one such lever: a refinement of Lamzouri's inequality that he says transfers to short intervals ([arXiv:2609.24167](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24167), l. 912) **[V]**. A second is n-level correlation. Gonçalves–de Laat–Leijenhorst bound multiplicity fractions for GL_m by combining Hejhal/Rudnick–Sarnak n-level asymptotics (support Σ|ξᵢ| < 2) with clustered low-rank SDP ([arXiv:2303.01095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01095); [Rudnick–Sarnak](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/papers/nlevelDuke.pdf)) **[S]**. The parity product (Q−S)(N−O) ≥ 2(N−S)² looks like the Cauchy–Schwarz instance of a moment LP over multiplicity distributions on {1, 2, 3, ...}. An upper bound on Σm_ρ² from a three-level input is exactly the kind of constraint that tightens such an LP **[I]**. This is the zeta analogue of moving from the Cohn–Elkies LP bound to the three-point SDP bound in sphere packing. No short-interval n-level theorem exists, though, and at total support below θ the three-level information may be weak. Finally, if the parity product or Lamzouri's inequality uses a Hilbert-type constant, Rodgers' result that the weighted Montgomery–Vaughan constant exceeds π ([arXiv:2608.12315](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12315)) **[S]** is a warning against assuming classical sharp constants. It also points to finite-N spectral constants as a possible lever.

## Only finite Weil-form representations have a proportion channel today

The question asked which representations from other fields could be explored. The honest answer is a ranking by whether a representation yields a finite certificate and whether that certificate connects to proportions at all.

**Finite Weil compression is the only representation that has already produced a proportion theorem.** Alpöge–Furman compress Weil's Hermitian form to a finite matrix. They apply a rank-trace inequality with Sylvester inertia to handle off-line pairs, then run a second-moment computation over the zeros ([arXiv:2608.13637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13637); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta)). Lamzouri replaced the matrices with a Hilbert-space inequality. Zhu's arXiv:2608.24827 reduces positivity on a whole window to PSD-ness of one matrix, with certified two-sided bounds 8.9×10⁻¹⁸ ≤ λ_min(0.8) ≤ 2.27×10⁻¹⁷. He also reports an empirical law, −ln λ_min(L) ≈ 2π²N(T\*)/ln N(T\*) ([arXiv:2608.24827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24827)) **[S, unrefereed]**. Third-party replays exist only as hobbyist GitHub items ([replay issue](https://github.com/murillo128/mathia/issues/152)), and they are not validation. For the program, the useful idea is Landau–Widom counting **[I, heuristic]**. The Weil form restricted to a window of length T^θ with frequency support λ acts as a time-frequency concentration operator whose count of "effective degrees of freedom" caps what any Gram/rank method can see. That offers a candidate explanation for why every onset clusters near θ ≈ ½. If the heuristic holds, it gives a principled lower barrier for Gram-type detectors and tells the program when to stop tuning kernels.

**The spectral realizations are strong mathematics with no proportion channel.** Connes–Consani–Moscovici's zeta spectral triples are self-adjoint rank-one perturbations of the scaling operator on [λ⁻¹, λ], built from Euler factors with p ≤ λ². They reproduce the first zero to 2.5×10⁻⁵⁵ using primes ≤ 13 ([arXiv:2511.22755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22755)). Connes–van Suijlekom prove that the Fourier transform of a simple, even minimal eigenvector has only real zeros ([arXiv:2511.23257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23257)) **[S]**. Suzuki identifies the Weil-distribution Hilbert space with a de Branges space ([arXiv:2301.00421](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.00421); [arXiv:2606.09096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09096)). All three are finite and certifiable, but none says anything about proportions or short windows. They belong on a watch list.

**Families and function fields support treating a short interval as a family.** A window of length H = T^θ acts as an orthogonality relation for the twists n^{−it}. The mean-value theorem ∫|Σa_n n^{−it}|² = (H + O(N))Σ|a_n|² gives diagonal dominance only for effective length T^{½+ν} ≲ H, which is exactly ν < θ − ½ **[I]**. Families beat single-function barriers through the asymptotic large sieve: Conrey–Iwaniec–Soundararajan get 56%, and Sono gets 61.07% critical and 60.44% simple ([CIS](https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/78.pdf); [Sono arXiv:2105.07422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07422)). The short-window analogue of the large sieve is off-diagonal (Kloosterman/Voronoi) information inside the window. The closest existing template is the hybrid Q^ε ≤ T ≤ Q^{1/3−ε} mean value in arXiv:2607.00282, released with public code ([Kwan code](https://github.com/Chung-Hang-Kwan/GL3-Asymptotic-Large-Sieve)). Over F_q[t], short-interval statistics become unitary matrix integrals with piecewise-polynomial transitions ([KRRR arXiv:1504.07804](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.07804)), and nothing singles out half the degree. That supports reading θ = ½ as an artifact of the approximate functional equation's length √T, not a property of the zeros **[I]**.

**Derivative operators are a free knob, but they are already in use.** Conrey (1983) showed that the proportion of zeros of Ξ^(m) on the line tends to 1 as m grows ([JNT 16](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022314X83900318)). In short windows, a longer operator Q(d/ds) costs no Dirichlet-polynomial length, so it never tightens ν < θ − ½. The program's degree-201 detectors already exploit this. The remaining question is whether CFKLT's variational optimum beats 0.7170 at leading order in ν, or keeps κ bounded away from zero as ν → 0. The latter would make the slope framing itself suboptimal **[I]**.

**The route below θ = ½ through Karatsuba and Selberg is effectively closed.** Karatsuba gives c(ε)H log T odd-order critical zeros for H = T^{27/82+ε} ([Russian Math. Surveys 40:5](https://www.mathnet.ru/links/effb84beb8228976cac13d5467675901/rm2762_eng.pdf)). Converting odd-order zeros into simple zeros needs multiplicity control. A crude count works **[I]**. Montgomery-type bounds give Σ_distinct m² ≤ QN, so the number of distinct zeros of multiplicity ≥ 3 is at most (Q−1)N/6. Then **simple critical ≥ (c − (Q−1)/6)·N**. At λ ≈ 0.55 (Q ≈ 2.0006) this needs an explicit odd-order critical proportion **c > 0.167**. At λ = 0.5 (Fejér Q ≈ 2.167) it needs c > 0.19. The best global Selberg-method constant is Pearce-Crump's 7% ([arXiv:2609.15329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15329)), and Karatsuba's localized constants are inexplicit and tiny. The hybrid would need a localized Selberg proportion 2.4–2.7 times the best global one, so it is not a live route. The step "Σ_distinct m² ≤ QN unconditionally in the window" must also be checked against the program's own framework, which handles off-line zeros through the parity product.

**Low-value fields for this program.** De Bruijn–Newman (0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.2, unchanged since 2020; [Rodgers–Tao](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-pi/article/de-bruijnnewman-constant-is-nonnegative/D4B85BA067E2D5A71D87E4FFB0D21E46)) offers finite certificates but no proportion link. So do Jensen hyperbolicity ([GORZ](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1902572116); [arXiv:2609.14295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14295)), Zagier's horocycle criterion, Lee–Yang/Pólya–Schur stability and Fyodorov–Hiary–Keating/multiplicative chaos ([arXiv:2307.00982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00982)). Machine-learned test functions are also unnecessary. The one-dimensional kernel problems are solved to high precision by LP/SDP with rational dual certificates. AlphaEvolve-style search ([arXiv:2511.02864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02864)) earns its place only in multivariate n-level problems.

## Conclusion

The September 2026 literature changes the program's search strategy more than its standings. The short-interval onset is a race among three kinds of input. Kernel quality is nearly exhausted (Montgomery–Taylor 0.55019, Cohn–Elkies about 0.5487). Second-order inequalities produced every gain so far (0.5458838, then 0.534). Admissible mollifier length is where the one large prize sits: every θ > ½, but only if a Steuding-type moment is extended to operators nobody has treated. Reading Steuding correctly turns RH-027 from a citation into a research theorem. The cheapest decisive step is to learn from R2 how little of that theorem is actually needed.

The largest untested lever is not in short intervals at all. It is whether "F ≥ 0 beyond the support" has an unconditional substitute inside the Weil-form/Lamzouri framework. If it does, the whole Cohn–Elkies/SDP toolkit carries over and the global constant moves by thousandths rather than by 10⁻⁸. If it does not, that failure is itself the explanation for why every unconditional 2026 constant stops exactly at the RH bandlimited value, and it should be written up. Either outcome is worth more than further tuning of existing kernels, and both depend first on the primary sources that this session could not open.
