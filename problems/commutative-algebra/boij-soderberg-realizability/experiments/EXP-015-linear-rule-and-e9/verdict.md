## Deviations from the hypothesis

- The routine of EXP-010 raised an overflow error in its tensor of structure constants at e = 13, n = 49 (an exact
  coefficient 13! C(36, 13) exceeds 2^63), and the first run of family L stopped there with eight cases stored. The
  runner now replaces that function, at import, by one that reduces each coefficient modulo p before storing it
  (the same tensor modulo p; tested equal to the routine in three cases); the file of EXP-010 was not edited.
  Family L was resumed for its last four cases. The earlier experiments are not affected: each entry of the tensor
  is assigned once, an entry that does not fit raises an error instead of wrapping, and for e <= 12 and n <= 4e - 3
  the largest coefficient is 12! C(33, 12), about 1.7 x 10^17.
- The values of r written in the delegation brief for e = 10 to 13 were wrong by 2 (an error of the coordinating
  session); the runner uses r = 2 u(2e) - 2 = 240, 286, 336, 390, as the hypothesis defines it.
- Where N is above the budget of 100,000 the members are random ((10, 19), (11, 19), (12, 23), (13, 23), and the two
  cases without a pure member (12, 19), (13, 19)); the early stop cut the enumeration at (10, 17) after 28,000 of
  94,249 members and the samples of the four other pairs with pure members after 24,000.
- Time: 4,327 seconds accounted by the runner plus about 1,026 seconds of the run that stopped, of the 14,400
  allowed; at most 14 worker processes.

## How could this be wrong?

- A pure pair is a finite computation repeated by a second system and a second construction. A sampled case
  without a pure member ((12, 19), (13, 19)) is "none found" as a computation; there the absence follows from the
  proposition, not from the sample.
- The shares of members with the pure Hilbert function are over the members examined before an early stop; they
  are not estimates of a density.
- At (9, 5) the pure pair is one member among 200,000: the stratum S(4; 0, 1) was sampled, and nothing is said
  about how many pure pairs it holds.
