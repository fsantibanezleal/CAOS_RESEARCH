## What is established, and by what

1. The point (1, 2, 2, 1) of (0, 7, 21, 28) is realized over U in characteristic 7, for example by
   f = x^7 + y z^3, g = y^7 + x z^3: length 343, pure table. [MV, two instruments, and rebuilt by the coordinating
   session; the theorem of `context/2026-11-20-central-pair-theorem.md`, reviewed, proves it]
2. The point of (0, 9, 27, 36) is realized in characteristic 3, for example by f = x^9 + y z^4, g = y^9 + x z^4:
   length 729, pure table. [MV, as above]
3. The same for (0, 11, 33, 44) in characteristic 11, (0, 13, 39, 52) in characteristic 13 and (0, 15, 45, 60) in
   characteristics 3 and 5. [MV, two instruments]
4. The statement holds with other linear forms ((x + y, x - y), (2 y, 3 x), (x, y), (x + y, y)) and with central forms
   that are not monomials (cases G1 to G3), as the theorem says. [MV]
5. Each hypothesis is used: dropping one of them (independent linear forms, forms without common factor, p dividing
   e) gives a module that is not pure in the five cases tried. [MV]
