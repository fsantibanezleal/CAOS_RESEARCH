## What is established, and by what

1. Characteristic 3 and 5 at e = 3. Over GF(3) the pair f = y^3 + xz, g = x^3 + x y^2 + xz (PBW monomials x^i y^j z^k)
   gives a module U/(Uf + Ug) of length 27 with the pure Betti table (1, 2, 2, 1) on (0, 3, 9, 12); over GF(5) so does
   f = -yz - y^3 - x y^2 + x^2 y - 2 x^3, g = -2 yz - 2 xz - 2 x y^2 + x^3. Betti tables do not change under an
   extension of the base field, so the point is realized over every field of characteristic 3 and of characteristic 5.
   With Theorem A (characteristic 0 or at least 6) and Theorem C (characteristic 2): the point (1,2,2,1) of
   (0, 3, 9, 12) is realized over U exactly in the characteristics other than 2. [MV, two instruments, and rebuilt by
   the coordinating session; the theorems are those of the manuscript]
2. Characteristic 3, 5 and 7 at e = 4. Pure pairs, each of length 64 with the table (1, 2, 2, 1) on (0, 4, 12, 16):
   over GF(3), f = z^2 - y^2 z - xyz + x^2 y^2 + x^3 y + x^4, g = z^2 - y^2 z - y^4 - xyz - x^2 z - x^2 y^2 + x^4;
   over GF(5), f = 2 z^2 + 2 y^2 z - y^4 - 2 xyz - x y^3 - 2 x^2 z - 2 x^2 y^2 - x^3 y + 2 x^4,
   g = z^2 + 2 y^2 z + 2 y^4 - xyz - 2 x^3 y; over GF(7), f = 2 y^2 z + 2 y^4 - 2 xyz - x y^3 + 3 x^2 z - 2 x^2 y^2 + x^4,
   g = -2 y^2 z - xyz - x y^3 + 2 x^2 z + 3 x^2 y^2 - 3 x^3 y + 2 x^4. With Theorem A (characteristic 0 or at least 8)
   and Theorem C: the point (1,2,2,1) of (0, 4, 12, 16) is realized over U exactly in the characteristics other
   than 2. [MV, as above]
3. With the case e = 2 (EXP-003: the pair x^2 + y^2 + yx, x^2 + yx - z in characteristic 3): for e = 2, 3 and 4 the
   only characteristic in which the point (1,2,2,1) of (0, e, 3e, 4e) is not realized over U is 2.
4. e = 5: pure pairs exist over GF(3), for example f = y^5 + x z^2 + x y^2 z - x^2 yz - x^2 y^3 - x^3 y^2,
   g = y^3 z + y^5 - x z^2 + x y^4 - x^2 yz + x^4 y + x^5 (length 125). So the point of (0, 5, 15, 20) is realized in
   characteristic 3; characteristics 5 and 7 were not searched and stay open (Theorem A covers 0 and at least 11). [MV]
5. The family U/U(l_1^e, l_2^e) is not the only pure cyclic module on the ray: over GF(11) and GF(13), where it is
   pure, every one of the 217 pure spaces found at random lies outside its orbit. This agrees with the statement,
   derived before the run and reviewed since, that the pure pairs form a family of dimension at least 4e - 4 through
   <x^e, y^e> (context note `2026-11-20-pure-pairs-family-dimension.md`). The share of pure spaces among random ones

   GF(7), GF(9)): the order of magnitude that a pure locus of dimension 12 in a Grassmannian of dimension 14 gives.
   [MV for the counts; the comparison with q^(-2) is a heuristic]
6. The Hilbert function does not certify purity. Among the spaces with the pure Hilbert function, 1,248 of 3,192 over
   GF(3) at e = 3 have another Betti table (with a cancelling pair in degree 10, in degree 11 or in both), and so do 497 of 917 over GF(3)

## The refuted prediction

P2 rested on derivation 3 of the hypothesis: the locus D of spaces with rank of mu_12 at most 48 was taken to have
the expected codimension 2 in Gr(2, 9), with class 300 sigma_2 + 325 sigma_(1,1). That is false. D contains the
spaces whose two forms have images in U/(z) = k[x, y] with a common root: for such W the module maps onto
k[x, y]/(l), which has dimension 1 in every degree, so the rank of mu_12 is at most 48. That locus is the resultant
hypersurface, of codimension 1, and its modules have infinite length. So D is reducible, about 1/q of the spaces over
GF(q) lie in it, and the Porteous expression is not its class. The independent review of the family-dimension note
found this without reading the results of this experiment (record `bs-pure-family-dimension-review-2026-11-20` in the
coordination repository, finding R1). The conclusions drawn from derivation 3 in the hypothesis (D is not empty;
the components that contain pure points have dimension at least 12) hold for other reasons, given in the reviewed
note. Heuristic 5 concerns the pure locus and is not contradicted.

## Deviations from the hypothesis

- The text of gate G1 lists the pair (x^3, y^3) over GF(5) both among the pure cases and among the cases that are not
  pure. That was an error of the hypothesis: 5 is less than 2e = 6, so by Theorem A the pair is not pure, and it was
  tested and found so. Over GF(7) it was tested as pure.
- "Index in its family" was read as the index inside each family and field, so the fields of one family share seeds.
- The Hilbert function was computed only for the spaces with rank of mu_3e equal to the pure value (families A, C) or
  at most 48 (families B); the distribution of the rank is stored for all spaces.
- The extension fields are those of `uhlib` (GF(9) = GF(3)[a]/(a^2 + 1), GF(25) = GF(5)[a]/(a^2 + a + 1),
  GF(27) = GF(3)[a]/(a^3 + 2 a^2 + 1)); the same moduli were given to Singular.
- The results file kept in the repository is reduced (`reduce_results.py`): every count, every pure space with its
  basis, every space with the pure Hilbert function and another table without its basis. The full file (13.5 MB) is
  under the data root with its SHA-256 in the reduced file; the summary generated from the reduced file is identical.
- The whole run took 22 minutes.

## How could this be wrong?

- A realization is one module: its table was computed in two systems and rebuilt a third time from the printed
  polynomials. The risk left is a shared misreading of the algebra (the relation xy = yx + z, left modules), which the
  known answers of G1 and the agreement with Singular's own noncommutative arithmetic guard.
- The counts of pure spaces in the random families are samples and say nothing exact about the pure locus. The
  exhaustive count over GF(3) at e = 3 is exact for that field only.
- The statements "exactly in the characteristics other than 2" combine these modules with Theorems A and C, proved in
  the manuscript and reviewed.
- Nothing here bears on modules that are not cyclic; a module with this table is cyclic with two relations of degree e,
  so nothing is lost.
