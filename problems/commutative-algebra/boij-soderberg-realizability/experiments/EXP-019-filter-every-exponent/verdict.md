## Deviations from the hypothesis

- The order of the last cases was changed and two runs were interrupted, by the coordinating session. The machine
  was shared with other jobs and the filter ran at 10,000 to 17,000 members per second per process at d = 5 and
  d = 6 (35,000 at d = 4), so the budget of five hours could not hold the four cases that remained after (14, 5).
  The run of (15, 17), (16, 17), (16, 19) was stopped after 162 seconds and 13,107,200 members of (15, 17), and the
  remaining budget was given to (14, 11), which the hypothesis placed last, because it was the last pair with
  12 <= e <= 14 that was not settled. The run of (14, 11) was then stopped after 3,000 seconds and 380,108,800
  members, when EXP-020 had found pure pairs at that pair. Both decisions were taken without a passing member in
  the interrupted case; the cases are reported as not finished and the predictions on them as not decided.
- Time: 4,984 seconds accounted by the runner, plus about 162 and 3,000 seconds of the two interrupted runs, of
  the 18,000 allowed. At most 12 processes; the three cases with t = e - 2 at e = 15, 16 and the cases with d <= 3
  were run by the implementer with 6 processes for the tests in U, after 12 processes had brought the free memory
  down to 726 MB.
- The early stop overshoots 5 pure members where tables were already being computed when the fifth arrived (up to
  18 tables in a case); every table computed is reported.
- At the control (9, 13) the exponent of the table of the hypothesis, t = 3, was used, not the largest allowed
  (t = 5).
- The tests were written after the routine (the implementer's report says which tests were seen to fail first).

## How could this be wrong?

- A pure pair is a finite computation repeated by a second system and a second construction.
- Nothing is claimed at (14, 11), (15, 17), (16, 17), (16, 19) by this experiment: no passing member was found in
  the part of the samples that was run, which says little (fewer than two were expected at (14, 11)).
- The shares rest on few members where the conditions are many: 5 members at (13, 11), (13, 7), (14, 5).
