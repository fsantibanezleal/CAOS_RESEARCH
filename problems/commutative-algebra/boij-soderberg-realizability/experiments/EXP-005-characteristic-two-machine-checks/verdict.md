   among the 651 pairs of cubics, 530 are eventually constant with value 4 and 121 grow; the 63 single cubics grow. None
   has finite length. These are the counts the reviewer of the theorem obtained with separate code (530 and 121; 29 and
   6). [MV]
2. The random cases: every pair of quartics, quintics, sextics and septics, and every 2 by 3 or 3 by 4 matrix tested,
   has infinite length in characteristic 2, with the eventual value (a (a + 1) / 2) L.L whenever the function becomes
   constant. Over GF(16) every one of the 305 presentations becomes constant. [MV]
3. The bound b <= a + 1 of the theorem is sharp on the four shapes tested: with one more relation the general
   presentation has finite length. [MV]
4. Theorem A is confirmed at e = 6 and e = 7 for every prime up to 31 and over QQ, by two instruments. With EXP-002
   and EXP-003 (the rationals for e <= 8, the primes up to 23 for e <= 5) the machine range of the characteristic
   condition is the primes up to 31 for e = 6, 7 and up to 23 for e <= 5. [MV]

None of this adds a statement: the theorems are proved in the context notes and in the manuscript. The experiment

## A case to note

One presentation of the sharpness family, three quartics over GF(16) (case `C3|GF(2^4)|21`), does not have finite
length: its Hilbert function is 1, 2, 4, 6, 6, 6, 4, 2 and then 1 in every degree to the bound, in both instruments.
The prediction P3 is about a general presentation and holds (9 of 10 in that shape). In the geometric picture of the
proof this is what a common zero of the three sections looks like (three sections of the line bundle that meet in
one point of the surface), which is a condition of codimension one on the triple and so occurs over a field of 16
elements. Nothing is concluded from it beyond that.

## Deviations from the hypothesis

- The fields GF(4) and GF(16) were built with the moduli the hypothesis states (w^2 + w + 1, w^4 + w + 1), in both
  instruments, because the field constructor of `uhlib` chooses w^4 + w^3 + 1 for GF(16).
- "Index in its family" was read as the index inside each family and field (and 0 to 39 over the four shapes of C3),
  so the same seed is used in several families and fields; the draws differ because the field or the shape differs.
- For the 11 modules of infinite length in C4 the first instrument has no Betti table (its Tor computation needs
  finite length): they are judged by the Hilbert function and by Singular's table, as in EXP-003.
- The Hilbert function of the second instrument is read from its table of graded Betti numbers.
- No case reached its time limit; the whole run took 43 seconds.

  the extension fields rebuilt with the stored moduli (all equal, among them the case noted above); the 24 modules of
  C4 tallied against 2e.

## How could this be wrong?

- Both instruments could share a misreading of the stored relations. The second instrument's scripts are generated
  from the stored relations by code that shares no arithmetic with the first; the gate G1 ties the first instrument to
  an earlier experiment with its own second system.
- "Growing" and "eventually constant" are read on a finite range (six degrees at the bound). A function that became 0
  beyond the bound would contradict a proved theorem; a function that changed value beyond the bound would contradict
  S2. Neither can be excluded by a finite computation; the theorem does not rest on it.
- The random cases are samples. The exhaustive families cover quadrics and cubics over GF(2) only.
