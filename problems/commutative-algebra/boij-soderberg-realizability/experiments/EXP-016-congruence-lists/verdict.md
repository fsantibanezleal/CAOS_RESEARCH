## What is established, and by what

1. Pure pairs f = x^e + y^e, g = G(xy, z) over the prime field at eleven pairs that were not settled: (10, 17), (10,
   19), (12, 7), (12, 13), (12, 17), (12, 19), (12, 23), (14, 17), (14, 19), (16, 11), (16, 13). For example over
   GF(7) at e = 12: g = x^6 y^6 + 3 x^5 y^5 z + 5 x^4 y^4 z^2 + 6 x^2 y^2 z^4 + 6 x y z^5 + 2 z^6, of length 1728.
   [MV, two instruments, and rebuilt by the coordinating session] With the earlier results, every odd prime below 2e
   has a pure pair over GF(p) at e = 10 and at e = 12.
2. At (12, 7) the list has exactly one form, so over GF(7) the pairs of this shape with (C) that are pure are the
   pairs (x^12 + b y^12, G(xy, z)) for that one G; the sample of EXP-014 (150,000 of 1,098,056 members) had missed
   them.
3. Empty lists, by complete enumeration: over GF(5) and GF(11) at e = 14 (and over GF(3), where the pair is settled by
   a pull-back), over GF(9) at e = 8 and e = 14. So no pair of this shape with (C) is pure over those fields. The
   pairs (14, 5) and (14, 11) stay open.
4. Every form of a list that was checked in U is pure (71 forms): no counterexample to the theorem that, under (C),
   the congruence gives a pure pair (note `context/2026-11-20-one-congruence-and-the-betti-table.md`, reviewed). Every
   pure member of this shape stored by EXP-014 has its form in the list, so (C) held for all of them.
5. Over GF(169) at e = 6 there are 148 forms although over GF(13) there is none: the curve of forms has points over
   the quadratic extension.
6. The number of forms with the congruence and without (C) is much larger than the list in most cases (for example
   13,868 of 13,876 at (16, 11)): these are the forms with roots in the prime field or with two roots differing by 2e,
   which the theorem does not cover and which, in the cases tabled in earlier experiments, are not pure.

## Deviations from the hypothesis

- The run was made in parts. The implementer ran the gates, families A, N, X and 22 cases of family B, then reached
  the limit of its turns; its report is PARTIAL and is recorded as such in the coordination repository. The
  coordinating session ran the rest of (14, 19), started (14, 23) and stopped it after 71,303,168 of 3,404,825,447
  forms to keep the time for the other work of the day, ran (16, 13), the second instrument and the table, with the
  same runner and no change of code. So (14, 23) is not finished: its list is empty on the part examined, which says
  nothing about the pair. The two optional cases (16, 17), (16, 19) were not run.
- The implementer ran the cases with e = 16 before (14, 23), against the order of the hypothesis.
- For GF(p^2) the first instrument uses a table-driven field passed in memory to the numpy module construction of
  `uhlib`; the library was not edited.
- The time accounted by the runner covers only the commands that ended by themselves; the wall time of the whole
  run was about 105 minutes of the three hours allowed.

## How could this be wrong?

- A pure pair is a finite computation repeated by a second system and a second construction; that part does not
  depend on the theorem or on the enumeration.
- An empty list is a statement about the forms with (C) over that field. It rests on the fast test of the
  congruence never rejecting a form that satisfies it; the unit tests compare it with an exact reference on
  complete cases, and in the run the fast and the exact tests agreed on every form that passed.
- (14, 23) is unfinished, and (14, 5), (14, 11) are open: nothing here says that the point is not realized there.
