# The projection on the weight-zero summand: a necessary condition for finite length in characteristic p, and the linear family below 2e - 3

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: derivation [D] by the coordinating session; NOT yet reviewed by a reader other than the
writer. Machine checks by the coordinating session, listed in the last section (scripts and logs under the data
root, `caos-research/boij-soderberg-realizability/EXP-015-probe/`).

## Setting

U = U(H) over a field k, yx = xy - z, left modules. h = xy, D = k[h, z], sigma: d(h, z) -> d(h + z, z), tau its
inverse, A_n(h) = h (h + z) ... (h + (n - 1) z), B_n(h) = (h - z) ... (h - n z), with A_0 = B_0 = 1. From the note
`2026-11-20-pairs-over-the-invariants.md` (reviewed): x d = sigma(d) x and y d = tau(d) y for d in D (Lemma 1),
x^n y^n = A_n(h), y^n x^n = B_n(h), and with e_n = x^n for n >= 0 and e_n = y^(-n) for n < 0,

    U is the direct sum of the D e_n, n in Z, each free of rank 1 over D        (Lemma 2).

The monomial x^a y^b z^c lies in D e_(a-b). Call a - b the D-weight of the monomial (for a form of degree e it is
e - 2w, where w = b + c is the weight of the note `2026-11-20-weight-class-strata.md`). For u in U write u as the sum
of the u_n e_n with u_n in D, and let

    pi_0 : U -> D,     pi_0(u) = u_0.

pi_0 is left D-linear and onto. It sends U_(2d) onto the forms of D of degree d (as polynomials in h, z) and is zero
on U_n for n odd.

## The lemma

Lemma 1. For k, n in Z, e_k e_n lies in D e_(k+n). For n > 0, e_(-n) e_n = y^n x^n = B_n(h); for n < 0,
e_(-n) e_n = x^(-n) y^(-n) = A_(-n)(h).

Proof. For a, b >= 0: x^a x^b = e_(a+b) and y^a y^b = e_(-a-b). x^a y^b = A_b(h + (a - b) z) x^(a-b) for a >= b and
A_a(h) y^(b-a) for a < b (proof of Lemma 2 of the first note). y^b x^a = B_b(h) x^(a-b) for a >= b, and for a < b,
y^b x^a = y^(b-a) B_a(h) = B_a(h - (b - a) z) y^(b-a). QED.

Proposition 2. Let f be an element of U with f_0 = 0, and let b0 be the least n > 0 with f_n not zero and a0 the
least t > 0 with f_(-t) not zero (if there is no such n, or no such t, the corresponding form below is omitted).
Then for every u in U,

    pi_0(u f)  lies in the ideal ( A_(a0)(h), B_(b0)(h) ) of D.

Proof. u f is the sum over k, n of u_k e_k f_n e_n = u_k s_k(f_n) e_k e_n, where s_k = sigma^k (so s_k = tau^(-k) for
k < 0), and e_k e_n lies in D e_(k+n) by Lemma 1. Its part in the summand 0 comes from k = -n:

    pi_0(u f) = sum over n of  u_(-n) s_(-n)(f_n) e_(-n) e_n,

and e_(-n) e_n is B_n(h) for n > 0 and A_(-n)(h) for n < 0, by Lemma 1. B_n is divisible by B_(b0) for n >= b0 and
A_t by A_(a0) for t >= a0. QED.

Corollary 3. Let f, g be elements of U without component of D-weight 0, and let b0 (a0) be the least positive
D-weight (the least absolute value of a negative D-weight) occurring in f or in g. Then pi_0 induces a surjection of
left D-modules

    M = U/(Uf + Ug)  ->  D/( A_(a0)(h), B_(b0)(h) ),

which maps M_(2d) onto the part of degree d. Consequently:

(i) if no negative D-weight occurs, or no positive one, M is not of finite length;

(ii) if k has characteristic p > 0 and a0 + b0 >= p + 1, M is not of finite length;

(iii) in every case dim M_(2d) is at least the dimension of D/(A_(a0), B_(b0)) in degree d.

Proof. pi_0 is D-linear and onto, and by Proposition 2 it maps Uf + Ug into the ideal, which gives the surjection
and (iii). (i) The ideal is then principal, generated by a form of positive degree (or zero), and D modulo a
principal proper ideal is of infinite dimension. (ii) A_(a0) is the product of the h + a z for 0 <= a <= a0 - 1 and
B_(b0) that of the h - b z for 1 <= b <= b0. They share a linear factor exactly when a + b = 0 in k for some such a,
b; the sums a + b take every value from 1 to a0 + b0 - 1, so this happens exactly when p <= a0 + b0 - 1. Then the
ideal lies in (L) for a linear form L, and D/(L) is of infinite dimension. QED.

Remarks.

1. In characteristic 0, A_(a0) and B_(b0) are coprime and the corollary gives no obstruction to finite length; (iii)
   says that the part of M of D-weight 0 has dimension at least a0 b0.
2. The statement does not use that f and g are homogeneous for the degree, nor that there are two of them: it holds
   for any left ideal generated by elements without component of D-weight 0.
3. The same computation for the summand r in place of 0 gives the ideal (B_(b0-r)(h), sigma^r(A_(a0))) for
   0 <= r < b0, whose two generators share a factor when p lies between r + 1 and a0 + b0 - 1; r = 0 is the
   strongest case.

## The linear family

For e >= 4 the linear family is

    f = x^e + a_1 x y^(e-1) + a_2 y^(e-2) z,        g = y^e + b_1 x^(e-1) y + b_2 x^(e-2) z,

and the stratum S(e - 1; 0, 1) of the weight-class note consists of the pairs with f in the span of x^e, x y^(e-1),
y^(e-2) z and g in the span of y^e, x^(e-1) y, x^(e-2) z. The D-weights of these monomials are e, 2 - e, 2 - e and
-e, e - 2, e - 2. So for every pair of the stratum that spans a plane, a0 >= e - 2 and b0 >= e - 2 (or one sign does
not occur).

Corollary 4. Let k have characteristic p with p <= 2e - 5. Then for every pair of the stratum S(e - 1; 0, 1), in
particular for every member of the linear family, U/(Uf + Ug) is not of finite length. So the stratum holds no pure
pair, over k or over any extension.

Proof. a0 + b0 >= 2e - 4 >= p + 1, or one sign is missing; Corollary 3 (ii) or (i). QED.

This proves one half of the rule read off the computations (EXP-009, EXP-010, EXP-014): for e < p < 2e the stratum
of the linear family held pure members exactly when 2e - p <= 3. The half "none when 2e - p >= 5" is Corollary 4,
for every e. The other half, that pure members exist for p = 2e - 3 and p = 2e - 1, is computed for the eight pairs
(4, 5), (4, 7), (5, 7), (6, 11), (7, 11), (7, 13), (8, 13), (9, 17), which are all the pairs with e <= 9 and
2e - 3 <= p < 2e [MV: EXP-009, EXP-010, EXP-014], and is not proved here.

## The strata

Corollary 5. Let S(m; i, j) be a weight-class stratum in degree e, W the set of the w in {0, ..., e} congruent to i
or to j modulo m, and suppose e/2 is not in W (always the case for odd e). Let w_minus be the largest element of W
below e/2 and w_plus the smallest above. If one of them does not exist, or if k has characteristic p and

    2 (w_plus - w_minus) >= p + 1,

then no pair of the stratum has a module of finite length; in particular the stratum holds no pure pair over any
field of characteristic p.

Proof. A monomial of weight w has D-weight e - 2w. So b0 >= e - 2 w_minus and a0 >= 2 w_plus - e for every pair of
the stratum, and a0 + b0 >= 2 (w_plus - w_minus). Corollary 3. QED.

## How could this be wrong?

- The proof is a computation of one component of a product. The points to check are Lemma 1 (the four cases, and
  that no product e_k e_n with k + n = 0 other than the two named exists), the divisibility B_(b0) | B_n and
  A_(a0) | A_t, and the range of the sums a + b in Corollary 3 (ii).
- "Not of finite length" is stronger than "not pure" and is what the checks test; a single pair of an excluded
  stratum with a module of finite length would refute the lemma.
- Corollary 4 says nothing for p >= 2e - 3; the existence of pure members there is a computed fact on nine pairs.
- Novelty is not claimed: this is the standard weight decomposition of a generalized Weyl algebra, and no search of
  the literature was made for this note.
