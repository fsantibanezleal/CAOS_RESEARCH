# The modules U(H)/U(H)(x^e, y^e): Hilbert series, socle and resolution for every e

Date: 2026-11-19. Status: derivation [D], steps 1 to 7 complete, reviewed on 2026-11-19 by a reader other than the
writer (independent re-derivation of every step and an independent computation; record
`bs-proofs-review-2026-11-19` in the coordination repository; verdict: correct after minor fixes, all of them applied
in this revision; the converse of step 7 is the reviewer's). Machine support: EXP-002 and EXP-003 (e <= 8 over QQ, the
bad primes for e <= 5) and the reviewer's own runs (e <= 7, p <= 17).

## Setting and statement

U = U(H) = k<x,y,z>/(xy - yx - z, xz - zx, yz - zy), deg x = deg y = 1, deg z = 2; left modules.
M_e = U/U(x^e, y^e) (left ideal). The pure ray (0, e, 3e, 4e) has primitive point (1,2,2,1) (the Herzog-Kuhl ratios are
1/(12e^3) : 1/(6e^3) : 1/(6e^3) : 1/(12e^3)); over k[x,y,z] that point is excluded for every e and every field (a cyclic
module S/I with I generated by two forms has Krull dimension at least 1). Over U its Hilbert series is

    h_e(t) = (1 - 2t^e + 2t^(3e) - t^(4e)) / ((1-t)^2 (1-t^2)) = (1 + t + ... + t^(e-1))^2 (1 + t^2 + ... + t^(2e-2)).

Statement. Let k be a field and e >= 1. The module M_e has the pure resolution
0 <- U <- U(-e)^2 <- U(-3e)^2 <- U(-4e) <- 0 if and only if char k = 0 or char k >= 2e. In that case M_e has Hilbert
series h_e(t), length e^3 and a one-dimensional socle, in degree 4e - 4. For every prime p <= 2e - 1 and char k = p,
M_e is nonzero in degree 4e - 2 and does not have that resolution.

The case e = 1 is the trivial module (Erman and Sam). The case e = 2 is derived by hand in the EXP-002 hypothesis.

## Step 1: normal ordering, and M_e as a cokernel over k[z]

Use the PBW basis y^b x^a z^c (x to the right).

Normal ordering. From xy = yx + z and z central, x y^e = y^e x + e z y^(e-1) (induction on e). Hence, for all a, e >= 0,

    x^a y^e = sum over j of j! binom(a, j) binom(e, j) z^j y^(e-j) x^(a-j).

Proof by induction on a: multiplying the formula for a on the left by x and using x y^(e-j) = y^(e-j) x + (e-j) z y^(e-j-1),
the coefficient of z^j y^(e-j) x^(a+1-j) is j! binom(a,j) binom(e,j) + (j-1)! binom(a,j-1) binom(e,j-1) (e-j+1)
= j! binom(e,j) (binom(a,j) + binom(a,j-1)) = j! binom(a+1,j) binom(e,j). The coefficients are integers, so the identity
holds over Z and in every characteristic; this is what makes the analysis in characteristic p possible.

The cokernel. Right multiplication by x^e maps the PBW basis into itself (x is the rightmost letter:
(y^b x^a z^c) x^e = y^b x^(a+e) z^c), so U x^e is spanned by the monomials with a >= e and P = U/U x^e is a free
k[z]-module (z acting by left multiplication) with basis y^b x^a, 0 <= a < e, b >= 0. Let N be the image of U y^e in P.
Then M_e = P/N, and N is the image of the k[z]-linear map

    Phi(u_(a,b)) = sum over j with 0 <= j <= min(a, e) and a - j < e of j! binom(a,j) binom(e,j) z^j y^(b+e-j) x^(a-j),

over all a >= 0, b >= 0 (u_(a,b) stands for y^b x^a y^e). Every term has a - j < e and j <= e, so Phi(u_(a,b)) = 0 for
a >= 2e. The weight w = (exponent of y) - (exponent of x) is preserved (z has weight 0): a source u_(a,b) of weight
b - a + e maps to targets y^(b') x^(a') with b' - a' = b - a + e. So P, N and M_e split into blocks indexed by w.

## Step 2: the blocks are binomial Toeplitz matrices

In the block of weight w the rows are a' in [max(0, -w), e - 1] (with b' = a' + w) and the columns are a >= max(0, e - w)
(with b = a + w - e). The entry at (a', a) is (a - a')! binom(a, a - a') binom(e, a - a') z^(a - a')
= (a!/a'!) binom(e, a - a') z^(a - a'). The class of z^(n - a') y^(a' + w) x^(a') has total degree 2n + w, and column a
reaches that degree after multiplication by z^(n - a). So, in every characteristic, the piece of M_e of weight w and
degree 2n + w has dimension

    |R_n| - rank [ (a!/a'!) binom(e, a - a') ] on rows R_n = [max(0, -w), min(n, e - 1)] and columns C_n = [max(0, e - w), n].

The entries vanish unless 0 <= a - a' <= e, and a' <= e - 1, so the columns with a >= 2e are zero. Scaling row a' by a'!
(a' <= e - 1) and the nonzero columns by 1/a! (a <= 2e - 1) turns the numeric matrix into

    T[R_n, C_n] = [ binom(e, a - a') ].

These scalings are units exactly when (2e - 1)! is a unit of k, that is when char k = 0 or char k >= 2e. Under that
hypothesis

    HS(M_e)(t) = sum over w and n of ( |R_n| - rank T[R_n, C_n] ) t^(2n + w).

## Step 3: the ranks

T = [binom(e, a - a')] has a nonzero entry exactly when 0 <= a - a' <= e. Only contiguous blocks occur.

- 0 <= w <= e, n <= e - 1: rows 0..n, columns (e-w)..n. The square block on rows and columns (e-w)..n is upper
  unitriangular, so the rank is the number of columns, n - e + w + 1 (zero when n < e - w).
- 0 <= w <= e, n >= e: rows 0..e-1; the usable columns are (e-w)..min(n, 2e-1), in number U = min(n, 2e-1) - e + w + 1.
  The k by k block on rows 0..k-1 and columns (e-w)..(e-w+k-1), k = min(e, U), has determinant
  det[e_(c - s + s')](1^e) with c = e - w, where e_m(1^e) = binom(e, m) is the elementary symmetric polynomial at e ones.
  By the dual Jacobi-Trudi identity s_lambda = det[e_(lambda'_i - i + j)], with lambda' = (c^k), this is the Schur
  polynomial of the rectangle with c = e - w rows and k columns at e ones: the number of semistandard tableaux of that
  shape with entries at most e, positive because c <= e. So the rank is min(e, U).
- w = -q, 1 <= q <= e - 1 (rows from q, columns from e + q): for n < e + q there is no column; for n >= e + q the usable
  columns are (e+q)..min(n, 2e-1) and the block on rows q..q+k-1 and columns (e+q)..(e+q+k-1) is lower unitriangular
  (binom(e, e) on the diagonal, zero above), so the rank is min(e - q, U) with U = min(n, 2e-1) - e - q + 1.
- w > e: the columns start at 0 and the block on rows and columns 0..min(n, e-1) is upper unitriangular, so the block
  is surjective and contributes nothing. w <= -e: there are no rows.

The ranks cannot be larger (number of rows, number of nonzero columns). In characteristic 0 the minors above are nonzero.
In characteristic p >= 2e they are nonzero too: the unitriangular ones trivially, and the tableau count by the
hook-content formula s_(k^c)(1^e) = product over the cells of (e + content)/hook, in which every factor e + content lies
in [e - c + 1, e + k - 1], inside [1, 2e - 1], and every hook length is at most c + k - 1 <= 2e - 1; so the count is
prime to p. Steps 2 to 4 therefore hold verbatim when char k = 0 or char k >= 2e.

## Step 4: the count

Write q = |w| and n' = n - max(0, -w). From step 3 the dimension of the block of weight w in z-degree n is

    D_q(n') = min(n' + 1, e - q) for 0 <= n' <= e - 1,    D_q(n') = max(0, 2e - 1 - q - n') for n' >= e,

the same for w and -w, and zero for |w| >= e. Its generating function in s is the trapezoid
(1 + s + ... + s^(e-q-1)) (1 + s + ... + s^(e-1)). The class sits in total degree 2n + w = 2n' + q. Hence

    HS(M_e)(t) = (1 + t^2 + ... + t^(2e-2)) * sum over w, |w| < e, of t^|w| (1 + t^2 + ... + t^(2(e-|w|-1))),

and the last sum is (1 + t + ... + t^(e-1))^2 (group the monomials t^(a+b), a, b < e, by b - a). So HS(M_e)(t) = h_e(t)
for every e when char k = 0 or char k >= 2e. In particular M_e has finite length e^3. [Agrees with the machine Hilbert
functions of EXP-002 and EXP-003 for e = 2, ..., 8 over QQ, both instruments.]

## Step 5: the socle is one-dimensional

Hypothesis of this step: char k = 0 or char k >= 2e.

Left multiplication by y on P is the shift y^b x^a z^c to y^(b+1) x^a z^c: it is injective and its image yP is spanned by
the basis elements with b >= 1.

Kernel of y on M_e. Let m in P with y m in N, and write y m = sum of c_(a,b)(z) [y^b x^a y^e]. The terms with b >= 1 are
y times an element of N. For b = 0: x^a y^e vanishes in P when a >= 2e; for a < e it equals y g_a with

    g_a = sum over j <= a of j! binom(a, j) binom(e, j) z^j y^(e-j-1) x^(a-j)

(every term of x^a y^e has y-exponent e - j >= 1); for e <= a <= 2e - 1 its only term with y-exponent 0 is j = e, namely
e! binom(a, e) z^e x^(a-e) = (a!/(a-e)!) z^e x^(a-e), and these involve distinct basis elements x^(a-e). Since y m and all
the other terms lie in yP, comparing the components of y-exponent 0 gives c_(a,0)(z) (a!/(a-e)!) z^e = 0 for
e <= a <= 2e - 1; the integers a!/(a-e)! are products of factors at most 2e - 1, units under the hypothesis, so these
coefficients vanish. Injectivity of y on P then gives m = nu + sum over a < e of c_a(z) g_a with nu in N. Conversely
y g_a = x^a y^e lies in N. So the kernel of y on M_e is the k[z]-span of the classes of g_0, ..., g_(e-1), and g_a has
weight e - 1 - a >= 0.

Symmetry. sigma: x to y, y to -x, z to z is an automorphism of U (it respects the centrality of z, and
sigma(xy - yx) = -yx + xy = z; its inverse is x to -y, y to x). sigma(x^e) = y^e and sigma(y^e) = (-1)^e x^e, so sigma
preserves the left ideal U(x^e, y^e) and induces a sigma-semilinear bijection of M_e (sigma(u m) = sigma(u) sigma(m)).
M_e is graded by the weight because x^e and y^e are weight-homogeneous; the bijection sends weight w to -w and maps the
kernel of x onto the kernel of y and the kernel of y onto the kernel of x. So the kernel of x on M_e lies in the
weights <= 0.

The socle. x and y are weight-homogeneous, so every weight component of a socle element is in the socle; by the two
kernels the socle lies in weight 0, inside the weight 0 part of the kernel of y, which is the cyclic k[z]-module
generated by the class of g_(e-1) (the summands k[z][g_a] sit in distinct weights). A socle element is also killed by
z = xy - yx, and the elements of a homogeneous cyclic torsion k[z]-module killed by z form a space of dimension at
most 1. So the socle of M_e has dimension at most 1; it is not zero because M_e is a nonzero module of finite length;
and it contains the top degree 4e - 4, which is one-dimensional. So the socle of M_e is M_e in degree 4e - 4.

## Step 6: the resolution

Write r_1 = [x,[x,y]] = (yx - 2xy) x + x^2 y and r_2 = [y,[x,y]] = (-y^2) x + (2yx - xy) y. The minimal resolution of
the left trivial module is 0 <- U <- U(-1)^2 <- U(-3)^2 <- U(-4) <- 0 with maps (u, v) to ux + vy, (s, t) to (s, t) A with

    A = [[yx - 2xy, x^2], [-y^2, 2yx - xy]],

and w to w (y, -x): indeed y (yx - 2xy) + x y^2 = -r_2 = 0 and y x^2 - x (2yx - xy) = r_1 = 0 in U. The anti-automorphism
of U that fixes x and y maps r_1 and r_2 to themselves and turns this complex into the minimal resolution of the right
trivial module, whose last map is w to (y w, -x w). Tensoring it with a left module M gives
Tor_3(k, M) = {m in M : y m = 0 = x m}(-4), the socle of M shifted by 4 (no finiteness needed).

For M_e under the hypothesis of step 5: Tor_0 = k, Tor_1 = k(-e)^2 (x^e and y^e are minimal generators of the left ideal,
which has nothing below degree e), Tor_3 = k(-4e) by step 5, and the alternating sum of the Betti series is
HS(M_e)(t) (1-t)^2 (1-t^2) = 1 - 2t^e + 2t^(3e) - t^(4e) by step 4. Tor_0, Tor_1 and Tor_3 being known exactly and the global
dimension being 3, Tor_2 = k(-3e)^2 with no cancellation. The resolution is pure of type (0, e, 3e, 4e).

## Step 7: the converse (the reviewer's argument)

Let p be a prime with p <= 2e - 1 (so e >= 2) and char k = p. The integers e, ..., 2e - 1 contain a multiple m of p (e
consecutive integers when p <= e; m = p when e < p <= 2e - 1). Take the block of weight 0 with n = 2e - 1: rows
0..e-1, columns e..2e-1, an e by e matrix. The unscaled entries of column m are (m!/a'!) binom(e, m - a') with
a' <= e - 1 < m, and m!/a'! = (a'+1)(a'+2)...m contains the factor m; the whole column is 0 in k. So the rank is at most
e - 1, and by the characteristic-free formula of step 2 the piece of M_e of weight 0 and degree 2(2e - 1) = 4e - 2 is not
zero. A module with the resolution 0 <- U <- U(-e)^2 <- U(-3e)^2 <- U(-4e) <- 0 has Hilbert series h_e, of degree
4e - 4, so M_e does not have that resolution in characteristic p. (The ranks are forced: for a finite length module
with a pure resolution of type (0, e, 3e, 4e) and Betti numbers beginning 1, 2, the numerator
1 - 2t^e + b_2 t^(3e) - b_3 t^(4e) vanishes to order at least 2 at t = 1, which gives b_2 - b_3 = 1 and 3b_2 - 4b_3 = 2,
so b_2 = 2 and b_3 = 1.)

Together with steps 2 to 6: M_e has the pure resolution of type (0, e, 3e, 4e) with table (1,2,2,1) if and only if
char k = 0 or char k >= 2e. Machine: for e = 2, ..., 5 the primes p <= 23 at which M_e is not pure are exactly {2,3}, {2,3,5},
{2,3,5,7}, {2,3,5,7} (EXP-003 C3); the reviewer's run and a re-check with the experiment library give the same pattern
for e = 6 (not pure at p <= 11, pure at 13) and e = 7.

## What the computation at e = 2 shows about the characteristic

The only numeric matrices that matter at e = 2 are (2, 1) (weight 1, n = 1), [[2,1],[1,2]] (weight 1, n = 2), (1, 2)
(weight 0, n = 2) and [[1,0],[2,1]] (weight 0, n = 3), after scaling. The second has determinant 3: in characteristic 3
its rank drops and M_2 is too large in degree 5; in characteristic 2 the scaling by a!/a'! is not invertible and the
element 4xyz - 2z^2 vanishes. Both are instances of step 7 (p <= 2e - 1 = 3).

## Structure over k[z] (a correction of a remark in the EXP-002 hypothesis)

M_e is not free over k[z]/(z^e): at e = 2, M_2 = k[z]/(z^3) + (k[z]/(z^2))^2 (-1) + k[z]/(z) (-2), generated by the classes
of 1, x, y and xy - z/2. For e <= 5 (EXP-003 C2) there is one summand k[z]/(z^(2e - 1 - a - b)), generated in degree
a + b, for each pair a, b < e; this has the Hilbert series h_e(t) (both equal the sum over a, b < e of
t^(a+b) (1 + t^2 + ... + t^(2(2e - 2 - a - b)))). The statement for every e is not derived here and nothing above uses it.

## Remarks

- Not generic for e >= 4. The Hilbert series of M_e is not the maximal-rank series of two forms of degree e over U for
  e >= 4: in degree 4e - 4 the series (1 - 2t^e)/((1-t)^2 (1-t^2)) has a negative coefficient (for e = 4: 49 - 50), while
  M_e is one-dimensional there. So for e >= 4 a general pair of forms of degree e gives a smaller quotient and the pair
  (x^e, y^e) is special; EXP-003 D1 confirms that a random pair is not pure for e = 4, 5, 10.
- The ray (0, 2, 6, 8) in small characteristic. (x^2, y^2) realizes the point exactly in characteristic 0 and p >= 5. In
  characteristic 3 another pair does: f = x^2 + y^2 + yx, g = x^2 + yx - z over GF(3) has Hilbert function (1,2,2,2,1)
  and the Betti table (1,2,2,1) on (0,2,6,8) (the reviewer's pair, re-checked with the experiment library; it passes to
  every field of characteristic 3 by base change). The Hilbert function alone does not certify purity: the pair
  f = 2x^2 + 2y^2 + z + yx, g = y^2 + 2z over GF(3) has the same Hilbert function and a socle in degrees 3 and 4. In
  characteristic 2 no pair of quadrics has a finite length quotient (EXP-003).
- Literature. The searches of the review (Erman and Sam arXiv:1606.01867; Jiang, Li, Sun, Wang, Xiao and Yu
  arXiv:2605.25259, whose Theorem 3.7 answers Question 6.1 negatively over every field by a parity test over U(H); Sam
  and VandeBogert arXiv:2408.10408, Remark 5.11) found no statement about the modules U/U(x^e, y^e), about pure
  resolutions over U(H) beyond the trivial module and the sp_4 remark, or about a dependence on the characteristic.
