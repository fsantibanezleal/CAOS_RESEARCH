# Characteristic 2: a presentation with at most one more relation than generators, in one degree e >= 2, never gives a finite length module over U(H)

Date: 2026-11-19. Status: derivation [D] by the coordinating session, written after EXP-003; not yet reviewed. It
generalizes the statement of EXP-003 (pairs of quadrics) and explains its three observations on the rays (0,2,6,8),
(0,3,9,12) and (0,2,10,12). Three numbers it predicts were then read in the EXP-003 artifacts and agree (last
section). To be read by a reviewer other than the writer before any claim (backlog BSB-017).

## Statement

Let k be a field of characteristic 2 and U = U(H) = k<x,y,z>/(xy - yx - z, z central), deg x = deg y = 1, deg z = 2;
left modules.

Theorem. Let e >= 2, let 1 <= a and b <= a + 1, and let M = U^a / (U F_1 + ... + U F_b) with F_1, ..., F_b in (U_e)^a.
Then M has infinite length.

Corollary 1. For every e >= 2 and every two forms f, g of degree e, U/(Uf + Ug) has infinite length. So over a field of
characteristic 2 the primitive point (1,2,2,1) of the ray (0, e, 3e, 4e) is the Betti table of no finite length module
over U(H), for every e >= 2; it is the Betti table of no finite length module over k[x,y,z] in any characteristic (two
forms cannot cut out a finite length quotient); and in characteristic 0 it is realized over U(H) by U/U(x^e, y^e) for
every e (the family of the other context note). The answer to Question 6.1 of Erman and Sam on these points depends on
the characteristic for every e >= 2.

Corollary 2. More generally, in characteristic 2 a primitive point with beta_1 = beta_0 + 1 on a ray with d1 >= 2 is
realizable over neither ring (over k[x,y,z] a finite length module has beta_1 >= beta_0 + 2). Among the rays with
d3 <= 12 these are (0,2,6,8), (0,3,9,12) (table (1,2,2,1)) and (0,2,10,12) (table (2,3,3,2)): the three rays on which
the searches of EXP-003 never found a finite length module in characteristic 2.

The hypothesis e >= 2 is necessary: U/(Ux + Uy) = k, and the Kostant-type points on rays with d1 = 1 are realized in
characteristic 2 (EXP-003, claim B1).

## Step 1: the centre and the two parities (characteristic 2)

x^2, y^2 and z are central (x^2 y - y x^2 = 2xz, y^2 x - x y^2 = -2yz), C = k[X, Y, Z] with X = x^2, Y = y^2, Z = z is a
polynomial ring, and U is free over C on 1, x, y, yx (EXP-003, addendum 1 of the verdict). Left multiplication on that
basis, from xy = yx + z:

    x.x = X      x.y = yx + Z      x.yx = X y + Z x
    y.x = yx     y.y = Y           y.yx = Y x
    yx.x = X y   yx.y = Y x + Z y  yx.yx = XY + Z yx

Put U+ = C + C yx (the even part) and U- = C x + C y (the odd part); C lies in even degrees. Left multiplication by yx
preserves each part, and left multiplication by x or y exchanges them. On U+ in the basis (1, yx), and on U- in the
basis (x, y), left multiplication by yx is the C-linear map with matrix

    T+ = [[0, XY], [1, Z]]        T- = [[0, Y], [X, Z]]

(columns are the images of the basis vectors), and both satisfy t^2 = Z t + XY.

## Step 2: one parity of M is a module over a quadric

Let B = C[t]/(t^2 + Z t + XY). Give X, Y, Z, t degree 1 (so the degree in U is twice this degree, plus the degree of
the basis element). B is a graded hypersurface ring of dimension 3, free over C on 1, t, and Sigma = Proj B is the
quadric t^2 + Z t + XY = 0 in P^3; its partial derivatives are Z, t, Y, X, so it is smooth, and over the algebraic
closure it is P^1 x P^1 with O(1) = O(1,1).

Through the action of yx, U+ and U- are graded B-modules. U+ is free of rank 1 on the vector 1 (T+ sends (1,0) to
(0,1)), so U+ = B, with alpha + beta yx corresponding to alpha + beta t. U- is not free: the map
phi(alpha x + beta y) = X alpha + t beta is B-linear (phi(yx.(alpha x + beta y)) = X Y beta + t (X alpha + Z beta)
= t X alpha + (XY + tZ) beta = t (X alpha + t beta)) and injective, with image the ideal J = (X, t); a second
embedding, alpha x + beta y to (t + Z) alpha + Y beta, has image J' = (t + Z, Y). J and J' have no common zero on
Sigma (X = t = Y = t + Z = 0 forces the origin), so the sheaf N- of U- on Sigma is a line bundle. J is the ideal of the
line X = t = 0, which lies on Sigma, and phi raises the degree by 1, so N- = O(1)(-line) = O(0,1) for one of the two
rulings. N+ = O.

Let e = 2m (even) or e = 2m + 1 (odd), m >= 1 in both cases. If e is even every entry of F_j lies in U+ and has the
form alpha + beta yx with alpha of degree m and beta of degree m - 1 in X, Y, Z; if e is odd every entry lies in U- and
has the form alpha x + beta y with alpha, beta of degree m. Write P for the part of U that contains the entries (U+ or
U-) and P^a for the corresponding part of U^a. The left submodule generated by F_j is C F_j + C x F_j + C y F_j
+ C yx F_j, and its part in P^a is C F_j + C yx F_j = B F_j. So M has the direct summand (as a C-module)

    M_P = P^a / (B F_1 + ... + B F_b),

a graded B-module. If M has finite length, so has M_P.

## Step 3: M_P is not of finite length

Suppose M_P has finite length. Then the map of sheaves on Sigma

    psi: O^b to L^a,   L = N(m) = O(m, m) (e even) or O(m, m + 1) (e odd),

given by F_1, ..., F_b is surjective at every point (the cokernel of psi is the sheaf of M_P). Both bidegrees are
positive because m >= 1, so L is ample and L.L = 2m^2 (e even) or 2m(m + 1) (e odd) is positive.

- b < a: a surjection from a bundle of smaller rank does not exist.
- b = a: psi is an isomorphism, so the determinant gives O = L^a (tensor power), impossible for an ample L.
- b = a + 1: the kernel K of psi is a line bundle and 0 to K to O^(a+1) to L^a to 0 is exact, so c(K) c(L^a) = 1. In
  degree 1, c_1(K) = -a L; in degree 2, binom(a, 2) L.L + (a L).(-a L) = 0, that is (a(a + 1)/2) L.L = 0,
  impossible.

Hence M_P has a point of Sigma (over the algebraic closure) in its support, its Krull dimension is at least 1, and M is
not of finite length. For a = 1 and b = 2 this is the statement that two curves of the same ample class on P^1 x P^1
meet. For e even and b <= a + 1 there is a proof without sheaves: M_P = coker(B^b to B^a) and the ideal of maximal
minors, generated by forms of positive degree, has height at most b - a + 1 <= 2 in the three-dimensional
Cohen-Macaulay ring B.

Where the argument stops: with e = 1 (m = 0, odd) L = O(0,1) has L.L = 0 and two sections of it need not meet (the
trivial module); with b >= a + 2 the expected codimension of the degeneracy locus is 3 and nothing is forced.

## Step 4: the corollaries

A module with Betti table (1,2,2,1) on (0, e, 3e, 4e) has Hilbert series (1 - 2t^e + 2t^(3e) - t^(4e))/((1-t)^2 (1-t^2)),
a polynomial, so it has finite length; it is cyclic with two relations of degree e, that is U/(Uf + Ug): excluded by the
theorem with a = 1, b = 2. In general a module with a pure table on (0, d1, d2, d3) with beta_0 = a and beta_1 = b is
U^a modulo b elements of degree d1; Question 6.1 asks for finite length modules, and the theorem excludes a finite
length module with b <= a + 1 and d1 >= 2 in characteristic 2.

## Predictions read against EXP-003 (machine support, not a proof)

When the zero locus of psi is finite and reduced, M_P is supported on that many lines of the cone and its Hilbert
function is eventually that number in every second degree; the other parity behaves in the same way (not derived
here). The numbers of points are L.L for a = 1 and, by Porteous' formula for the corank one locus of O^(a+1) to L^a,
(a(a + 1)/2) L.L in general:

| presentation | e | m | predicted number | eventual Hilbert function in the EXP-003 searches (characteristic 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| two quadrics (ray (0,2,6,8)) | 2 | 1 | 2 m^2 = 2 | 2 (the 29 subspaces over GF(2) with nonzero determinant; the reviewer's enumeration) |
| two cubics (ray (0,3,9,12)) | 3 | 1 | 2 m (m + 1) = 4 | 4 on 34 of 40 seeds over GF(2), 39 of 40 over GF(4), 40 of 40 over GF(16) |
| 2 by 3 matrix of quadrics (ray (0,2,10,12)) | 2 | 1 | 3 . 2 m^2 = 6 | 6 on 28 of 40 seeds over GF(2), 39 of 40 over GF(4), 40 of 40 over GF(16) |

The remaining seeds have Hilbert functions that grow linearly (a positive-dimensional zero locus), as small fields
allow. The three numbers were derived before the artifacts were read.
