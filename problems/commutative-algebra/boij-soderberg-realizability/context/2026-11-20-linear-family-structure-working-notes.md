# The linear family as a sum of D-modules governed by one quadratic: working notes

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: WORKING NOTES of the coordinating session. Not reviewed, not a derivation to cite, not for
the manuscript. They record a structure checked by machine on complete small cases and the open steps, so that a
later session can turn it into statements with proofs (backlog BSB-036). What is proved about the linear family is
in `2026-11-20-weight-zero-projection.md` (Corollary 4).

## The structure (checked by machine, not proved in writing)

Notation of `2026-11-20-pairs-over-the-invariants.md`: h = xy, D = k[h, z], sigma, tau, A_n, B_n, e_n, and U the
direct sum of the D e_n. The linear family, with c = e - 2 and w = 2e - 2:

    f = x^e + l(h, z) y^c,      g = y^e + l'(h, z) x^c,
    l = a_1 h + a_2 z,          l' = b_1 (h + c z) + b_2 z

(f = x^e + a_1 x y^(e-1) + a_2 y^(e-2) z and g = y^e + b_1 x^(e-1) y + b_2 x^(e-2) z). The products e_n f and e_n g link
the generators v_j and v_(j+w) only, so M = U/(Uf + Ug) splits over the classes of j modulo w. Put

    a_f(n) = A_n (n <= c),  sigma^(n-c)(A_c) (n >= c);        a_g(n) = A_n (n <= e),  sigma^(n-e)(A_e) (n >= e);
    b_g(t) = B_t (t <= c),  tau^(t-c)(B_c) (t >= c);          b_f(t) = B_t (t <= e),  tau^(t-e)(B_e) (t >= e);
    T_j  = a_g(j + e) - sigma^(j+e)(l') sigma^(j+c)(l) a_f(j + c),
    T'_j = b_f(e - j) - tau^(e-j)(l) tau^(c-j)(l') b_g(c - j).

With the quadratic

    q = h (h + z) - l(h) l'(h + 2z)

one finds T_j = a_f(j + c) sigma^(j+c)(q) and T'_j = b_g(c - j) tau^(e-j)(q): every annihilator below is a product of
consecutive linear factors and of a shift of the one form q.

- For 2 - e <= i <= e - 2 the class of i gives a cyclic summand (D/J_i) v_i, with v_i of degree |i| and J_i
  generated by T_i, T'_i, T_(i+w) sigma^(i+c)(l) a_f(i + c), T'_(i-w) tau^(c-i)(l') b_g(c - i) and further forms of
  higher
  degree. T_i has degree e - max(0, -i) and T'_i degree e - max(0, i); the further generators have degree above the
  socle degree of D/(T_i, T'_i) when T_i and T'_i are coprime, so then J_i = (T_i, T'_i) and the summand has
  dimension e (e - |i|).
- The class of e - 1 gives a summand N on two generators a = v_(1-e), b = v_(e-1), both of degree e - 1, with the
  relations tau(l) a + (h - z) b = 0, h a + sigma(l') b = 0, T'_(1-e) a = 0, T_(e-1) b = 0 and further ones of higher
  degree. The determinant of the first two is - tau(q).
- The sum of the e (e - |i|) over |i| <= e - 2 is e^3 - 2e, and if N has the Hilbert function 2, 2, ..., 2 in the
  degrees e - 1, e + 1, ..., 3e - 3 (length 2e) the total is the pure Hilbert function (the series add up to
  (1 - t^e)^2 (1 - t^(2e))/((1 - t)^2 (1 - t^2)); computed by hand).

Machine check (`check_linear_structure.py`, data root `caos-research/boij-soderberg-realizability/EXP-015-probe/`):
the Hilbert function predicted from these generators equals the one computed in U with `uhlib` for every one of the
625 members over GF(5) at e = 4, and for random members at four more pairs: 400 at (5, 7), 300 at (5, 5), 150 at
(6, 11), 150 at (6, 7), without a difference.

## What it explains and what it suggests

1. The "none" half of the rule (proved in the note on the weight-zero projection): for p <= 2e - 5 the factors
   A_(e-2) of T_0 and B_(e-2) of T'_0 share a linear factor, and every generator of J_0 carries one of the two.
2. For p >= 2e - 3 the products of linear factors in T_i and T'_i are coprime for every i. If q is irreducible over
   k the shifts of q are coprime to them and to each other (for p not dividing e - 1), so the cyclic summands have
   the right dimensions. At (4, 5): 160 members have q irreducible; 132 of them have the pure Hilbert function and
   28 have length 65, the excess being one dimension of N in degree 3e - 1 (`probe_linear_q.py`).
3. A condition for N (not proved): N has length 2e exactly when the two relations T'_(1-e) a and T_(e-1) b are
   independent in degree e modulo the first two relations. With theta a root of q in the quadratic extension and
   a bar for its conjugate this reads: T'_(1-e)(theta + 1) theta T_(e-1)(theta-bar + 1) l(theta-bar) is not in k. It
   fails on about one member in p, which matches 28 of 160.
4. A possible proof of existence at p = 2e - 1 over the prime field, for every e (even and odd), from the short
   pairs l = b z, l' = d z (q = h^2 + h z - b d z^2): the condition of item 3 becomes (theta + 1) Pi(theta)^2 not in
   GF(p), with Pi the product of the theta - j for e - 1 <= j <= 2e - 4, and the polynomial
   F(t) - F(-1 - t), F(t) = (t + 1) Pi(t)^2, has degree 2e - 3 = p - 2, fewer than the p - 1 elements theta of
   GF(p^2) outside GF(p) with theta + theta-bar = -1. So some b d works for the Hilbert function. NOT checked: the
   formula of item 3, and the Betti table.
5. The Betti table is a separate question: in the linear family the pure Hilbert function does not give the pure
   table (EXP-009 at (4, 5): 64 of the 159 members examined with the pure Hilbert function have another table). A
   computation of the socle through this decomposition, as in the note on one congruence, is the step to do.

## The stratum of the central pairs for odd e (sketch, nothing checked by machine)

For odd e and s = (e - 1)/2 the stratum S((e + 1)/2; 0, (e - 1)/2), which holds the central pairs and the pure pairs
found at (7, 3), (7, 5), (7, 7), (9, 7), consists of the pairs

    f = x^e + alpha(h, z) y,      g = y^e + beta(h, z) x,      alpha, beta forms of degree s in D.

The products e_n f and e_n g link v_j and v_(j+e+1) only. Computing them as for the linear family gives, on paper:

- one cyclic summand, generated by v_0, killed by Theta_0 = A_e - h sigma(alpha) sigma^e(beta) and by
  Theta'_0 = B_e - (h - z) tau^e(alpha) tau(beta);
- for u = 1, ..., e a summand N_u on the two generators v_(-u), v_(e+1-u), with the relations

      tau^(u-1)(alpha) v_(-u) + B_(u-1)(h) v_(e+1-u) = 0,        A_(e-u)(h) v_(-u) + sigma^(e-u)(beta) v_(e+1-u) = 0,
      tau^u(Theta'_0) v_(-u) = 0,                                sigma^(e+1-u)(Theta_0) v_(e+1-u) = 0,

  and further ones of higher degree.

One form governs all of them: with

    Delta = alpha(h) beta(h + (e - 1) z) - A_(e-1)(h)        (degree e - 1),

the determinant of the first two relations of N_u is tau^(u-1)(Delta), and Theta_0 = - h sigma(Delta),
Theta'_0 = - (h - z) tau^e(Delta). So the conditions for the pure Hilbert function should be conditions on the roots of
Delta and a small number of scalar conditions, as one congruence is for even e. The shares of members with the pure
Hilbert function in the samples of EXP-010 and EXP-014 (about p^(-4) at e = 7 and p^(-6) at e = 9) suggest e - 3
conditions on the 2s + 2 = e + 1 coefficients, not the (e - 1)(e - 3)/4 of the count by degrees. If that is right, the
pure pairs of this stratum could be listed by a computation in D, as EXP-016 does for even e, and the pairs (9, 5),
(9, 11), (9, 13) decided inside this stratum. Backlog BSB-035.

### Added later on 2026-11-20: the decomposition is checked by machine, and a filter that matches every stored table

The decomposition above for odd e was then compared with the computation in U (`check_odd_structure.py`, data root
`caos-research/boij-soderberg-realizability/EXP-017-probe/`): the predicted Hilbert function equals the computed one
on random members at (5, 3), (5, 5), (5, 7), (7, 3), (7, 5) (150, 150, 340, 80, 80 members), without a difference.

In the normalized form (apply sigma^(u-1)): N_u is the quotient of the ideal (A_(u-1), alpha) of R = D/(Delta) by
X A_(u-1) and X' alpha, where X = (h - 2z) tau^(e+1)(Delta) and X' = (h + e z) sigma^(e+1)(Delta) do not depend on u.
For
u = 1 and u = e the length e (e - 1) asks for

    (F1)  X' alpha = c X   modulo Delta,  c of degree at most s,
    (F2)  X sigma^(e-1)(beta) = c' X'   modulo Delta,  c' of degree at most s,

which are s - 1 conditions each, e - 3 together. Then c c' = A_(e-1) + mu Delta for a scalar mu, and
(c/alpha)(c'/sigma^(e-1) beta) = mu at the points h = 0, -1, ..., -(e - 2).

Observed, on settled pairs (`probe_odd_ratios.py`, `probe_odd_filter_on_stored.py`):

- at (5, 7), among 60 random members with Delta of degree 2s coprime to X and X' (F0) and with (F1), (F2): the Hilbert
  function is pure exactly when mu is not zero (F3), or when mu = 0 with c proportional to sigma^s(A_s) and c' to A_s;
  for the other factorizations c c' = A_(e-1) the middle summands N_u are too long;
- on the 130 members of this stratum stored by EXP-010 and EXP-014 with a computed Betti table (seven settled pairs,
  e = 5, 7, 9), the table is pure exactly when (F0), (F1), (F2), (F3) hold: 59 pure members, all passing; 71 members
  with the pure Hilbert function and another table, 65 failing (F0) and 6 with mu = 0. No exception.

So the conjecture to prove (BSB-035) is the analogue of the two theorems for even e: for these pairs, pure if and
only if (F0) to (F3), with (F0) in the role of the hypothesis (C) and mu not zero in the role of lambda not zero.
EXP-017 uses (F0) to (F3) as a filter for a random search at (9, 11), (9, 13), (11, 7), (13, 5) and checks in U what
passes.

Why (F3) appears (a computation by hand, to be written as a proof). Suppose Delta has no root among 0, -1, ...,
-(e - 2), so that A_(u-1) and alpha are coprime to Delta. By (F1), X' alpha = c X in R, and X is not a zero divisor,
so N_u is the quotient of I_u = (A_(u-1), alpha) by X K_u with K_u = (A_(u-1), c), and

    length N_u = e (e - 1) + length R/K_u - length R/I_u.

Now (Delta, A_(u-1), alpha) = (A_(u-1), alpha) because Delta = alpha sigma^(e-1)(beta) - A_(e-1) and A_(u-1) divides
A_(e-1); its colength is (u - 1) s. If mu is not zero, Delta = (c c' - A_(e-1))/mu lies in (A_(u-1), c), so
(Delta, A_(u-1), c) = (A_(u-1), c), of colength (u - 1) s as well, since c does not vanish at the points 0, ..., -(e
- 2)
(there (c/alpha)(c'/sigma^(e-1) beta) = mu). So every N_u has length e (e - 1), with (F1) and (F2) alone: the middle
summands impose nothing more. If mu = 0, c divides A_(e-1) and shares a factor with A_(u-1) for some u unless
c = sigma^s(A_s) (then use the mirror presentation for u > s + 1): this is the special factorization seen at (5, 7).
What is missing for a statement: the cyclic summand and the generators of higher degree, the lower bound
length N_u >= e (e - 1) for the converse, the case where Delta has roots in the prime field (pure members with such
roots exist), and the socle for the Betti table.
