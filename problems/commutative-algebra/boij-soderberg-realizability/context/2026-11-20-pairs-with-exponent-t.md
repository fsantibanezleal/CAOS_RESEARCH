# The pairs x^e + alpha(xy, z) y^t, y^e + beta(xy, z) x^t: the decomposition over k[xy, z], two congruences, and the pure Hilbert function

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: derivation by the coordinating session, checked by machine on small cases (section 7);
reviewed by a reader other than the writer on 2026-11-20 (correct; eight minor repairs, applied here; the review
recomputed the statements with its own code on 1,250 members). It proves that the members that
pass the filter of EXP-017 and EXP-019 have the pure Hilbert function (section 5) and, when Delta has no common
factor with A_(e-t), the pure Betti table (section 5b); the Betti table without that condition is not proved
(section 6).

## 0. Setting and notation

k is a field of characteristic p >= 0. U is the enveloping algebra of the Heisenberg Lie algebra, generated by x, y,
z with xy - yx = z and z central, graded by deg x = deg y = 1, deg z = 2. Modules are left modules. Notation of
`2026-11-20-pairs-over-the-invariants.md`:

- h = xy and D = k[h, z], a polynomial ring inside U; D_n is the space of forms of D-degree n (elements of degree 2n
  of U); sigma is the automorphism of D with sigma(h) = h + z, sigma(z) = z, and tau = sigma^(-1);
- x d = sigma(d) x and y d = tau(d) y for d in D;
- A_n = h (h + z) ... (h + (n-1) z) = x^n y^n and B_n = (h - z)(h - 2z) ... (h - nz) = y^n x^n, for n >= 0;
- e_n = x^n for n >= 0 and e_n = y^(-n) for n < 0; U is the direct sum of the D e_n, a free left D-module, and
  e_n d = sigma^n(d) e_n.

Identities used below: A_(a+b) = A_a sigma^a(A_b), B_(a+b) = B_a tau^a(B_b), sigma^n(B_n) = A_n, tau^n(A_n) = B_n.

Fix e >= 3 and t with 1 <= t <= e - 2 and t = e modulo 2; put d = (e - t)/2 and w = e + t. Fix alpha, beta in D_d and

    f = x^e + alpha y^t,      g = y^e + beta x^t,      M = U/(Uf + Ug).

f and g are homogeneous of degree e. Put

    Delta = alpha sigma^(e-t)(beta) - A_(e-t)                     (in D_(2d)),
    L  = tau^t(B_t)   = (h - (t+1) z)(h - (t+2) z) ... (h - 2t z),
    L' = sigma^e(A_t) = (h + e z)(h + (e+1) z) ... (h + (e+t-1) z),
    X  = L tau^w(Delta),        X' = L' sigma^w(Delta)            (in D_e),

and, for n, m >= 0,

    a_f(n) = A_n (n <= t),  sigma^(n-t)(A_t) (n >= t);        a_g(n) = A_n (n <= e),  sigma^(n-e)(A_e) (n >= e);
    b_f(m) = B_m (m <= e),  tau^(m-e)(B_e) (m >= e);          b_g(m) = B_m (m <= t),  tau^(m-t)(B_t) (m >= t),

of D-degrees min(n, t), min(n, e), min(m, e), min(m, t).

The conditions:

- (C0) z does not divide Delta; Delta is coprime to X and to X'; p = 0 or p >= 2t.
- (F1) X' alpha = c X modulo Delta for a form c in D_d.
- (F2) X sigma^(e-t)(beta) = c' X' modulo Delta for a form c' in D_d.
- (F3) c c' is not equal to A_(e-t).

Two consequences of (C0). The first clause follows from the second: if z divides Delta it divides tau^w(Delta) and
X. And p does not divide w = e + t: in characteristic p, sigma^p is the identity, so p dividing w would give
tau^w(Delta) = Delta and X = L Delta.

When z does not divide Delta and Delta is coprime to X and X', the forms c and c' are unique (if c_1 X = c_2 X modulo
Delta then Delta divides c_1 - c_2, of smaller degree), and under (F1) and (F2)

    X X' alpha sigma^(e-t)(beta) = c c' X X'  modulo Delta,   so   c c' = alpha sigma^(e-t)(beta) = A_(e-t)  modulo Delta,

hence c c' - A_(e-t) = mu Delta for a scalar mu (both sides are in D_(2d)). (F3) says mu is not zero.

With z = 1 these are the tests (F0) to (F3) of the filter of EXP-017 (t = 1) and EXP-019 (every t): (F0) there is "Delta
has degree 2d and is coprime to X and X'", that is (C0) without the condition on p.

## 1. The presentation

**Lemma 1 (products).** For n >= 0 and m >= 0:

    e_n f    = e_(n+e) + sigma^n(alpha) a_f(n) e_(n-t),
    e_(-m) g = e_(-m-e) + tau^m(beta) b_g(m) e_(t-m),

and for m >= 1 and n >= 1:

    e_(-m) f = b_f(m) e_(e-m) + tau^m(alpha) e_(-m-t),
    e_n g    = a_g(n) e_(n-e) + sigma^n(beta) e_(n+t).

*Proof.* x^n alpha y^t = sigma^n(alpha) x^n y^t, and x^n y^t = A_n y^(t-n) for n <= t and
x^(n-t) A_t = sigma^(n-t)(A_t) x^(n-t) for n >= t. y^m x^e = B_m x^(e-m) for m <= e and
y^(m-e) B_e = tau^(m-e)(B_e) y^(m-e) for m >= e, and y^m alpha y^t = tau^m(alpha) y^(m+t). The two formulas for g are
obtained in the same way. []

Write v_j for the class of e_j in M; it has degree |j|. Since U is the direct sum of the D e_n, the left ideal
Uf + Ug is the D-span of the e_n f and e_n g, so M is the quotient of the free D-module on the v_j, j in Z, by the
relations

    R1(n), n >= 0:   v_(n+e) + sigma^n(alpha) a_f(n) v_(n-t) = 0,
    R2(m), m >= 1:   b_f(m) v_(e-m) + tau^m(alpha) v_(-m-t) = 0,
    R3(m), m >= 0:   v_(-m-e) + tau^m(beta) b_g(m) v_(t-m) = 0,
    R4(n), n >= 1:   a_g(n) v_(n-e) + sigma^n(beta) v_(n+t) = 0.

Every relation involves two indices that differ by w. Put, for j >= -t and i <= t,

    up(j) = sigma^(j+t)(alpha) a_f(j+t),            down(i) = tau^(t-i)(beta) b_g(t-i),
    T_j  = a_g(j+e) - sigma^(j+e)(beta) up(j),       T'_i = b_f(e-i) - tau^(e-i)(alpha) down(i),

and U_k(j) = up(j) up(j+w) ... up(j+(k-1)w), V_k(i) = down(i) down(i-w) ... down(i-(k-1)w), with U_0 = V_0 = 1.

**Proposition 1 (splitting).** M is, as a graded D-module, the direct sum of

- for -t <= j <= t, a cyclic module M_j = (D/J_j) v_j with v_j in degree |j|, where J_j is generated by the forms
  G+_k(j) = T_(j+kw) U_k(j) and G-_k(j) = T'_(j-kw) V_k(j), k >= 0 (so G+_0 = T_j and G-_0 = T'_j);
- for 1 <= m <= e - t - 1, a module N_m on two generators a = v_(-m-t) (degree m + t) and b = v_(e-m) (degree
  e - m), with the relations

      tau^m(alpha) a + B_m b = 0,          A_(e-t-m) a + sigma^(e-t-m)(beta) b = 0,
      G-_k(-m-t) a = 0,                    G+_k(e-m) b = 0          (k >= 0).

*Proof.* The relations link v_j and v_(j+w) only, so M is the direct sum over the classes of j modulo w. R1(n) is
v_(n+e) = - up(n-t) v_(n-t); it expresses every v_j with j >= e through v_(j-w), and j - w >= -t. Repeating, for
j = j_0 + kw with -t <= j_0 < e and k >= 1, v_j = (-1)^k U_k(j_0) v_(j_0); each R1(n) is used once, for the
generator v_(n+e). In the same way R3(m) is v_(-m-e) = - down(t-m) v_(t-m), and for i = i_0 - kw with -e < i_0 <= t
and k >= 1, v_i = (-1)^k V_k(i_0) v_(i_0). So M is the quotient of the free module on the v_j with |j| < e by the
relations R2 and R4 after substitution. In R4(n), put j = n - e > -e. If j < -t, then j + w lies between t + 1 and
e - 1, both generators remain, and with j = -m - t the relation is A_(e-t-m) v_(-m-t) + sigma^(e-t-m)(beta) v_(e-m)
(here n = e - t - m <= e). If j >= -t, write j = j_0 + kw with -t <= j_0 < e, k >= 0; the relation becomes
(-1)^k (a_g(n) - sigma^n(beta) up(j)) U_k(j_0) v_(j_0) = (-1)^k T_j U_k(j_0) v_(j_0) = (-1)^k G+_k(j_0) v_(j_0). In
R2(m), put i = e - m < e. If i > t, then i - w lies between -e + 1 and -t - 1, both generators remain and the
relation is B_m v_(e-m) + tau^m(alpha) v_(-m-t). If i <= t, write i = i_0 - kw with -e < i_0 <= t; the relation
becomes (-1)^k T'_i V_k(i_0) v_(i_0) = (-1)^k G-_k(i_0) v_(i_0). The indices j_0 with -t <= j_0 <= t carry both
kinds of relations and no other generator of their class remains; the indices between t + 1 and e - 1 are paired
with those between -e + 1 and -t - 1. []

## 2. The forms

**Lemma 2.** (i) T_j = - a_f(j+t) sigma^(j+t)(Delta) for j >= -t, and T'_i = - b_g(t-i) tau^(e-i)(Delta) for i <= t.

(ii) For i >= -t: T_(i+w) up(i) = - sigma^(i+t)(X' alpha) a_f(i+t). For i <= t:
T'_(i-w) down(i) = - tau^(e-i)(X sigma^(e-t)(beta)) b_g(t-i).

(iii) For -t <= j <= 0: tau^(j+t)(T_j) = - B_(j+t) Delta and tau^(j+t)(T'_j) = - X. For 0 <= j <= t:
sigma^(e-j)(T'_j) = - sigma^(e-t)(A_(t-j)) Delta and sigma^(e-j)(T_j) = - X'.

(iv) For 1 <= m <= e - t - 1: sigma^m(T'_(-m-t)) = - X and sigma^m(T_(e-m)) = - X'.

*Proof.* (i) Let j >= 0. Then a_g(j+e) = sigma^j(A_e) and a_f(j+t) = sigma^j(A_t), so
T_j = sigma^j(A_e - A_t sigma^t(alpha sigma^(e-t)(beta))) = sigma^j(A_e - A_t sigma^t(A_(e-t)) - A_t sigma^t(Delta))
= - sigma^j(A_t) sigma^(j+t)(Delta), because A_t sigma^t(A_(e-t)) = A_e. Let -t <= j <= 0. Then a_g(j+e) = A_(j+e),
a_f(j+t) = A_(j+t), and sigma^(j+t)(Delta) = sigma^(j+t)(alpha) sigma^(j+e)(beta) - sigma^(j+t)(A_(e-t)), so
T_j = A_(j+e) - A_(j+t) sigma^(j+t)(A_(e-t)) - A_(j+t) sigma^(j+t)(Delta) = - A_(j+t) sigma^(j+t)(Delta). For T'_i:
tau^(e-i)(Delta) = tau^(e-i)(alpha) tau^(t-i)(beta) - tau^(e-i)(A_(e-t)). For i <= 0, b_f(e-i) = tau^(-i)(B_e) and
b_g(t-i) = tau^(-i)(B_t), and B_t tau^e(A_(e-t)) = B_e (the factors h - lz, t < l <= e), so
T'_i = tau^(-i)(B_e - B_t tau^e(alpha) tau^t(beta)) = - tau^(-i)(B_t) tau^(e-i)(Delta). For 0 <= i <= t,
b_f(e-i) = B_(e-i), b_g(t-i) = B_(t-i) and B_(t-i) tau^(e-i)(A_(e-t)) = B_(e-i), so T'_i = - B_(t-i) tau^(e-i)(Delta).

(ii) For i >= -t, i + w >= 0, so by (i) T_(i+w) = - sigma^(i+w)(A_t) sigma^(i+w+t)(Delta), and
sigma^(i+t)(X' alpha) = sigma^(i+w)(A_t) sigma^(i+t+w)(Delta) sigma^(i+t)(alpha); multiply by a_f(i+t). For i <= t,
i - w <= 0, so T'_(i-w) = - tau^(w-i)(B_t) tau^(e-i+w)(Delta), and
tau^(e-i)(X sigma^(e-t)(beta)) = tau^(w-i)(B_t) tau^(e-i+w)(Delta) tau^(t-i)(beta); multiply by b_g(t-i).

(iii) and (iv) follow from (i): tau^(j+t)(A_(j+t)) = B_(j+t); tau^(j+t) tau^(-j)(B_t) = L and
tau^(j+t) tau^(e-j) = tau^w; sigma^(e-j) sigma^j(A_t) = L' and sigma^(e-j) sigma^(j+t) = sigma^w;
sigma^(e-j)(B_(t-j)) = (h + (e-t) z) ... (h + (e-j-1) z) = sigma^(e-t)(A_(t-j)); sigma^m tau^(m+t)(B_t) = L and
sigma^m tau^(e+m+t) = tau^w; sigma^m sigma^(e-m)(A_t) = L' and sigma^m sigma^(e-m+t) = sigma^w. []

## 3. The cyclic summands

**Proposition 3.** Assume (C0).

(a) For -t <= j <= t the forms T_j and T'_j are coprime, of D-degrees e - max(0, -j) and e - max(0, j). So M_j has
finite length at most e (e - |j|), its Hilbert function is at most the one of a complete intersection of these
degrees, and equality holds (for the length, or for the function) exactly when J_j = (T_j, T'_j).

(b) If M_(-t) has length e (e - t), then (F1) holds. If M_t has length e (e - t), then (F2) holds.

(c) If (F1) and (F2) hold, then J_j = (T_j, T'_j) for every j, and the Hilbert series of M_j is
s^|j| (1 - s^(2(e-|j|))) (1 - s^(2e)) / (1 - s^2)^2.

*Proof.* (a) By Lemma 2 (iii), an automorphism of D carries (T_j, T'_j) to (B_(j+t) Delta, X) for j <= 0 and to
(sigma^(e-t)(A_(t-j)) Delta, X') for j >= 0. z divides none of the four factors. Delta is coprime to
X = L tau^w(Delta) by (C0). B_(j+t) divides B_t, whose factors are the h - iz with 1 <= i <= t; they are coprime to
L (factors h - lz, t < l <= 2t) because p divides none of 1, ..., 2t - 1, and B_t is coprime to tau^w(Delta) because
sigma^w(B_t) = L' is coprime to Delta. For j >= 0: Delta is coprime to X'; sigma^(e-t)(A_t) has the factors
h + iz, e - t <= i <= e - 1, coprime to L' (factors h + lz, e <= l <= e + t - 1) for the same reason, and coprime to
sigma^w(Delta) because tau^w sigma^(e-t)(A_t) = tau^(2t)(A_t) = L is coprime to Delta. The degrees are those of
a_f(j+t) and b_g(t-j) plus 2d. A quotient of a complete intersection of finite length by a larger ideal is shorter.

(b) For j = -t: T_(-t) = - Delta, T'_(-t) = - X, and by Lemma 2 (ii) with i = -t, G+_1(-t) = - X' alpha. If the length
is e (e - t) then J_(-t) = (Delta, X) by (a), so X' alpha = c X + q Delta with forms c, q; taking the homogeneous
parts, c is in D_d. For j = t: T'_t = - tau^(e-t)(Delta), T_t = - sigma^t(A_t) sigma^(2t)(Delta), and by Lemma 2 (ii)
with i = t, G-_1(t) = - tau^(e-t)(X sigma^(e-t)(beta)). Applying sigma^(e-t) to J_t = (T_t, T'_t) gives
(X', Delta), which then contains X sigma^(e-t)(beta).

(c) Write (F1) as X' alpha = c X + q Delta. By Lemma 2 (i) and (ii), for i >= -t,

    T_(i+w) up(i) = - sigma^(i+t)(c) sigma^(i+t)(X) a_f(i+t) + sigma^(i+t)(q) T_i.                (1)

Let -t <= j <= t. We show by induction on k >= 1 that G+_k(j) lies in (T_j, T'_j). For k = 1, take i = j in (1):
sigma^(j+t)(X) = sigma^j(B_t) tau^(e-j)(Delta); for j <= 0 this is - T'_j, and for j > 0 it is a multiple of
T'_j = - B_(t-j) tau^(e-j)(Delta), because sigma^j(B_t) has the factors h - lz, 1 - j <= l <= t - j. For k >= 2 put
i = j + (k-1)w and i'' = j + (k-2)w. Then G+_k(j) = T_(i+w) up(i) U_(k-1)(j), the second term of (1) gives
sigma^(i+t)(q) G+_(k-1)(j), and in the first term sigma^(i+t)(X) is a multiple of
sigma^(i+t-w)(Delta) = sigma^(i''+t)(Delta) while U_(k-1)(j) = U_(k-2)(j) up(i'') is a multiple of
U_(k-2)(j) a_f(i''+t); their product is a multiple of T_(i'') U_(k-2)(j) = G+_(k-2)(j). Symmetrically, write (F2)
as X sigma^(e-t)(beta) = c' X' + q' Delta; for i <= t,

    T'_(i-w) down(i) = - tau^(e-i)(c') tau^(e-i)(X') b_g(t-i) + tau^(e-i)(q') T'_i,                (2)

and tau^(e-j)(X') = sigma^j(A_t) sigma^(j+t)(Delta), which is - T_j for j >= 0 and a multiple of
T_j = - A_(j+t) sigma^(j+t)(Delta) for j < 0 (sigma^j(A_t) has the factors h + iz, j <= i <= j + t - 1); the
induction for G-_k(j) is the same with i = j - (k-1)w and i'' = j - (k-2)w. So J_j = (T_j, T'_j), and the series is
that of a complete intersection of two forms of U-degrees 2(e - |j|) and 2e in two variables of degree 2, generated
in degree |j|. []

Remark (the condition on p is necessary). The proof of (c) uses (F1) and (F2) only. So if 0 < p < 2t and (F1), (F2)
hold, then J_0 = (T_0, T'_0), which tau^t carries to (B_t Delta, L tau^w(Delta)) by Lemma 2 (iii); p divides one of
the integers 1, ..., 2t - 1, so B_t (factors h - iz, 1 <= i <= t) and L (factors h - lz, t < l <= 2t) have a common
factor, and M_0, hence M, has infinite length.

## 4. The summands on two generators

For an automorphism phi of D and a D-module presented by a matrix, applying phi to the entries of the matrix gives a
module with the same Hilbert function (phi on the coordinates is a bijection of graded vector spaces that carries
one module of relations onto the other). Apply sigma^m to the presentation of N_m in Proposition 1. With
sigma^m(B_m) = A_m and Lemma 2 (iv), the result is the module N'_m on a, b (degrees m + t and e - m) with the
relations

    rho_1 = alpha a + A_m b,       rho_2 = sigma^m(A_(e-t-m)) a + sigma^(e-t)(beta) b,
    X a,    X' b,     sigma^m(G-_k(-m-t)) a,    sigma^m(G+_k(e-m)) b      (k >= 1).

Let E_m be the module on a, b with the relations rho_1, rho_2 alone. The determinant of (rho_1, rho_2) is
alpha sigma^(e-t)(beta) - A_m sigma^m(A_(e-t-m)) = Delta, not zero, so rho_1 and rho_2 are independent, Delta
annihilates E_m, and the Hilbert series of E_m is

    H_E(s) = (s^(m+t) + s^(e-m) - s^(e+m) - s^(2e-t-m)) / (1 - s^2)^2

(rho_1 has degree e + m and rho_2 degree 2e - t - m).

**Proposition 4.** Assume that z does not divide Delta, that Delta is coprime to X and to X', and (F1), (F2), (F3).
Then for 1 <= m <= e - t - 1 the Hilbert series of N_m is (1 - s^(2e)) H_E(s); in particular N_m has length e (e - t).

*Proof.* Step 1: N'_m = E_m / (D X a + D X' b). By (1) with i = e - m, and Lemma 2 (iv),
sigma^m(G+_1(e-m)) = - sigma^w(c) sigma^w(X) sigma^m(a_f(e-m+t)) - sigma^w(q) X', and sigma^w(X) = sigma^w(L) Delta
annihilates E_m; so sigma^m(G+_1(e-m)) b lies in D X' b. For k >= 2, with i = e - m + (k-1)w and
i'' = e - m + (k-2)w, G+_k(e-m) = T_(i+w) up(i) U_(k-1)(e-m); by (1) it is sigma^(i+t)(q) G+_(k-1)(e-m) plus a
multiple of sigma^(i''+t)(Delta) a_f(i''+t) U_(k-2)(e-m) = - G+_(k-2)(e-m), so by induction sigma^m(G+_k(e-m)) b
lies in D X' b (for k = 2 the second term is a multiple of sigma^m(T_(e-m)) = - X'). The same argument with (2)
shows that sigma^m(G-_k(-m-t)) a lies in D X a.

Step 2: the relations between u = X a and v = X' b in E_m. A pair (r_1, r_2) in D^2 satisfies r_1 u + r_2 v = 0 in
E_m exactly when (r_1 X, r_2 X') = lambda_1 rho_1 + lambda_2 rho_2 with lambda_1, lambda_2 in D. Solving by
Cramer's rule,

    lambda_1 = (r_1 X sigma^(e-t)(beta) - r_2 X' sigma^m(A_(e-t-m))) / Delta,
    lambda_2 = (r_2 X' alpha - r_1 X A_m) / Delta.

Modulo Delta the two numerators are X' (r_1 c' - r_2 sigma^m(A_(e-t-m))) by (F2) and X (r_2 c - r_1 A_m) by (F1), and
X, X' are coprime to Delta. So (r_1, r_2) is a relation exactly when Delta divides both entries of

    (r_1, r_2) Psi,       Psi = [[c', - A_m], [- sigma^m(A_(e-t-m)), c]],
    det Psi = c c' - A_m sigma^m(A_(e-t-m)) = c c' - A_(e-t) = mu Delta.

Since mu is not zero, (r_1, r_2) Psi = Delta (s_1, s_2) is equivalent to (r_1, r_2) = (1/mu)(s_1, s_2) adj(Psi) with
adj(Psi) = [[c, A_m], [sigma^m(A_(e-t-m)), c']]. So the module of relations between u and v is free on

    (c, A_m)      and      (sigma^m(A_(e-t-m)), c'),

of degrees 2e + (e + m) and 2e + (2e - t - m) (u and v have degrees 2e + m + t and 2e + e - m).

Step 3. By step 2 the submodule D u + D v of E_m has the Hilbert series s^(2e) H_E(s), and by step 1 the series of
N'_m is H_E(s) - s^(2e) H_E(s). Its value at s = 1 is the length: with N(s) = s^(m+t) + s^(e-m) - s^(e+m) -
s^(2e-t-m), N(1) = 0 and N'(1) = - 4d, the series (1 - s^(2e)) N(s)/(1 - s^2)^2 tends to 2e 4d / 4 = e (e - t). []

Remark (mu = 0). If mu = 0 the matrix Psi is singular and step 2 does not apply; then c c' = A_(e-t), so c is a
scalar multiple of a product of d of the factors l_i = h + iz, 0 <= i <= e - t - 1. Observed (section 7 and the
checks of the review of this note): N_m is longer than e (e - t) unless c is a multiple of
sigma^d(A_d) = l_d l_(d+1) ... l_(2d-1); in that case M has the pure Hilbert function and its Betti table is not
pure (at (6, 7, 2) and (6, 11, 4), every member computed). So (F3) is not needed for the Hilbert function in that
one case, and it is needed for the Betti table.

## 5. The theorem

**Theorem.** Let alpha, beta be forms of degree d = (e - t)/2 in D with (C0), (F1), (F2) and (F3). Then
M = U/(U(x^e + alpha y^t) + U(y^e + beta x^t)) has finite length e^3 and the Hilbert series

    (1 - s^e)^2 (1 - s^(2e)) / ((1 - s)^2 (1 - s^2)),

the one of the pure Betti table (1, 2, 2, 1) on (0, e, 3e, 4e).

*Proof.* By Propositions 1, 3 (c) and 4 the series of M is (1 - s^(2e))/(1 - s^2)^2 times

    sum over -t <= j <= t of (s^|j| - s^(2e-|j|))  +  sum over 1 <= m <= e-t-1 of (s^(m+t) + s^(e-m) - s^(e+m) - s^(2e-t-m)).

In the second sum, s^(m+t) and s^(e-m) each run once over the exponents t + 1, ..., e - 1, and s^(e+m), s^(2e-t-m)
each once over e + 1, ..., 2e - t - 1. With the first sum this is

    1 + 2 (s + ... + s^(e-1)) - 2 (s^(e+1) + ... + s^(2e-1)) - s^(2e) = (1 - s^e)(1 + 2s + ... + 2 s^(e-1) + s^e)
        = (1 - s^e)^2 (1 + s)/(1 - s),

and (1 - s^(2e)) (1 - s^e)^2 (1 + s) / ((1 - s^2)^2 (1 - s)) is the series stated. Its value at s = 1 is e^3. []

**Corollary 1 (the filter).** Over a field of odd characteristic p, a member (alpha, beta) of the family with
exponent t that passes the tests (F0) to (F3) of EXP-017 and EXP-019 has the pure Hilbert function, provided
2t <= p - 1. In characteristic 0 the same holds for every t.

**Corollary 2 (the Betti table, conditional).** Under the hypotheses of the theorem, M has the pure Betti table
(1, 2, 2, 1) on (0, e, 3e, 4e) if and only if its socle {u : xu = yu = zu = 0} has dimension one.

*Proof.* As in the proof of Theorem 2 of `2026-11-20-one-congruence-and-the-betti-table.md`: Tor_3(k, M) is the
socle of M shifted by 4 (the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex), Tor_0 is k in degree 0 and Tor_1 is two-dimensional in
degree e (f and g are independent of degree e), and the alternating sum of the graded Betti numbers is
1 - 2 s^e + 2 s^(3e) - s^(4e) by the theorem, and Tor_i = 0 for i >= 4 (the Chevalley-Eilenberg resolution of k has
length 3). The top degree 4e - 4 of M lies in the socle and has dimension one.
If the socle has dimension one, Tor_3 is k in degree 4e and the alternating sum leaves Tor_2 = k^2 in degree 3e.
Conversely the pure table has Tor_3 of dimension one. []

**Corollary 3 (the linear family).** For t = e - 2 (d = 1; f = x^e + alpha y^(e-2), g = y^e + beta x^(e-2) with
alpha, beta linear) the conditions (F1) and (F2) hold whenever z does not divide Delta and Delta is coprime to X and
X': the quotient D/(Delta, X) is a complete intersection of degrees 2 and e and vanishes in degree e + 1. So a
member with (C0) and mu not zero has the pure Hilbert function; (C0) asks p = 0 or p >= 2e - 4, that is 2e - p <= 3
for an odd prime p < 2e.

This is the half of the rule of the linear family that the note on the weight-zero projection does not give, for the
Hilbert function: what remains for the rule itself is a member with (C0) and mu not zero over GF(p) for every e and
p = 2e - 3, 2e - 1 (found in every case computed), and the socle.

## 5b. The socle and the Betti table, under one more coprimality (added later on 2026-11-20)

Write l_i = h + iz, so that A_m = l_0 l_1 ... l_(m-1). The further condition:

- (C1) Delta is coprime to A_(e-t) (no l_i with 0 <= i <= e - t - 1 divides Delta).

Throughout this section (C0), (F1), (F2), (F3) hold. For 0 <= m <= e - t let N'_m be the module on two generators
a, b with the relations

    rho_1 = alpha a + A_m b,     rho_2 = sigma^m(A_(e-t-m)) a + sigma^(e-t)(beta) b,     X a,     X' b.

For 1 <= m <= e - t - 1 this is the module of section 4 (step 1 of Proposition 4). For m = 0, rho_1 gives
b = - alpha a and N'_0 = D/(Delta, X, X' alpha) = D/(Delta, X), which is M_(-t) (generators a = v_(-t), b = v_e). For
m = e - t, rho_2 gives a = - sigma^(e-t)(beta) b and N'_(e-t) = D/(Delta, X', X sigma^(e-t)(beta)) = D/(Delta, X'),
which is M_t with its presentation twisted by sigma^(e-t) (generators a = v_(-e), b = v_t). So in every case N'_m
is the summand of M that contains v_(-m-t) and v_(e-m), with the coordinates r_1 v_(-m-t) + r_2 v_(e-m) replaced by
(sigma^m(r_1), sigma^m(r_2)).

**Lemma 5 (x and y along the chain).** In these coordinates the multiplication by x from the summand of
v_(-m-t), v_(e-m) to the summand of v_(-m-t+1), v_(e-m+1) is, for 1 <= m <= e - t, the D-linear map

    xi_m: N'_m -> N'_(m-1),      a -> l_(m-1) a,   b -> b,

and the multiplication by y is, for 0 <= m <= e - t - 1, the D-linear map

    eta_m: N'_m -> N'_(m+1),     a -> a,   b -> l_m b.

*Proof.* x v_j = h v_(j+1) for j < 0 and x v_j = v_(j+1) for j >= 0 (x y^n = h y^(n-1)), and x (r v) = sigma(r) x v;
so r_1 v_(-m-t) + r_2 v_(e-m) goes to sigma(r_1) h v_(-m-t+1) + sigma(r_2) v_(e-m+1), and in the coordinates of
N'_(m-1) this is (sigma^(m-1)(sigma(r_1) h), sigma^m(r_2)) = (l_(m-1) sigma^m(r_1), sigma^m(r_2)).
y v_j = v_(j-1) for j <= 0 and y v_j = (h - z) v_(j-1) for j > 0 (y x^n = (h - z) x^(n-1)), and y (r v) = tau(r) y v;
so the element goes to tau(r_1) v_(-m-t-1) + tau(r_2)(h - z) v_(e-m-1), which in the coordinates of N'_(m+1) is
(sigma^m(r_1), sigma^m(r_2) sigma^(m+1)(h - z)) = (sigma^m(r_1), l_m sigma^m(r_2)). []

**Lemma 6 (ideals).** Assume (C1). Let R = D/(Delta), and for 0 <= m <= e - t let I_m = (A_m, alpha) R and
K_m = (A_m, c) R. Then a -> - A_m, b -> alpha induces an isomorphism of N'_m with I_m/(X K_m); under it xi_m is
the map induced by the inclusion of I_m in I_(m-1), and eta_m is the multiplication by l_m from I_m/(X K_m) to
I_(m+1)/(X K_(m+1)).

*Proof.* The map psi_m from the module E_m on a, b with the relations rho_1, rho_2 to R, a -> - A_m, b -> alpha, is
well defined (rho_1 goes to 0 and rho_2 to Delta) with image I_m. It is injective: if - u A_m + v alpha = lambda
Delta, subtract lambda rho_2, which satisfies the same equation; then v alpha = u A_m, and A_m and alpha are coprime
(a common factor l_i would divide A_(e-t) and alpha sigma^(e-t)(beta) - A_(e-t) = Delta, against (C1)), so (u, v) is
a multiple of rho_1. psi_m sends X a to - X A_m and X' b to X' alpha = c X in R, so the submodule generated by X a and
X' b goes onto X K_m. Finally psi_(m-1)(xi_m(a)) = - l_(m-1) A_(m-1) = - A_m = psi_m(a) and
psi_(m-1)(xi_m(b)) = alpha = psi_m(b); psi_(m+1)(eta_m(a)) = - A_(m+1) = l_m psi_m(a) and
psi_(m+1)(eta_m(b)) = l_m alpha = l_m psi_m(b). []

**Proposition 7.** Assume (C1). For 1 <= m <= e - t - 1 the kernels of xi_m and eta_m meet in 0.

*Proof.* By Lemma 6 an element of the kernel of xi_m is the class of u = X k with k in K_(m-1), and
eta_m(u) = 0 means l_m X k in X K_(m+1). X is not a zero divisor of R (Delta is coprime to X), so l_m k lies in
K_(m+1), that is, in D, in (A_(m+1), c, Delta) = (A_(m+1), c): Delta = (c c' - A_(e-t))/mu and A_(m+1) divides
A_(e-t). Write k = g_1 A_(m-1) + g_2 c. Then g_1 l_m A_(m-1) lies in (l_(m-1) l_m A_(m-1), c). The forms l_m A_(m-1)
and c are coprime (a common factor l_i would divide A_(e-t) and c c' - A_(e-t) = mu Delta, against (C1)), so g_1 lies
in (l_(m-1), c), g_1 A_(m-1) lies in (A_m, c) and k lies in K_m: the class of u is 0. []

**Theorem 2.** Let alpha, beta be forms of degree d in D with (C0), (C1), (F1), (F2) and (F3). Then the socle of M
has dimension one, and M has the pure Betti table (1, 2, 2, 1) on (0, e, 3e, 4e).

*Proof.* An element killed by x and y is killed by z = xy - yx, so the socle is the intersection of the kernels of x
and y. x and y carry each summand of Proposition 1 into another one, so the socle is the sum of its parts in the
summands.

(i) -t <= j <= -1: x is injective on M_j. Here x (r v_j) = sigma(r) h v_(j+1) for r in D, and by Lemma 2 (i)
T_(j+1) = h sigma(T_j) (A_(j+1+t) = h sigma(A_(j+t))) and T'_(j+1) = sigma(T'_j), so by Proposition 3 (c)
J_(j+1) = (h sigma(T_j), sigma(T'_j)). If sigma(r) h = u_1 h sigma(T_j) + u_2 sigma(T'_j), then h divides u_2 (h
divides T_(j+1), which is coprime to T'_(j+1)), so sigma(r) lies in sigma(J_j) and r in J_j.

(ii) 1 <= j <= t: y is injective on M_j. Here y (r v_j) = tau(r)(h - z) v_(j-1), T_(j-1) = tau(T_j) and
T'_(j-1) = (h - z) tau(T'_j) (B_(t-j+1) = (h - z) tau(B_(t-j))), and the same argument applies.

(iii) j = 0. x (r v_0) = sigma(r) v_1 and J_1 = (sigma(T_0), sigma(T'_0)/h) (sigma(B_t) = h B_(t-1)), so the kernel
of x on M_0 is (T_0, T'_0/(h - z))/J_0. y (r v_0) = tau(r) v_(-1) and J_(-1) = (tau(T_0)/(h - z), tau(T'_0))
(tau(A_t) = (h - z) A_(t-1)), so the kernel of y is (T_0/h, T'_0)/J_0. Put F = T_0/h and G = T'_0/(h - z); they are
coprime, and h F, (h - z) G are coprime. If u_1 h F + u_2 G = u_3 F + u_4 (h - z) G then
(u_1 h - u_3) F = (u_4 (h - z) - u_2) G, so u_1 h - u_3 = lambda G and the element is
u_1 h F + u_4 (h - z) G - lambda F G: the intersection of the two kernels is generated by F G modulo
J_0 = (h F, (h - z) G). h F G and (h - z) F G lie in J_0, and F G does not: if F G = u_1 h F + u_2 (h - z) G with
forms u_1, u_2, then F (G - u_1 h) = u_2 (h - z) G, so G divides u_1 h and hence u_1 (h divides T_0, which is
coprime to T'_0); but u_1 has D-degree e - 2 and G has e - 1, so u_1 = 0 and F = u_2 (h - z), while h - z
divides T'_0, which is coprime to T_0 = h F. So this part of the socle is the line of F G, in degree
2 (2e - 2) = 4e - 4.

(iv) The summands N_m, 1 <= m <= e - t - 1, carry no socle, by Lemma 5 and Proposition 7.

So the socle has dimension one, and Corollary 2 gives the Betti table. []

## 6. What is not proved

- The Betti table without (C1). In every case computed a member with (C0), (F1), (F2), (F3) has the pure table,
  with or without (C1) (section 7); Theorem 2 covers the members with (C1) only. When Delta and A_(e-t) have a
  common factor the maps psi_m of Lemma 6 need not be injective and the argument of Proposition 7 does not apply.
- The converse. Among the stored members with the pure Hilbert function and another Betti table, every one fails
  (F0) or (F3) (section 7), and no member examined that satisfies (C0) and fails (F1) or (F2) has the pure Hilbert
  function (more than 100 such members in the samples of section 7); but Proposition 3 (b) gives only that (F1) and (F2) are necessary for the two end summands to have their
  length, not that the Hilbert function of M sees it (a longer summand N_m could compensate in the length).
- Members with z dividing Delta have infinite length (by Lemma 2 (i) every generator of J_(-t) is then a multiple
  of z). Members with Delta not coprime to X or X' are not covered, and pure Hilbert functions occur among them (the
  stored members with another table that fail (F0)).
- Existence: the theorem does not produce a member over a given field.

## How could this be wrong?

- An index error in Lemma 1 or in the elimination of Proposition 1 would be seen by the comparison with the
  Hilbert function computed in U, which holds on every member tried (388 over eight cases, and more than 500 in the
  two other scripts); but the cases have e <= 8, and the proof, not the machine, covers larger e.
- Step 2 of Proposition 4 uses that X and X' are units modulo Delta in the sense that Delta | X y implies
  Delta | y; this needs Delta coprime to X and X' as forms, which is (C0) with z not dividing Delta.
- The theorem is about the Hilbert function. It says nothing on the Betti table without the socle, and the title
  of any statement that uses it must say so.
- Corollary 3 depends on the vanishing of a complete intersection of degrees 2 and e in degree e + 1 in two
  variables: its socle degree is 2 + e - 2 = e.
