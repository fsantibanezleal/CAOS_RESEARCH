# The pairs (x^e + b y^e, G(xy, z)) for even e: a decomposition over k[xy, z] and the conditions for the pure Hilbert function

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: derivation [D] by the coordinating session, not yet read by a reviewer. Machine check by a
script of the coordinating session: for every form G up to a scalar at (e, p) = (4, 5), (4, 7), (4, 13), (6, 5) and
for 200 forms at (6, 7), 627 forms in all, the Hilbert function predicted by the proposition below equals the one
computed in U from the ranks of the multiplication maps, and the forms with the pure Hilbert function are the same
(1, 7, 32, 2, 2 of them); the function does not change with b on a sample (script and log under the data root,
`caos-research/boij-soderberg-realizability/EXP-014-probe/`). The note explains what EXP-010 found in the strata
S(e; 0, e/2) and what EXP-014 looks for.

## Setting

U = U(H) over a field k, yx = xy - z, left modules. Put h = xy and D = k[h, z], a commutative polynomial ring in two
variables inside U (h and z commute, and the products h^i z^j have distinct leading PBW monomials x^i y^i z^j). D is
graded with h and z in degree 2; "degree" of a form of D below means its degree as a polynomial in h, z.

For n >= 0 put

    A_n(h) = h (h + z) ... (h + (n - 1) z),        B_n(h) = (h - z)(h - 2z) ... (h - n z).

Lemma 1. x h = (h + z) x and y h = (h - z) y. Hence x^n d(h, z) = d(h + n z, z) x^n and
y^n d(h, z) = d(h - n z, z) y^n for d in D. Moreover x^n y^n = A_n(h) and y^n x^n = B_n(h).

Proof. h x = x (y x) = x (h - z), so x h = h x + z x. y h = (y x) y = (h - z) y. By induction,
x^(n+1) y^(n+1) = x A_n(h) y = A_n(h + z) x y = A_(n+1)(h) and y^(n+1) x^(n+1) = y B_n(h) x = B_n(h - z)(h - z)
= B_(n+1)(h). QED.

Lemma 2. Put e_n = x^n for n >= 0 and e_n = y^(-n) for n < 0. Then U is the direct sum of the D e_n, n in Z, each a
free left D-module of rank 1; e_n has degree |n|.

Proof. By Lemma 1, x^a y^b = A_b(h + (a - b) z) x^(a-b) for a >= b and x^a y^b = A_a(h) y^(b-a) for a < b, so every
PBW monomial lies in the sum. The elements h^i z^j e_n have distinct leading PBW monomials, so they are linearly
independent. QED.

This is the decomposition of U into the weight spaces of the torus x -> s x, y -> s^(-1) y, which fixes h and z; U is
a generalized Weyl algebra over D.

## The decomposition

Let e be even, m = e/2, b in k not zero, and G a nonzero form of degree m in D (so of degree e in U). Put

    f = x^e + b y^e,      g = G(h, z),      M = U/(Uf + Ug),      G_j = G(h + j z, z)   for j in Z.

For n in Z define the forms a(n) (for n >= 0) and b(n) (for n < 0) of D by

    a(n) = A_e(h + (n - e) z)  if n >= e,       a(n) = A_n(h)  if 0 <= n < e,
    b(n) = B_(-n)(h)  if -e <= n < 0,           b(n) = B_e(h + (n + e) z)  if n < -e,

and for r in Z and k >= 1

    c_(r, k) = a(r + e) a(r + 3e) ... a(r + (2k - 1) e),      c_(r, -k) = b(r - e) b(r - 3e) ... b(r - (2k - 1) e),

with c_(r, 0) = 1.

Proposition. As a graded left D-module, M is the direct sum over r = -e, ..., e - 1 of the cyclic modules
(D/J_r) v_r, where v_r has degree |r| and J_r is the ideal of D generated by the forms G_(r + 2ek) c_(r, k), k in Z,
for which every factor a(.) or b(.) in c_(r, k) is defined. Consequently the Hilbert function of M is

    dim M_N = sum over r of dim (D/J_r) in degree (N - |r|)/2,

and it does not depend on b.

Proof. By Lemma 2 the left ideal I = Uf + Ug is the sum of the D e_n f and the D e_n g. By Lemma 1, e_n g = G_n e_n.
For n >= 0, e_n f = x^(n+e) + b x^n y^e = e_(n+e) + b a(n) e_(n-e): x^n y^e = x^(n-e) A_e(h) = A_e(h + (n - e) z) x^(n-e)
for n >= e and x^n y^e = A_n(h) y^(e-n) for n < e. For n < 0, with n = -m', e_n f = y^m' x^e + b y^(m'+e)
= b(n) e_(n+e) + b e_(n-e): y^m' x^e = B_m'(h) x^(e-m') for m' <= e and
y^m' x^e = y^(m'-e) B_e(h) = B_e(h - (m' - e) z) y^(m'-e) for m' > e. So M is the D-module on generators v_n (the
classes of the e_n), n in Z, with the relations

    v_(n+e) = - b a(n) v_(n-e)   (n >= 0),        v_(n-e) = - b^(-1) b(n) v_(n+e)   (n < 0),        G_n v_n = 0.

The first family expresses every v_j with j >= e through v_(j-2e), and the second every v_j with j < -e through
v_(j+2e). So the v_r with -e <= r <= e - 1 generate, there is no relation between different r, and the relations
G_n v_n = 0, written in the v_r, are G_(r+2ek) c_(r,k) v_r = 0 up to the units (-b)^k. QED.

## The pure Hilbert function

Hypothesis (C). For every r with -e <= r <= e - 1, the form G_r has no common factor with G_(r+2e) c_(r,1) nor with
G_(r-2e) c_(r,-1). (For example: G irreducible over k of degree m >= 2 with G(h + 2e z, z) not proportional to G, which holds in
characteristic 0 and in characteristic p not dividing 2e when m < p; the factors of the c are linear forms h + i z,
which an irreducible G of degree at least 2 does not divide.)

Theorem. Assume (C). For each r let F_r be the generator of smaller degree among G_(r+2e) c_(r,1) and
G_(r-2e) c_(r,-1), of degree m + e - |r|, and H_r the other one, of degree m + e (for r = 0 both have degree m + e;
take either as F_0).

(a) M has finite length at most e^3.

(b) The following are equivalent: M has length e^3; M has the pure Hilbert function h_e; for every r with
|r| <= m - 2, H_r lies in the ideal (G_r, F_r).

(c) The condition of (b) for a given r is a set of m - |r| - 1 linear conditions on the form H_r, so (b) is
(m - 1)^2 = h_e(3e) conditions in all.

Proof. For 0 <= r <= e - 1, c_(r,1) = a(r + e) = A_e(h + r z) has degree e and c_(r,-1) = b(r - e) = B_(e-r)(h) has
degree e - r; for r < 0 the roles are exchanged, with degrees e + r = e - |r| and e. So F_r has degree
d_r = m + e - |r| and H_r degree m + e. By (C), G_r and F_r have no common factor, so D/(G_r, F_r) is a complete
intersection of length m d_r, zero in degrees above m + d_r - 2, and J_r contains (G_r, F_r). Hence the length of M
is at most the sum over r of m (m + e - |r|) = m (2 e m + 2 e^2 - e^2) = e^3, since the sum of |r| over
r = -e, ..., e - 1 is e^2 and 2m = e. This is (a), with equality exactly when J_r = (G_r, F_r) for every r. The
generators of J_r other than G_r and F_r are H_r, of degree m + e, and the forms with |k| >= 2, of degree at least
m + 2e >= m + d_r - 1; the latter lie in (G_r, F_r) for reasons of degree, and so does H_r when
m + e >= m + d_r - 1, that is when |r| >= m - 1. So equality holds exactly when H_r lies in (G_r, F_r) for
|r| <= m - 2. In that case the Hilbert series of M is the sum over r of t^|r| (1 - t^e)(1 - t^(3e - 2|r|))/(1 - t^2)^2,
and with the sum of t^|r| over r equal to (1 + t)(1 - t^e)/(1 - t) and the sum of t^(3e - |r|) equal to
t^(2e) (1 + t)(1 - t^e)/(1 - t), this is (1 - t^e)^2 (1 - t^(2e))/((1 - t)^2 (1 - t^2)), the pure series. Conversely
the pure Hilbert function gives length e^3. This is (b). For (c): D/(G_r, F_r) has dimension m - (j - d_r + 1) in
degree j for d_r <= j <= m + d_r - 2, so in degree m + e it has dimension m - |r| - 1; and
(m - 1) + 2 ((m - 2) + ... + 1) = (m - 1)^2. QED.

## Consequences

1. The second form alone decides. For the pairs of this shape the Hilbert function of M depends on G only; b is any
   nonzero scalar. (EXP-010 found each pure G with every nonzero b.)
2. e = 4 (m = 2): one condition on the two parameters of G = h^2 + alpha h z + gamma z^2. So the forms G with the pure
   Hilbert function make a curve in the plane of (alpha, gamma), in every characteristic in which (C) can hold,
   characteristic 0 included. Counts over GF(p): 1, 1, 7, 12, 32, 35 forms at p = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 (reducible forms
   that satisfy the conclusion included).
3. e >= 6: (m - 1)^2 conditions on the m parameters of G, more conditions than parameters. Pure forms G are then
   isolated when they exist: 2 at (6, 5) and 2 at (6, 7), exchanged by the swap of x and y. The number of conditions
   is h_e(3e), the number that governs the shares measured by the random searches in the whole Grassmannian
   (EXP-008), so nothing is gained in this stratum for large e; the prediction P1 of EXP-014 at e = 8, 10, 12 was frozen before this count and
   is likely to fail.
4. The statement is about the Hilbert function. Whether the Betti table is then pure is not claimed (in EXP-010 every
   table computed in these strata on a member with the pure Hilbert function was pure).
5. The same decomposition applies to every pair in which f = x^e + b y^e and g is a weight vector d(h, z) x^t or
   d(h, z) y^t (the strata S(e; 0, j) of the weight-class note); only the shape g in D is worked out here.

## How could this be wrong?

- The proposition is elementary (Lemmas 1 and 2 and an elimination). The reviewer should recompute the relations
  e_n f for the four ranges of n and the forms a(n), b(n), in particular the shifts.
- The theorem uses (C) to make D/(G_r, F_r) a complete intersection. Without (C) the proposition still gives the
  Hilbert function, and the script checks the proposition for every G, reducible ones included.
- The counts of consequence 2 come from a probe of the stratum (pure Hilbert function by ranks in U), not from the
  equation of the curve, which is not written here.
