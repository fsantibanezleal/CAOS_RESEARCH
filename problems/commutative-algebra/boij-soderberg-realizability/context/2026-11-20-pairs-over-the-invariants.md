# The pairs (x^e + b y^e, G(xy, z)) for even e: a decomposition over k[xy, z] and the conditions for the pure Hilbert function

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: derivation [D] by the coordinating session. Reviewed on 2026-11-20 by a reader other than
the writer (record `bs-pairs-over-invariants-review-2026-11-20` in the coordination repository): the two lemmas, the
proposition and the theorem hold, with nine minor repairs; two of the consequences of the first text were wrong as
written (one overstated what the count of conditions gives, one was contradicted by the record of EXP-010) and a
third mixed forms inside and outside the hypothesis of the theorem. This text applies every repair; the first text
is in commit d4d5d811. Machine checks: by the coordinating session, the Hilbert function predicted by the proposition
against the one computed in U on 627 forms at (e, p) = (4, 5), (4, 7), (4, 13), (6, 5), (6, 7), with no difference; by
the review, with its own code, on every form at (4, 3), (4, 11), (4, 17), (8, 3), on 150 forms at (6, 11) and on 376
forms in characteristic 0 for which the hypothesis of the theorem fails, with no difference (scripts and logs of the
first under the data root, `caos-research/boij-soderberg-realizability/EXP-014-probe/`).

## Setting

U = U(H) over a field k, yx = xy - z, left modules. Put h = xy and D = k[h, z], a commutative polynomial ring in two
variables inside U (h and z commute, and the products h^i z^j have distinct leading PBW monomials x^i y^i z^j). D is
graded with h and z in degree 2; "degree" of a form of D below means its degree as a polynomial in h, z.

For n >= 0 put

    A_n(h) = h (h + z) ... (h + (n - 1) z),        B_n(h) = (h - z)(h - 2z) ... (h - n z).

Lemma 1. x h = (h + z) x and y h = (h - z) y. Hence x^n d(h, z) = d(h + n z, z) x^n and
y^n d(h, z) = d(h - n z, z) y^n for d in D. Moreover x^n y^n = A_n(h) and y^n x^n = B_n(h).

Proof. h x = x (y x) = x (h - z), so x h = h x + z x. y h = (y x) y = (h - z) y. By induction,
x^(n+1) y^(n+1) = x A_n(h) y = A_n(h + z) x y = A_(n+1)(h) and y^(n+1) x^(n+1) = y B_n(h) x = B_n(h - z)(h - z)
= B_(n+1)(h). QED.

Lemma 2. Put e_n = x^n for n >= 0 and e_n = y^(-n) for n < 0. Then U is the direct sum of the D e_n, n in Z, each a
free left D-module of rank 1; e_n has degree |n|.

Proof. By Lemma 1, x^a y^b = A_b(h + (a - b) z) x^(a-b) for a >= b and x^a y^b = A_a(h) y^(b-a) for a < b, so every
PBW monomial lies in the sum. For the filtration of U by the degree in x and y, the element h^i z^j e_n has the
leading PBW monomial x^(i+n) y^i z^j (n >= 0) or x^i y^(i-n) z^j (n < 0); these are distinct, so the elements are
linearly independent. QED.

This is the decomposition of U into the weight spaces of the torus x -> s x, y -> s^(-1) y, which fixes h and z. U is
a generalized Weyl algebra over D (for the enveloping algebras of Heisenberg algebras this is stated, for example,
in Benkart and Ondrus, Whittaker modules for generalized Weyl algebras, arXiv:0803.3570).

## The decomposition

Let e be even, m = e/2, b in k not zero, and G a nonzero form of degree m in D (so of degree e in U). Put

    f = x^e + b y^e,      g = G(h, z),      M = U/(Uf + Ug),      G_j = G(h + j z, z)   for j in Z.

For n in Z define the forms a(n) (for n >= 0) and b(n) (for n < 0) of D by

    a(n) = A_e(h + (n - e) z)  if n >= e,       a(n) = A_n(h)  if 0 <= n < e,
    b(n) = B_(-n)(h)  if -e <= n < 0,           b(n) = B_e(h + (n + e) z)  if n < -e,

and for -e <= r <= e - 1 and k >= 1

    c_(r, k) = a(r + e) a(r + 3e) ... a(r + (2k - 1) e),      c_(r, -k) = b(r - e) b(r - 3e) ... b(r - (2k - 1) e),

with c_(r, 0) = 1.

Proposition. As a graded left D-module, M is the direct sum over r = -e, ..., e - 1 of the cyclic modules
(D/J_r) v_r, where v_r has degree |r| and J_r is the ideal of D generated by the forms G_(r + 2ek) c_(r, k), k in Z.
Consequently the Hilbert function of M is

    dim M_N = sum over r of dim (D/J_r) in degree (N - |r|)/2,

and it does not depend on b. (For b = 0 the statement fails: the module is then not of finite dimension.)

Proof. By Lemma 2 the left ideal I = Uf + Ug is the sum of the D e_n f and the D e_n g. By Lemma 1, e_n g = G_n e_n.
For n >= 0, e_n f = x^(n+e) + b x^n y^e = e_(n+e) + b a(n) e_(n-e): x^n y^e = x^(n-e) A_e(h) = A_e(h + (n - e) z) x^(n-e)
for n >= e and x^n y^e = A_n(h) y^(e-n) for n < e. For n < 0, with n = -m', e_n f = y^m' x^e + b y^(m'+e)
= b(n) e_(n+e) + b e_(n-e): y^m' x^e = B_m'(h) x^(e-m') for m' <= e and
y^m' x^e = y^(m'-e) B_e(h) = B_e(h - (m' - e) z) y^(m'-e) for m' > e. So M is the D-module on generators v_n (the
classes of the e_n), n in Z, with the relations

    v_(n+e) = - b a(n) v_(n-e)   (n >= 0),        v_(n-e) = - b^(-1) b(n) v_(n+e)   (n < 0),        G_n v_n = 0.

The first family expresses every v_j with j >= e through v_(j-2e), and the second every v_j with j < -e through
v_(j+2e). So the v_r with -e <= r <= e - 1 generate, there is no relation between different r, and the relations
G_n v_n = 0, written in the v_r, are G_(r+2ek) c_(r,k) v_r = 0 up to the units (-b)^k. QED.

## The pure Hilbert function

For each r let F_r be the generator of smaller degree among G_(r+2e) c_(r,1) and G_(r-2e) c_(r,-1), of degree
d_r = m + e - |r|, and H_r the other one, of degree m + e (for r = 0 both have degree m + e; take either as F_0).

Hypothesis (C). For every r with -e <= r <= e - 1, the form G_r has no common factor with F_r.

In terms of the roots of G(h, 1) over an algebraic closure, (C) fails exactly when z divides G, or two roots differ by
2e, or a root lies in {-(e - 1), ..., -1} or in {2, ..., e}, or 0 and 1 are both roots (review). Example: G
irreducible over k of degree m >= 2 with G(h + 2e z, z) not proportional to G; this holds over a field of
characteristic 0 that is not algebraically closed, for example G = h^2 - h z + 9 z^2 over the rationals, and in
characteristic p not dividing 2e when m < p. If p divides 2e, (C) fails for every G and the theorem says nothing.

Theorem. Assume (C).

(a) M has finite dimension at most e^3.

(b) The following are equivalent: M has dimension e^3; M has the pure Hilbert function h_e; for every r with
|r| <= m - 2, H_r lies in the ideal (G_r, F_r).

(c) For a given r the condition of (b) is m - |r| - 1 linear conditions on the form H_r, once G_r and F_r are fixed;
in all, (m - 1)^2 = h_e(3e) conditions, which are polynomial conditions on the coefficients of G.

Proof. For 0 <= r <= e - 1, c_(r,1) = a(r + e) = A_e(h + r z) has degree e and c_(r,-1) = b(r - e) = B_(e-r)(h) has
degree e - r; for r < 0 the roles are exchanged, with degrees e - |r| and e. By (C), D/(G_r, F_r) is a complete
intersection of dimension m d_r, zero in degrees above m + d_r - 2, and J_r contains (G_r, F_r). Hence the dimension
of M is at most the sum over r of m (m + e - |r|) = m (2 e m + 2 e^2 - e^2) = e^3, since the sum of |r| over
r = -e, ..., e - 1 is e^2 and 2m = e. This is (a), with equality exactly when J_r = (G_r, F_r) for every r. The
generators of J_r other than G_r and F_r are H_r, of degree m + e, and the forms with |k| >= 2, of degree at least
m + 2e - |r|, which is at least m + d_r - 1; the latter lie in (G_r, F_r) for reasons of degree, and so does H_r when
m + e >= m + d_r - 1, that is when |r| >= m - 1. So equality holds exactly when H_r lies in (G_r, F_r) for
|r| <= m - 2. In that case the Hilbert series of M is the sum over r of t^|r| (1 - t^e)(1 - t^(3e - 2|r|))/(1 - t^2)^2,
and with the sum of t^|r| over r equal to (1 + t)(1 - t^e)/(1 - t) and the sum of t^(3e - |r|) equal to
t^(2e) (1 + t)(1 - t^e)/(1 - t), this is (1 - t^e)^2 (1 - t^(2e))/((1 - t)^2 (1 - t^2)), the pure series. Conversely
the pure Hilbert function gives dimension e^3. This is (b). For (c): D/(G_r, F_r) has dimension m - (j - d_r + 1) in
degree j for d_r <= j <= m + d_r - 2, so in degree m + e it has dimension m - |r| - 1; and
(m - 1) + 2 ((m - 2) + ... + 1) = (m - 1)^2. QED.

## Consequences and what the computations add

1. The second form alone decides the Hilbert function: for the pairs of this shape it depends on G only; b is any
   nonzero scalar. (EXP-010 found each such G with every nonzero b; the review checked two values of b on 271 forms.)

2. e = 4 (m = 2): one condition. For G = h^2 + alpha h z + gamma z^2 it is P(alpha, gamma) = 0 with

       P = 3 alpha^5 + 7 alpha^4 gamma - 66 alpha^4 + 3 alpha^3 gamma^2 - 136 alpha^3 gamma + 513 alpha^3
           + 2 alpha^2 gamma^3 - 167 alpha^2 gamma^2 + 1545 alpha^2 gamma - 1698 alpha^2 - 20 alpha gamma^3
           + 800 alpha gamma^2 - 3980 alpha gamma + 2400 alpha - 12 gamma^4 + 740 gamma^3 - 6536 gamma^2
           + 9560 gamma - 1152,

   obtained independently by the coordinating session (remainders modulo G) and by the review (a determinant), and
   irreducible over the rationals (review). So under (C) the forms G with the pure Hilbert function are the points
   of the quintic curve P = 0 outside the locus where (C) fails, in every characteristic other than 2. Over GF(p)
   the forms with (C) on the curve number 1, 1, 1, 2, 16, 13 at p = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, and for these P = 0 matches
   the pure Hilbert function computed in U without exception (review).

3. Characteristic 0 at e = 4. The curve has the rational points (alpha, gamma) = (-1, 9) and (-1, 1/2), both with
   (C). Over the rationals the pair

       f = x^4 + b y^4,      g = (xy)^2 - (xy) z + 9 z^2      (b = 1 and b = -3 were computed)

   has the pure Hilbert function and the pure Betti table (1, 2, 2, 1) on (0, 4, 12, 16), by two instruments of the
   review (`uhlib` over the rationals and over GF(1000003), and a count of minimal second syzygies over the
   rationals); the same for 2 (xy)^2 - 2 (xy) z + z^2. These are pure pairs over the rationals in which the second
   form is a polynomial in xy and z.

4. Outside (C) the picture is different, and the theorem says nothing there. There are forms without (C) that have
   the pure Hilbert function: 0, 0, 6, 10, 16, 22 of them at e = 4 for p = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, and in characteristic
   0 a dense open subset of each of the lines G(-2, 1) = 0 and G(3, 1) = 0 at e = 4 and of the line
   G = (h + 4z)(h + z)(h - c z) at e = 6 (review, a computation at the generic point). In every case computed their
   Betti table is not pure.

5. The Hilbert function and the Betti table. In every case computed (the review's forms at e = 4 for p = 7, 11, 13
   and over the rationals; the members stored by EXP-010 and EXP-014), a form with (C) and the pure Hilbert function
   has the pure Betti table, and a form without (C) and with the pure Hilbert function does not. This is an
   observation, not a statement proved here. In particular the pure Hilbert function alone does not give a pure
   pair in these strata (EXP-010 at (6, 11): most members of S(6; 0, 3) with the pure Hilbert function have another
   table).

6. e >= 6: (m - 1)^2 polynomial conditions on the m parameters of G, more conditions than parameters. This is a
   count, not a proof that forms with (C) and the pure Hilbert function are finite in number or absent: their
   independence is not shown. What is known is by computation: at (6, 5) and at (6, 7) there are exactly two such
   forms, irreducible cubics exchanged by the swap of x and y, and EXP-014 finds pure pairs of this shape at e = 8 over
   GF(5), GF(7), GF(11) and GF(13) (rebuilt by the coordinating session; whether those forms satisfy (C) was not
   examined). A lift of the forms found at three primes to the integers is not pure at larger primes
   (checked at e = 8 for p = 17 to 29), so no single form with integer coefficients explains them.

7. The same method applies to every pair in which f = x^e + b y^e and g is a weight vector d(h, z) x^t or
   d(h, z) y^t (the strata S(e; 0, j) of the weight-class note): every relation coming from g involves one generator,
   and the elimination is the same, with other ideals J_r. Only the shape g in D is worked out here.

## How could this be wrong?

- The proposition is elementary (Lemmas 1 and 2 and an elimination); it was recomputed by the review in the four
  ranges of n and checked by two independent codes on more than 1,500 forms.
- The theorem needs (C); without it the proposition still gives the Hilbert function, and the examples of
  consequence 4 show that the conclusion of the theorem can fail.
- Consequence 5 is an observation on the cases computed.
- The literature: the search of the review found the generalized Weyl algebra structure of U stated, and did not find
  the decomposition or the count of conditions; Bavula's paper of 1992 on generalized Weyl algebras and the paper of
  Drozd, Guzner and Ovsienko on their weight modules were not read. This is a search, not a claim of novelty.
