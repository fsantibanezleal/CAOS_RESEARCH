# Pure pairs that are homogeneous for the weight modulo m: a necessary condition and the strata it leaves

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: derivation [D] by the coordinating session. Reviewed on 2026-11-20 by a reader other than
the writer (record `bs-weight-class-strata-review-2026-11-20` in the coordination repository): the grading, the
proposition, the range of the definition and the table hold in every characteristic, with no blocker; one sharpening
(a stratum contained in a stratum of another modulus that is not admissible holds no pure pair, which removes most
strata with m = e) and nine minor repairs, all applied below. The first text is in commit 59aa24aa, the text EXP-010
was frozen with. The condition is elementary; its use is to say where a pure pair with a cyclic symmetry can lie, in
every characteristic, and so where EXP-010 looks. The counts quoted in the last section come from a probe on settled
cases only, run before EXP-010 was frozen (scripts and logs under the data root,
`caos-research/boij-soderberg-realizability/EXP-010-probe/`).

## Setting

Notation of the note `2026-11-20-pure-pairs-family-dimension.md`: U = U(H) over a field k, PBW monomials
x^a y^b z^c with yx = xy - z, deg x = deg y = 1, deg z = 2, u(n) = dim U_n, G_e = Gr(2, U_e), mu_n(W), W_0 = <x^e, y^e>,
h_e(n) = u(n) - 2 u(n - e) + 2 u(n - 3e) - u(n - 4e), r = 2 u(2e) - 2.

The weight. Give x the weight 0 and y, z the weight 1, so that x^a y^b z^c has weight w = b + c. The defining relation
yx = xy - z is homogeneous of weight 1 and z is central, so U is graded by the pairs (degree, weight):
U = sum of U_(n, w), and U_(n, w) U_(n', w') lies in U_(n + n', w + w'). A basis of U_(n, w) is the set of monomials
x^(n - w - c) y^(w - c) z^c with 0 <= c <= min(w, n - w), so

    dim U_(n, w) = min(w, n - w) + 1    for 0 <= w <= n,  and 0 otherwise.

This is the grading by the characters of the diagonal torus x -> s x, y -> t y (z -> s t z) of the group GL_2 of
graded automorphisms, taken modulo the scalars, which act on U_e by one character and so act trivially on G_e.

For m >= 1 and a class k modulo m write U_(n, [k]) for the sum of the U_(n, w) with w = k modulo m, and
u_k(n) = dim U_(n, [k]) (the modulus m is understood). U is graded by the pairs (degree, class modulo m).

The strata. For m >= 2 and classes i, j modulo m,

    S(m; i, j) = { <f, g> in G_e :  f in U_(e, [i]),  g in U_(e, [j]) }.

Over a field that contains the m-th roots of unity and whose characteristic does not divide m, the union of the S(m;
i, j) is the fixed locus on G_e of the group of points of the subgroup mu_m of the torus (for the group scheme mu_m no
assumption is needed: a grading by classes modulo m is an action of mu_m). The definition by supports needs neither
assumption and is the one used here. Examples: the power pair W_0 lies in S(m; 0, e mod m) for every m, since x^e has
weight 0 and y^e has weight e; the four-parameter linear family of the note
`2026-11-20-linear-family-weight-argument.md` is the affine chart "coefficient of x^e and of y^e equal to 1" of S(e -
1; 0, 1), because the monomials of degree e and weight 0 or e - 1 are x^e, x y^(e-1), y^(e-2) z, and those of weight 1
or e are x^(e-1) y, x^(e-2) z, y^e; S(e + 1; 0, e) is the single point W_0.

The swap x <-> y (with z -> -z) sends weight w to e - w on U_e, so it sends S(m; i, j) to S(m; e - i, e - j).

## The condition

Proposition. Let W = <f, g> be a pure pair of forms of degree e (the module M = U/(Uf + Ug) has the Betti table
(1, 2, 2, 1) on the degrees (0, e, 3e, 4e)), with f in U_(e, [i]) and g in U_(e, [j]) for some m >= 2. Then there are
classes s_1, s_2, t modulo m such that for every degree n and every class k

    H_k(n) := u_k(n) - u_(k-i)(n - e) - u_(k-j)(n - e) + u_(k-s_1)(n - 3e) + u_(k-s_2)(n - 3e) - u_(k-t)(n - 4e)  >=  0,

and H_k(n) is the dimension of the part of M of degree n and class k. In particular the sum over k of H_k(n) is h_e(n).

Proof. The left ideal I = Uf + Ug is generated by elements that are homogeneous for the grading by (degree, class), so
I and M = U/I are graded for it. M has a minimal free resolution by free modules with homogeneous generators and
homogeneous maps for this finer grading: at each step one chooses a minimal system of generators of the kernel that
is homogeneous for the finer grading, which is possible because the kernel of a homogeneous map is graded and, in each
degree n, a complement of the part generated in lower degrees can be chosen as a sum of its class components. A
resolution built this way is minimal for the grading by degree alone (its maps have no entry of degree 0), and the
minimal resolution for the degree is unique up to isomorphism, so its ranks in each degree are the Betti numbers of
M: one generator in degree 0, two in degree e, two in degree 3e, one in degree 4e. The generator of degree 0 has
class 0 and the two of degree e are the images of f and g, of classes i and j. Call s_1, s_2 the classes of the two
generators of degree 3e and t the class of the generator of degree 4e. The resolution is

    0 -> U(-4e)[t] -> U(-3e)[s_1] + U(-3e)[s_2] -> U(-e)[i] + U(-e)[j] -> U -> M -> 0,

where U(-d)[s] is the free module on one generator of degree d and class s; its part of degree n and class k has
dimension u_(k-s)(n - d). The complex is exact in each (degree, class), so the alternating sum of the dimensions is
dim M_(n, [k]), which is not negative. QED.

Definition. A stratum S(m; i, j) is admissible for e when some (s_1, s_2, t) makes H_k(n) >= 0 for all k and all 0 <=
n <= 4e + 6m + 2. The range is enough for every n (4e + 6m - 1 would do): the total over k is h_e(n), which is 0 from
n = 4e - 3 on, so on that range each H_k(n) that is not negative is 0; and for n >= 4e each u_k(n - d) in the formula
is a quasi-polynomial in n of degree at most 2 whose period divides 2m, so H_k(n) is one too, and a quasi-polynomial
of degree at most 2 and period 2m that vanishes at 6m consecutive integers vanishes at all later ones. By the
proposition a pure pair lies in admissible strata only. The condition is a finite computation in the numbers min(w, n
- w) + 1 and does not involve the field.

The condition is necessary and not sufficient. It does not see the characteristic: EXP-009 shows that the
four-parameter linear family, the chart of S(e - 1; 0, 1) in which x^e and y^e have coefficient 1, holds no pure
pair over GF(p) in the eight pairs (e, p) with p <= e <= 7 it ran; and the rest of that stratum holds no pure pair
in any characteristic (review: if f has no term x^e then f lies in Uy, and U/(Uy + Ug) is a quotient of M that
contains k[x], so M is not of finite length; the same with x and y exchanged). And it is not sufficient in any
characteristic: see the containment remark below.

Containment (added after the review). A stratum is contained in strata of other moduli: if the weights in [0, e] of
class i modulo m all lie in one class i' modulo m', and those of class j in one class j', then S(m; i, j) lies in
S(m'; i', j'). A pure pair in S(m; i, j) therefore needs every such S(m'; i', j') to be admissible. Moduli above
e + 1 add nothing. The main case: for m = e and 1 <= i, j <= e - 1 the class of i is the single weight i, so f and g
are homogeneous for the integer weight and the pair lies in S(e + 1; i, j), which is not admissible (for m = e + 1
only (0, e) is). So these strata hold no pure pair in any characteristic, and for e <= 10 the only pure pair that
is homogeneous for the integer weight is the power pair, where it is pure. Call a stratum refined admissible when
it and every stratum that contains it are admissible.

What the table shows (observations on e = 4 to 8, no proof for general e):

- The stratum of the power pair, S(m; 0, e mod m), is admissible for every e and m in the table (it must be in
  characteristic 0, where W_0 is pure).
- For e = 5 and e = 7 it is the only admissible stratum for each m below e; for m = e + 1 it is the only one for every
  e in the table.
- When m divides e the condition excludes nothing in the table (a computed fact for e = 4 to 10; the review found
  the witness (s_1, s_2, t) = (i, j, 0) in every case; no proof for general e). By the containment remark most of
  these strata still hold no pure pair.
- For e = 4, 6, 8 and m not dividing e, either only the stratum of the power pair is admissible (m = e + 1; e = 6

- The second observation does not persist to e = 9 (review: there m = 3 admits all six strata and m = 6 three).
- The admissible list is stable under the swap, as it must be.

So the strata of the power pair are linear families through W_0, one for each m, ordered by the divisibility of m
(for m' dividing m the stratum of m lies in that of m'); among them are the stratum of the four-parameter linear
family (m = e - 1) and the point W_0 (m = e + 1).

## Characteristic p dividing m

Added on 2026-11-20 after the review of the rest of the note and after EXP-010 recorded, on more than ten million
members, that in characteristic p no member of a stratum S(m; i, j) with p dividing m has the rank r. Derivation [D]
by the coordinating session. Checked on 2026-11-20 by the reader who reviewed the rest of the note (addendum of the
same record): every step holds, no blocker and no major finding; six minor repairs, applied below (the first text of
the section is in commit 7ab11132). The reader built the syzygy of the proof explicitly in four cases and checked the
bound on 941 random pairs at (4, 3), (9, 3), (9, 5), (9, 7), (10, 7), (11, 11) and, for p = 2, at (4, 2), (5, 2), (6,
2).

Notation of the note `2026-11-20-pairs-over-the-invariants.md`: h = xy, D = k[h, z], x d(h, z) = d(h + z, z) x,
y d(h, z) = d(h - z, z) y, x^n y^n = A_n(h), y^n x^n = B_n(h), and U is the direct sum of the D x^n (n >= 0) and the
D y^n (n >= 1).

Proposition. Let k have characteristic p > 0 and let f, g be nonzero forms of degree e, each homogeneous for the
weight modulo p (f in U_(e, [i]), g in U_(e, [j]) for the modulus p). Then there are a, b in U, not both zero, with a
f + b g = 0 and with a f of degree at most 2e + p - 1 when p is odd, and at most 2e + 2 when p = 2.

Proof. Put X = x^p, Y = y^p, central in U, and C = k[h, z, X, Y], a commutative subalgebra of U. The monomial x^a y^b
z^c lies in D x^(a-b) or in D y^(b-a), and its weight b + c and its degree a + b + 2c determine b - a, so a form of
degree e whose weights are all congruent to i modulo p is a sum of terms d_n x^n and d'_n y^n with d_n, d'_n in D,
where n runs over one residue class modulo p in the first sum and over the opposite class in the second. With n_0 in
[0, p - 1] the residue of the first class, n_0 = e - 2i modulo p, x^n = X^t x^(n_0) for n = n_0 + p t, and y^n = Y^t
y^(p - n_0) for n = p - n_0 + p t when n_0 >= 1, y^n = Y^t for n = p t when n_0 = 0. So

    f = alpha x^(n_0) + alpha' y^(p - n_0)   with alpha, alpha' in C     (f = alpha + alpha' in C when n_0 = 0),

and likewise g = beta x^(n_1) + beta' y^(p - n_1). If n_0 = 0 put m_f = 1. If 1 <= n_0 <= (p - 1)/2 put m_f = y^(n_0):
then m_f f = alpha(h - n_0 z) B_(n_0)(h) + alpha'(h - n_0 z) Y. If n_0 > (p - 1)/2 put m_f = x^(p - n_0): then m_f f =
alpha(h + (p - n_0) z) X + alpha'(h + (p - n_0) z) A_(p - n_0)(h). In each case c_f = m_f f lies in C, is not zero
because U is a domain, and m_f has degree at most (p - 1)/2 for odd p (for p = 2 and n_0 = 1 take m_f = y, of degree
1). Define m_g and c_g = m_g g in the same way. The elements of C commute, so

    (c_g m_f) f - (c_f m_g) g = c_g c_f - c_f c_g = 0,

with c_g m_f not zero, and the degree of c_g m_f f is (e + deg m_g) + (e + deg m_f), at most 2e + p - 1 for odd p and
2e + 2 for p = 2. QED.

Corollary. In odd characteristic p <= e no pair of forms of degree e that are both homogeneous for the weight modulo p
is pure. In particular, for p <= e a stratum S(m; i, j) with p dividing m holds no pure pair, admissible or not. (For
p = 2 the same holds for e >= 3, in agreement with the theorem on characteristic 2.)

Proof. A pure pair has no syzygy of degree below 3e, and 2e + p - 1 < 3e when p <= e. A form that is homogeneous for
the weight modulo m is homogeneous modulo p when p divides m. QED.

Remarks.

1. The rank. A syzygy of degree n gives u(3e - n) independent syzygies of degree 3e, and u(3e - n) >= u(2) = 4 when n
   <= 3e - 2. So for p <= e - 1 the rank of mu_(3e) is at most 2 u(2e) - 4, below r: this is the observation of
   EXP-010. For p = e the bound of the proposition is 3e - 1 in general; it is at most 3e - 2 as soon as one of the
   two forms needs no multiplier or a multiplier of degree below (p - 1)/2, for example in the strata with i = 0,
   where the same conclusion follows. In the remaining strata with m = p = e (both forms need a multiplier of degree
   (p - 1)/2; at e = p = 5 these are S(5; 1, 1), S(5; 1, 4), S(5; 4, 4)) the corollary still excludes a pure pair, and
   the rank below r is a computed fact (EXP-010; confirmed by the reader at (5, 5)), not a consequence of the bound.
2. Degrees observed (four random members each): the first syzygy appears in degree 16 at (e, p) = (7, 5) in S(5; 0, 2)
   (bound 18), in degrees 12 and 13 at (6, 3) in S(3; 0, 1) and S(3; 1, 2) (bound 14), in degree 10 at (5, 5) and 14
   at (7, 7) in strata with m = e (bound 2e + p - 1; the central case gives 2e).
3. The pure pairs of the notes on the central commutator are not homogeneous for the weight modulo p, in agreement
   with the corollary. Case A: x^e + y z^s has weights 0 and s + 1 = (e + 1)/2, and p divides e. Case B: x^e + z^(e/2)
   has weights 0 and e/2, and p divides e - 1. Case C: x^e + z^(e/2) + y^(p+1) z^j has the weight (e + p + 1)/2, which
   is 1/2 modulo p. Case D: x^e + y z^s + x^p z^j has the weight (e + 1)/2, which is 1 modulo p.
4. So in characteristic p <= e the search for pure pairs with a cyclic symmetry can leave out every modulus divisible
   by p (for p = e + 1 the bound is 3e and excludes nothing). This sharpens the admissible list in a way that depends
   on the characteristic: with the containment remark, the strata left at the pairs of EXP-010 number 18 at (6, 5), 11
   at (7, 3), 12 at (7, 5) and 6 at (7, 7), and the list at (6, 7) is not affected (count of the reader).
5. Machine check of the bound (script and log under the data root, `caos-research/boij-soderberg-realizability/
   EXP-010-probe/`): on 1,239 random pairs homogeneous for the weight modulo p, at (e, p) = (5, 3), (6, 3), (7, 3),
   (5, 5), (6, 5), (7, 5), (7, 7), (8, 5), every pair has a syzygy in degree at most 2e + p - 2. The value 2e + p - 2
   is what was observed, not a proved bound; the reader found it reached at (4, 3) and (9, 3).

## How could this be wrong?

- The proposition uses only the existence of a minimal resolution that is homogeneous for the finer grading and the
  uniqueness of the Betti numbers; the review confirmed both steps. EXP-010 tests the exclusion on settled pairs (its
  gate G4).
- Two candidate strengthenings do not shrink the list (review): the nonnegativity of the image and syzygy modules
  with a duality bound, and a symmetric Hilbert function per class, which is not a necessary condition (pure pairs
  without it exist, for example in S(4; 0, 3) at e = 4 over GF(13)).
- The table is computed, not proved for general e; the three observations are statements about e = 4 to 8.
