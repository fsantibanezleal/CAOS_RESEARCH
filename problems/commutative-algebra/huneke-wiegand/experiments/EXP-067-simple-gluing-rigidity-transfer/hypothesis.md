# EXP-067 hypothesis: simple-gluing transfer of two-generated rigidity

Date: 2026-09-20. Frozen after the source and invariant preflight and before
the producer, independent auditor, or manuscript text is written.

## Question

Let `Gamma` be a numerical semigroup of multiplicity `m`, let `s>0` be a gap,
and suppose the normalized two-generated monomial ideal `(1,t^s)` is rigid.
Choose any `a` with `a,a+s in Gamma`, so its integral translate is
`(t^a,t^(a+s))`.
For an integer `q>=1` with `gcd(q,m)=1`, does the simple gluing

```text
Gamma^(q) = m*N + q*Gamma
```

preserve symmetry and rigidity at the scaled shift `q*s`?

## Falsifiable predictions

### P1: semigroup and Gorenstein transfer

`Gamma^(q)` is a numerical semigroup of multiplicity `m`, with

```text
Ap(Gamma^(q),m) = q*Ap(Gamma,m)
```

after the residue permutation induced by multiplication by `q`. If `Gamma` is
symmetric with Frobenius number `F`, then `Gamma^(q)` is symmetric and

```text
F^(q) = q*(F+m)-m.
```

The scaled shift `q*s` remains a gap.

### P2: exact colon-exponent transfer

Put

```text
E = {a : a,a+s in Gamma},
D = {a : a,a+s,a+2s in Gamma}.
```

For the glued semigroup and shift `q*s`, the complete nonnegative exponent
sets satisfy

```text
E^(q) = m*N + q*E,
D^(q) = m*N + q*D.
```

Consequently `D=E+E` implies
`D^(q)=E^(q)+E^(q)`. The ideal
`(t^(qa),t^(q(a+s)))` in the localized semigroup ring of `Gamma^(q)` is
nonprincipal and rigid. For the CAOS family, `a=m`.

### P3: two-parameter CAOS consequence

For every `p>=4` and every `q>=1` coprime to `24p`, EXP-009 yields a symmetric
counterexample semigroup

```text
Gamma_(p,q) = 24p*N + q*Gamma_p
```

with multiplicity `24p`, embedding dimension `11p`, and Frobenius number

```text
q*(102p-1)-24p.
```

The rigid ideal is generated by exponents `24pq` and `30pq`. The case `q=1`
is exactly EXP-009; every `q>1` is a new gluing descendant.

### P4: adverse controls

- If `gcd(q,m)>1`, the displayed generators must have nontrivial common gcd,
  so the construction is not a numerical semigroup.
- The nonrigid control `Gamma=<4,5>`, shift `s=1`, must remain nonrigid for
  `q=1,3,5,7`; no implementation may turn the transfer into a false universal
  rigidity claim.
- Perturbing the scaled shift from `q*s` to `q*s+1` is not covered and must not
  be reported as a consequence.

## Method

1. Prove the Apery formula and symmetry transfer directly; cite gluing
   literature for context but do not import the new rigidity conclusion.
2. Prove a simultaneous alignment lemma for the representations of
   `n`, `n+q*s`, and `n+2q*s`. This must establish P2 without finite fitting.
3. Implement a deterministic exact checker using shortest paths for Apery sets
   and the existing complete `D=E+E` checker.
4. Check every `p=4,...,12` and the first eight admissible `q` values, plus the
   adverse controls. An independent implementation must reconstruct the
   semigroup from generators and must not import producer artifacts.
5. Emit JSON artifacts with exact invariants, first-failure fields, and SHA-256
   aggregate hashes.

## What PASS and FAIL prove

A PASS requires the symbolic proof plus both exact implementations. It proves
the general transfer theorem and therefore the two-parameter CAOS corollary;
finite agreement alone cannot confirm either statement.

A FAIL of an exact formula supplies a counterexample to the proposed transfer
and closes this focus. A finite PASS with an incomplete proof is only supporting
evidence and does not authorize a manuscript theorem.

## Invariant-first note

The coprimality invariant decides whether the generators can define a numerical
semigroup. Apery thresholds decide membership and symmetry without enumeration.
The representation congruence modulo `m`, not another Kunz-face sweep, is the
candidate one-line invariant for P2.
