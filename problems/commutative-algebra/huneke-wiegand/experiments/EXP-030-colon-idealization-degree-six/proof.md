# EXP-030 proof - cubic-colon idealization and the degree-six third strand

## Theorem

Let `p>=4`, let `P_p=k[X_a:a in E_(p,1)]`, let `Q_p` be the quadratic part of the defining ideal
of the conductor special fiber `C_p`, and put

```text
f_p=X_0^2X_(3p)-X_p^3.
```

Define

```text
H_p={a in E_(p,1):a>=6p},
L_p=[0,p] union [3p,4p-2],
A_i=X_i                    (0<=i<=p),
B_j=X_(3p+j)               (0<=j<=p-2).
```

Then `|H_p|=8p`, and the complete cubic colon is

```text
Q_p:f_p=Q_p+(X_h:h in H_p).                                    (1)
```

After the high variables are killed, its quotient is the canonical idealization

```text
P_p/(Q_p:f_p) isomorphic to R_p semidirect omega_(R_p),          (2)
R_p=k[s,t]^(p),
A_i -> s^(p-i)t^i,
B_j -> epsilon*s^(p-2-j)t^j,
epsilon^2=0.
```

Here `omega_(R_p)_d=k[s,t]_(dp-2)`. In particular, the quotient is a two-dimensional graded
Gorenstein algebra with

```text
H(z)=(1+(2p-2)z+z^2)/(1-z)^2.                                  (3)
```

For every field and every offset `b`, define

```text
l_p(t)=sum_(a in L_p)t^a,
h_p(t)=sum_(a in H_p)t^a,
e_r(t)=sum_(F subset L_p, |F|=r)t^(sum F),
u_0(t)=1,
u_d(t)=sum_(v=0)^(dp)t^v+sum_(v=3p)^((d+3)p-2)t^v  (d>=1),
q_p(t)=l_p(t)u_1(t)-u_2(t)-e_2(t),
s_p(t)=u_3(t)-l_p(t)u_2(t)+e_2(t)u_1(t)-e_3(t).
```

Then

```text
beta_(3,(6,b))(C_p)=[t^(b-3p)](s_p(t)+h_p(t)q_p(t)).            (4)
```

The integral relative homology supporting (4) is free abelian. Its support is

```text
[3p+4,29p-5] minus ([6p-3,6p+1] union [9p-3,9p]),              (5)
```

and therefore contains `26p-17` offsets. Summing (4) gives

```text
beta_(3,6)(C_p)=8p(7p^2-12p+2)/3.                              (6)
```

## 1. The low image of the quadratic ideal

Set `S_p=k[A_0,...,A_p,B_0,...,B_(p-2)]`. Modulo the high variables, the image of `Q_p` is the
ideal `I_p` generated by

```text
A_iA_j-A_kA_l      when i+j=k+l,
A_iB_j-A_kB_l      when i+j=k+l,
B_iB_j              for all i,j.                               (7)
```

Indeed, a low-low degree-two monomial has one of three types. An `AA` total lies in `[0,2p]`, and
an `AB` total lies in `[3p,5p-2]`. These are the two low nonzero components of `E_(p,2)`, so the
only quadratic moves within them preserve the displayed index sum. A `BB` total lies in
`[6p,8p-4]`. Every such value is a high offset, and

```text
B_iB_j-X_0X_(6p+i+j) in Q_p.
```

The second term vanishes after the high variables are killed. Conversely, no `AA` or `AB` total
has a high-variable factorization, and none is zero in the degree-two fiber. Thus (7) is exact.

Map `S_p/I_p` to the square-zero algebra in (2). The relations in (7) vanish. Every surviving
monomial reduces uniquely to one of two kinds:

```text
A-only:                 indexed by its sum in [0,dp],
one B and d-1 A's:      indexed by its sum in [3p,(d+3)p-2].    (8)
```

The two kinds remain independent where their intervals overlap because they lie in the ring and
canonical-module summands. Their images are the monomial bases of `k[s,t]_(dp)` and
`epsilon*k[s,t]_(dp-2)`. Hence the map is injective and proves (2). Counting (8) gives dimension
`2pd` in every degree `d>=1`, which is equivalent to (3). The standard canonical-idealization
criterion also proves the Gorenstein assertion.

## 2. The complete colon

EXP-027 proves that every `X_h`, `h in H_p`, belongs to `Q_p:f_p`. Since `Q_p` itself is also
contained in the colon, the right side of (1) is contained in the left side.

In (2), the image of `f_p` has one nonzero component in `R_p` and one in `omega_(R_p)`. If `(r,m)`
annihilates it, its ring component gives `r*t^(3p)=0`. The Veronese ring is a domain, so `r=0`.
The module component then gives `t^(3p)m=0`. Its canonical module is torsion-free, so `m=0`.
Thus `f_p` is a nonzerodivisor on `P_p/(Q_p+(X_h:h in H_p))`. If `g f_p` belongs to `Q_p`, the
class of `g` annihilates `f_p` in that quotient and is zero. This proves (1).

## 3. The colon's first nonlinear Betti coefficient

Formula (8) gives the multigraded Hilbert profiles `u_d(t)`. Multiplying the Hilbert series by
`product_(a in L_p)(1-zt^a)` shows that its coefficient in total degree two is

```text
u_2-l_pu_1+e_2=-q_p.                                           (9)
```

The ideal in (7) is generated by quadrics. There are no linear equations, so (9) is exactly minus
the first Betti profile. The total-degree-three coefficient is

```text
u_3-l_pu_2+e_2u_1-e_3=s_p.                                    (10)
```

There is no cubic minimal generator, and the minimum-shift bound excludes homological degree
three in internal degree three. Thus (10) is exactly the low-variable `beta_(2,3)` profile.

At `t=1`, put `c=2p-2`. Equations (9)-(10) give

```text
q_p(1)=c(c+1)/2-1=p(2p-3),
s_p(1)=c(c-2)(c+2)/3=8p(p-1)(p-2)/3.                           (11)
```

Returning to all `10p` variables tensors the low resolution with the Koszul complex on the `8p`
killed high variables. The total-degree-three, homological-degree-two profile is therefore

```text
s_p(t)+h_p(t)q_p(t).                                           (12)
```

Its total is

```text
8p*p(2p-3)+8p(p-1)(p-2)/3
=8p(7p^2-12p+2)/3.                                             (13)
```

All arguments through (13) work over the integers and after base change to every field.

## 4. Integral relative normal form in total degree six

For an offset `b`, use the relative squarefree-divisor chain complex from EXP-027. A cell `F` of
size `r` occurs in total degree six exactly when

```text
b-sum(F) in E_(p,6-r).                                         (14)
```

The relevant residual sets are

```text
E_(p,4)=[0,24p-1],
E_(p,3)=[0,24p-1] minus {6p-1},
E_(p,2)=[0,2p] union [3p,5p-2] union [6p,24p-1].               (15)
```

Apply increasing-vertex algebraic Morse matching over `Z` through cells of size four. Every
matched incidence is `+1` or `-1`. Choose, in each equal-sum low quadratic fiber, its
lexicographically first monomial as root. The remaining monomials give the basis counted by
`q_p(t)`. In every low degree-three fiber, choose a lexicographic spanning forest in the graph of
rooted quadratic reductions. Its primitive cycle basis is counted by `s_p(t)`, by (10).

After those choices, the complete unmatched-triangle table is

| triangle type | primitive labels left after tetrahedron cancellation |
|---|---|
| no high vertex | the low reduction-graph cycles counted by `s_p(t)` |
| one high vertex `h` | `h` times a rooted low quadratic label counted by `t^h q_p(t)` |
| at least two high vertices | none |

To verify completeness, toggle the least available vertex inside the same interval block of
`E_(p,1)`. A toggle can fail only across one of the two tetrahedron-residual gaps

```text
[2p+1,3p-1], [5p-1,6p-1],                                    (16)
```

or at the triangle-residual hole `6p-1`. With no high vertex, the failed toggles are exactly the
edges outside the chosen low spanning forests, hence the `s_p` cycles. With one high vertex, the
failure is exactly a nonroot monomial in a low quadratic fiber, hence the `h_p q_p` labels. With
two or more high vertices, the next vertex in the same high block supplies a tetrahedron; at a
block endpoint the first vertex of the next high block does so. The only apparent exception would
use offset `8p-1`, but that offset is not in `E_(p,1)`. This is the gap whose accidental insertion
invalidated the first audit implementation.

Order transient triangles first by the failed interval in (16), then by number of high vertices,
then lexicographically. Their chosen tetrahedron boundaries form an upper-triangular matrix with
unit diagonal. No chosen boundary enters a rooted low cycle or a high-times-quadratic label. Thus,
for a transient index set `T_b` and the label set

```text
U_b=S_(b-3p) disjoint-union
    union_(h in H_p)({h} times Q_(b-3p-h)),                    (17)
```

the part of the integral reduced complex bearing on second homology is

```text
0 -> Z^(T_b) --(I,0)--> Z^(T_b) direct-sum Z^(U_b) -> 0.      (18)
```

This proves that integral `H_2` is free on `U_b`. Equations (12), (17), and the cubic offset shift
give (4). In particular, there is no hidden mapping-cone cancellation in total degree six.

## 5. Support and total

Expanding the interval polynomials in (9)-(12) gives positive coefficients precisely on

```text
[3p+4,29p-5] minus ([6p-3,6p+1] union [9p-3,9p]).
```

The two deleted intervals have lengths five and four. The ambient interval has length `26p-8`,
so the support count is `26p-17`. This proves (5). Summing the coefficients is (13), which proves
(6). Divisibility by three follows by checking the three residue classes of `p` modulo three.
