# EXP-022 - symbolic refutation and exact quadratic count

Fix `p>=4`. Write `C_p=F(T_p)`, let `q=24p`, and index the degree-one variables by the offsets

```text
E_1={0} union D_1
```

from EXP-021, so `X_a` maps to the class of `t^(q+a)`. In particular
`0,p,3p` belong to `E_1`.

## 1. The homogeneous kernel is a value congruence

For a degree-`n` monomial `M=product_i X_(a_i)`, put `sigma(M)=sum_i a_i`. Its image is the
class of `t^(nq+sigma(M))` in `T_p^n/m_pT_p^n`. Monomial classes in the numerical-semigroup ring
are linearly independent. Therefore:

1. `M` belongs to the defining ideal exactly when `nq+sigma(M)` lies in `v(m_pT_p^n)`;
2. two nonzero monomials have the same image exactly when their offset sums agree.

Thus each homogeneous kernel component is generated by zero monomials and binomial differences
inside equal-sum fibers. This proves P1 independently of the finite computation.

## 2. Exact number of quadratic equations

There are `10p` degree-one variables and no linear defining equation. Hence every vector in the
quadratic kernel is a minimal defining equation. EXP-021 gives

```text
dim_k(C_p)_2=22p.
```

It follows that

```text
beta_(1,2)(C_p)
  = dim_k(S_p)_2-dim_k(C_p)_2
  = binom(10p+1,2)-22p
  = 50p^2-17p.                                             (1)
```

This part of P3 is proved for every `p>=4`.

## 3. A cubic that quadrics cannot generate

The monomials

```text
M_p=X_0^2 X_(3p),
N_p=X_p^3
```

have the same offset sum `3p`. This is a nonzero degree-three offset because `3p` lies in the
EXP-021 degree-one block `[3p,4p-2]`, hence in the cumulative degree-three fiber basis. The value
congruence therefore gives

```text
F_p=M_p-N_p in J_p.                                        (2)
```

To lie in `S_(p,1)(J_p)_2`, a cubic relation must be generated by replacing a quadratic divisor
with another quadratic monomial of the same nonzero sum, or by multiplying a zero quadratic.
Neither monomial in (2) admits such a move:

- In `E_1`, the only unordered pair summing to `3p` is `{0,3p}`. Indeed, two offsets in
  `[0,p]` sum to at most `2p`, while every other positive block begins at `3p`.
- The only unordered pair summing to `2p` is `{p,p}`. Every offset below `3p` lies in `[0,p]`,
  so equality forces both entries to be `p`.
- The remaining pair in `M_p` has sum zero and is uniquely `{0,0}`.

Consequently `M_p` and `N_p` are distinct isolated vertices in the degree-three congruence graph
generated by quadratic relations. Their difference is not in `S_(p,1)(J_p)_2`. Therefore

```text
beta_(1,3)(C_p)>=1                                         (3)
```

for every `p>=4`. Equations (2)--(3) disprove quadratic generation uniformly, not only at a
sampled parameter.

## 4. What remains open after the refutation

The exact computations at `p=4,5,6` find equality in (3) and no degree-four or degree-five
minimal equations. This supports the corrected candidate

```text
beta_(1,3)=1, beta_(1,4)=beta_(1,5)=0,
J_p=((J_p)_2,F_p),
```

but EXP-022 does not prove those upper bounds for all `p`. They require a separately declared
parametric congruence-connectivity theorem. Abdolmaleki--Kumashiro supplies the independent fact
that no minimal defining equation can occur above degree five.
