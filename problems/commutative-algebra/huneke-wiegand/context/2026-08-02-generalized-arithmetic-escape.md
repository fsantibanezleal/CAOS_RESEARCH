# Why the public candidate escapes the generalized-arithmetic positive theorem

Date: 2026-08-02. This is a deductive consequence of the closed EXP-001 semigroup data and the
primary theorem below; it is not a new computational experiment.

## Primary theorem

Landeros, O'Neill, Pelayo, Peña, Ren and Wissman, *Families of numerical semigroups and a special
case of the Huneke-Wiegand conjecture*, arXiv:2404.12519, Theorem 1.2, proves the two-generated
monomial-ideal Huneke-Wiegand statement for semigroups generated by

```text
<a, ah+d, ah+2d, ..., ah+kd>,  gcd(a,d)=1.
```

Lemma 2.1 also states that for a minimal generator `n_j`, reducibility of the canonical length-two
arithmetic sequence forces

```text
gcd(all minimal generators except n_j) divides s.
```

Primary source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12519`.

## Proposition

The public candidate semigroup

```text
Gamma = <56,57,58,63,64,70,71,72,73,74,75,76,77,78,79,80,81,82,83,
         87,89,90,93,95,96,97>
```

is not generated by a generalized arithmetic sequence.

### Proof

The multiplicity is 56. No positive sum of two elements of `Gamma` is below 112, so each member
of `Gamma` in `[56,111]` must itself occur in every generating set.

In a generalized-arithmetic presentation, `a` is the smallest displayed generator because
`ah+jd>a` for `h,j,d>=1`; hence `a=56`. Since 57 is in `Gamma`, some displayed term satisfies

```text
56h + jd = 57.
```

This forces `h=1`, `j=1`, and `d=1`. Since 63 is also in `Gamma` and cannot be a sum of positive
semigroup elements, the displayed sequence must reach 63, so `k>=7`. It would then contain
59, 60, 61 and 62. EXP-001's exact semigroup vector shows these are gaps, a contradiction. Thus no
such presentation exists.

## Why the diagonal gcd lemma does not catch the candidate

For every displayed minimal generator `n_j`, the gcd of all the others is 1:

- after deleting 56, the remaining set contains consecutive 57 and 58;
- after deleting 57, it contains 56, 58 and 63, whose gcd is 1;
- after deleting 58, it contains consecutive 56 and 57;
- after deleting any other generator, it still contains 56 and 57.

Therefore every deletion gcd divides the candidate shift `s=14`. Lemma 2.1's necessary condition
for reducibility is always satisfied and supplies no irreducible arithmetic sequence. This pinpoints
why the 2024 diagonal-line certificate cannot reject the candidate, consistently with rigidity.

## Consequence for the programme

Generalized arithmetic sequences remain a valid surviving positive family, but they cannot contain
the public candidate or any family specializing to its exact minimal-generator pattern. A family
search must instead explain the block-and-hole pattern near the multiplicity and the equality of
the length-two Leamer layer with sums of length-one elements.
