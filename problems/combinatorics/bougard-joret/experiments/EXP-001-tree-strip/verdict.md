# EXP-001 verdict: CONFIRMED

Date: 2026-09-04. The universal result is [D]; the enumerated controls are [MV].

## Result

For every $k\ge3$, $f(2k,k-1,k)=k^2$. All extremal graphs are characterized by a nonstar residual tree and fibers of sizes $\deg_T(t)-1$. The complete proof is [proof.md](proof.md), Sections 1-4. Its derivation is independent of the published preceding-boundary theorem. Section 5 distinguishes the new $k\ge4$ numerical family from the known $k=3$ case.

## Adversarial proof audit

A separate research lane independently reviewed necessity, independence, connectivity and prior scope. It found an omitted case in the initial informal connectivity reasoning: exactly two surviving tree vertices need not share a surviving independent-set neighbor. This was repaired before the computational run and explicitly included in the committed hypothesis. The double-star argument in proof Section 3 closes that case. It is not permissible to infer connectivity from degree equality alone.

The audit also rejected an unwarranted stronger interpretation: tree representations are not asserted to be unique for unmarked graphs. Novelty searches and primary final-section reads found no matching prior classification, but cannot prove absolute priority. Das-Gupta retain priority for the original disproof and boundary classification.

## Exploration moment and consequence

The invariant-first view converts an unrestricted extremal graph search into a tree classification. The missing-neighbor fibers explain why the residual-connectivity cost obstructs the preceding boundary yet permits regular extremals one vertex later. Next: generalize the fiber method to $n=\alpha+k+1$ with $k-\alpha\ne1$, where the residual graph need not be a tree. Declare a new experiment before computing that extension.

## How could this be wrong?

The written proof could contain a reasoning error missed by the adversarial audit; this is not a proof-assistant formalization or peer review. Tests cover only $k\le8$ and do not prove the universal quantifier. An unlocated prior publication could reduce novelty. The result does not solve the entire Bougard-Joret problem or certify the corrected general conjecture. Publication must retain these scope distinctions.
