## 1. Definitions and exact problem statements

### 1.1 Ext-index, uniform Auslander condition, AB ring

[HJ03, Section 3, arXiv p. 7] [V]: "Let R be a commutative ring. We define the Ext-index of R to be sup{ n | Ext^i_R(M,N) = 0 for all i > n and Ext^n_R(M,N) != 0 }, where the sup is taken over all pairs of finitely generated R-modules (M,N) with Ext^i_R(M,N) = 0 for all i >> 0."

[HJ03, Definition 3.0] [V]: "If R is a local Gorenstein ring of finite Ext-index, we say that R is an AB ring." The name AB stands for both Auslander-Bridger and Avramov-Buchweitz [HJ03, intro] [V].

[JS04, Section 1] [V]: "(uac) There exists an integer n such that for all finitely generated R-modules M and N, if Ext^i_R(M,N) = 0 for all i >> 0 then Ext^i_R(M,N) = 0 for all i >= n." [JS04, 4.] [V]: "(ab) R is Gorenstein and there exists an integer n such that ... Ext^i_R(M,N) = 0 for all i >> 0 implies Ext^i_R(M,N) = 0 for all i > n. ... Note that the condition (ab) is the Uniform Auslander Condition (uac) from Section 1 together with the requirement that R is Gorenstein."

[LN26, intro] [V] uses the same formulation: "R is an AB ring if it is Gorenstein and satisfies the uniform Auslander condition, that is, the value Ext-index(R) := sup{ n | Ext^{i>n}_R(M,N) = 0 and Ext^n_R(M,N) != 0 } is finite; here, the supremum is taken over all pairs of finitely generated R-modules M and N with Ext^{i>>0}_R(M,N) = 0. When R is Gorenstein, by [HJ03, Proposition 3.2] we have Ext-index(R) < infinity if and only if Ext-index(R) = dim(R)."

Trivial vanishing / Tor-friendly. [JS04, 1.2] [V]: "(tv) For any pair (M,N) of finitely generated R-modules, if Ext^i_R(M,N) = 0 for all i >> 0, then either M has finite projective dimension or N has finite injective dimension." [AINS22, 3.1] [V]: "We say that R is Tor-friendly if for every pair (M,N) of finite R-modules Tor^R(M,N) is bounded only if proj dim_R M or proj dim_R N is finite." [LM20, Section 3] [V] calls the Tor version "trivial Tor-vanishing" and the Ext version "trivial vanishing" and proves they agree when R has a canonical module (their Prop. 3.2(2), quoted as "(2) If R has a canonical module, the two trivial vanishing conditions (Ext and Tor) [agree]"). [LN26, footnote 1] [V]: "The Tor-friendly condition is same as the trivial vanishing condition considered in [JS04] and [LM20]." Tor-friendly implies (uac) [JS04, 1.2 proof of Prop 1.1] [V].

Known AB classes (all [V] in the cited source): complete intersections [HJ03, Cor. 3.4 arXiv / 3.5 journal]; Gorenstein rings of minimal multiplicity e = embdim - dim + 2 [HJ03, Thm 3.5 arXiv / 3.6 journal] (they even satisfy (tv)); Gorenstein rings of codimension <= 4 [Se03, Theorem 2 = Thm 3.4]; Golod rings satisfy (uac) [JS04, Prop. 1.4]; small rings with m^2 = 0, or edim - dim <= 2, or m^3 = 0 and edim = 3, or m^3 = 0 and length <= 7 satisfy (uac) [JS04, Prop. 1.1]. Characterization by depth formulas: "A Gorenstein local ring of positive dimension satisfies (dep) if and only if it is an AB ring" [KLOT23, Thm 1.2] [V]; for CM rings, "R satisfies (UAC) with b_R = d" is equivalent to derived (ldep) [KLS25, Thm 1.1 (6)] [V].

### 1.2 The localization problem, exact wording

[HJ03, Section 6 "Questions", arXiv p. 21] [V], verbatim:

> "This work leaves quite a few questions unresolved. We list a few for further study. Perhaps the most intriguing is
> 1. Are all local Gorenstein rings AB rings?
> Some other interesting questions are:
> 2. Let R and S be AB rings which are essentially of finite type over a field k. Is R (x)_k S locally an AB ring?
> 3. Are localizations of AB rings AB rings?
> 4. Are AB rings Ext-bounded?"

Question 1 was answered negatively by [JS04] (artinian Gorenstein non-AB rings; needs a field not algebraic over a finite field) [V]. Question 3 is the localization problem, answered negatively by [LN26] [V]. Questions 2 and 4 are not addressed by [LN26]; the scout found no source settling them [U, absence of evidence only]. Note that [HJ03, Prop. 3.3(3) arXiv] proves Ext-bounded implies AB, so Question 4 asks for the converse.

[NSTV19, Remark 3.2] [V] records the state before [LN26]: "we would love to have an example like the above one with type 1, since that would answer the question [HJ03, Section 6, #3] of whether the AB property localizes. ... since Gorenstein rings with decomposable maximal ideals are hypersurfaces by Corollary 2.7, and hypersurfaces are AB rings ..., our methods here will not yield such an example." Their Theorem A [V]: a complete local CM ring R of Krull dimension 1, type 2, multiplicity 13, embedding codepth 6 satisfying (UAC), with a prime p such that R_p is Gorenstein of multiplicity 12, embedding dimension 5 and does not satisfy (AC); R_p is exactly the [JS04] ring A_alpha over k((Y)). So (UAC) was already known not to localize among CM rings; the open point was the Gorenstein (AB) case.

### 1.3 Conjectures (L), (E), (P) of Nasseh-Yoshino

[NY09, Section 1] [V], verbatim:

> "Conjecture (L): Let R be a ring and let p in Spec(R). If R is of finite Ext-index, then so would be the localization R_p.
> Conjecture (E): Let R be an algebra over a field k and let L be a finitely generated extension field of k. If R is of finite Ext-index, then so would be the ring R (x)_k L.
> Conjecture (P): Let R be a ring. If R is of finite Ext-index, then so would be the polynomial ring R[x]."

Here "ring" means commutative Noetherian ring with unity and Ext-index is defined for non-local rings too [NY09, p.1] [V]. Relations [NY09, Prop. 2.8] [V]: (1) (P) for all CM local rings of dimension one implies (L) for all CM local rings with dualizing module; (2) (P) for a k-algebra R implies (E) for R and simple algebraic extensions; (3) (L)+(E) for all Gorenstein rings containing a field implies (P) for Gorenstein rings of finite Krull dimension containing a field. Tools [NY09, Lemma 2.1] [V]: (1) faithfully flat R -> S gives Ext-index(R) <= Ext-index(S); (3) for a non-zero-divisor x, Ext-index(R/xR) <= Ext-index(R) - 1; (4) for CM local R with dualizing module and x in m a nzd, finite Ext-index of R/xR implies finite Ext-index of R. [NY09, Cor. 2.3] [V]: "If R is an Artinian ring of finite Ext-index, then so is R_p for every prime ideal p." [NY09, Lemma 2.4 and Cor. 2.5] [V]: finite Ext-index localizes at maximal ideals of CM rings and passes to completion of CM local rings. [NY09, Prop. 2.9] [V]: (P) holds for artinian Gorenstein R of finite Ext-index whose residue fields are algebraically closed. Positive case of (E): [NY09, Thm 4.2] for finite dimensional k-algebras and k(x) "under a mild assumption" [V, statement not transcribed].

Consequence of [LN26] for these conjectures: (L) is false (the AB counterexample is a Gorenstein local ring of finite Ext-index whose localization has infinite Ext-index); (P) is false [LN26, Cor. 2.8]; (E) is not addressed in [LN26] [V]. Observation [U, scout]: [LN26] Theorem 2.4 exhibits R_p = A_{1+t} over k((t)) as non-AB, and R/tR = A_1 over k is AB; note that A_1 (x)_k k((t)) = A_1 over k((t)) is still A_1, not A_{1+t}, so (E) is not refuted by this example; whether A_alpha (x)_k L can fail (uac) for some finite extension L is not touched.

## 2. The counterexample in full detail

### 2.1 The Jorgensen-Sega family A_alpha (originally Gasharov-Peeva)

[JS04, Section 2] [V], with alpha re-extracted from the PDF: "Let k be a field and let alpha in k be a nonzero element. ... Consider the polynomial ring k[X1,X2,X3,X4,X5] in five (commuting) variables and set A_alpha = k[X1,...,X5]/I_alpha, where I_alpha is the ideal generated by the following quadric relations:

    alpha X1X3 + X2X3,  X1X4 + X2X4,  X3^2 + alpha X1X5 - X2X5,
    X4^2 + X1X5 - X2X5,  X1^2,  X2^2,  X3X4,  X3X5,  X4X5,  X5^2.

By [GP90], A_alpha is a local Gorenstein ring with Hilbert series 1 + 5t + 5t^2 + t^3. As a k-vector space, it has a basis consisting of the 12 elements 1, x1, x2, x3, x4, x5, x1x2, x1x3, x1x4, x1x5, x2x5, x1x2x5." Also B_alpha = A_alpha/(x5), Hilbert series 1 + 4t + 3t^2, length 8, m^3 = 0, edim 4 [JS04, Section 2 and 3.5] [V]. Both are Koszul (the generators form a Grobner basis in revlex) [JS04, 2.1] [V]. The module M is the image of the 2x2 matrix [[x1, alpha x3],[x4, x2]] on A^2; its minimal resolution is the doubly infinite complex C with differential d_i = [[x1, alpha^i x3],[x4, x2]] (constant Betti numbers 2, nonperiodic when o(alpha) = infinity) [JS04, Lemma 2.2] [V]. Test modules T_q = A/(x1 - x2, x1 - alpha^q x3, x1 - x4, x5), Hilbert series 1 + t [JS04, Section 3] [V].

Which alpha give non-AB rings [JS04, Prop. 3.1, Cor. 3.3, Remark 3.4] [V]: let o(alpha) be the order of alpha in k^* and s = 0 if o(alpha) = infinity, s = o(alpha) otherwise. Then for every integer q and every i > 0: Tor^A_i(M,T_q) != 0 iff i is congruent to q-1 or q mod s; Ext^i_A(M,T_q) != 0 iff i is congruent to q-1 or q mod s; Ext^i_A(T_q,M) != 0 iff i is congruent to -q or -q-1 mod s. Hence when o(alpha) = infinity, Ext^i_A(M,T_q) != 0 iff i in {0, q-1, q}, which violates (uac) (and even Auslander's conjecture for fixed M), so "when o(alpha) = infinity, the rings A = A_alpha and B = B_alpha provide counterexamples to Auslander's Conjecture" (Remark 3.4). When o(alpha) = s is finite, the nonvanishing recurs periodically with period s (Remark 3.7), so the T_q do not witness failure of (uac); [JS04] makes no claim on whether A_alpha is AB for alpha of finite order [V, absence in text]. Also [JS04, Prop. 3.9] [V]: there is an A-module V (Hilbert series 2 + 2t) with Tor^A_i(M,V) = 0 and Ext^i_A(M,V) = 0 for all i > 0 while neither module has finite projective dimension, so A_alpha (any alpha? the proof is stated for the fixed alpha of the section; the scout did not verify whether it needs o(alpha) = infinity [U]) fails (te) and (tv). [JS04, Prop. 3.13] [V]: when o(alpha) = infinity, A_alpha satisfies the symmetry (4) Ext^{>>0}(M,N) = 0 iff Ext^{>>0}(N,M) = 0 for this particular M. [JS04, 4.7] [V]: these examples are minimal with respect to inf{n : m^n = 0} (= 4) and with respect to embedding dimension (Gorenstein rings with edim - dim <= 4 are AB by [Se03]), and in the standard graded case minimal with respect to length (< 12 forces m^3 = 0 or edim <= 4).

Field hypothesis: the main theorem of [JS04] assumes "k a field which is not algebraic over a finite field" so that an alpha of infinite order exists [V].

### 2.2 The Lyle-Nasseh ring R

[LN26, Convention 2.1] [V], verbatim: "Throughout this section, let k be any field and S := k[[x1, x2, x3, x4, x5]]. Consider the ideal I of S[[t]] generated by the forms

    (1+t) x1x3 + x2x3,  x1x4 + x2x4,  x3^2 + (1+t) x1x5 - x2x5,
    x4^2 + x1x5 - x2x5,  x1^2,  x2^2,  x3x4,  x3x5,  x4x5,  x5^2

and let R := S[[t]]/I. Note that R is a complete local ring."

So R is the [JS04] family with alpha = 1 + t treated as a variable over any field. Invariants [V unless marked]: complete local, Gorenstein, Krull dimension 1, t a non-zero-divisor, R/tR = A_1 over k (length 12, Hilbert series 1+5t+5t^2+t^3), so multiplicity e(R) = 12 [U, scout: e(R) = length(R/tR) since t is a parameter and R is CM]; embedding dimension 6 [U-count, see 0]; codimension (embedding codepth) 5 [U-count]; R is a finite free k[[t]]-module of rank 12 (proof of Prop. 2.2) [V]. The prime is p = (x1, x2, x3, x4, x5) R [LN26, Thm 2.4] [V]; R_p = A_{1+t} over the field k((t)) [V].

Ten relations, all quadrics; four of the ten depend on t. Compared with [NSTV19, Thm A], which needed a fiber product S x_k k[[Z]] with S = A_alpha[[Y]] (type 2, edim 7), [LN26] keeps type 1 by letting t enter the coefficients rather than as a new direction [U, scout comparison].

### 2.3 Theorems and proof architecture

Proposition 2.2 [V]: "R is Gorenstein of dimension 1 with non-zero-divisor t." Proof: R is a finitely generated module over the DVR V = k[[t]]; write R = V^r + T with T torsion; r + mu_V(T) = 12 from R/tR = A_1; R (x)_V K = A_{1+t} over K = k((t)) has K-dimension 12 so r = 12 and T = 0; hence depth R > 0, R is CM of dimension 1, t is a parameter and a nzd, and R/tR Gorenstein forces R Gorenstein.

Theorem 2.3 [V]: "R is an AB ring." Proof: it suffices (HJ Prop. 3.2(1) arXiv) to show U := R/tR = A_1 is AB. Set Q = S/J, where J is generated by

    x1x3 + x2x3,  x1x4 + x2x4,  x3^2 + x1x5 - x2x5,
    x4^2 + x1x5 - x2x5,  x1^2 - x2^2,  x3x4,  x3x5,  x4x5,  x5^2.

Then U = Q/(x1^2). Claim: x1^2 is a nzd in Q. Proof: view Q over V = k[[x1]]; A := Q/(x1) = k[[x2,x3,x4,x5]]/(x2x3, x2x4, x3^2 - x2x5, x4^2 - x2x5, x2^2, x3x4, x3x5, x4x5, x5^2) has k-basis 1, x2, x3, x4, x5, x2x5, so m_A^3 = 0 and length 6; Q (x)_V K with y_i = x_i/x1 is K[[y2..y5]]/((1+y2)y3, (1+y2)y4, y3^2 + y5(1-y2), y4^2 + (1-y2)y5, 1 - y2^2, y3y4, y3y5, y4y5, y5^2) with K-basis 1, y2, y3, y4, y5, y2y5; so Q = V^6, depth Q > 0, Q is CM of dimension 1 with x1 and x1^2 non-zero-divisors. Then Q and A are Gorenstein (A = Q/(x1)). "As A has embedding codimension 4, satisfies the equality m_A^3 = 0, and has the k-vector space dimension 6, it follows from [Se03, Corollary 1.8] that A is Tor-friendly" (A is Gorenstein of minimal multiplicity with codim >= 3). "Then, by [AINS22, Proposition 2.3] or [LM20, Proposition 3.6] the ring Q is also Tor-friendly. Therefore, Q satisfies the uniform Auslander condition; see, e.g., [JS04, 1.2]. This means that Q is an AB ring. It then follows from [HJ03, Proposition 3.3(1)] that R/tR = U = Q/(x1^2) is also an AB ring. Another application of [HJ03, Proposition 3.3(1)] implies that R is also an AB ring."

So the architecture is: A (artinian Gorenstein, Hilbert series 1 + 4t + t^2, Tor-friendly) <- Q (1-dimensional Gorenstein, Tor-friendly by ascent along a regular element in m minus m^2) -> U = Q/(x1^2) (artinian, AB because AB is stable under modding out a nzd in both directions, but NOT Tor-friendly since x1^2 lies in m^2, an embedded deformation) -> R (1-dimensional AB, R/tR = U). The key phrase [LN26, intro] [V]: "build R in such a way that modding out an appropriate linear form gives a ring with embedded deformation, and to take advantage of the fact that such deformations preserve the AB property."

Theorem 2.4 [V]: "Consider the prime ideal p = (x1,x2,x3,x4,x5) of R. The localization R_p is not an AB ring." Proof: R_p is A_alpha over k((t)) with alpha = 1 + t; alpha has infinite order in k((t)) for every field k (if (1+t)^n = 1 then char k = p > 0; write n = p^q m with p not dividing m; then 1 = (1 + t^{p^q})^m has t^{p^q} coefficient m != 0, contradiction); by [JS04, Remark 3.4] R_p is not AB.

Corollary 2.8 [V]: "The polynomial ring R[z] does not satisfy the uniform Auslander condition, that is, Ext-index(R[z]) is not finite." Proof: take a nzd f in (x1..x5,t) minus p; R_p = (R_f)_{pR_f}; R_f is artinian and R_p is not AB so by [NY09, Cor. 2.3] R_f is not AB; f z - 1 is a nzd in R[z] and R_f = R[z]/(fz - 1), so [NY09, Lemma 2.1(3)] gives that R[z] is not of finite Ext-index. The proof "uses the same argument as in [NY09, Theorem 2.6(1)]" (this is Prop. 2.8(1) in the arXiv version) [V]. This provides "a negative answer to [NY09, Conjecture (P)]" [V].

Remarks [V]:
- Remark 2.5: "a key point of the proof that R/tR is an AB ring lies in the fact that R/tR has an embedded deformation in the sense of Avramov. It follows from [Se03, Corollary 4.4] that if R is an artinian ring that is not a hypersurface, then embedded deformations always preclude Tor-friendliness. In particular, from [LM20, Proposition 3.6], we have that the ring R, even though it is AB, is not Tor-friendly."
- Remark 2.6: in characteristic 2 the change of variables x1 -> b, x2 -> a + b, x3 -> c, x4 -> d, x5 -> e gives U = k[[a,b,c,d,e]]/(a^2, b^2, ac, ad, c^2 + ae, d^2 + ae, cd, ce, de, e^2) = B (x)_k k[[b]]/(b^2) with B = k[[a,c,d,e]]/(a^2, ac, ad, c^2 + ae, d^2 + ae, cd, ce, de, e^2); A = B and Q = U[[b]] in this situation. (The text says "the ring U from the proof of Theorem 2.4" and "the A of Theorem 2.4"; both are defined in the proof of Theorem 2.3; a typo [U, scout].)
- Remark 2.7: for general alpha, with Q_alpha = S/(alpha x1x3 + x2x3, x1x4 + x2x4, x3^2 + alpha x1x5 - x2x5, x4^2 + x1x5 - x2x5, x1^2 - x2^2, x3x4, x3x5, x4x5, x5^2) one still has A_alpha = Q_alpha/(x1^2), but (alpha^2 - 1) x1^2 x3 = 0 in Q_alpha (equation 2.7.1), so x1 and x1^2 are zero-divisors unless alpha = 1 or alpha = -1; "In fact, it follows as in the proof of Theorem 2.3 that A_alpha cannot have an embedded deformation when alpha has infinite order in k."

## 3. What remains open after [LN26]

Explicitly stated in [LN26] [V]:
- (O1) Whether the authors' original, larger candidate ring is itself a counterexample ("remains open", AI disclosure).
- (O2) The role of alpha: A_alpha with alpha = +-1 admits the embedded deformation Q_alpha; A_alpha with alpha of infinite order has none (Remark 2.7). Nothing is said about alpha of finite order > 2; whether A_alpha is AB for such alpha is not addressed in [LN26] or [JS04] [U, absence in both texts]. Note (uac) is trivially inherited from finite order only in the sense that the T_q witnesses become periodic (Prop. 3.1); a different witness may exist.

Not stated in [LN26] but left open by the construction (scout's reading, each marked [U]):
- (O3) Minimal dimension. An artinian counterexample to localization is impossible: [NY09, Cor. 2.3] shows finite Ext-index localizes for artinian rings (localizing an artinian ring at a prime is a direct factor). So dimension 1 is the minimum for a counterexample, and [LN26] attains it [V for the bound, U that this is recorded anywhere as a remark].
- (O4) Minimal embedding dimension / codimension / multiplicity of a counterexample. [LN26] has edim 6, codim 5, e = 12, R_p with edim 5, e = 12 [U-count]. Since Gorenstein rings of codim <= 4 are AB [Se03, Thm 2] and localization can only drop codimension [U, standard], any counterexample needs codim(R_p) >= 5, hence codim R >= 5; [LN26] is codim 5 (if the count is right) and therefore minimal in codimension [U]. For multiplicity of R_p, [JS04, 4.7] gives 12 as minimal in the standard graded case only; [NSTV19, Remark 3.4 and Question 3.5] asked for type 2 examples with multiplicity 8 and codepth 4 or 5 for the (UAC)-vs-(AC) problem (non-Gorenstein) and noted it was unclear "what happens, say, when r(R_p) = 2 or e(R) = 8" [V]. Whether an AB counterexample with e(R_p) < 12 exists is open [U].
- (O5) Characteristic dependence. None: k is arbitrary in [LN26] [V]; the only place characteristic enters is the pleasant form of Remark 2.6 in char 2 [V]. Whether a counterexample exists over a finite residue field with R_p having a finite residue field is a different matter: R_p has residue field k((t)), never finite, and [JS04] needs alpha of infinite order, so an example with all residue fields algebraic over finite fields would require a new non-AB artinian Gorenstein ring [U].
- (O6) Excellent/essentially-of-finite-type versions: [LN26] R is complete. [NSTV19, Thm A(b)] made their (UAC) example the completion of a ring essentially of finite type; the same should hold here since I has polynomial generators [U]. [KLOT23, Thm 1.1] provides a machine to pass from complete local rings to UFDs with isolated singularities preserving properties; combining it with [LN26] to get a Gorenstein UFD that is AB with a non-AB localization is plausible but not done [U].
- (O7) Huneke-Jorgensen Question 2 (tensor products of AB rings essentially of finite type) and Question 4 (are AB rings Ext-bounded?) remain open as far as the scout found [U]. Note [Se03, Example 3.7] uses a tensor-product-type ring R = (S (x)_k T)_p with e_R(M,N) <= dim R for non-CI Gorenstein S, T, in the direction of Question 2 but for a different property (finite CI-dimension) [V].
- (O8) Conjecture (E) of [NY09] remains unrefuted [V, absence]. Conjecture (L) and (P) are refuted [V].
- (O9) The Gorenstein case of "does (uac) localize in dimension d" for d fixed is settled for d = 1 (no) and d = 0 (yes, trivially). The non-Gorenstein CM version was settled by [NSTV19]. Nothing else to settle in this direction except invariants (O4) [U].
- (O10) Whether AB is preserved by polynomial extension in the local-to-local sense (R -> R[z]_{(m,z)}): [LN26, Cor. 2.8] refutes finiteness of Ext-index for the whole ring R[z]. The scout notes that Ext-index of R[z] localized at (m, z) is finite (R[z]_{(m,z)}/(z) = R, apply [NY09, Lemma 2.1(4)]) [U, standard]; the failure is at the maximal ideal (p, fz - 1), i.e. at a point of the "generic fibre". This is the same phenomenon as (L) [U].
- (O11) [KLS25, Example 5.15] [V] shows (ldep) and (rdep) need not localize for CM rings via a fiber product; whether the [LN26] ring gives a Gorenstein example where derived (ldep) fails to localize follows from [KLS25, Thm 1.1(6)] plus [LN26] [U, immediate consequence, not stated anywhere the scout read].

## 4. Feasibility of exact computation

Local toolchain check (2026-09-03): no Macaulay2 or Singular binary is on this Windows machine (`which M2 Singular` empty) [V]. Python 3.13 with pypdf is available; a CAS would have to be installed (M2 via WSL or the Windows build; Singular via Cygwin build) before any of the computations below [V].

What is computable and certifiable:
- (C1) Ext^i_A(M,N) for fixed i over an artinian ring A of length 12 for modules of small length: exact linear algebra over k. [JS04, 2.6.1-2.6.3] [V] reduce Ext^i_A(M,N) and Tor^A_i(M,N) to kernels and images of the 2x2 matrices d_i = [[x1, alpha^i x3],[x4, x2]] acting on N^2, so for a fixed N of k-dimension e each i is a rank computation on a 2e x 2e matrix over k (or over k(alpha) with alpha transcendental, or over a finite field for alpha of finite order). Macaulay2 computes Ext^i(M,N) over any quotient ring for any specified i via a length-limited resolution (the `Ext^ZZ(Module,Module)` core function; the resolution over a non-regular ring is computed to the requested degree) [U, from M2 documentation index; the specific doc page was not fetched successfully]. Over an artinian Koszul ring with constant Betti numbers 2, resolving M to degree 200 is cheap [U].
- (C2) Certifying (uac) or its failure is NOT a finite computation in general: it quantifies over all pairs of finitely generated modules. Failure is certified by a finite witness (a pair (M,N) and exhibited vanishing/nonvanishing pattern proved for all i, as [JS04] does with the modular pattern, which is a proof, not a computation). Success is certified only via a structural theorem: Tor-friendliness from [Se03, Thm 1] (codim <= 4 Gorenstein with no embedded deformation), [Se03, Cor. 1.8] (minimal multiplicity), [AINS22, Thm 4.5] (m^3 = 0 with Hilbert series 1 + ez + sz^2: Tor-friendly if s = 0 or e^2 - 4s is not a perfect square), [AINS22, Thm 5.1 cases (a)-(h)] (includes: length <= 7, codim <= 3, Gorenstein codim <= 4, Golod, etc., all [V] in the source though the full list of cases (a)-(h) was not transcribed [U]); then (uac) via [JS04, 1.2] and AB via [HJ03, Prop. 3.2 arXiv] along regular sequences. All of these hypotheses (Hilbert series, codim, m^3 = 0, minimal multiplicity, Golod via Poincare series of k, embedded deformation via the type of the denominator polynomial d(t) with d(1) = 0 [Se03, 2.2]) are finitely computable in Macaulay2 (Hilbert series; `DGAlgebras` package for deviations, acyclic closure, Tor algebra, Koszul homology; Golod test via Poincare series comparison) [V for the package's function list: deviations, deviationsToPoincare, acyclicClosure, torAlgebra, homologyAlgebra, koszulComplexDGA].
- (C3) Detecting an embedded deformation of a complete local ring is decidable for Gorenstein codim <= 4 via [Se03, 2.2]: "The ring R admits an embedded deformation if and only if d(1) = 0, if and only if R is of type GH(p), with p = l" [V], and the type is read off from the Tor algebra Tor^P(R,k) (Kustin-Miller classification), computable with `torAlgebra` [U that the classification map is implemented; the classification itself is in the literature]. For codim 5 (the relevant case for (O4)) there is no general decision procedure known to the scout [U]; Avramov's characterization (embedded deformation implies a specific structure of the homotopy Lie algebra; "R admits an embedded deformation if and only if d(1) = 0" is specific to codim <= 4) [U].
- (C4) A search over "small Gorenstein artinian rings or their deformations" as a finite certifiable surface: the Ext-index question over a fixed artinian ring is not finitely decidable in general (C2), so a search can only (i) hunt for non-AB witnesses of the [JS04] shape (modules with constant Betti numbers and a one-parameter twist alpha^i), which is a finite computation per (ring, module, N, i-range) but yields a proof only when the pattern can be closed by hand, or (ii) certify AB via the structural criteria in (C2), which fail exactly on the interesting rings (those with embedded deformations and codim >= 5). Concretely, over k with alpha of finite order s, the [JS04] argument yields periodic nonvanishing; a certificate that A_alpha is AB for such alpha would require a new theorem, not a computation [U]. The one finite, certifiable sub-surface the scout can identify: for a fixed candidate one-parameter family R_alpha over k[[t]] (or over k[t]_{(t)}) with fibre A_1 having an embedded deformation Q with Q/(x) Tor-friendly by (C2) criteria, everything needed to reproduce the [LN26] argument (rank over the DVR, Hilbert series, Gorensteinness, minimal multiplicity of the deformed fibre) is finite exact linear algebra plus Grobner bases; the non-AB side then requires an explicit witness family as in [JS04]. So "deformations of Jorgensen-Sega-type witnesses" is a finite surface; "all small Gorenstein artinian rings" is not [U].
- (C5) Field independence of the nonvanishing computations: the ranks in [JS04, Prop. 3.1] depend only on whether alpha^{i-q} = 1 [V], so a computer check over Q(alpha) or over F_p(alpha) is a faithful model; over finite fields every alpha has finite order, which is exactly why [JS04] needs a non-algebraic field and why [LN26] passes to k((t)) [V].

## 5. Candidate extension questions with attributable novelty

Each item lists what is new relative to the sources read; novelty claims are [U] unless a source explicitly poses the question.

- (E1) Minimal invariants of a localization counterexample. Determine the minimal (edim, e, codim) of a Gorenstein local ring R that is AB with a non-AB localization. Lower bounds available: dim >= 1 [NY09, Cor. 2.3]; codim(R_p) >= 5 [Se03, Thm 2]; e(R_p) >= 12 in the standard graded artinian case [JS04, 4.7]. Novelty: [LN26] does not discuss minimality; [NSTV19, Remark 3.4, Question 3.5] posed the analogous question for their type 2 examples [V]. Attributable as "the AB analogue of NSTV Question 3.5".
- (E2) Alpha of finite order. Decide whether A_alpha is AB when o(alpha) = s is finite and s > 2, starting with s = 3, 4 over F_4, F_5 or Q(zeta_s). [LN26, Remark 2.7] proves A_alpha has no embedded deformation Q_alpha of the given shape unless alpha = +-1 (the shape argument) and no embedded deformation at all when o(alpha) = infinity; the finite-order case is stated for neither property [V]. Novelty: explicitly the gap left by Remark 2.7.
- (E3) Counterexamples with all residue fields finite. Every known non-AB Gorenstein ring has a residue field that is not algebraic over a finite field ([JS04] hypothesis; [LN26] R_p has residue field k((t))). Question: is every Gorenstein local ring with residue field algebraic over F_p an AB ring, and if not, does the AB property localize for rings essentially of finite type over F_p? Novelty: not posed in any source read [U].
- (E4) Conjecture (E) of [NY09]. Test A_alpha (x)_k L for finite extensions L and for L = k(x): [NY09, Thm 4.2] covers k(x) under a mild assumption [V, assumption not transcribed]. A refutation would need a ring whose Ext-index is finite over k but infinite over some L, i.e. a witness module appearing only after base change; the [JS04] witnesses exist over the base field already, so a new mechanism is required [U]. Novelty: (E) is explicitly stated open in [NY09] and untouched by [LN26].
- (E5) Ext-boundedness (HJ Question 4). Is the [LN26] ring R Ext-bounded? R/tR = U = Q/(x1^2) with Q Tor-friendly; [HJ03, Prop. 3.2(3) arXiv] moves Ext-boundedness across nzd's, and Tor-friendly rings have bounded Ext-gaps trivially? Not automatic: Tor-friendly gives that vanishing for i >> 0 forces finite pd, but Ext-gap concerns finite windows of vanishing followed by nonvanishing [U]. Novelty: the first concrete AB, non-Tor-friendly ring with an explicit embedded deformation is a natural test case for Question 4, which no source read addresses.
- (E6) UFD / isolated singularity upgrade. Apply [KLOT23, Thm 1.1] to R (or to U) to obtain a Gorenstein UFD with isolated singularity that is AB but has a non-AB localization; check whether the KLOT technique preserves AB-ness of the ring while producing the same localization behaviour [U]. Novelty: immediate combination, not written anywhere the scout read.
- (E7) Depth-formula failure under localization for Gorenstein rings. Via [KLS25, Thm 1.1] and [KLOT23, Thm 1.2], the [LN26] ring gives a Gorenstein ring of positive dimension satisfying (dep) whose localization R_p (artinian) satisfies... note (dep) for artinian rings is not equivalent to AB (KLOT remark that the "only if" fails in dimension 0) [V], so the right statement is "derived (ldep) does not localize among Gorenstein rings"; [KLS25, Example 5.15] gives it only via non-Gorenstein fiber products [V]. Novelty: a one-line corollary worth recording.
- (E8) Tensor products (HJ Question 2). With R AB and essentially of finite type over k (the polynomial-generated version of [LN26] R, before completion [U]), is R (x)_k R locally AB? The localization failure already lives inside R, so the tensor question is not the obstruction; but tensoring two copies of A_1 or of Q might create embedded deformations that break Tor-friendliness while keeping AB [U]. Novelty: Question 2 is still open in the literature read.
- (E9) A computational atlas of the A_alpha family: tabulate, for alpha in {1, -1, primitive s-th roots for s <= 12, transcendental}, the Hilbert series of Q_alpha-type deformations, existence of embedded deformations via [Se03, 2.2] where applicable, and the Ext^i_A(M, T_q) patterns, as a certified dataset; this is finite (C1, C4) and would support (E2) and (E1) [U]. Novelty: no such tabulation exists in the sources read.
