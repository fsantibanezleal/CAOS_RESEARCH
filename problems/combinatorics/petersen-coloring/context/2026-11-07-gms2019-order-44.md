# PCB-014: Goedgebeur, Macajova, Skoviera 2019 read against the consequence-audit manuscript (2026-11-07)

Source: J. Goedgebeur, E. Macajova, M. Skoviera, "Smallest snarks with oddness 4 and cyclic connectivity 4 have
order 44", Ars Math. Contemp. 16 (2019) 277-298, DOI 10.26493/1855-3974.1601.e75; read in arXiv:1712.07867v2
(revised 2019-01-10), archived in the data root under `caos-research/petersen-coloring/sources/pcb014/`
(SHA-256 f8cce9e0029adac3e71c85f3977da21e15e23ba48846cf05e88bca8460f53e7e). Marks: [V] read in the primary text.

- Theorem 1 [V]: "The smallest number of vertices of a snark with cyclic connectivity 4 and oddness at least 4 is
  44. The girth of each such snark is at least 5."
- Definition [V] (Section 2.4): in that paper a snark is any connected cubic graph that cannot be 3-edge-coloured
  (the widest definition; triangles, digons and bridges allowed). Oddness is the least number of odd circuits in a
  2-factor of a bridgeless cubic graph.
- Scope [V] (Section 1): Theorem 1 does not determine the smallest nontrivial snark of oddness at least 4, because
  it does not exclude cyclically 5-edge-connected snarks of oddness at least 4 below 44 vertices. The sequel,
  arXiv:1901.10911, lists 31 snarks with cyclic connectivity 4 and oddness 4 on 44 vertices.

Against our record: `manuscripts/petersen-coloring/consequence-audit/main.tex` lines 302-304 say "The oddness 4 of
the 112-vertex graphs is consistent with the theorem that the smallest snarks with oddness at least 4 and cyclic
connectivity 4 have order 44 [GMS2019]". The paraphrase matches Theorem 1 as stated, and the consistency claim
holds (112 > 44; if a 112-vertex graph has cyclic connectivity above 4, the theorem does not apply and nothing
conflicts). The comparison moves from [U] to [V]; no manuscript change is needed.
