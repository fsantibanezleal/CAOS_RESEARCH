# Phase 0 gate: the dissipative question is already published, and it gives us a ground truth

Date: 2026-09-12. Closes NS-001 and NS-002 in part. This is the gate the plan said would either let
the work proceed or force a rewrite. It forces a rewrite, in a direction that makes the work sharper.

## What the gate asked

Whether the dissipative analysis derived on 2026-09-11 is already contained in the released
Alpoge-Buckmaster papers, and what the Cordoba-Martinez-Zoroa program already says about dissipation.

## Finding 1: the released papers are dissipation-free

Keyword census over the full extracted text of all four September 2026 manuscripts:

| manuscript | "viscos" | "dissipat" | "diffus" | "hypodissip" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alpoge-Buckmaster Boussinesq | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| Alpoge-Buckmaster Euler | 0 | 3 | 0 | 2 |
| Alpoge-Buckmaster-Coiculescu IPM | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| OpenAI Navier-Stokes | 60 | 13 | 12 | 1 |

In the Boussinesq paper the single `dissipat` hit and the single `hypodissip` hit are both inside the
AI-statement section, referring to their own unreleased writeup; the 24 `hypo` matches are 18
"hypotheses" and 4 "hypothesis". The three Alpoge-Buckmaster results are inviscid or Darcy throughout.
So the dissipative extension we derived is not duplicated in what they released.

## Finding 2: but the hypodissipative result is already published, by the originators

The Alpoge-Buckmaster Euler paper cites it, and we then obtained it in the primary source:

> D. Cordoba, L. Martinez-Zoroa, F. Zheng, *Finite time blow-up for the hypodissipative Navier Stokes
> equations with a force in $L^1_t C^{1,\epsilon}_x \cap L^{\infty}_t L^2_x$*, Arch. Ration. Mech.
> Anal. 250 (2026), article 38, [doi:10.1007/s00205-026-02198-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00205-026-02198-0),
> [arXiv:2407.06776v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06776) (v2 dated 2024-08-05).
> 395,139 bytes, sha256 `56801b195f3e378bf7b91dd6a04fc06b42da2a300e06250bf586ee83c35e4a34`. `[V]` abstract,
> Theorem 1 and Section 1.1; `[P]` the rest.

Their Theorem 1, transcribed:

> There is $\alpha_0 = \dfrac{22-8\sqrt{7}}{9} > 0$ such that for any $\alpha \in [0,\alpha_0)$, there
> exist solutions of the forced 3D incompressible fractional Navier-Stokes equations in
> $\mathbb{R}^3\times[0,1]$ with $\nu>0$ and an external forcing in
> $L^1_t([0,1])C^{1,\epsilon}_x \cap L^{\infty}_t L^2_x$, such that
> $u\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}\cap L^{\infty}_t L^2_x$, the velocity is smooth in $x$ on $0\le t<1$, and
> $\lim_{t\to 1}\int_0^t \lVert\nabla u(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\,ds = \infty$.

Their dissipation is written $|\nabla|^{\alpha} = (-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}$, so classical Navier-Stokes is
their $\alpha=2$. Ours was written $(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$, so classical is our $\alpha=1$ and
$\alpha_{\text{theirs}} = 2\alpha_{\text{ours}}$.

## Finding 3: our bound is consistent with theirs, and is loose by a factor of about five

$$\alpha_0 = \frac{22-8\sqrt{7}}{9} = 0.0926655\ldots \quad\text{(their convention)}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \alpha < 0.0463328\ldots \quad\text{(our convention)}.$$

Our derived cap was $\alpha \le 1/4$ in our convention. The published construction achieves blowup
only for $\alpha < 0.0463$, which is comfortably inside our cap. So:

- **The bound is not refuted.** A necessary condition that the published result satisfies.
- **The bound is not tight.** The ratio is $0.25/0.0463 = 5.40$. Something binds roughly five times
  harder than the frequency cap alone.
- **The two omissions we already flagged are the obvious candidates** for that factor: the recursion
  had no time axis, and dissipation was not charged during the holding intervals. Both push the real
  threshold down, which is the direction the discrepancy sits in.

## The landscape, assembled, in our convention $(-\Delta)^{\alpha}$

| $\alpha$ | status |
|---|---|
| $< 0.0463$ | blowup PROVED with rough forcing (Cordoba-Martinez-Zoroa-Zheng) |
| $\le 0.25$ | our frequency cap permits the layer cascade (necessary, not sufficient) |
| $= 1$ | classical Navier-Stokes; open, except the unverified OpenAI forced claim |
| $\ge 1.25$ | global regularity PROVED (Lions; their $\alpha\ge5/2$) |

The open window is $0.0463 < \alpha < 1.25$. Our cap is a statement about **this method** sitting
inside that window: the layer cascade specifically cannot reach beyond $1/4$, whatever else might.

## What this does to the plan

The bare threshold question is not ours to claim; Cordoba, Martinez-Zoroa and Zheng published it in
2024 and it appeared in ARMA in 2026. What the finding hands us instead is better than an open
question: **a published exact number to calibrate a reduced model against.**

The reframed target, replacing "locate $\alpha_c$":

> Does the reduced modulation model, with the time budget and the hold-interval damping put back in,
> predict $\alpha_0 = (22-8\sqrt{7})/9$?

This is a far stronger experiment than the original. It has a positive control with a known answer, it
is falsifiable in one number, and it is the exact check that our own methodology demands of any
reduced model before that model is used to say anything. If the reduction reproduces $0.0463$ from the
cascade constraints, the reduction has demonstrated predictive power and can then be pointed at
questions that are open, such as what smooth forcing costs in exponent. If it does not reproduce it,
we have learned that the layer bookkeeping is not what sets $\alpha_0$, which is also worth knowing
and is publishable as a negative result inside the problem record.

Note also that the Cordoba-Martinez-Zoroa-Zheng forcing is **rough**, $L^1_t C^{1,\epsilon}_x$. The
Alpoge-Buckmaster unreleased hypodissipative work is therefore almost certainly the smooth-forcing
upgrade of this exact theorem, the same relationship their IPM paper has to the published
Cordoba-Martinez-Zoroa IPM result. That is the pattern of the whole program: the originators prove it
with rough forcing, the upgrade makes the forcing smooth.
